Daycare business plan

Write a daycare or childcare center business plan with licensing steps, enrollment and staffing ratios, startup costs, pricing and financial projections.

Published
November 26, 2026
Works with
ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini
Level
Intermediate
By
GoodAIPromt editorial team

Starting a daycare involves more planning than most small businesses: licensing, staff-to-child ratios, safety requirements and tight margins all shape whether it works. This daycare business plan prompt builds a complete plan around your location, capacity and budget, with the costs and calculations shown so you can check them against local rules and quotes.

The prompt

Act as a small business consultant who has worked with childcare providers. Write a daycare business plan.

Type: 
Location: 
Ages served and planned capacity: 
Hours and schedule options: 
My background: 
Budget and funding: 
Local competition and prices (if known): 
Educational approach: 

Write the plan with:
1. Executive summary.
2. Services and educational approach.
3. Market analysis: local demand, competitors, target families — with gaps I should research marked {RESEARCH}.
4. Licensing and compliance checklist (licence, inspections, background checks, health and safety, insurance), noting these vary by location and must be confirmed with the local licensing agency.
5. Operations: daily schedule, staffing plan by age group with ratio placeholders to confirm locally, hiring and training.
6. Marketing plan for the first 6 months.
7. Startup cost list and monthly operating budget.
8. Pricing and a 3-year financial projection with enrollment ramp-up, showing the formulas.
9. Risks and how to manage them.

0 of 8 blanks filled

How to use it

  1. Look up your local licensing rules first and paste ratios and requirements into the prompt.
  2. Replace estimates with real quotes for rent, insurance and renovation.
  3. Run the plan past a local small business advisor before applying for funding.

Tips

  • Enrollment rarely starts full; plan cash for the ramp-up months.
  • Staff costs are usually the largest expense, so model several enrollment levels.
