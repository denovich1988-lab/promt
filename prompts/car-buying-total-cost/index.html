Compare cars by total cost of ownership

Compare cars by total cost of ownership, including price, financing, fuel or charging, insurance, maintenance and resale, to see which one really costs less.

Published
November 26, 2026
Works with
ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini
Level
Beginner
By
GoodAIPromt editorial team

The car with the lowest sticker price isn't always the cheapest to own. Fuel, insurance, maintenance, financing and depreciation can change the picture completely over five years. This prompt compares two or three cars by total cost of ownership using your own numbers, shows the yearly and monthly cost, and highlights which assumptions matter most.

The prompt

Act as a careful personal finance analyst. Compare these cars by total cost of ownership.

Cars (make, model, year, new/used, price incl. taxes and fees): , , 
How long I plan to keep the car: 
Miles I drive per year: 
Fuel or electricity: 
Financing: 
Insurance quotes: 
Expected maintenance and repairs: 
Expected resale value at the end: 
Incentives or rebates: 

Do this:
1. A table per car: purchase cost, total interest, fuel/charging, insurance, maintenance, registration, minus resale value = total cost, then cost per year, per month and per mile. Show the formulas.
2. A side-by-side summary and which car is cheapest overall.
3. Sensitivity: how the ranking changes if fuel prices rise 25%, if I drive 30% more, or if resale is 20% lower.
4. Non-cost factors to weigh (reliability record, safety, space), framed as questions to research.
5. A list of the numbers I marked "unknown" and where to get real quotes.

Use only my numbers; where one is missing, keep the line visible as {NEED QUOTE} instead of guessing.

0 of 10 blanks filled

How to use it

  1. Get real insurance quotes for each car; they often differ more than people expect.
  2. Use the same number of years for every car so the comparison is fair.
  3. Check the sensitivity section before deciding; a small gap can flip under different assumptions.

Tips

  • Include home charger installation if you're comparing an electric car.
  • This is a planning tool, not financial advice; check loan terms carefully before signing.
