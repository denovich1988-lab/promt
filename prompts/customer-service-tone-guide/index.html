Customer service tone guide for your team

Create a support tone-of-voice guide with do and don't examples, phrases for tricky moments and rewrites of your real replies.

Published
November 21, 2026
Works with
ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini
Level
Beginner
By
GoodAIPromt editorial team

Customers notice when one agent sounds warm and the next sounds like a legal notice. A tone guide keeps every reply consistent and on brand, especially as the team grows or uses AI drafts. This prompt builds a practical guide from your brand and a few of your real replies, with examples for the moments that are hardest to get right.

The prompt

You are a customer experience lead. Create a tone of voice guide for our support team.

Company and what we sell: 
Our customers and how they usually feel when they contact us: 
Brand personality in 3–4 words: 
Support channels (email, chat, phone, social): 
3–5 real replies from our team (anonymised): 

Create:
1. 4 tone principles, each with a one-line explanation, a "do" example and a "don't" example.
2. How tone should adjust by situation: simple question, bug or outage, angry customer, refund or cancellation, saying no, good news.
3. A phrase bank: openers, apologies that sound sincere, explaining delays, saying no kindly, closing lines. Plus 10 phrases to avoid and better alternatives.
4. Channel notes: length and formality for each channel.
5. Rewrites of my sample replies in the new tone, with a note on what changed.

Keep the guide short enough to read in 10 minutes. Use plain, human language.

0 of 5 blanks filled

How to use it

  1. Pick real replies that show both good and weak examples.
  2. Review the guide with your team and adjust phrases they would never say.
  3. Add it to your help desk and use it to brief any AI reply assistant too.

Tips

  • Saying no clearly and kindly is the hardest skill — practise it with real scenarios.
  • Update the phrase bank when you see a reply customers praise.
