An office Secret Santa can be a fun end-of-year tradition or an awkward obligation. The difference is in the rules: a clear budget, a real opt-out, inclusive wording, gift guidelines and a plan for remote staff. This prompt sets up the whole exchange (rules, timeline, sign-up message, reminders and a gift-ideas list) so whoever organises it can do so in an afternoon.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an HR coordinator organising a workplace gift exchange.

Team size and setup: [NUMBER OF PEOPLE, OFFICE / REMOTE / HYBRID]
Budget per gift: [AMOUNT]
Exchange date and format: [DATE, IN PERSON OR VIDEO CALL]
How names will be drawn: [TOOL OR METHOD]
Company culture and anything to avoid: [E.G. NO ALCOHOL, KEEP IT NEUTRAL FOR ALL BELIEFS]

Create:
1. Simple rules: budget, opt-in, gift guidelines (what's fine, what to avoid), anonymity, and what happens if someone can't attend.
2. A timeline from sign-up to exchange.
3. A sign-up message that makes it clearly optional and inclusive, with a name for the event that doesn't have to be religious (offer 3 options).
4. A short wish-list form with 5 questions (interests, likes, dislikes, allergies, delivery address for remote staff, kept private).
5. Two reminder messages.
6. 20 gift ideas within the budget, suitable for colleagues of any background.
7. A plan for remote staff, including shipping deadlines.
```

## How to use it

1. **Set the budget and date first**, then send the sign-up message.
2. **Keep wish lists and addresses private**, shared only with the matched giver.
3. **Send reminders** one week and two days before the exchange.

## Tips

- Have one or two spare gifts ready in case someone's giver drops out.
- Make sure nobody feels pressured to join; the opt-out should be easy and private.
