December is when many buyers spend remaining budget, and also when deals quietly slip to next year. A year-end sales push email works when it gives a real reason to decide now (budget timing, a price change, onboarding before January) instead of fake scarcity. This prompt writes short emails for each deal stage, using only the reasons you can actually stand behind.

## Prompt

```prompt
Write year-end sales push emails for my open deals.

What I sell: [PRODUCT OR SERVICE]
Typical buyer and decision process: [BUYER ROLE AND WHO SIGNS]
Real reasons to decide before year-end (only true ones): [REASONS, e.g. price increase on Jan 1, use this year's budget, go live before Q1 planning, implementation slots]
Offer I'm allowed to make, if any: [OFFER, or "none"]
Deadline: [DATE]

Write one email (under 120 words) for each stage:
1. Engaged but no proposal yet.
2. Proposal sent, waiting.
3. Verbally agreed, stuck in procurement or legal.
4. Went quiet over the last month.
For each: a subject line, one clear reason tied to their situation using [PROSPECT NAME] and [THEIR GOAL], one simple next step with a date, and a polite way out ("if January is better, tell me and I'll stop chasing").
Then write a short 3-touch schedule from now until the deadline. No fake urgency, no invented discounts.
```

## How to use it

1. **List only real reasons**; buyers can tell when urgency is invented.
2. **Sort your pipeline by stage** and personalize the first line of each email.
3. **Respect the "January is better" answer** and book the follow-up instead.
