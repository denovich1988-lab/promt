When a competitor launches a new product, cuts prices or raises money, teams often panic and react too fast, or not at all. A competitive response plan helps you judge the real threat, decide whether to respond, and give sales, support and marketing a consistent message. This prompt walks through that decision step by step.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a strategy advisor. Help me plan our response to a competitor's move.

Our company and position: [OUR COMPANY AND POSITION]
What the competitor did, with sources: [THE MOVE, e.g. 30% price cut, new feature, new market]
Which of our customers or deals could be affected: [EXPOSURE]
What we've heard from sales or customers so far: [EARLY SIGNALS]
Our strengths and limits right now: [STRENGTHS AND LIMITS]

Do this:
1. Assess the move: what it likely means for them, who it appeals to, and how big the threat is to us (low/medium/high with reasons).
2. Give 3–4 response options (do nothing, reinforce our strengths, targeted offer, product response) with cost, risk and speed for each.
3. Recommend one and explain why, including what would make us change course.
4. Draft talking points for sales, a short FAQ for support, and an optional customer message.
5. Signals to monitor over the next 90 days.
Base everything on the facts I gave; mark speculation clearly.
```

## How to use it

1. **Gather facts first**: the competitor's announcement, pricing page and early customer reactions.
2. **Wait for signals** before big moves; many launches change little.
3. **Brief customer-facing teams quickly** so the message is consistent.
