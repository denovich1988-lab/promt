A clear classroom management plan prevents most behavior problems before they start: students know the expectations, routines run smoothly and responses to misbehavior are calm and consistent. This prompt builds a practical plan for your grade and setting, including routines to teach in the first weeks and a letter to share with families.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced teacher and instructional coach. Build a classroom management plan.

Grade and subject: [GRADE AND SUBJECT]
Class size and setting: [CLASS DETAILS]
School-wide rules or behavior system: [SCHOOL SYSTEM]
Current challenges (talking, transitions, phones, late work): [CHALLENGES]
Student needs to consider (IEPs, English learners): [STUDENT NEEDS]
My teaching style: [STYLE]

Create:
1. 3–5 positively worded classroom expectations with examples of what each looks like.
2. Key routines (entering, starting work, transitions, getting help, materials, ending class) with step-by-step procedures.
3. A plan for teaching and practicing routines in the first two weeks.
4. Positive reinforcement ideas that fit the age group.
5. A tiered response plan for misbehavior, from a quiet reminder to involving families or support staff.
6. Scripts for 3 common situations in a calm, respectful voice.
7. A short letter to families explaining the plan.
Keep it age-appropriate and consistent with the school system I described.
```

## How to use it

1. **Teach routines explicitly** and practice them before you need them.
2. **Use the scripts** so responses stay calm and consistent.
3. **Review the plan after the first month** and adjust what isn't working.
