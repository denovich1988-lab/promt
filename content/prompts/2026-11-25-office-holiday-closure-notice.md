When an office closes over the holidays, several people need different messages: employees need dates, pay and on-call details, customers need to know when orders and support stop, and your website and email need a short notice and auto-reply. Writing each one separately leads to mismatched dates. This prompt writes the whole set from one list of facts, so every notice says the same thing.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an HR and communications assistant.

Company: [COMPANY NAME AND WHAT IT DOES]
Closure dates: [LAST WORKING DAY, CLOSED FROM–TO, FIRST DAY BACK]
Paid days and leave rules: [WHICH DAYS ARE PAID HOLIDAYS, WHICH NEED LEAVE]
Who stays on call and how to reach them: [ROLES, CONTACT METHOD]
Customer impact: [LAST ORDER OR SHIPPING DATE, SUPPORT HOURS, RESPONSE TIMES]
Tone: [WARM / FORMAL]

Write:
1. An internal email to employees with dates, which days are paid, leave rules, on-call details, what to finish before the break and a thank-you line.
2. A customer email with closure dates, last order and shipping dates, support availability and when replies resume.
3. A website banner (under 25 words) and a short FAQ entry.
4. An out-of-office auto-reply for staff (under 60 words).
5. A one-line social media post.

Use exactly the dates I gave. Put any date I didn't provide as [CONFIRM DATE].
```

## How to use it

1. **Confirm the dates and paid-holiday rules** with management first.
2. **Send the employee email at least two weeks ahead** and the customer email one to two weeks ahead.
3. **Turn on the banner and auto-replies** on the last working day.

## Tips

- Check that holiday pay rules match your contracts and local law.
- Pin the on-call contact in your team chat for the whole closure.
