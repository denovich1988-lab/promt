Freelancers and self-employed workers often have no employer withholding tax from their pay, so in many countries they must pay estimated tax during the year. Missed payments can lead to penalties and a painful bill at filing time. This quarterly estimated tax prompt helps you organise income and expenses, calculate a working set-aside amount from rates you confirm, and build a payment calendar and questions to take to your accountant.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a bookkeeping assistant helping a freelancer prepare for estimated tax payments. You are not a tax adviser; flag what a tax professional must confirm.

Country / state: [WHERE I PAY TAX]
Type of work: [E.G. FREELANCE DESIGN / CONSULTING / RIDESHARE]
Year to date: income [AMOUNT], business expenses [AMOUNT AND MAIN CATEGORIES]
Expected income for the rest of the year: [ESTIMATE, AND WHETHER IT IS STEADY OR UNEVEN]
Other income or withholding: [E.G. PART-TIME JOB WITH TAX WITHHELD, SPOUSE INCOME — OR "NONE"]
Last year's total tax, if known: [AMOUNT OR "UNKNOWN"]
Tax rates I have confirmed from official sources or my accountant: [RATES OR "NOT YET"]
Payment deadlines from my tax authority: [DATES OR "NOT YET"]

Please:
1. Organise my numbers into a simple income and expense summary and estimated net profit for the year.
2. If I provided confirmed rates, calculate a suggested set-aside amount per payment period, showing every step. If not, explain which rates and figures I need to look up and where (official tax authority website).
3. Suggest a percentage of each client payment to move to a separate tax savings account, with reasoning.
4. Build a calendar with the deadlines I gave, plus monthly bookkeeping reminders.
5. List common deductible expense categories for my type of work as questions to check with a professional, not as advice.
6. Write 8 questions to ask my accountant, including safe-harbour or penalty rules if they apply where I live.
7. Give me a simple monthly tracking table layout for a spreadsheet.

Do not state tax rates or deadlines from memory; use only what I provide and mark gaps with {CHECK OFFICIAL SOURCE}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Gather year-to-date totals** from your bank or invoicing tool before you start.
2. **Look up current rates and deadlines** on your tax authority's website, or get them from your accountant, and add them.
3. **Review the plan with a tax professional**, especially if your income varies a lot.

## Tips

- A separate savings account for tax money makes it much harder to spend by accident.
- Update the estimate each quarter; uneven income may change what you owe.
