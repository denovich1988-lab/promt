Focus groups can reveal how people talk about a product or problem, but a poor guide leads to one loud participant and polite, useless answers. This prompt builds a timed moderator guide with neutral questions and activities that get everyone involved.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a qualitative research moderator. Write a focus group discussion guide.

Research objective: [OBJECTIVE]
Product, concept or topic: [TOPIC]
Participants (who, how many): [PARTICIPANTS]
Session length: [MINUTES]
Materials I can show (concepts, ads, prototypes): [MATERIALS]

Include:
1. Moderator introduction script: purpose, no right or wrong answers, recording consent, ground rules.
2. Warm-up (5–10 minutes): easy questions about their habits related to the topic.
3. 3–4 main sections, each with 3–5 open questions and 2 neutral probes.
4. One or two activities (e.g. card sorting, rating concepts individually before discussing).
5. Techniques to involve quiet participants and manage dominant ones.
6. Wrap-up questions and thank-you.
7. Timings for each section that add up to the session length.
8. A note-taking template for observers.

Avoid leading questions and anything that reveals what answer we hope for.
```

## How to use it

1. **Agree on the objective** with stakeholders before writing questions.
2. **Run a short pilot** with one or two people to test timing.
3. **Summarise themes** across groups, not individual comments.

## Tips

- Have participants write their opinion first, then discuss — it reduces groupthink.
- Ask for a screener questionnaire to recruit the right participants.
