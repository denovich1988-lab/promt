A literature review is not a list of summaries; it shows how studies relate, agree and leave gaps. A matrix makes those links visible before you write. This prompt builds the matrix from papers you provide and drafts a thematic synthesis, citing only the sources you gave it.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an academic research assistant. Help me build a literature review matrix.

Research question: [YOUR QUESTION]
Field and level: [E.G. PUBLIC HEALTH, MASTER'S THESIS]
Sources (paste abstracts or notes, each with author and year): [SOURCES]
Citation style: [APA / HARVARD / MLA / OTHER]

Do this:
1. Create a table with columns: source (author, year), aim, method and sample, key findings, limitations, relevance to my question.
2. Group the sources into 3–5 themes and list which sources belong to each.
3. Identify where sources agree, where they disagree and possible reasons (method, sample, context).
4. List research gaps that my study could address.
5. Draft one synthesis paragraph per theme (120–180 words), comparing sources rather than summarising one by one, with in-text citations.

Rules: use only the sources I provided. Never add papers, authors or findings that are not in my input. If an abstract lacks a detail (e.g. sample size), write "not stated".
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste abstracts or your reading notes,** not just titles.
2. **Check every finding against the original paper** before using the draft.
3. **Rewrite the synthesis in your own words** and follow your institution's AI policy.

## Tips

- Add sources in batches of 8–10 and merge the matrices.
- Ask which themes have the weakest evidence; that's often your best gap.
