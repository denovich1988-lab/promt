Proposals often focus on the provider's process when clients want to see their own goals reflected. Clear scope and options also make saying yes easier. This prompt turns your discovery notes into a focused proposal with three pricing options and explicit boundaries.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a consultant who writes winning proposals. Write a project proposal.

My business and service: [WHAT YOU DO]
Client and their situation: [CLIENT, CONTEXT]
Discovery notes (goals, problems, budget hints, deadline): [NOTES]
What I plan to deliver: [DELIVERABLES]
Timeline: [PHASES AND DATES]
Pricing: [YOUR PRICES OR RATES]
Relevant past work I can mention: [REAL EXAMPLES]

Structure:
1. Executive summary: their goal and the result of the project, in their language.
2. Understanding of the situation (show you listened).
3. Approach: phases with what happens and what they get.
4. Scope: included, and clearly not included.
5. Timeline table.
6. Three options (e.g. essential, recommended, complete), with what changes in each and the price.
7. What I need from the client.
8. Terms: payment schedule, revisions, change requests, validity of the proposal.
9. Next steps to start.

Keep it under 2 pages. Use only past work I listed. Mark any detail I need to confirm with {CONFIRM}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Send it within 48 hours** of the discovery call.
2. **Walk the client through it on a call** rather than only emailing it.
3. **Point to the "not included" list** whenever scope creep starts.

## Tips

- Most clients pick the middle option — make it the one you want to deliver.
- Add an expiry date so the decision doesn't drag on.
