Hiring a freelancer or contractor on a handshake works until something goes wrong: a missed deadline, a dispute over who owns the work, or a question about whether the person was really an employee. An independent contractor agreement sets out the scope, payment, ownership of work, confidentiality and how either side can end the arrangement. This prompt produces a first draft and a plain-English summary to review with a lawyer before signing.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a contracts drafter. Prepare a first draft of an independent contractor agreement for review by a qualified lawyer.

Client (hiring party): [NAME, ADDRESS, ENTITY TYPE]
Contractor: [NAME, BUSINESS NAME, ADDRESS]
Governing location: [STATE / COUNTRY]
Services: [DETAILED SCOPE, DELIVERABLES, MILESTONES]
Timeline: [START DATE, END DATE OR ONGOING]
Payment: [FIXED FEE / HOURLY RATE / RETAINER, INVOICING SCHEDULE, PAYMENT TERMS, EXPENSES]
Intellectual property: [CLIENT OWNS ALL DELIVERABLES / CONTRACTOR KEEPS PRE-EXISTING TOOLS / LICENCE]
Confidentiality needs: [WHAT INFORMATION IS SENSITIVE]
Other terms: [NON-SOLICITATION, EQUIPMENT, INSURANCE, SUBCONTRACTING ALLOWED?]
Termination: [NOTICE PERIOD, TERMINATION FOR CAUSE, PAYMENT FOR WORK DONE]

Draft:
1. The agreement with numbered clauses: parties, services and deliverables, term, compensation and invoicing, expenses, independent contractor status (control over methods, own tools, taxes, no benefits), IP and work product, confidentiality, warranties, limitation of liability, indemnity, insurance, termination, dispute resolution, governing law, entire agreement, signatures.
2. A statement of work template as an attachment.
3. A plain-English summary of each clause (one or two sentences).
4. A list of points to raise with a lawyer, including worker classification risks in my location and any terms that may not be enforceable there.
Mark choices I need to make with [DECISION NEEDED].
```

## How to use it

1. **Be specific about scope and deliverables**; most disputes come from vague scope.
2. **Review the classification points**; calling someone a contractor doesn't make them one legally.
3. **Have a qualified lawyer review** the draft before either party signs.

## Tips

- Make sure the IP clause covers who owns drafts and source files, not just the final deliverable.
- This is a drafting aid, not legal advice.
