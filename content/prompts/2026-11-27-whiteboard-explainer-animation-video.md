Whiteboard animation (a hand sketching simple drawings on a white board as a voiceover explains an idea) is a classic format for training, lessons and product explainers because it's easy to follow. AI video models can produce the hand-drawn look in short clips if you describe the drawings in order and keep each clip to one idea. This whiteboard explainer prompt is built for scene-by-scene generation that you join with your voiceover.

## Prompt

```prompt
Whiteboard explainer animation, [DURATION, E.G. 6–8 SECONDS], [16:9].

Look: clean white whiteboard filling the frame, black marker line drawings with [ONE ACCENT COLOR, E.G. BLUE] highlights, simple doodle style.
Hand: a realistic hand holding a black marker draws the sketches in real time, moving naturally, never covering the drawing for long.
Concept for this scene: [ONE IDEA, E.G. HOW A CUSTOMER ORDER MOVES FROM WEBSITE TO WAREHOUSE TO DOORSTEP]
Drawing order:
1. [FIRST SKETCH, E.G. A LAPTOP WITH A SHOPPING CART ON SCREEN] on the left
2. [SECOND SKETCH, E.G. AN ARROW TO A SIMPLE WAREHOUSE BUILDING] in the middle
3. [THIRD SKETCH, E.G. AN ARROW TO A HOUSE WITH A PACKAGE AT THE DOOR] on the right
Text labels (only if rendered cleanly): "[LABEL 1]", "[LABEL 2]", "[LABEL 3]"; otherwise no text.
Camera: static, straight-on, even lighting, no shadows from the hand over key areas.
Ends with the full drawing visible for 1–2 seconds.
No extra drawings, no scribbles, no logos.
```

## How to use it

1. **Split your script into one idea per scene** and write the drawing order for each.
2. **Generate each scene**, keep the clearest takes, and join them in order.
3. **Record the voiceover to match the drawings**, then add labels in your editor if the AI text isn't clean.

## Tips

- Simple icons (arrows, boxes, stick figures) render far more reliably than detailed drawings.
- Keep the accent color for the single most important element in each scene.
