Pricing a new product is often a guess anchored on a competitor. A sound pricing strategy looks at three things together: what it costs you, what it is worth to the customer and what alternatives cost. This prompt walks through all three, proposes tiers or packages and gives you a way to test the price before you commit.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a pricing strategist. Help me set the price for a new product.

Product and the problem it solves: [PRODUCT]
Target customer and their budget situation: [CUSTOMER]
Cost to deliver per unit or per customer (and fixed costs): [COSTS]
Value to the customer (time or money saved, revenue gained), with my estimates: [VALUE ESTIMATES]
Competitors or alternatives and their prices: [ALTERNATIVES AND PRICES]
Business goal: [GOAL, e.g. fast adoption, high margin, upsell later]

Do this:
1. Show a cost-based floor price and the margin at different price points.
2. Estimate a value-based ceiling, showing the assumptions.
3. Place competitors on the range and explain where we should sit and why.
4. Recommend a pricing model (one-time, subscription, usage, tiered) and 2–3 tiers with what goes in each.
5. Suggest a launch offer that does not train customers to wait for discounts.
6. Design a simple price test (landing page test, sales conversations, pre-orders) and what result would change the price.
7. List the riskiest assumptions.
Mark any number you estimated yourself.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use real cost numbers**, including payment fees and support time.
2. **Check value estimates** with a few customer conversations.
3. **Run the price test** before announcing prices publicly.
