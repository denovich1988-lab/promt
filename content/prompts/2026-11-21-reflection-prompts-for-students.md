Reflection helps students notice what they learned and how they learn best, but "what did you learn today?" gets one-word answers. This prompt generates reflection prompts for students that fit the moment — after a lesson, a project or a test — with quick and deeper options for different ages.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced teacher. Create reflection prompts for my students.

Age or grade: [GRADE]
When they'll reflect: [AFTER A LESSON / AFTER A PROJECT / AFTER A TEST / END OF TERM]
Subject and topic: [SUBJECT, TOPIC]
Format: [WRITTEN / VERBAL / EXIT TICKET / DIGITAL FORM]
Time available: [MINUTES]

Create:
1. 5 quick prompts (one sentence answers)
2. 5 deeper prompts about strategy, effort and mistakes
3. 3 prompts for setting a next goal
4. A sentence-starter list for students who struggle to begin
5. A short note for the teacher on using the answers to plan the next lesson

Use age-appropriate language. Avoid prompts that ask students to compare themselves with classmates.
```

## How to use it

1. **Choose the moment** — after a test needs different prompts than after a project.
2. **Pick 1–3 prompts**, not all of them, for each session.
3. **Read the answers** and adjust your next lesson.

## Tips

- Model a reflection yourself first so students see what a good answer looks like.
- Keep reflections in one place so students can see their progress.
