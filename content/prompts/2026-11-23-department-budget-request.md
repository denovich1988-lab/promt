Budget season rewards managers who can show what each dollar buys and what happens if it is cut. Most requests are a spreadsheet with no story. This prompt turns your planned spend into a clear department budget request: what you are asking for, why, what it delivers, and what you would drop at a lower number, so finance can say yes faster.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me write a budget request for my department for [YEAR].

Department and team size: [DEPARTMENT AND HEADCOUNT]
Company goals for next year: [COMPANY GOALS]
This year's budget and actual spend: [THIS YEAR BUDGET VS ACTUAL]
Planned spend by line item (people, tools, vendors, programs, travel): [LINE ITEMS WITH AMOUNTS]
New requests and what they enable: [NEW REQUESTS]
Who approves and what they care about: [APPROVER AND PRIORITIES]

Write:
1. A one-paragraph summary: total ask, change versus this year, and the main outcome it funds.
2. A table: line item, amount, change vs this year, link to a company goal, risk if cut.
3. A short justification for each new or increased item, with the measure we'll report on.
4. Three scenarios: full ask, flat budget, 10% cut, and what each one means in plain terms.
5. Likely questions from finance and short answers.
Use only my numbers; mark any assumption clearly.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pull actuals first** so every increase is compared with real spend.
2. **Tie each line to a company goal**; unlinked items are the first to be cut.
3. **Bring the scenarios** to the meeting so the trade-off conversation is yours.
