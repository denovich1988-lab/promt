Teams often jump to a solution before they agree on the problem, and then argue about features. A clear problem statement names who struggles, in what situation, what it costs them and why current options fall short. This prompt sharpens your idea into one, tests it for hidden assumptions and suggests how to validate it.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced product manager. Help me write a clear problem statement.

My idea or the problem I think exists: [IDEA]
Who I think has this problem: [TARGET USERS]
What I've seen or heard that suggests it is real: [EVIDENCE]
How people deal with it today: [CURRENT ALTERNATIVES]

Do this:
1. Rewrite my idea as a problem statement in this form: "[User] needs a way to [job] when [situation], because [current obstacle], which costs them [time, money, risk or frustration]." Give 3 versions of different scope.
2. Point out any part that describes a solution instead of a problem, and rewrite it.
3. List the assumptions behind the statement, ranked by risk.
4. Describe what "solved" looks like: 3 measurable outcomes for the user.
5. Suggest 3 quick ways to validate the riskiest assumption in under two weeks, with what result would change my mind.
6. Write 5 neutral interview questions to test the problem without leading people.

Be direct and point out weak evidence. Do not invent market data.
```

## How to use it

1. **Include what you've actually seen**, such as complaints, workarounds or requests.
2. **Pick the version** with a scope your team can tackle.
3. **Run the validation steps** before planning features.

## Tips

- If the cost of the problem is unclear, the problem may not be painful enough to pay for.
- Put the final statement at the top of every product brief and review it often.
