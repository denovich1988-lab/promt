Regular investor updates build trust and often bring introductions and help, but founders skip them when busy. A fixed structure makes them quick to write. This prompt turns your raw notes and numbers into an honest, scannable update that includes the bad news as well as the good.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a startup founder's chief of staff. Write this month's investor update from my notes.

Company and stage: [COMPANY, WHAT IT DOES, STAGE]
Month: [MONTH]
Key metrics this month and last month: [E.G. REVENUE, USERS, BURN, RUNWAY]
Wins: [WINS]
Challenges and what we're doing about them: [CHALLENGES]
Product and team updates: [UPDATES]
Specific asks (intros, hires, advice): [ASKS]

Format:
1. Subject line: "[Company] update — [Month]: [one-line headline]".
2. TL;DR in 3 bullets.
3. Metrics table: metric, this month, last month, change.
4. Wins (3–5 bullets).
5. Challenges (honest, with the plan).
6. Product and team.
7. Asks — numbered and specific (who, what, why).
8. Short thank-you.

Under 400 words. Use only my numbers; never round up or add metrics. Tone: direct and confident, no hype.
```

## How to use it

1. **Keep a running notes doc** through the month and paste it in.
2. **Make asks specific** — "intro to a VP Sales at a logistics company" gets results.
3. **Send on the same day every month** to build the habit.

## Tips

- Include the same metrics every month so trends are clear.
- Bad news shared early builds more trust than a surprise later.
