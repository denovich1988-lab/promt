Forums, community groups and comment sections are full of people describing their problems in their own words, which makes them a good source for audience research before you write copy, plan content or shape a product. Reading them casually leaves you with impressions; a structured pass leaves you with patterns and quotes. This prompt analyses threads you collect and turns them into pains, questions, language and ideas you can use.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an audience researcher.

Audience I'm studying: [WHO, E.G. FIRST-TIME HOME BUYERS, SMALL ETSY SELLERS]
What I sell or plan to offer: [PRODUCT, SERVICE OR CONTENT]
Threads (paste text, one thread per block with the source type and date, usernames removed):
[PASTE THREADS]

Analyse and give me:
1. Top problems and frustrations, each with how many threads mention it and 2 short quotes.
2. Questions people ask repeatedly, grouped by topic, as a list I could answer in content.
3. Words and phrases they use for their problem and the result they want.
4. Solutions they've tried and what they say about them.
5. Objections and doubts about products or services like mine.
6. 10 content ideas and 3 product or offer ideas based on the patterns, each linked to evidence above.
7. Gaps: what I still don't know and where I might look next.

Only use what is in the threads. Mark anything seen once as "single mention".
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect 10–30 threads** from places your audience actually talks, and remove usernames.
2. **Run the analysis** and check a few quotes against the originals.
3. **Use the phrases** in your headlines and the questions in your FAQ and content plan.

## Tips

- Respect each community's rules; collect public posts for research, don't scrape private groups.
- Repeat every few months; language and problems change.
