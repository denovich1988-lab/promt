Summarizing depositions is one of the most time-consuming paralegal tasks: long transcripts have to be condensed into something an attorney can scan before a hearing or mediation. This deposition summary prompt for paralegals creates a page-line summary, a topic index and a list of key admissions and inconsistencies. Use it only with a tool your firm has approved for client data, and always check every citation against the transcript.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced litigation paralegal. Summarize this deposition transcript for the supervising attorney.

Case and issues: [CASE NAME OR MATTER NUMBER, CLAIMS AND DEFENSES, WHAT WE NEED TO PROVE]
Deponent: [NAME/ROLE, E.G. PLAINTIFF, FORMER MANAGER, EXPERT]
Our side: [PLAINTIFF / DEFENDANT]
Format wanted: [PAGE-LINE / TOPICAL / NARRATIVE]
Transcript (with page and line numbers): [PASTE OR ATTACH]

Produce:
1. A one-paragraph overview of who the deponent is and the most important testimony.
2. A page-line summary table: page:line range, topic, concise summary of the testimony (neutral, no interpretation).
3. A topic index grouping testimony by issue, with page:line references.
4. Key admissions or statements helpful to our side, and statements harmful to our side, each with citations.
5. Inconsistencies with other testimony or documents I list here: [OPTIONAL: PASTE KEY FACTS OR EXHIBITS].
6. Exhibits referenced, with page:line where each was introduced.
7. Objections and instructions not to answer, with citations.
8. Follow-up items: documents mentioned, people named, questions left open.

Quote exactly where the wording matters, keep summaries factual and mark anything unclear in the transcript as {UNCLEAR}. Do not guess page or line numbers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use a transcript with page and line numbers** so every summary line can be cited.
2. **Split very long transcripts** into sections and merge the summaries afterwards.
3. **Spot-check citations** against the original before the summary goes to the attorney.

## Tips

- Ask the attorney which format they prefer; some want topical summaries for trial prep.
- Keep confidential material in firm-approved tools only.
