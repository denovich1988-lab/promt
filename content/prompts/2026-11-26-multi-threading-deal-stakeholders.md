Deals that depend on one contact stall or die when that person goes quiet, changes jobs or loses budget. Multi-threading means building relationships with several people in the buying group. This prompt maps the stakeholders in your deal, shows who's missing, and writes tailored outreach for each role, done with your champion's help rather than behind their back.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an enterprise sales coach. Help me multi-thread this deal.

What we sell and typical deal size: [PRODUCT, ACV]
Account: [COMPANY, INDUSTRY, SIZE]
Deal stage and close date: [STAGE, DATE]
People we're talking to (name, title, role, how engaged): [LIST]
What we know about their goals and the decision process: [NOTES]
Risks: [E.G. SINGLE CONTACT, NEW CFO, COMPETITOR]

Create:
1. A buying-group map: economic buyer, champion, technical evaluator, end users, procurement/legal, security, possible blockers; mark each as known, engaged, unknown or missing.
2. For each missing or weak role: likely title at this company, what they care about, and the risk if we don't reach them.
3. A script to ask my champion for introductions, explaining why it helps them win internally.
4. Tailored first messages (email, under 100 words) for 3–4 roles, each focused on that person's priorities, not features.
5. A meeting plan: which roles to bring together, and what each of our team members (AE, SE, exec) should own.
6. Warning signs that the deal is single-threaded again, and a weekly check.

Don't invent people; use role titles where names are unknown.
```

## How to use it

1. **List everyone** you've met, even briefly, with how engaged they are.
2. **Go through the champion first**; surprise outreach to their boss can damage trust.
3. **Update the map after each meeting** and review it in pipeline calls.

## Tips

- Offer each stakeholder something useful for their job (a benchmark, a security doc, an ROI model), not just a meeting.
- Match seniority: your exec reaches out to their exec.
