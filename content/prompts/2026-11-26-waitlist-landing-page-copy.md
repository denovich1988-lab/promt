A waitlist page is often the first thing people see of a new product, and it has one job: explain the idea fast and get an email address. Vague "something big is coming" pages convert poorly. This waitlist landing page prompt writes specific copy around the problem you solve, a real early-access reason to join, and the confirmation email that keeps people interested until launch.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a conversion copywriter. Write copy for a pre-launch waitlist landing page.

Product: [WHAT IT IS IN ONE SENTENCE]
Who it's for: [TARGET USER]
Problem it solves: [MAIN PAIN, IN THE CUSTOMER'S WORDS IF POSSIBLE]
Key benefits: [3 BENEFITS]
Launch timing: [E.G. EARLY 2027 / "IN A FEW MONTHS"]
Early-access offer: [E.G. FIRST ACCESS, FOUNDING PRICE, BETA INVITE — ONLY WHAT'S REAL]
Proof available: [E.G. FOUNDER BACKGROUND, BETA RESULTS, OR NONE]
Brand tone: [E.G. PLAYFUL / CALM AND PREMIUM]

Write:
1. 5 headline options and 3 subheadlines.
2. Page sections: hero with email field and button text, problem, 3 benefits with short descriptions, how it works (3 steps), early-access offer, founder note (optional), FAQ (5 questions including "When does it launch?" and "What will it cost?"), final call to action.
3. Microcopy: form placeholder, privacy line, success message.
4. A confirmation email that thanks them, sets expectations and asks one question about their needs.
5. A referral line encouraging people to share for earlier access (only if I confirm I'll offer it).
No fake urgency, fake numbers or invented testimonials.
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe the problem in customer language**; it makes the headline land.
2. **Only promise early-access perks you'll deliver**.
3. **Read replies to the confirmation email**; they're free customer research.

## Tips

- One form field (email) usually converts better than several.
- Update the page with real beta feedback as soon as you have it.
