Income statements and balance sheets are full of numbers, but ratios turn them into answers: can the company pay its bills, is it profitable, how much debt does it carry? This financial ratio analysis prompt calculates the standard ratios from figures you paste, shows the formulas, compares years and explains what each result means in plain language.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a financial analyst. Do a ratio analysis from these statements.

Company and industry: [NAME, INDUSTRY]
Purpose: [E.G. LENDING DECISION, INVESTMENT RESEARCH, CHECKING MY OWN BUSINESS, CLASS ASSIGNMENT]
Income statement (2–3 years): [PASTE]
Balance sheet (2–3 years): [PASTE]
Cash flow statement (optional): [PASTE]
Industry benchmarks, if I have them: [PASTE OR "NONE"]

Do this:
1. Calculate, for each year, with the formula and the numbers used:
   - Liquidity: current ratio, quick ratio, cash ratio
   - Profitability: gross, operating and net margin, return on assets, return on equity
   - Leverage: debt-to-equity, debt-to-assets, interest coverage
   - Efficiency: asset turnover, inventory turnover and days, receivables days, payables days
   - Cash flow: operating cash flow to net income, free cash flow (if data allows)
2. A table of ratios by year with the trend (improving / worsening).
3. Plain-language interpretation: strengths, warning signs and what may be driving them.
4. Questions to investigate further.
Only use benchmarks I provide; otherwise don't claim what's "normal" for the industry. Flag any figure that looks inconsistent.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste statements in a consistent format** with the same line items each year.
2. **Check one or two calculations yourself** to confirm the numbers were read correctly.
3. **Compare with industry benchmarks** from a reliable source if you need context.

## Tips

- Trends over several years say more than a single year's ratios.
- This is analysis, not investment advice; consult a qualified professional for decisions.
