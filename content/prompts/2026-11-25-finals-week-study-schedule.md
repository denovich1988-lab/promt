Finals week goes badly when every subject gets crammed into the last night. A good finals study schedule works backwards from each exam date, gives more time to harder subjects and spaces out review so you see each topic several times. This prompt builds that schedule from your real exam dates, topics and available hours, and keeps sleep and breaks in the plan because tired studying sticks less.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a study coach. Build my finals study schedule.

Today's date: [DATE]
Exams: [SUBJECT | DATE AND TIME | FORMAT, E.G. MULTIPLE CHOICE, ESSAY, PROBLEMS]
Topics per subject and how confident I feel (1–5): [LIST]
Hours I can study each day: [HOURS, AND ANY DAYS WITH WORK OR CLASSES]
Times I study best: [MORNING / AFTERNOON / EVENING]
Resources I have: [NOTES, TEXTBOOK, PAST PAPERS, PRACTICE PROBLEMS]

1. Rank subjects by priority (exam date, weight, my confidence).
2. Make a day-by-day schedule up to my last exam in blocks of 45–60 minutes with short breaks. Mix subjects, and give low-confidence topics more blocks.
3. Use active methods in each block: practice questions, self-testing, explaining out loud, past papers; not just rereading.
4. Schedule each topic at least twice with a gap of a few days.
5. Keep at least 7 hours for sleep and one lighter evening before each exam.
6. Add a 10-minute daily check-in where I mark what I finished, and tell me how to adjust if I fall behind.

Show the schedule as a table.
```

## How to use it

1. **Enter every exam with its date and format**; format changes how you should practise.
2. **Rate your confidence honestly** so weak topics get more time.
3. **Copy the table into your calendar** and do the daily check-in.

## Tips

- Practise under exam conditions at least once per subject with a timer.
- If you fall behind, cut low-weight topics first, not sleep.
