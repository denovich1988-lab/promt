A pay rise should feel like recognition, but a vague or overly formal letter can leave employees confused about the amount, timing or reason. This salary increase letter prompt writes a short, warm letter with all the details HR and payroll need — new salary, percentage, effective date and what earned it — plus a version for merit, promotion or cost-of-living increases.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an HR manager. Write a salary increase letter.

Employee name and job title: [DETAILS]
Type of increase: [MERIT / PROMOTION / MARKET ADJUSTMENT / COST OF LIVING / ANNUAL REVIEW]
Current salary and new salary: [AMOUNTS AND PAY PERIOD]
Effective date and first paycheck it appears on: [DATES]
Reason / achievements to mention: [2–3 SPECIFIC CONTRIBUTIONS]
Other changes: [E.G. NEW TITLE, BONUS ELIGIBILITY, BENEFITS — OR NONE]
Company name and signer: [NAME, TITLE]
Tone: [WARM / FORMAL]

Write:
1. A letter under 250 words: opening congratulation, the increase (new salary, percentage, effective date), specific reasons, any other changes, who to contact with questions, closing.
2. A short email version to send with the letter.
3. Talking points for the manager to share the news in person first.
Calculate the percentage from my numbers and show it. Don't add promises about future raises.
```

## How to use it

1. **Tell the employee in person or on a call first**, then send the letter.
2. **Double-check amounts and dates** with payroll before sending.
3. **Keep a signed copy** in the employee's file.

## Tips

- Naming specific achievements makes the raise feel earned, not routine.
- Follow your local laws and company policy on pay communications and confidentiality.
