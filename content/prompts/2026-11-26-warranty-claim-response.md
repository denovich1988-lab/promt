Warranty replies are where customers decide whether to trust a brand again. A cold denial or a vague "we're looking into it" leads to complaints and bad reviews. This warranty claim response prompt writes clear, fair replies for approved, denied and incomplete claims, quoting the relevant warranty terms and giving the customer a concrete next step every time.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a senior customer support specialist. Write a reply to a warranty claim.

Product and purchase date: [DETAILS]
Warranty terms (paste relevant section): [TERMS]
Customer's message: [PASTE]
Our decision: [APPROVED — REPAIR / REPLACE / REFUND] or [DENIED — REASON] or [NEED MORE INFO — WHAT]
Evidence we have: [E.G. PHOTOS SHOW WATER DAMAGE, SERIAL NUMBER VALID]
Goodwill options we can offer: [E.G. DISCOUNTED REPAIR, 15% OFF REPLACEMENT, OR NONE]
Brand tone: [E.G. FRIENDLY / FORMAL]

Write:
1. The reply email (under 180 words): acknowledge the problem, the decision in the first lines, the reason with a plain-language reference to the warranty terms, exact next steps (shipping label, timeline, what to include), and a contact for questions.
2. If denied, include the goodwill option and the right to ask for a review, without sounding defensive.
3. A short SMS or chat version.
Don't promise anything outside the terms or goodwill options I listed.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the actual warranty wording** so the reply quotes it correctly.
2. **Decide the outcome first** using your policy; the prompt writes it, it doesn't decide it.
3. **Save the best replies** as templates for your team.

## Tips

- Lead with the decision; customers scan for "approved" or "denied" first.
- Consumer protection laws may give rights beyond your warranty; check local rules before denying.
