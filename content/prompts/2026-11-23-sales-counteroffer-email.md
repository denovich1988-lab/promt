When a buyer asks for a lower price by email, the easy reply is to cut the price, which teaches them to push again. A good counteroffer email holds your value, asks for something in return for any concession, and offers options instead of a single yes or no. This prompt writes that email in your tone, with a few trade options you set limits on.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a B2B sales negotiation coach. Write a counteroffer email to a buyer who asked for a lower price.

Product or service and the quoted price: [OFFER AND PRICE]
What the buyer asked for and why (as they said it): [BUYER REQUEST]
Value they get (results, savings, risks avoided): [VALUE]
My limits: lowest price, discounts I can give, and what I want in return: [LIMITS AND TRADES, e.g. longer contract, upfront payment, case study, smaller scope]
Relationship and deal stage: [CONTEXT]
Tone: [TONE]

Write:
1. An email under 180 words that thanks them, restates the value in their terms, and offers 2–3 options (e.g. same price with added value, lower price with smaller scope, discount for a longer commitment).
2. A short subject line.
3. A version for a phone call (talking points) in case they call instead.
4. Replies for likely answers: they accept an option, they insist on the price, they go silent.
5. One line on when to walk away.
Never go below my limits. Do not use fake urgency or claim other buyers are waiting.
```

## How to use it

1. **Set your limits before you write**, not during the conversation.
2. **Always trade**: any concession should come with something in return.
3. **Follow up once** if they go quiet, then use the reply templates.
