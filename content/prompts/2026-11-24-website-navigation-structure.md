A messy menu hurts twice: visitors cannot find what they need, and search engines struggle to understand which pages matter most. Good site structure groups pages the way customers think and keeps important pages a few clicks from the home page. This prompt plans your website navigation and site structure, from the main menu to the URL hierarchy and internal linking.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an information architect and SEO specialist. Plan the navigation and site structure for my website.

Website type and business: [BUSINESS / SITE TYPE]
Main audiences and what they come to do: [AUDIENCES AND TASKS]
Current pages or planned pages (list): [PAGE LIST]
Priority pages for revenue or leads: [PRIORITY PAGES]
Main keyword themes: [KEYWORD THEMES]
Constraints (CMS, max menu items, languages): [CONSTRAINTS]

Deliver:
1. A sitemap tree: sections, subsections and pages, with suggested URL paths.
2. The main menu (max 7 top-level items) and dropdown contents, with labels in customer language.
3. Footer navigation and utility links.
4. Hub pages to create and which pages each one links to.
5. Click depth for priority pages (aim for 3 clicks or fewer) and how to achieve it.
6. Breadcrumb format.
7. Pages to merge, remove or redirect, with reasons.
8. A simple card-sorting or tree-test plan to validate the menu labels with real users.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste your full page list**, including old blog posts and landing pages.
2. **Check menu labels with users** before you rebuild templates.
3. **Map redirects** for every URL that changes.
