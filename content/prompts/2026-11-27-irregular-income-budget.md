Standard budgets assume the same pay every month. Freelancers, commission earners, gig workers and seasonal workers don't have that, so a great month gets spent and a slow one becomes a crisis. A budget for irregular income works differently: you plan around a baseline, hold a buffer, pay yourself a steady amount and set rules for what happens to extra money. This prompt builds that system from your own numbers.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a practical budgeting coach for people with variable income. Build my budget system.

Work type: [E.G. FREELANCE DESIGNER, REAL ESTATE AGENT, RIDESHARE DRIVER, SEASONAL]
Income for the last 6–12 months: [MONTHLY AMOUNTS]
Taxes: [ARE TAXES WITHHELD? IF NOT, MY ROUGH SET-ASIDE RATE]
Essential monthly costs: [RENT, UTILITIES, FOOD, INSURANCE, DEBT MINIMUMS, TRANSPORT]
Flexible spending: [ROUGH AMOUNTS]
Current savings and buffer: [AMOUNTS]
Debts: [BALANCES AND RATES]
Goals: [E.G. 3-MONTH BUFFER, PAY OFF CARD, RETIREMENT SAVING]

Do this:
1. Calculate my baseline (essential costs) and my lowest and average income months. Show the math.
2. Design a system: income goes to a holding account, I pay myself a fixed "salary" each month, and set aside tax money first. Suggest the salary amount and explain it.
3. A buffer target (in months of baseline) and a plan to build it.
4. Rules for good months (what % goes to tax, buffer, debt, goals, fun) and for lean months (what to cut first, in order).
5. A monthly checklist I can follow in 15 minutes.
6. A simple spreadsheet layout to track income, salary, buffer and tax set-aside.
This is general budgeting help, not tax or financial advice; flag where I should check with a professional.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use real monthly income** from your bank or invoices for at least six months.
2. **Open a separate holding account** so business income and personal spending don't mix.
3. **Revisit the salary amount** every quarter as your income pattern becomes clearer.

## Tips

- If taxes aren't withheld, setting tax money aside first prevents the most painful surprises.
- Build the buffer before increasing your salary after a few good months.
