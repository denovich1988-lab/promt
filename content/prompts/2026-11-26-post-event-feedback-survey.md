After a conference, webinar or workshop, a quick feedback survey tells you what to repeat and what to fix, but long surveys get ignored. This prompt builds a post-event feedback survey of under 10 questions that respondents can finish in two minutes, plus the email that sends it and a simple way to analyse results.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an event marketing manager. Create a post-event feedback survey.

Event: [NAME, TYPE (CONFERENCE, WEBINAR, WORKSHOP, MEETUP), IN PERSON OR ONLINE]
Audience: [WHO ATTENDED]
What I most want to learn: [E.G. SPEAKER QUALITY, VENUE, CONTENT RELEVANCE, LIKELIHOOD TO RETURN, SPONSOR VALUE]
Next event or offer I'd like to promote: [OR "NONE"]
Survey tool: [E.G. GOOGLE FORMS, TYPEFORM]

Create:
1. A survey of 7–10 questions that takes under 2 minutes: an overall rating, session or speaker ratings, logistics, one "what was most valuable", one "what should we change", likelihood to attend again or recommend, and an optional contact opt-in.
2. For each question: exact wording, answer type and scale with labels.
3. Logic: which follow-up appears after a low score.
4. A follow-up email (under 120 words) with a subject line, sent within 24 hours, that thanks attendees, shares any promised slides or recordings and links the survey.
5. A reminder email for 3 days later.
6. An analysis template: how to summarise scores, group open comments into themes and pick the top 3 changes.

Keep questions neutral (no leading wording) and avoid asking for data I don't need.
```

## How to use it

1. **Send it within 24 hours** while the event is fresh.
2. **Share slides or recordings in the same email**; it gives people a reason to open it.
3. **Publish a short "you said, we did" note** before the next event to show feedback matters.

## Tips

- Ask one open question at most per topic; open questions are where the best ideas come from but tire respondents fast.
- Compare results across events with the same core questions.
