Carousels get saved and shared when each slide carries one clear idea and the first slide makes people swipe. Many fail because they cram a blog post onto ten slides. This prompt writes short slide text, adds simple layout notes for your designer and finishes with a caption.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a social media content writer. Create an Instagram carousel.

Topic: [TOPIC]
Audience: [WHO FOLLOWS YOU]
Goal: [SAVES, FOLLOWS, CLICKS TO LINK, DMs]
Brand voice: [E.G. FRIENDLY AND DIRECT]
Number of slides: [7–10]

For each slide give:
- Slide number
- Headline (max 8 words)
- Body text (max 25 words, or none)
- Visual note (layout, icon or image idea)

Rules:
- Slide 1 is a hook that names a problem or promises a result; no generic "Tips for X".
- One idea per slide, in a logical order.
- The second-to-last slide sums up the key takeaway.
- The last slide has one clear call to action that matches my goal.

Then write a caption under 150 words with a first line that works on its own, and suggest 5 relevant hashtags.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pick a narrow topic,** such as "5 mistakes when pricing freelance work", rather than "freelancing tips".
2. **Ask for 3 alternative hook slides** and pick the strongest before designing.
3. **Design in a template** in Canva or Figma so every carousel looks consistent.

## Tips

- The same script works for LinkedIn document posts; ask for a more professional caption.
- Repurpose your best-performing blog post into a carousel each week.
