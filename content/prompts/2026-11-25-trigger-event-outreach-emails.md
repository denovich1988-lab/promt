Cold emails work better when there's a real reason to write now. A trigger event, such as a new leadership hire, a funding round, an office opening or a burst of job posts, often signals a new priority or budget. This prompt connects one specific event to a likely problem you solve and writes a short email that mentions the event without sounding like you've been watching the company too closely.

## Prompt

```prompt
Write a trigger-event sales email sequence.

Prospect name, role and company: [PROSPECT]
Trigger event (with source and date): [e.g. hired a new VP of Sales, announced on LinkedIn on March 3]
What we sell and the problem it solves: [OFFER]
A relevant customer result we can mention (real only): [RESULT OR "none"]
Call to action: [e.g. 15-minute call, send a short video, share a checklist]

1. List 3 likely priorities this trigger creates for someone in the prospect's role, and pick the one most linked to our offer. Say why.
2. Email 1 (max 90 words): mention the event in one natural line, connect it to that priority, one sentence on how we help, a low-pressure question as the CTA.
3. Follow-up 1 (day 3, max 60 words): add one useful idea or resource, no "just checking in".
4. Follow-up 2 (day 8, max 50 words): a polite close-the-loop message.
5. 3 subject lines under 6 words.

Don't congratulate excessively, don't invent facts about the company, and don't claim results we haven't listed.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use a verified, recent event** — older than 2–3 months usually feels stale.
2. **Check the priority list** and pick the one you genuinely help with.
3. **Personalize the first line** further if you know something only a human would notice.

## Tips

- Keep a list of trigger types that led to meetings and focus your research on those.
- Job posts often reveal the exact problems a team is trying to fix.
