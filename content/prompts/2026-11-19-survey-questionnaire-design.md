Bad survey questions produce misleading data: leading wording, double questions and answer scales that don't fit. Short, neutral surveys also get more responses. This prompt designs the survey around the decisions you need to make and checks every question for bias.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a survey research specialist. Design a survey.

Purpose: [WHAT DECISION THE RESULTS WILL INFORM]
Audience: [WHO WILL ANSWER AND HOW YOU WILL REACH THEM]
Max time to complete: [MINUTES]
Topics I want to cover: [TOPICS]

Create:
1. 3–5 research questions the survey must answer.
2. The survey: 8–15 questions in a logical order (easy first, sensitive last). For each give question text, type (multiple choice, scale, ranking, open text), answer options and which research question it serves.
3. One screening question if needed.
4. A short intro text explaining purpose, time and privacy.
5. A bias check: point out any question that could be leading, double-barrelled or confusing, and fix it.
6. How to analyse the results: what to count, compare or segment.

Keep scales consistent (e.g. all 1–5 with labelled ends) and always include "Other" or "Not sure" where it fits.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with the decision** you need to make; drop questions that don't help it.
2. **Test the survey on 3–5 people** and fix anything they misunderstood.
3. **After collecting answers,** paste the results back and ask for a summary by segment.

## Tips

- Shorter surveys usually get more complete responses; cut anything "nice to know".
- Don't collect personal data you don't need, and follow privacy rules where you work.
