Horizon scanning is how strategy and foresight teams spot changes before they become obvious: new regulations in draft, odd customer behaviour, emerging technologies, shifts in other industries. The hard part is turning scattered notes into something leaders can act on. This horizon scanning prompt organises your collected signals, rates impact and certainty, and turns the result into a watch list with indicators and owners.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a strategic foresight analyst.

Organisation: [COMPANY OR TEAM], operating in [INDUSTRY / MARKET], key strategic question: [QUESTION, E.G. HOW WILL OUR CUSTOMERS BUY IN 5 YEARS?]
Time horizon: [E.G. 3-7 YEARS]

Signals I have collected (one per line: signal | source | date):
[PASTE SIGNALS: NEWS ITEMS, REPORTS, PATENTS, STARTUP LAUNCHES, CUSTOMER OBSERVATIONS, REGULATORY DRAFTS]

Please:
1. Group signals using STEEP categories (social, technological, economic, environmental, political/regulatory) and merge duplicates.
2. For each cluster, name the possible trend or change it points to, and say whether it is a weak signal, an emerging trend or an established trend.
3. Rate each on impact on us (1–5) and certainty (1–5), and place them in a 2x2: act now, prepare, watch, park.
4. For the top 5, describe how this could affect our customers, operations and business model, including one opportunity and one threat.
5. Suggest leading indicators to monitor for each, and what level would trigger action.
6. List gaps: categories or sources I have not scanned, and 5 places to look next.
7. Write a one-page briefing for leadership.

Base everything on my signals. Clearly label speculation as speculation and do not invent sources, numbers or events.
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect 20–50 signals** over a few weeks from varied sources before running the prompt.
2. **Discuss the impact and certainty ratings** with colleagues; disagreement is useful information.
3. **Repeat the scan quarterly** and update the watch list with new signals.

## Tips

- Look outside your industry; many shifts arrive from adjacent markets.
- Assign an owner to each watch-list item, or it will be forgotten.
