A white paper earns downloads when it helps a buyer solve a real problem or make a decision, not when it is a long sales brochure. Most fail because the angle is vague and the evidence is thin. This prompt picks a sharp angle, builds a B2B white paper outline, drafts the sections and lists exactly which facts you still need to source.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a B2B content strategist. Help me plan and draft a white paper.

Company and product: [COMPANY AND PRODUCT]
Target reader (role, industry, seniority): [READER]
Problem or decision the reader faces: [PROBLEM]
Our point of view or method: [POINT OF VIEW]
Evidence we have (customer results, internal data, expert interviews): [EVIDENCE]
Length target: [LENGTH, e.g. 2,500 words]

Deliver:
1. 3 possible angles with working titles; recommend one and explain why it will interest the reader.
2. Detailed outline: executive summary, the problem and its cost, why common approaches fall short, our framework, implementation steps, evaluation checklist, conclusion. Adjust if a different structure fits better.
3. A first draft of the executive summary and the framework section.
4. Where charts, tables or checklists would help, with what each shows.
5. A list of claims that need a source, marked {SOURCE NEEDED}.
6. A 120-word landing page summary and 3 bullet points of what the reader will learn.
Keep product mentions to the final section. No invented statistics or customer quotes.
```

## How to use it

1. **Bring real evidence**; original data and customer results are what make a white paper credible.
2. **Source every {SOURCE NEEDED} claim** before design.
3. **Draft the remaining sections** one at a time with the same chat.
