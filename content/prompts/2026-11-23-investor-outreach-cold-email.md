Investors receive many cold emails and decide in seconds whether to read on. The ones that get replies are short, specific and clearly a fit for that investor's focus. This prompt writes a founder's investor outreach email with a sharp one-line pitch, real traction, the ask and a polite follow-up, plus notes on tailoring it to each investor.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a startup advisor. Write a cold outreach email to an investor.

Startup and one-line description: [STARTUP]
Problem, solution and who pays: [PROBLEM, SOLUTION, CUSTOMER]
Traction (revenue, users, growth, pilots), only real numbers: [TRACTION]
Team highlights relevant to this problem: [TEAM]
Round: amount, stage, committed so far: [ROUND]
Investor name, firm and why they are a fit (portfolio, thesis, posts): [INVESTOR AND FIT]

Write:
1. Three subject lines (under 50 characters).
2. An email under 150 words: why this investor, one-line pitch, 2–3 traction points, the ask (a 20-minute call), and a link placeholder for the deck.
3. A follow-up for 5–7 days later with one new update.
4. A version for a warm intro request to a mutual connection, with a forwardable blurb.
5. A checklist for tailoring each email (what to research, what to change).
Plain language, no hype words, no invented numbers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Research each investor's focus** before sending; a poor fit wastes both your time.
2. **Prefer warm intros** where you can, using the forwardable blurb.
3. **Track replies** in a simple list and send the follow-up on schedule.
