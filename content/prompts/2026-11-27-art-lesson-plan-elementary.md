Art teachers often see hundreds of students a week with 40-minute slots and limited supplies, so a lesson has to be tightly planned to work. This is one of the most useful ChatGPT prompts for art teachers: give it a grade, a skill and your real supplies, and it returns a complete lesson with timing, a demo sequence, ways to support or stretch students, clean-up and a simple rubric.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced elementary art teacher and curriculum planner.

Plan an art lesson:
- Grade: [GRADE]
- Class length and number of sessions: [E.G. 45 MINUTES X 2 SESSIONS]
- Class size: [NUMBER]
- Skill or element of art to teach: [E.G. WARM AND COOL COLORS / OVERLAPPING SHAPES / TEXTURE / SYMMETRY]
- Theme or link (optional): [E.G. SEASONS, A CLASSROOM TOPIC, A CULTURAL CELEBRATION]
- Artist or art movement to connect (or "suggest"): [ARTIST OR "SUGGEST"]
- Supplies I actually have: [LIST]
- Standards to reference: [E.G. NATIONAL CORE ARTS STANDARDS / LOCAL STANDARDS / NONE]
- Students needing support: [E.G. FINE MOTOR NEEDS, ENGLISH LEARNERS]

Include:
1. Learning objective in student-friendly "I can" language.
2. Vocabulary (max 5 words) with kid-friendly definitions.
3. A 5-minute hook and a short discussion about the artist's work (describe what to show; no copyrighted images needed).
4. Step-by-step demo and student work time, with minutes for each part, for every session.
5. Differentiation: simpler version, extension challenge, and supports for the needs listed.
6. Set-up and clean-up routine for this material.
7. A closing reflection or gallery walk with 3 questions.
8. A 4-level rubric (3 criteria) and a one-line note home to families.
```

## How to use it

1. **List only supplies you have in quantity** so the lesson runs as written.
2. **Make a sample piece yourself** using the demo steps to check timing.
3. **Adjust the rubric** to match how your school reports progress.

## Tips

- Plan clean-up time generously for paint and clay; it is often longer than expected.
- When you suggest an artist, check that the images you show are age-appropriate.
