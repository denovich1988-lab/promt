An hour with an expert can save weeks of reading, but only if you ask the right questions. Generic questions get generic answers; specific ones about decisions, mistakes and what outsiders get wrong get real insight. This prompt prepares a question plan around what you need to learn, and a way to capture what you hear.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a research consultant who interviews experts for a living. Prepare me for an expert interview.

Topic or field: [TOPIC]
What I need to learn and why (decision, project, article): [GOAL]
Expert's background: [EXPERT BACKGROUND]
What I already know: [MY CURRENT KNOWLEDGE]
Interview length: [LENGTH]

Create:
1. 3 learning goals for the interview, ranked.
2. A short opening that explains the purpose and builds rapport.
3. 12–15 questions, grouped by goal and ordered from broad to specific. Include questions about: how decisions are really made, common mistakes, what outsiders misunderstand, recent changes, and how they would approach my situation.
4. Follow-up prompts to get examples and details ("Can you walk me through the last time...").
5. 3 questions to ask only if time allows.
6. A closing: who else I should talk to and what I should read.
7. A note template to fill in during and right after the call.

Mark the 5 must-ask questions. Avoid leading or yes/no questions.
```

## How to use it

1. **Research the expert's work** briefly so you don't ask what they've already published.
2. **Send a short agenda** beforehand so they can prepare.
3. **Fill in the note template** within an hour of the call.

## Tips

- Ask for stories about specific situations; they reveal more than general opinions.
- Always ask "Who else should I talk to?" — it builds your network of sources.
