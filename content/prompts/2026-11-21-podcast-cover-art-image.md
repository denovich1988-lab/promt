Podcast covers are mostly seen as tiny squares in a list of apps, next to many others. Detailed scenes and thin fonts become unreadable at that size. A strong cover uses one bold idea, big title text and high contrast. This prompt helps image models produce exactly that, and works for YouTube channel art and newsletter logos too.

## Prompt

```prompt
Square podcast cover art for a show called "[SHOW TITLE]", about [TOPIC], for [AUDIENCE]. Central visual: [ONE BOLD IDEA, E.G. A HEADPHONE CABLE FORMING A SPROUTING PLANT], [STYLE, E.G. BOLD FLAT GRAPHIC ILLUSTRATION], strong contrast, [COLOUR PALETTE, E.G. DEEP NAVY BACKGROUND WITH BRIGHT CORAL AND CREAM], the title "[SHOW TITLE]" in large [FONT STYLE, E.G. HEAVY ROUNDED SANS-SERIF] letters filling the top third, [OPTIONAL SUBTITLE OR HOST NAME] in smaller text, simple composition with clear negative space, readable at thumbnail size, no small details, no extra text
```

Use Ideogram or GPT Image when the title must be spelled correctly. With Midjourney or Flux, you may get better results generating the artwork only (remove the text lines) and adding the title in a design tool.

## How to use it

1. **Pick one visual idea** that represents the show — not a collage of topics.
2. **Shrink the result to about 60 pixels wide** to test readability.
3. **Export at the size your podcast host requires** and check their file guidelines.

## Tips

- Short titles of one to three words work best on covers.
- Keep the same colours and font for episode artwork so the brand stays recognisable.
