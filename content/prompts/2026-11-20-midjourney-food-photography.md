Good food photos depend on angle, light and a few carefully chosen props. Vague prompts give you oversaturated, plastic-looking dishes. This prompt sets the shot like a food photographer would, so you get images that look edible and match your brand.

## Prompt

```prompt
Professional food photograph of [DISH, WITH MAIN INGREDIENTS, COLOURS AND GARNISH], served in [PLATE OR BOWL STYLE].
Angle: [OVERHEAD FLAT LAY / 45-DEGREE / EYE LEVEL].
Setting: [TABLE SURFACE] with [2–3 SUBTLE PROPS, E.G. LINEN NAPKIN, FORK, SMALL BOWL OF HERBS].
Lighting: soft natural side light, gentle shadows, realistic highlights on sauces, no artificial glow.
Styling: fresh ingredients, natural textures, a few crumbs or drips for realism.
Colour palette: [WARM / FRESH / DARK AND MOODY].
Background: [CLEAN / SLIGHTLY BLURRED RESTAURANT INTERIOR].
Mood: [COSY HOMEMADE / FINE DINING / STREET FOOD].
--ar [4:5 / 1:1 / 3:2] --style raw
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe your real dish accurately** so customers get what the photo shows.
2. **Generate in batches** and choose the most natural-looking result.
3. **Use the same lighting and surface words** across your menu for a consistent look.

## Tips

- Overhead works for pizzas, bowls and spreads; 45 degrees for burgers and layered desserts.
- Check local rules: some delivery platforms require real photos of your food.
