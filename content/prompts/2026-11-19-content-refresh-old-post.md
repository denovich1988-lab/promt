Older articles often lose rankings slowly as information goes out of date and competitors publish better pages. Updating them is usually faster than writing something new. This prompt compares your post with what searchers need today and lists specific changes in order of impact.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an SEO editor. Help me refresh an old article.

Target keyword: [KEYWORD]
What happened: [E.G. "WAS IN POSITION 3, NOW 14; TRAFFIC DOWN"]
My article:
[PASTE ARTICLE TEXT OR OUTLINE]
What the current top-ranking pages cover (headings or notes):
[NOTES ON TOP 3 RESULTS]

Deliver:
1. Search intent check: does my article still match what people want for this keyword? If not, what should change?
2. Outdated parts: facts, screenshots, tools, years or advice that need updating.
3. Missing topics: sections competitors cover that I don't, and questions I should answer.
4. Parts to cut or merge: sections that are thin, repetitive or off-topic.
5. A new outline with headings, marking each as keep / update / new / remove.
6. A new title and meta description.
7. Internal link ideas: which related pages on my site should link to this one.

Rank the changes by likely impact. Don't invent facts; mark anything I should verify with {CHECK}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Find candidates** in Google Search Console: pages with falling clicks or rankings.
2. **Paste notes from the top 3 results** so the AI compares with today's search results.
3. **Update the post in place** (same URL), change the "last updated" date and request indexing.

## Tips

- Keep sections that still bring traffic; check which queries they rank for first.
- Refresh in small batches and track results for a few weeks before the next round.
