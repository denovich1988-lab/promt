When a key person leaves suddenly, companies without a succession plan scramble, lose knowledge and sometimes customers. This succession planning prompt helps HR leaders and managers identify critical roles, rate the risk, map potential successors by readiness and build development plans, plus a short emergency cover plan for each role.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an HR business partner specialising in talent management. Help me build a succession plan.

Company size and structure: [HEADCOUNT, DEPARTMENTS]
Key roles to cover: [ROLE – CURRENT HOLDER'S TENURE – WHY CRITICAL, ONE PER LINE]
Potential successors (role, strengths, gaps, aspirations as I know them): [LIST, USE INITIALS IF PREFERRED]
Known risks: [E.G. RETIREMENT IN 2 YEARS, FLIGHT RISK, SINGLE POINT OF KNOWLEDGE]
Development budget and options: [E.G. MENTORING, COURSES, STRETCH PROJECTS]

Create:
1. A criticality and risk table: role, impact if vacant, likelihood of vacancy, overall risk (high/medium/low) with reasoning.
2. A succession map per role: ready now, ready in 1–2 years, ready in 3+ years, and external hire if no internal candidate.
3. For each named successor: key gaps, 3 development actions with timing, and a mentor or sponsor role.
4. An emergency cover plan for each high-risk role: who covers in the first 30 days, critical knowledge to document now.
5. A knowledge transfer checklist for current role holders.
6. How to talk to potential successors without making promises.
7. A review rhythm and the metrics to track (bench strength, readiness over time, internal fill rate).

Keep it fair: base readiness on skills and evidence, and flag where my input sounds like bias. Mark legal or confidential issues as {CHECK WITH HR/LEGAL}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with the 5–10 most critical roles**, not the whole org chart.
2. **Use initials or codes** for people if you're working with sensitive information.
3. **Review the plan twice a year** with leadership and update readiness.

## Tips

- Document critical knowledge now; it's useful even if no one leaves.
- Rotate high-potential people through stretch projects to test readiness before a vacancy.
