Whether you're a student preparing an ethics application or a UX researcher running interviews, participants need to understand what they're agreeing to: what the study involves, what happens to their data and that they can withdraw. Templates are often full of legal language that people don't read. This prompt drafts a plain-language research participant information sheet and consent form from your study details. Your institution's ethics board or legal team has the final say on wording.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a research ethics advisor. Draft participant documents for my study.

Study title and purpose: [TITLE, WHAT YOU WANT TO LEARN AND WHY]
Researcher and organization: [NAME, UNIVERSITY OR COMPANY, CONTACT DETAILS]
Participants: [WHO, AGE RANGE, HOW RECRUITED, ANY VULNERABLE GROUPS]
What participants will do: [E.G. 45-MINUTE RECORDED INTERVIEW / ONLINE SURVEY / USABILITY TEST]
Data collected and how it's stored: [RECORDINGS, NOTES, PERSONAL DATA, WHERE STORED, WHO HAS ACCESS, HOW LONG KEPT]
Anonymity: [ANONYMOUS / PSEUDONYMOUS / IDENTIFIABLE QUOTES WITH PERMISSION]
Compensation: [AMOUNT OR NONE]
Risks and benefits: [ANY DISCOMFORT, SENSITIVE TOPICS, BENEFITS]
Ethics approval reference: [NUMBER OR "PENDING"]

Write:
1. A participant information sheet in plain language (reading age around 12), using headings: what is this study, why have I been invited, what will I do, do I have to take part, risks and benefits, what happens to my information, can I withdraw and until when, who to contact with questions or complaints.
2. A consent form with separate tick boxes for each item (taking part, recording, use of anonymized quotes, data storage, future contact) and space for signature and date.
3. A short verbal consent script for remote sessions.
4. A list of anything my ethics board is likely to ask about that I haven't covered.
```

## How to use it

1. **Give accurate data storage details**; that's where ethics reviewers focus.
2. **Check your institution's required template** and copy any mandatory wording into it.
3. **Pilot the sheet** with one person to see if it's easy to understand.

## Tips

- Separate consent items so people can agree to the interview but not to quotes.
- Keep signed consent forms separately from the research data.
