Long business plans take weeks and are often out of date before they're finished. A one-page plan forces clear thinking: who it's for, why they'd pay, how you'll reach them and what could go wrong. This prompt fills it in and flags the assumptions you should test first.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a startup advisor. Turn my idea into a one-page business plan.

Idea: [DESCRIBE THE IDEA]
Target customer: [WHO]
Location or market: [WHERE]
My resources: [BUDGET, TIME, SKILLS, CONTACTS]

Fill in these sections, a few lines each:
1. Problem: the top 3 problems the customer has.
2. Customer segment and early adopters.
3. Solution: the simplest version that solves the main problem.
4. Unique value: why choose this over alternatives.
5. Channels: how we will reach the first 100 customers.
6. Revenue: what we charge and how.
7. Costs: main startup and monthly costs.
8. Key metrics: 3 numbers to track in the first 6 months.
9. Unfair advantage: something hard to copy (or "none yet").

Then:
- List the 3 riskiest assumptions and a cheap way to test each within 2 weeks.
- Give a rough break-even estimate using only my numbers, showing the calculation. If numbers are missing, ask for them.
```

## How to use it

1. **Be specific about the customer,** e.g. "independent dog groomers in mid-size cities".
2. **Run the 2-week tests** before spending real money.
3. **Update the plan** as you learn; it should change often at the start.

## Tips

- Ask the AI to argue against the idea to find weak spots.
- This is a planning tool; check legal, tax and licensing needs with local professionals.
