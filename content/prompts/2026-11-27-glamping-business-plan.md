Glamping sites can charge hotel-level prices, but they come with zoning questions, weather risk and real building costs. A glamping business plan lays out the land, structures, guest experience and numbers so you can talk to planners, lenders and partners with confidence. This prompt builds the plan from your land and budget and shows profit at different occupancy levels.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a business advisor for outdoor hospitality startups.

Land: [E.G. 12 ACRES OF WOODLAND / PART OF A WORKING FARM], in [REGION]
Access and utilities: [ROAD ACCESS, WATER, POWER, SEWER OR SEPTIC OPTIONS]
Structure types I'm considering: [BELL TENTS / SAFARI TENTS / DOMES / YURTS / TINY CABINS / TREEHOUSES]
Number of units at launch: [NUMBER]
Target guests: [E.G. COUPLES FROM THE CITY 2 HOURS AWAY, FAMILIES, WELLNESS RETREATS, WEDDINGS]
Unique selling points: [E.G. HOT TUBS, STARGAZING, FARM BREAKFAST, RIVER ACCESS]
Budget and funding: [AMOUNT, SOURCE]
Open season: [YEAR-ROUND / SEASONAL]

Write the plan with:
1. Concept summary and guest experience from arrival to checkout.
2. Market check: who books glamping in this area, competitors, and how we're different (mark what I need to verify).
3. Site layout ideas: spacing, privacy, shared facilities (bathhouse, kitchen), parking.
4. Structure comparison table: cost range, lifespan, comfort in bad weather, permitting difficulty.
5. Permits and legal checklist: zoning, planning permission, building codes, health rules, fire safety, insurance, tourist taxes.
6. Pricing by season and weekday/weekend, plus add-ons.
7. Financial model: startup costs, operating costs, revenue at 35%, 55% and 75% occupancy, break-even. Label figures as estimates.
8. Operations: cleaning between stays, laundry, maintenance, staffing.
9. Marketing launch plan.
10. Risks and a 6-month timeline to opening.
```

## How to use it

1. **Talk to your local planning office early**; zoning can decide the whole project.
2. **Replace estimates with quotes** for structures, utilities and insurance.
3. **Start with fewer units** and add more once occupancy is proven.

## Tips

- Bathrooms and hot water are often what guests judge first; budget properly for them.
- Weather-proofing (heating, decks, drainage) extends your season and income.
