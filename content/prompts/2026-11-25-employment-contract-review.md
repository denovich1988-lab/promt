An employment contract often arrives with a deadline and a lot of dense wording, and many people sign without understanding the notice period, non-compete or who owns their side projects. This prompt walks through the contract clause by clause in plain English, flags terms that are unusual or one-sided, and turns them into polite questions you can ask HR. It is a reading aid, not legal advice; for anything serious, ask an employment lawyer in your country.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a careful reader helping me understand an employment contract. This is not legal advice; point out where I should ask a qualified employment lawyer.

Country or state: [WHERE THE JOB IS]
Role and what I was promised verbally: [ROLE, SALARY, BENEFITS, REMOTE OR OFFICE]
Contract text: [PASTE THE CONTRACT]

1. Summarise in plain English: pay and how it is paid, bonus or commission terms, hours and overtime, holidays and leave, probation, notice period on both sides, place of work.
2. Explain these clauses if present: non-compete, non-solicitation, confidentiality, intellectual property and side projects, termination, changes to terms, clawback or repayment of training costs.
3. Flag anything that differs from what I was told verbally, is missing, or seems one-sided, and rate each flag as minor, worth asking about, or get advice.
4. Write 5 polite questions I can send to HR or the hiring manager.
5. List what I should get in writing before signing.

Quote the clause number for each point. Do not guess what local law says; tell me where to check.
```

## How to use it

1. **Remove personal identifiers** you don't want to share, such as ID numbers.
2. **Add what you were told in the interview** so mismatches show up.
3. **Send the questions to HR** and ask a lawyer about anything marked "get advice".

## Tips

- Non-compete and IP rules vary a lot between countries and states, so check your local rules.
- Keep a copy of the signed contract and any written answers from HR.
