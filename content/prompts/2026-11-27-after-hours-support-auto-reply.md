When customers contact you after hours, a vague "we'll get back to you soon" leaves them guessing and often leads to repeat messages. A good after-hours auto-reply confirms the message arrived, says exactly when someone will answer, points to useful self-help and explains what to do if it's urgent. This prompt writes a set of auto-replies for every channel and for holidays.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a customer support lead writing automated replies.

Company: [COMPANY NAME]
Business hours and time zone: [E.G. MON–FRI 9:00–18:00 EST]
Channels: [EMAIL / LIVE CHAT / SMS / SOCIAL DM / PHONE VOICEMAIL]
Typical first response time when we're back: [E.G. WITHIN 4 BUSINESS HOURS]
Self-help resources: [E.G. HELP CENTRE URL, ORDER TRACKING PAGE, STATUS PAGE]
Urgent issues and what to do: [E.G. OUTAGES — CHECK STATUS PAGE; SECURITY ISSUES — EMAIL SECURITY@; NONE]
Upcoming holidays or closures: [DATES]
Brand voice: [E.G. WARM AND CASUAL / PROFESSIONAL]

Write:
1. An after-hours email auto-reply (subject line + body).
2. A short chat widget message for when no agents are online, with an option to leave a message.
3. An SMS reply under 160 characters.
4. A social media DM auto-reply.
5. A voicemail greeting script (under 30 seconds).
6. A holiday closure version for each of the above, with dates as placeholders.
7. A weekend version that sets expectations for Monday.
8. Rules for when to send (and not repeat) the auto-reply to the same customer.

Each message should: confirm receipt, give a specific response time, offer 1–2 self-help links, and explain urgent options if any. No promises we can't keep.
```

## How to use it

1. **Enter your real hours and response times** so the promises are accurate.
2. **Set the messages up in each tool** (help desk, chat, phone system).
3. **Swap in the holiday versions** a week before each closure.

## Tips

- Give a time range in the customer's terms ("by Monday noon EST"), not just "soon".
- Review auto-replies every quarter so links and hours stay current.
