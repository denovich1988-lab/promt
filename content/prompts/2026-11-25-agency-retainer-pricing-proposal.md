Retainers give agencies and freelancers steady income, but a badly scoped one turns into unlimited work for a fixed fee. Pricing should start from hours, costs and target margin, then be packaged so clients can see what's included and what isn't. This prompt does the math with your numbers, builds three retainer tiers and drafts a proposal section with clear limits and a change process.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me price and propose a monthly retainer.

Service: [e.g. social media management, SEO, bookkeeping, design support]
Client and their goals: [CLIENT]
Deliverables they want each month: [LIST]
My estimated hours per deliverable: [HOURS]
My target hourly rate or salary-equivalent: [RATE]
Monthly costs tied to this client (tools, contractors): [COSTS]
Target profit margin: [%]
Minimum term and notice period I prefer: [e.g. 3 months, 30 days]

1. Show the math: hours × rate + costs + margin = monthly price. Add a buffer for meetings and revisions and state it.
2. Three tiers (Essential, Growth, Premium): deliverables, hour cap or output cap, response time, reporting, price.
3. Scope limits: what's not included, what happens to unused hours, how extra work is quoted.
4. A proposal section (max 300 words) written for the client: their goals, recommended tier and why, what they get, terms.
5. 3 likely client objections about price and a calm answer to each.

Use only my numbers; flag where my hour estimates look low.
```

## How to use it

1. **Time-track one month** of similar work to get honest hour estimates.
2. **Recommend the middle tier** when it fits the client's goals.
3. **Review the retainer every quarter** against actual hours.

## Tips

- Price by outcome or output when hours vary a lot month to month.
- Put the change process in writing before the first extra request arrives.
