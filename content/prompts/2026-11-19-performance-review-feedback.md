Performance reviews go wrong when feedback is vague ("great team player") or based only on the last few weeks. Good reviews use specific examples, balance strengths and growth areas, and end with clear goals. This prompt shapes your notes into that structure and checks the wording for bias.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced people manager. Help me write a performance review.

Role and level: [ROLE, LEVEL]
Review period: [DATES]
Goals set for this period: [GOALS]
My notes with examples (projects, results, behaviours, feedback from others):
[NOTES]
Company values or competencies to cover (optional): [VALUES]
Overall rating I'm considering: [RATING OR "UNSURE"]

Write:
1. Summary (3–4 sentences) of overall performance.
2. Strengths: 3 points, each with a specific example and its impact.
3. Growth areas: 2 points, each with an example and a concrete suggestion.
4. Progress on each goal: met / partly met / not met, with evidence.
5. 3 goals for the next period, specific and measurable.
6. A bias check: point out vague, personality-based or potentially biased phrases in my notes and suggest neutral alternatives.

Use only my notes. If the evidence for a point is thin, say so.
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect examples from the whole period,** not just recent weeks.
2. **Edit the draft in your own voice;** the employee should hear you, not a template.
3. **Share it before the meeting** if that's your company practice, so the talk is a discussion.

## Tips

- Remove names and sensitive details if your company policy requires it.
- Ask for help phrasing difficult feedback in a direct but supportive way.
