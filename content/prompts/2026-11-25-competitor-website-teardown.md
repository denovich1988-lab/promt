A competitor's website shows how they want to be seen: who they target, what they promise, how they price and how they turn visitors into leads. A structured teardown turns a vague "they look better than us" into specific lessons and gaps. This prompt analyzes the pages you paste, separates what they say from what they prove, and ends with actions for your own site.

## Prompt

```prompt
Do a website teardown of a competitor.

Competitor: [NAME AND URL]
Our business, for comparison: [WHAT WE DO AND FOR WHOM]
Pages I'm pasting (home, pricing, product, about, a blog post, signup flow): [PASTE PAGE TEXT OR DESCRIBE SCREENSHOTS]

Analyze:
1. Positioning: target customer, main promise, category they claim, tone. Quote the lines that show it.
2. Offer and pricing: plans, anchors, free trial or guarantee, what's hidden or gated.
3. Proof: types of proof used and where claims are left unsupported.
4. Funnel: main CTA, lead magnets, number of steps to sign up, friction.
5. Content and SEO focus: topics, apparent target keywords, content types.
6. Strengths we should learn from (3) and weaknesses or gaps we can use (3).

Finish with a table comparing them to us on the 6 areas, and 5 specific actions for our website ranked by impact.
Only use what's in the pages I provide; label any inference as an inference.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste several page types**, not just the home page — pricing and signup reveal the most.
2. **Repeat for 2–3 competitors** and look for patterns.
3. **Act on the gaps**, not on copying their strengths.

## Tips

- Save each teardown with a date; competitors change messaging often.
- Check their reviews too; complaints show where their promises fall short.
