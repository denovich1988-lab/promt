New subscribers are usually most engaged in the first days after they sign up. A short welcome sequence uses that attention to deliver what you promised, build trust and make one clear offer. This prompt plans the whole sequence so each email has a single job.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an email marketer. Write a 5-email welcome sequence.

Business: [WHAT YOU SELL]
Subscriber signed up for: [LEAD MAGNET OR REASON]
Ideal reader: [WHO THEY ARE AND THEIR MAIN PROBLEM]
First offer: [PRODUCT, PRICE, ANY INTRO DISCOUNT]
Voice: [E.G. WARM, EXPERT, NO-NONSENSE]

Plan:
- Email 1 (immediately): deliver the promised resource, set expectations.
- Email 2 (day 1): a quick win they can use today.
- Email 3 (day 3): our story or approach and why it's different.
- Email 4 (day 5): proof — a real example or case study I provide: [EXAMPLE OR "NONE YET"].
- Email 5 (day 7): the offer, with one clear call to action.

For each email write: 2 subject line options (under 45 characters), preview text, body (120–200 words), and the CTA.
Do not invent testimonials or results. If I gave no example for email 4, use a worked example instead.
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in a real example** for email 4, or let the AI use a how-to example instead of a fake testimonial.
2. **Load the emails** into your email tool as an automation with the delays shown.
3. **Review open and click rates** after a few weeks and rewrite the weakest email.

## Tips

- Ask for a plain-text version; it often feels more personal than heavy designs.
- Add a branch: people who buy after email 2 should skip the sales email.
