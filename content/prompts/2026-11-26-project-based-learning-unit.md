Project-based learning works when students tackle a real question and create something for a real audience, but planning a unit with milestones, scaffolds and fair assessment takes time. This project-based learning prompt designs a complete unit around your standards and class, from the driving question to the final presentation and rubric.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced PBL teacher and instructional coach. Design a project-based learning unit.

Grade and subject(s): [E.G. GRADE 7 SCIENCE AND ENGLISH]
Standards or learning goals: [LIST]
Topic or real-world issue: [E.G. LOCAL WATER QUALITY / DESIGNING A SCHOOL GARDEN]
Unit length: [E.G. 4 WEEKS, 5 LESSONS/WEEK, 50 MINUTES]
Class details: [SIZE, RANGE OF NEEDS, ENGLISH LEARNERS, TECHNOLOGY AVAILABLE]
Possible audience or partners: [E.G. PARENTS, LOCAL BUSINESS, CITY COUNCIL]

Create:
1. 3 driving question options (open-ended, real, age-appropriate) and the one you recommend.
2. The final product and public presentation.
3. A week-by-week plan with milestones, key lessons, student tasks and checkpoints.
4. Teams and roles, and how to handle uneven participation.
5. Scaffolds for different learners (sentence frames, graphic organisers, extension tasks).
6. Formative checks for each milestone.
7. A rubric for the final product covering content knowledge, collaboration and presentation.
8. A student-facing project brief and a short parent letter.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pick a topic connected to your community**; local issues raise engagement.
2. **Book the audience early**, such as a parent evening or local expert visit.
3. **Share the rubric on day one** so students know what success looks like.

## Tips

- Build in time for feedback and revision before the final presentation.
- Keep a short daily check-in so teams don't fall behind unnoticed.
