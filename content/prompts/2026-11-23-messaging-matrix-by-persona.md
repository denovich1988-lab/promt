When marketing, sales and the website each describe the product differently, buyers get confused. A messaging matrix fixes that: one table that shows, for each buyer persona, their main pains, the value you bring, the proof, the objections and the words to use at each stage. This prompt builds it from what you know about your customers, so every team can write from the same source.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a product marketing manager. Build a messaging matrix for my product.

Product and category: [PRODUCT AND CATEGORY]
Core positioning or value proposition: [POSITIONING]
Buyer personas (role, goals, pains; include who uses, who decides, who pays): [PERSONAS]
Proof we have (results, case studies, reviews, data): [PROOF]
Main competitors or alternatives: [ALTERNATIVES]
Common objections: [OBJECTIONS]

Create:
1. A matrix table with one row per persona and columns: top 3 pains, key message, 3 value points, proof to use, top objection and response, words to use, words to avoid.
2. Messages by funnel stage (awareness, consideration, decision) for each persona: one headline and one supporting line.
3. A short "what we never say" list (claims we cannot prove, jargon).
4. Examples of how the matrix turns into an ad headline, an email subject line and a sales opener for each persona.
5. Gaps: where we lack proof or understanding, and how to fill them.
Use only the proof I gave you; mark anything that needs evidence.
```

## How to use it

1. **Base personas on real interviews** and sales calls, not guesses.
2. **Share the matrix** with marketing, sales and support.
3. **Review it each quarter** as you learn more from customers.
