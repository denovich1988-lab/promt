"Happy Thanksgiving!" is easy to send but easy to forget. A message that names something specific you are grateful for means much more, whether it is to your grandmother, a coworker or a long-time client. This prompt writes Thanksgiving messages for different people in your life, plus a short toast, using your own details.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me write Thanksgiving messages.

People I'm writing to (name, relationship, one specific thing I'm grateful for about each): [PEOPLE AND DETAILS]
Format for each (text, card, email, social post): [FORMATS]
My tone: [TONE, e.g. warm and simple, funny, heartfelt]
Anything to be sensitive about (a recent loss, someone far away, a difficult year): [SENSITIVITIES]

Write:
1. A message for each person that mentions the specific detail I gave. Texts under 40 words, cards 40–80 words, emails under 120 words.
2. A professional version for coworkers or clients that is warm but not overly personal.
3. A short Thanksgiving toast (under 60 seconds spoken) for the dinner table.
4. 3 one-line options I can use for a group chat or social post.
Avoid clichés where possible and sound like a real person, not a greeting card. Keep it inclusive for people who celebrate differently.
```

## How to use it

1. **Give one real detail per person**; it is what makes the message land.
2. **Read each message aloud** and change words you wouldn't normally say.
3. **Send texts on the day** and cards a few days before.
