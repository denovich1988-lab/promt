Remote work without written rules leads to uneven treatment and confusion about hours, response times and expenses. A short, clear policy sets expectations for everyone. This prompt drafts a remote or hybrid work policy tailored to your team size and way of working, in plain language, with points to check with an employment adviser.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an HR advisor for small businesses. Draft a remote work policy for my company.

Company size, industry and country/region: [COMPANY DETAILS]
Work model: [FULLY REMOTE, HYBRID WITH X OFFICE DAYS, OR CASE BY CASE]
Roles that can or cannot work remotely: [ELIGIBILITY]
Core hours or time zone rules: [HOURS]
Tools we use for communication: [TOOLS]
Equipment and expenses we provide: [EQUIPMENT AND EXPENSES]
Security or data rules that matter: [SECURITY NEEDS]

Write a policy with these sections:
1. Purpose and scope
2. Eligibility and how to request remote work
3. Working hours, availability and response time expectations
4. Communication and meetings
5. Equipment, expenses and home office setup
6. Data security and confidentiality
7. Health, safety and wellbeing
8. Performance and how work is measured
9. Changes, review and ending remote arrangements

Write in plain, friendly language, in short paragraphs. After the policy, list points that depend on local employment law and should be checked by a qualified adviser. Do not state specific legal requirements as facts.
```

## How to use it

1. **Decide the work model first**; the policy should describe it, not decide it.
2. **Fill in what you already provide** for equipment and expenses.
3. **Have it reviewed** locally, then share it with the team and invite questions.

## Tips

- Measure output and agreed goals, not online status.
- Review the policy after six months; real use shows what is unclear.
