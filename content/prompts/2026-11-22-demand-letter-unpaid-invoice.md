When polite reminders have not worked, a formal demand letter often gets an invoice paid without going to court. It sets out the facts, the amount owed, a clear deadline and what you will do next. This ChatGPT prompt drafts a firm but professional demand letter for an unpaid invoice from your details. It is a draft to review, not legal advice; rules on collections and interest vary by place.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are helping a small business owner draft a demand letter for an unpaid invoice. This is a draft for me to review, not legal advice.

My business name and address: [MY BUSINESS DETAILS]
Client name and address: [CLIENT DETAILS]
Work or goods provided and dates: [WHAT WAS DELIVERED]
Agreement or contract terms (payment terms, late fees, interest if agreed): [TERMS]
Invoice number(s), dates and amounts: [INVOICES]
Payments received so far: [PAYMENTS]
Reminders already sent (dates and method): [REMINDERS]
Deadline to pay: [DEADLINE, e.g. 14 days from the letter date]
Payment methods accepted: [PAYMENT METHODS]
Next step I am prepared to take: [NEXT STEP, e.g. small claims court, collection agency]
Country or state: [JURISDICTION]

Write a one-page letter that:
1. States it is a formal demand for payment, with the date.
2. Summarizes the facts and the agreement briefly and neutrally.
3. States the exact amount due, itemized, including only fees or interest that the agreement allows.
4. Sets a clear deadline and how to pay.
5. States the next step if unpaid, without threats beyond what I listed.
6. Invites the client to contact me if there is a dispute.
Keep the tone firm and professional. Then list anything I should check with a lawyer or local rules for my jurisdiction.
```

## How to use it

1. **Gather the contract, invoices and reminder emails** before you start.
2. **Check late fees and interest** against your agreement and local law.
3. **Send it by a trackable method** and keep a copy.

## Tips

- Try one more friendly nudge first with the [invoice payment reminder emails](/prompts/invoice-payment-reminder-emails/).
- For large amounts, have a lawyer review the letter before sending.
