For a community supported agriculture (CSA) farm, the weekly newsletter does a lot of work: it tells members what's in the box, helps them use unfamiliar vegetables before they go to waste, and builds the connection that makes people renew next season. This farm CSA newsletter prompt writes a weekly issue from your harvest notes, plus sign-up and renewal emails, in a warm, honest farm voice.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a communications helper for a small farm. Write CSA member emails.

Farm: [NAME, LOCATION, GROWING METHODS]
Share types and prices: [E.G. FULL SHARE, HALF SHARE, PICKUP LOCATIONS, DELIVERY]
This week's box: [VEGETABLES, FRUIT, EXTRAS, AMOUNTS]
Farm news: [WEATHER, CROP PROBLEMS, NEW ANIMALS, CREW, EVENTS]
Upcoming dates: [PICKUP CHANGES, FARM DAY, END OF SEASON, SIGN-UP DEADLINE]
Member feedback or questions: [OPTIONAL]
Voice: [WARM AND CHATTY / SHORT AND PRACTICAL]

Write:
1. This week's newsletter: subject line, a short note from the farm, what's in the box with storage tips for each item, two simple recipes using the less familiar items, reminders, and a sign-off.
2. A "what to do with too much [VEGETABLE]" section with 3 quick ideas.
3. A pre-season sign-up email for new members: what a CSA is, what's included, pickup details, price and deadline, honest note on shared risk.
4. A renewal email for current members with an early-bird incentive.
5. An end-of-season thank-you email with a short survey (5 questions).
Keep facts to what I provide and don't promise specific harvests.
```

## How to use it

1. **Jot down the box contents and one or two farm stories** each week; that's all the prompt needs.
2. **Send on the day before pickup** so members can plan meals.
3. **Use the survey results** to tweak shares and your renewal email.

## Tips

- Photos from the field (crops, crew, weather) usually get more replies than polished graphics.
- Being upfront about a poor crop builds trust in the shared-risk model.
