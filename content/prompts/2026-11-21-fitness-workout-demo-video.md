Fitness brands and coaches need lots of short exercise clips for ads, apps and social posts. AI video can produce them, but bodies in motion are hard for models, so prompts need one exercise, a steady camera and a clear description of the movement. This template keeps those under control.

## Prompt

```prompt
Create a [LENGTH, E.G. 6-SECOND] [ASPECT, E.G. 9:16 VERTICAL] fitness video.

Athlete: [DESCRIPTION, E.G. ATHLETIC MAN IN HIS 20S, BLACK T-SHIRT, GREY SHORTS]
Location: [LOCATION, E.G. MODERN GYM WITH RUBBER FLOOR AND DUMBBELL RACK, OR LIVING ROOM WITH A YOGA MAT]
Exercise: [EXERCISE, E.G. GOBLET SQUAT WITH A KETTLEBELL], performed slowly with controlled form: [KEY FORM CUES, E.G. CHEST UP, KNEES TRACK OVER TOES, HIPS BELOW KNEES]
Reps: [NUMBER] smooth repetitions
Camera: [ANGLE, E.G. 45-DEGREE SIDE VIEW, FULL BODY IN FRAME], tripod-steady, no cuts
Lighting: [LIGHT, E.G. BRIGHT, CLEAN OVERHEAD LIGHT WITH SOFT SHADOWS]
Mood: energetic but realistic, natural sweat, real muscle definition
Avoid: extra limbs, warped equipment, people in the background, on-screen text, unsafe form.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use one exercise per clip** and name the 2–3 form cues that matter most.
2. **Pick a side or 45-degree angle** so the movement is easy to read.
3. **Check the form frame by frame** before publishing; reshoot with a real coach if it is wrong.

## Tips

- Slower movements render more reliably than jumps or fast lifts.
- Add rep counts and cues as captions in your editor, not in the prompt.
