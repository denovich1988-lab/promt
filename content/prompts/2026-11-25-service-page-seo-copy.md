Service pages are often the pages that bring in leads for agencies, consultants and local businesses, yet many are a few vague paragraphs that don't rank and don't convince. A strong service page targets one keyword, explains who the service is for and what it includes, shows how the process works and answers the questions buyers ask before they call. This prompt drafts the whole page from your facts and keyword research.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an SEO copywriter for service businesses.

Service: [THE SERVICE]
Business and location (if local): [BUSINESS, AREA SERVED]
Target keyword and related terms: [MAIN KEYWORD, 3–6 RELATED TERMS]
Ideal client: [WHO BUYS AND WHAT PROBLEM THEY HAVE]
What's included and how it works: [STEPS, DELIVERABLES, TIMELINE]
Pricing approach: [PRICES, "FROM" PRICES OR "ON REQUEST"]
Proof I can use: [REAL REVIEWS, CASE STUDIES, CERTIFICATIONS, YEARS IN BUSINESS]
Main action: [E.G. BOOK A CALL, GET A QUOTE]

Write:
1. Title tag (under 60 characters) and meta description (under 155).
2. H1 and an intro of 2–3 sentences that names the problem and the outcome.
3. Sections with H2s: who it's for, what's included, how it works (numbered steps), pricing, proof, why choose us, FAQ (5 real buyer questions).
4. A call to action after the intro, mid-page and at the end.
5. Suggested internal links and FAQ schema notes.

Use the keyword naturally in the title, H1, first paragraph and one H2. Use only proof I provided; no invented reviews or numbers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Check the search results for your keyword** first and note what top pages cover.
2. **Fill in real proof**; a short genuine review beats a long generic claim.
3. **Publish and track** enquiries from the page, not just rankings.

## Tips

- One service per page; combining services on one page weakens both.
- Add a photo of your team or work to make the page feel real.
