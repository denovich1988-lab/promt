When people need a dentist, they search locally and pick from the map results and the first few websites. Dental SEO is about being visible there and giving patients clear information about treatments, insurance and booking. This prompt builds a practical plan for a single or multi-dentist practice, with page outlines, local tasks and content that patients actually look for.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an SEO consultant for dental practices.

Practice: [PRACTICE NAME], [CITY / NEIGHBOURHOOD], [NUMBER] dentists
Services by priority: [E.G. GENERAL DENTISTRY, IMPLANTS, CLEAR ALIGNERS, EMERGENCY DENTAL, PEDIATRIC, COSMETIC]
Insurance and payment: [E.G. IN-NETWORK PLANS, MEMBERSHIP PLAN, FINANCING]
Current website: [URL OR DESCRIPTION]
Google Business Profile status: [CLAIMED / NOT CLAIMED, NUMBER OF REVIEWS]
Nearby areas patients come from: [LIST]
Monthly capacity for content: [E.G. 2 PAGES A MONTH]

Create:
1. A site structure tree: services, treatment sub-pages, location pages, dentist bios, insurance page, new patient page, blog.
2. An outline for one treatment page (e.g. dental implants): sections, FAQs, before/after guidance, cost and insurance section, booking CTA.
3. A Google Business Profile checklist: categories, services, photos, posts, Q&A.
4. A patient review request process that follows privacy rules (no health details in replies).
5. Review reply templates that don't confirm someone is a patient.
6. A 6-month content plan based on questions patients ask.
7. Local link and citation ideas (local directories, schools, community events).
8. Technical checks: page speed, mobile booking, schema (Dentist, MedicalProcedure, FAQPage), NAP consistency.
9. KPIs and how to track calls and online bookings from search.

Avoid medical claims I can't back up, and flag content that needs a dentist's review.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with your highest-value treatments** and the new patient page.
2. **Have a dentist review treatment content** for accuracy.
3. **Track booked appointments from organic search** each month.

## Tips

- Never reveal patient information in review replies; thank the reviewer in general terms.
- Use real photos of your team and office; they build trust better than stock images.
