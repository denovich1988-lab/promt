Pricing is one of the strongest levers a business has, yet it's often set by copying a competitor. Tiers work when each one fits a clear customer type and the step up is obvious. This prompt designs tiers from your segments and costs, and lists what to test rather than presenting one "right" price.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a pricing strategist. Help me design pricing tiers.

Product: [PRODUCT AND MAIN FEATURES]
Customer segments: [SEGMENTS, WITH WHAT EACH VALUES MOST]
Current pricing (if any): [CURRENT PRICES]
Competitor prices I know: [COMPETITOR PRICING]
Costs per customer: [ROUGH COSTS]
Business goal: [MORE CUSTOMERS, HIGHER REVENUE PER CUSTOMER, MOVE UPMARKET]

Deliver:
1. The value metric: what customers should pay more for (seats, usage, projects, locations) and why it fits.
2. Three tiers (entry, core, premium): target segment, price range, included features, limits. Explain why someone would upgrade.
3. Which tier should be the one most customers choose, and how the page design can make that clear.
4. Risks: features that might cannibalise upgrades, prices that might hurt margin.
5. A simple test plan: what to test first and what result would tell me to change.

Show your reasoning. Treat all prices as starting points to test, not final answers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe segments clearly,** for example "freelancers" vs "agencies with 10+ staff".
2. **Add real cost numbers** so tiers stay profitable.
3. **Talk to 5 customers** about the proposed tiers before changing your pricing page.

## Tips

- Ask for an annual plan discount option and its effect on cash flow.
- Grandfather existing customers or give notice when raising prices.
