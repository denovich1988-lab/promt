Finding the right people for user interviews is often harder than running them. Vague invites get no replies, or replies from the wrong people. This prompt writes a user research recruiting email that is short and clear about time, reward and privacy, a 5-question screener to filter participants, and two follow-up messages, so you fill your interview slots with the people you need.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a UX researcher. Help me recruit participants for a study.

Product and research goal: [PRODUCT AND GOAL]
Who I need (behaviors, roles, experience): [TARGET PARTICIPANTS]
Who I do not want: [EXCLUSIONS, e.g. employees, people who never used the product]
Session format and length: [FORMAT, e.g. 30-minute video call]
Thank-you reward: [INCENTIVE]
How I will reach them: [CHANNEL, e.g. email to customers, LinkedIn, in-app message]
Dates available: [DATES]

Write:
1. A recruiting email or message under 150 words: who we are, why we are asking, time needed, reward, privacy note, clear call to action. Two subject line options.
2. A 5-question screener with multiple-choice answers that do not reveal the "right" answer. Mark which answers qualify.
3. A confirmation message with date, link and what to expect.
4. A reminder message for the day before.
5. A short, polite message for people who do not qualify.
Keep the tone friendly and honest. Do not promise anything I did not list.
```

## How to use it

1. **Be specific about who you need**: behaviors are better filters than job titles.
2. **Put the screener in a form tool** and link it from the email.
3. **Invite a few more people** than you need, as some will cancel.
