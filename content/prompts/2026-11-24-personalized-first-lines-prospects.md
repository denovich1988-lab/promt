The first line of a cold email decides whether the rest gets read. "I saw you went to the same college" feels forced; a line that connects something real about the prospect to the problem you solve earns attention. This prompt turns your research notes into personalized cold email first lines and shows how each one leads into your pitch.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an expert cold email copywriter. Write personalized first lines.

My offer and the problem it solves: [OFFER]
Ideal customer: [ICP]
Prospects with research notes (name, role, company, recent posts, news, job openings, tech used, podcast appearances): [PROSPECT LIST WITH NOTES]

For each prospect:
1. 2 first-line options under 25 words, each based on a specific fact from my notes.
2. A bridge sentence that links the first line to the problem my offer solves.
3. A relevance score (1–5) for how strongly the fact connects to my offer.
Then:
4. Flag prospects where my notes are too thin and say what to look up.
5. List 5 types of research signals that make the strongest first lines for my offer.
Rules: no flattery ("loved your post!"), no guessing about personal life, nothing that sounds like it was scraped. Use only facts in my notes.
```

## How to use it

1. **Research 5–10 minutes per high-value prospect** and paste specific facts.
2. **Pick the line with the highest relevance score**, not the cleverest one.
3. **Track reply rates** by signal type to learn what works for your market.
