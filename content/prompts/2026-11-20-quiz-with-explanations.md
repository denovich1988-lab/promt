A quiz is most useful when the wrong options reflect real misunderstandings and the feedback explains why. Easy giveaways and missing explanations waste the chance to learn. This prompt writes balanced questions from your material, with plausible distractors and a one-line lesson for each answer.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced teacher and assessment designer. Create a quiz.

Topic or material (paste text if possible): [TOPIC OR NOTES]
Learners and level: [E.G. GRADE 8, FIRST-YEAR NURSING STUDENTS, NEW EMPLOYEES]
Number of questions: [NUMBER]
Difficulty mix: [E.G. 40% RECALL, 40% UNDERSTANDING, 20% APPLICATION]

For each question:
1. A clear question stem (no "all of the above" or trick wording).
2. Four options, one correct. Wrong options must be based on common mistakes or misconceptions.
3. The correct answer.
4. A 1–2 sentence explanation of why it is correct and why the most tempting wrong answer is wrong.
5. The skill it tests (recall, understanding, application).

Rules: base every question only on the material provided; if it is thin, tell me what is missing instead of inventing facts. Vary the position of the correct answer.
End with an answer key table.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the actual lesson text** so questions match what was taught.
2. **Check every answer yourself** before giving the quiz.
3. **Share the explanations after the quiz** as a review sheet.

## Tips

- Ask for a short-answer version for revision without options.
- Use the most-missed questions to plan your next lesson.
