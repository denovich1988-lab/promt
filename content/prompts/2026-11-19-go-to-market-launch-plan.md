Launches often fail not because the product is weak but because the plan is: too many channels, no clear first customer, and no way to tell if it's working. This prompt builds a focused go-to-market plan that starts with one segment and grows from there, with metrics for each stage.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a go-to-market strategist. Create a launch plan.

Product: [PRODUCT AND WHAT IT DOES]
Launch date: [DATE]
Target market: [MARKET AND SEGMENTS]
Price: [PRICE]
Budget: [MARKETING BUDGET]
Team: [WHO IS AVAILABLE AND THEIR SKILLS]
Assets I already have: [EMAIL LIST, SOCIAL FOLLOWING, PARTNERS, CONTENT]

Plan:
1. Beachhead segment: the one group to win first, and why.
2. Positioning statement and 3 key messages.
3. Channels: pick the 2–3 best channels for this segment, explaining why each fits and why I'm leaving others out.
4. Timeline: pre-launch (4 weeks), launch week, post-launch (4 weeks), with weekly tasks and owners.
5. Budget split across channels and activities.
6. Metrics: one leading and one result metric per stage, and what number would make me change course.
7. Top 5 risks and how to reduce each.

Be realistic about my team and budget. Do not invent market data.
```

## How to use it

1. **List the assets you already have;** an email list or partner often beats paid ads early on.
2. **Turn the timeline** into tasks in your project tool with owners and dates.
3. **Review the metrics weekly** after launch and move budget toward what works.

## Tips

- Ask for a one-slide summary to share with stakeholders.
- Run a pre-mortem on the plan to find what could make it fail.
