A warm introduction from someone both people trust gets far more replies than a cold email, but many requests make the connector do all the work: explain who you are, why it matters and what to say. This warm introduction request email prompt writes a short ask plus a ready-to-forward blurb, so saying yes takes your contact less than a minute. It works for sales, fundraising, hiring and job searches.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me ask for a warm introduction.

Me: [NAME, ROLE, COMPANY, ONE LINE ON WHAT I DO]
Connector (the person I'm asking): [NAME, HOW WE KNOW EACH OTHER, HOW CLOSE WE ARE]
Person I want to meet: [NAME, ROLE, COMPANY]
How the connector knows them: [IF KNOWN]
Why I want to meet them: [SPECIFIC REASON, E.G. THEY'RE EVALUATING TOOLS LIKE OURS / I'M RAISING A SEED ROUND IN THEIR SECTOR / I'M EXPLORING ROLES ON THEIR TEAM]
What's in it for them: [A REASON THEY'D WANT TO TALK]
Ask: [E.G. 20-MINUTE CALL]

Write:
1. An email or message to the connector (under 120 words): a friendly opener, the specific ask, why this person, and an easy out ("no worries if it's not a fit").
2. A separate forwardable blurb (60–80 words) written in the third person that the connector can paste or forward without editing.
3. A suggested subject line for the forwarded email.
4. A thank-you note to send the connector after the intro, whatever happens.
5. A first reply to the new contact once introduced, moving the connector to BCC and proposing two time slots.

Keep it respectful of everyone's time and make no claims I haven't given.
```

## How to use it

1. **Ask only people who genuinely know the contact**; a weak connection hurts both of you.
2. **Make the reason specific**; "I'd love to pick their brain" rarely gets an intro.
3. **Close the loop** with the connector afterwards so they're happy to help again.

## Tips

- Many connectors prefer to check with the other person first (a double opt-in); make that easy for them.
- Reply to the intro within a day; slow follow-up makes the connector look bad.
