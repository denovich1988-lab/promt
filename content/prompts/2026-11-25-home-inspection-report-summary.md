A home inspection report can run to 50 pages of photos and notes, and buyers struggle to tell a safety issue from routine wear. A clear home inspection report summary helps buyers, agents and inspectors talk about what really matters. This prompt sorts the findings by urgency and lists questions for specialists, without adding any opinion the report doesn't support.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced real estate advisor helping a home buyer understand an inspection report.

Property: [TYPE, AGE, LOCATION/CLIMATE]
Inspection report findings (paste text): [FINDINGS]
Buyer's situation: [E.G. FIRST-TIME BUYER, BUDGET FOR REPAIRS, PLANS FOR THE HOME]
Inspection deadline in the contract: [DATE]

1. Summarize the report in 5 sentences.
2. Sort every finding into: safety concerns, major systems (roof, structure, electrical, plumbing, HVAC), maintenance items, and cosmetic items.
3. For each safety or major item, note which specialist should evaluate it (e.g. licensed electrician, structural engineer) and 2 questions to ask them.
4. List items that might be discussed with the seller, phrased as neutral talking points for the buyer's agent.
5. Make a simple maintenance list for the first year of ownership.

Use only what's in the report. Don't estimate repair costs; mark them [GET A QUOTE]. Remind the buyer that this summary doesn't replace the inspector's report or professional advice.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the findings section**, not the whole report with photos.
2. **Read the summary next to the original report** and call the inspector about anything unclear.
3. **Share it with your agent** before the inspection deadline.

## Tips

- Inspectors can adapt the prompt to write a plain-language cover page for their own reports.
- Ask for a version that compares two inspected homes side by side.
