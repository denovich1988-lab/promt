Journalists skim press releases in seconds and skip anything that reads like an ad. The news must be in the headline and first paragraph, with facts they can check. This prompt writes a release in the standard structure and a short pitch email, using only the facts and quotes you supply.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a PR writer. Write a press release.

Company: [NAME, WHAT IT DOES, LOCATION]
The news: [LAUNCH, PARTNERSHIP, FUNDING, EVENT, HIRE]
Why it matters and to whom: [IMPACT]
Key facts (dates, numbers, availability, price): [FACTS]
Approved quote(s) from a named spokesperson: [QUOTE OR ROUGH POINTS FOR A QUOTE]
Target media: [E.G. LOCAL NEWS, TRADE PRESS FOR LOGISTICS]
Contact details: [MEDIA CONTACT]

Write:
1. Headline (under 12 words) stating the news, and a one-sentence subheadline.
2. Dateline and first paragraph answering who, what, when, where and why.
3. Second paragraph with context and details.
4. A quote that adds a view or insight, not just "we're excited". If I gave rough points, write a draft and mark it {NEEDS SPOKESPERSON APPROVAL}.
5. Availability or next steps.
6. Boilerplate "About [Company]" (50–80 words).
7. Media contact.
8. A pitch email to a journalist (under 120 words) with a news angle for their audience.

Rules: no hype words, no unverifiable superlatives, no invented numbers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Ask yourself if it's news** to the target outlet's readers before writing.
2. **Get the quote approved** by the person it's attributed to.
3. **Send personal pitches** to a few relevant journalists rather than a mass blast.

## Tips

- Offer an exclusive to one outlet for bigger stories.
- Add images or a demo link the journalist can use.
