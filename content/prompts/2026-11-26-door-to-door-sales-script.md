Door-to-door sales gives you about ten seconds before someone decides whether to listen. Long, pushy pitches get doors closed. This prompt writes a door-to-door sales script for your product or service with a quick, honest opener, a qualifying question, a short pitch focused on the homeowner, common objection replies and a polite way to leave when it's a no.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a field sales trainer. Write a door-to-door sales script.

What I'm offering: [PRODUCT/SERVICE, E.G. SOLAR, PEST CONTROL, ROOFING INSPECTION, INTERNET, LAWN CARE]
Company: [NAME, LOCAL OR NATIONAL]
Why I'm in this neighbourhood today: [E.G. WORKING ON A NEIGHBOUR'S HOUSE, SEASONAL SERVICE, NEW AREA]
Main benefit for homeowners: [BENEFIT]
Offer or next step: [E.G. FREE INSPECTION, QUOTE APPOINTMENT, SIGN-UP]
Local rules I must follow: [E.G. PERMIT, NO-KNOCK LIST, HOURS ALLOWED]

Write:
1. An opener under 15 seconds: who I am, company, honest reason I'm here, and a question.
2. 2–3 qualifying questions to see if it's relevant.
3. A pitch under 45 seconds that focuses on their situation, with one proof point I can show (photo, leaflet, neighbour reference with permission).
4. Replies to 6 objections: "not interested", "I'm busy", "we already have someone", "how much is it?", "I need to talk to my partner", "leave a flyer".
5. A close that asks for a small next step, plus how to book it on the spot.
6. A polite exit for a no, and how to leave material.
7. Do's and don'ts, including respecting "no soliciting" signs, not misrepresenting affiliation, and cooling-off rights where they apply.

Keep it honest, short and conversational. Mark rules to check locally as {CHECK LOCAL RULES}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Practise the opener out loud** until it sounds natural, not memorised.
2. **Carry your ID and any permit** and show them early.
3. **Log every door** (no answer, not interested, follow-up) to plan your next route.

## Tips

- Mention genuine local work nearby; it's the most credible reason to knock.
- Never claim to be from a utility or the city if you're not; it's misleading and often illegal.
