Ethics applications are often returned because risks are understated, consent procedures are vague or data handling isn't explained. Reviewers want to see that you've thought through what could go wrong for participants. This IRB ethics application prompt drafts the core sections from your study design and flags the questions your committee is likely to ask.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced research ethics advisor. Help me draft my ethics (IRB) application.

Institution and committee type: [E.G. UNIVERSITY IRB / NHS REC / FACULTY ETHICS PANEL]
Study title and aim: [DETAILS]
Design and methods: [E.G. SURVEY, INTERVIEWS, EXPERIMENT, OBSERVATION]
Participants: [WHO, NUMBER, AGE, ANY VULNERABLE GROUPS]
Recruitment: [HOW AND WHERE]
Procedures and time required: [WHAT PARTICIPANTS WILL DO]
Data collected: [TYPES, ANY SENSITIVE OR IDENTIFIABLE DATA, RECORDINGS]
Storage and sharing: [WHERE STORED, WHO HAS ACCESS, HOW LONG, ANONYMISATION]
Compensation: [IF ANY]

Draft these sections:
1. Lay summary (under 200 words, no jargon).
2. Risks and burdens (physical, psychological, social, legal, data) and how each is minimised.
3. Benefits (honest — often none directly).
4. Informed consent process, including withdrawal and how consent is recorded.
5. Recruitment and avoiding coercion (e.g. if I teach or manage participants).
6. Confidentiality and data management, matched to data protection requirements I should confirm.
7. Handling distress or disclosures during the study.
Then list likely reviewer questions and weak spots in my design. Use my institution's terms where I've given them; note where its forms may ask differently.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste your actual study protocol** details; vague inputs produce vague answers reviewers reject.
2. **Copy the drafts into your institution's official form** and adjust to its headings.
3. **Review with your supervisor** before submitting.

## Tips

- Be specific about data storage: name the system, access controls and deletion date.
- Never understate risks; reviewers trust applicants who name them and show mitigations.
