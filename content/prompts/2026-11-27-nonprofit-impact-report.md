An impact report is often the main thing donors see from a charity between appeals. Long reports full of activity counts ("we held 40 workshops") rarely show what changed for people. This nonprofit impact report prompt organises your numbers into outputs and outcomes, adds one honest beneficiary story, explains where the money went and thanks supporters, in a format you can turn into a PDF, web page or email.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a nonprofit communications director. Write an impact report from my information.

Organisation: [NAME, MISSION IN ONE SENTENCE]
Report period: [E.G. 2026 / JULY 2025–JUNE 2026]
Programs: [PROGRAM NAMES AND WHAT EACH DOES]
Outputs (activity numbers): [E.G. 1,200 MEALS, 85 FAMILIES SUPPORTED]
Outcomes (what changed, with how it was measured): [E.G. 72% OF PARTICIPANTS FOUND WORK WITHIN 6 MONTHS, FROM FOLLOW-UP SURVEY]
Story (with consent): [A BENEFICIARY, VOLUNTEER OR STAFF STORY]
Finances: [INCOME BY SOURCE, SPENDING BY PROGRAM / ADMIN / FUNDRAISING]
Challenges or lessons: [WHAT DIDN'T GO TO PLAN]
Plans and needs for next year: [GOALS, FUNDING GAP]
Audience: [INDIVIDUAL DONORS / FOUNDATIONS / BOARD / COMMUNITY]

Write:
1. A short letter from the director (150 words) that thanks donors.
2. Year at a glance: 4–6 key figures with one-line explanations, separating outputs from outcomes.
3. A section per program: the need, what we did, what changed, one quote or detail.
4. The story, written with dignity and without exaggeration.
5. Finances explained in plain language, with a suggested simple chart.
6. Lessons learned and what we'll change.
7. Next year's goals and a clear way to give or get involved.
Use only the numbers I provide. Mark gaps where a figure would strengthen the report.
```

## How to use it

1. **Separate outputs from outcomes** in your notes before you start; outcomes are what donors care about most.
2. **Get written consent** for any story or photo, and change names if needed.
3. **Reuse sections** in grant reports, social posts and your year-end appeal.

## Tips

- Being open about a challenge and what you learned tends to build trust rather than reduce it.
- Check that the financial figures match your audited accounts or board-approved numbers.
