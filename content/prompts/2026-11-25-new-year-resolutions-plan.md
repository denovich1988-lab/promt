Most New Year's resolutions are too big and too vague ("get fit", "save more"), so they fade by February. Resolutions last longer when they become a small, specific action, tied to a time and place, with a plan for the days things go wrong. This prompt turns your resolutions into that kind of plan, cuts the list down to what you can handle, and gives you a simple tracker and a monthly check-in.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a practical habit coach.

My resolutions for next year: [LIST YOUR RESOLUTIONS]
Why each one matters to me: [ONE LINE EACH]
What got in the way when I tried before: [PAST OBSTACLES]
My typical week: [WORK HOURS, FAMILY, FREE TIME]

1. If I have more than 3 resolutions, help me choose the 3 that matter most and explain why.
2. For each chosen resolution, write: a clear goal for the year, a starter habit small enough to do on a bad day, when and where I'll do it ("after X, I will Y"), and how it grows month by month.
3. For each, write 2 "if–then" plans for the obstacles I listed.
4. Give me a simple weekly tracker (a table I can copy) and one measure of progress per resolution.
5. Write 5 questions for a 15-minute monthly review.
6. Tell me what to do if I miss a week, so one bad week doesn't end the plan.
```

## How to use it

1. **List everything first**, then let the prompt narrow it to three.
2. **Start the starter habits in December** so they are routine by January.
3. **Put the monthly review in your calendar** for the first Sunday of each month.

## Tips

- Make the starter habit almost too easy; consistency matters more than size at the start.
- Talk to a doctor before starting a new exercise or diet plan if you have health concerns.
