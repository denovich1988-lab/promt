Holiday scheduling causes more staff frustration than almost anything else: everyone wants the same days off and someone always feels it's unfair. A clear holiday shift coverage plan with transparent rules helps. This prompt builds a fair request and rotation process, a coverage table and a staff announcement for retail, hospitality, healthcare support, call centers and other shift-based teams.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced operations manager planning holiday coverage.

Team: [NUMBER OF STAFF, ROLES, FULL-TIME/PART-TIME]
Holiday period: [DATES AND WHICH DAYS ARE PEAK OR CLOSED]
Minimum staffing per shift: [BY DAY/ROLE]
Last year's arrangement: [WHO WORKED WHICH HOLIDAYS, IF KNOWN]
Requests received: [NAME OR INITIALS + DATES REQUESTED OFF]
Rules or agreements: [E.G. UNION RULES, HOLIDAY PAY, SENIORITY POLICY]

1. Propose fair rules: request deadline, how conflicts are decided (rotation from last year, then seniority or lottery), shift swaps.
2. Build a coverage table by day and shift showing who is scheduled, using initials, meeting the minimum staffing.
3. Flag gaps and suggest options (voluntary extra shifts, split shifts, temporary help).
4. Write a staff announcement explaining the rules and deadline.
5. Write a short reply for declined requests that explains the reason and offers alternatives.

Follow any rules I listed first. Don't consider religion, family status or other personal characteristics unless the employee raised it as a request.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste last year's arrangement** so the rotation is fair over time.
2. **Check the table against your scheduling system** and local labor rules.
3. **Announce the rules before requests open**, not after.

## Tips

- Use initials or IDs instead of full names in AI tools.
- Ask for a version for on-call rotations in technical teams.
