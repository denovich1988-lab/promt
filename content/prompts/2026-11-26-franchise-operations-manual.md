A franchise only works if every location delivers the same experience, and the operations manual is what makes that possible. Writing one from scratch is a huge job, so many business owners delay franchising or hand over a few loose documents. This franchise operations manual prompt creates a full outline and first drafts of the key sections from how you run your business today. Have a franchise attorney review anything that touches your franchise agreement or disclosure documents.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a franchise development consultant. Help me draft an operations manual.

Business: [CONCEPT, E.G. COFFEE SHOP / CLEANING SERVICE / KIDS' COACHING / FITNESS STUDIO]
What makes it work: [THE KEY STANDARDS THAT MAKE CUSTOMERS COME BACK]
How we operate now: [PASTE NOTES: OPENING/CLOSING, SERVICE STEPS, SUPPLIERS, STAFFING, SYSTEMS, MARKETING]
Franchisee profile: [OWNER-OPERATOR / MULTI-UNIT INVESTOR]
Systems used: [POS, BOOKING, ACCOUNTING, INVENTORY]

Produce:
1. A table of contents with chapters and sub-sections: introduction and brand values, pre-opening, site and fit-out standards, staffing and training, daily operations, customer service standards, products/services and quality control, suppliers and inventory, health and safety, marketing and brand use, technology, financial reporting and royalties, support and field visits, compliance.
2. For each chapter: its purpose, what must be mandatory vs recommended, and who uses it.
3. Full first drafts of the daily operations chapter, including opening, shift and closing checklists.
4. A customer service standards section with service steps and how to handle complaints.
5. A quality audit checklist with scoring for field visits.
6. A list of the information I still need to provide, marked {NEED INFO}.

Write procedures as short numbered steps, plain enough for a new manager to follow.
```

## How to use it

1. **Record how your best location actually works** before writing; that's the standard.
2. **Draft one chapter at a time** and test it with a manager who didn't write it.
3. **Have a franchise attorney review** the manual alongside your agreement.

## Tips

- Separate mandatory standards from suggestions clearly; it matters legally and practically.
- Use photos for layout and presentation standards wherever possible.
