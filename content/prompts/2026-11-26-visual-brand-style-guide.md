Without written brand rules, every new flyer, slide and social post drifts a little further from the original design. A visual brand style guide gives designers, freelancers and colleagues the same reference. This prompt drafts a complete guide from your existing brand assets, with do's and don'ts and space for the examples your designer adds.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a brand designer. Draft a visual brand style guide.

Brand and what it does: [NAME, ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
Brand personality: [3–5 ADJECTIVES]
Audience: [WHO]
Logo versions I have: [E.G. FULL COLOUR, MONO, ICON ONLY, HORIZONTAL/STACKED]
Colours (HEX values): [PRIMARY, SECONDARY, NEUTRALS]
Fonts: [HEADING FONT, BODY FONT, FALLBACKS]
Imagery style now: [E.G. NATURAL LIGHT PHOTOS OF REAL CUSTOMERS / FLAT ILLUSTRATIONS]
Where the brand appears: [WEBSITE, SOCIAL, PRINT, PACKAGING, SLIDES]

Write a guide with:
1. Brand overview: purpose, personality, how the visual style expresses it.
2. Logo: versions, when to use each, clear space, minimum size, backgrounds, and 6 don'ts.
3. Colour: palette with HEX, RGB and CMYK placeholders, usage proportions, accessible text/background pairs (state which to check for contrast).
4. Typography: hierarchy (H1–H3, body, captions) with sizes for web and print, line spacing, do's and don'ts.
5. Imagery and illustration: style rules, examples to look for and avoid.
6. Layout and graphic elements: grid, spacing, icons, shapes, buttons.
7. Applications: rules for social posts, slides, email and print.
8. A checklist for reviewing any new design.
Mark places where visual examples should be inserted with {EXAMPLE}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pull exact HEX codes and font names** from your design files before running it.
2. **Have a designer add visual examples** at each {EXAMPLE} marker.
3. **Share it as a PDF or web page** with everyone who creates content.

## Tips

- Check colour contrast for text with an accessibility checker; brand colours often fail on white.
- Keep it short enough that people will actually read it.
