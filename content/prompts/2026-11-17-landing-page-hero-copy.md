Visitors decide in a few seconds whether a page is for them. The hero — headline, subheadline and button — has to answer three questions fast: what is it, who is it for, and why should I care. This prompt gives you several distinct options to test.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a conversion copywriter. Write hero sections for my landing page.

Product: [PRODUCT NAME]
What it does: [ONE SENTENCE]
Who it's for: [TARGET CUSTOMER]
The main problem it solves: [PROBLEM]
What makes it different from alternatives: [DIFFERENCE]
Proof I can mention (optional): [NUMBER OF USERS, RESULT, AWARD]

Write 5 hero versions. Each must use a different angle:
1. Outcome — the result the customer gets
2. Pain — the problem it removes
3. Speed or ease — how fast or simple it is
4. Contrast — compared with the old way of doing it
5. Proof — built around the strongest proof point

For each version give:
- Headline: maximum 8 words, no jargon
- Subheadline: one sentence, maximum 20 words, says what the product is and who it's for
- Button text: 2–4 words, starts with a verb, describes what happens on click
- One sentence on why this angle could work for my audience

Then tell me which version you would test first and why.
```

## How to use it

1. Be precise in **"Who it's for"**. "Small businesses" is weak; "owners of 1–3 location coffee shops" is strong.
2. Pick your two favourite versions and **A/B test them** if your site builder supports it.
3. Ask for more variants of the winner: *"Give me 5 more headlines in the style of version 3."*

## Tips

- A good test: show the headline and subheadline to someone for 5 seconds, then ask what the product does. If they can't say, keep going.
- Avoid button text like "Submit" or "Learn more". "Start free trial" or "Get my report" tells people what they get.
