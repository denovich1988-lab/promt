When a decision feels stuck, it's often because facts, worries and enthusiasm are all mixed together. The six thinking hats method separates them: you look at the problem through one lens at a time. This prompt runs your decision through all six hats — facts, feelings, caution, benefits, creativity and process — and ends with a clear recommendation and next step.

## Prompt

```prompt
Use the six thinking hats method to help me think through a decision.

Decision or problem: [E.G. SHOULD WE MOVE TO A 4-DAY WEEK? SHOULD I ACCEPT A ROLE ABROAD?]
Context: [BACKGROUND, CONSTRAINTS, DEADLINE]
Options I'm considering: [LIST]
Who is affected: [PEOPLE OR TEAMS]

Go through each hat separately, with 4–6 points each:
- White hat: facts we know, facts we're missing, and how to get them.
- Red hat: gut feelings and emotions — mine and likely others' — without justification.
- Black hat: risks, weaknesses, what could go wrong.
- Yellow hat: benefits and best realistic outcomes.
- Green hat: creative alternatives, including options I haven't listed.
- Blue hat: summary, what matters most, a recommended decision or experiment, and the next 3 steps.

Keep each hat strictly to its role. Ask me up to 3 questions at the end if missing facts would change the recommendation.
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe the decision in one sentence** before adding context.
2. **Add your own points under each hat** after reading the AI's; your knowledge is the real input.
3. **Use it with a team** by having everyone answer one hat at a time in a meeting.

## Tips

- The green hat often produces a third option that beats both originals.
- Don't skip the red hat; unspoken feelings tend to derail decisions later.
