Asking customers what features they want gives you a wish list. Asking about the moment they decided to switch shows the real job they hired your product for. This prompt builds a jobs-to-be-done interview guide around that timeline and an analysis template for spotting patterns.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a product researcher experienced in jobs-to-be-done interviews. Create an interview guide.

Product: [WHAT IT IS]
Who to interview: [RECENT CUSTOMERS WHO BOUGHT IN THE LAST X MONTHS / CUSTOMERS WHO LEFT]
What we want to learn: [E.G. WHY PEOPLE SWITCH FROM SPREADSHEETS]
Interview length: [MINUTES]

Create:
1. A short intro script that sets the person at ease and explains there are no wrong answers.
2. Questions in timeline order: first thought (when they first realised they needed something), passive looking, active looking, deciding, first use, and now. 3–5 open questions per stage.
3. Follow-up probes to dig deeper ("What happened right before that?", "What else did you try?").
4. Questions to uncover the four forces: push of the current situation, pull of the new solution, anxieties, and habits holding them back.
5. Things the interviewer should avoid (leading questions, pitching, asking about the future).
6. An analysis template: job statement ("When I…, I want to…, so I can…"), triggers, alternatives considered, forces, outcomes wanted.
```

## How to use it

1. **Interview people who bought recently;** their memory of the decision is fresh.
2. **Record and transcribe** with permission, then fill the analysis template per interview.
3. **Look for repeated triggers and forces** after 8–10 interviews.

## Tips

- Interview people who chose a competitor too, if you can reach them.
- Use the job statements to rewrite your homepage headline.
