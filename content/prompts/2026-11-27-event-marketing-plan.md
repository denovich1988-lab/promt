Most event marketing problems come from timing: promotion starts too late, the reminder emails are an afterthought, and nobody follows up after the event when interest is highest. This event marketing plan prompt builds a week-by-week schedule for any in-person, virtual or hybrid event, with channels, messages, registration targets, tactics to reduce no-shows and a follow-up plan that turns attendees into leads or customers.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an event marketing manager. Build a marketing plan for my event.

Event: [NAME, TYPE E.G. CONFERENCE, WORKSHOP, WEBINAR, LAUNCH PARTY, MEETUP]
Format and location: [IN PERSON / VIRTUAL / HYBRID, VENUE OR PLATFORM]
Date and weeks until the event: [DATE, WEEKS]
Audience: [WHO, HOW MANY WE WANT]
Price: [FREE / TICKET PRICE / EARLY BIRD]
Speakers and highlights: [NAMES OR TOPICS]
Goal after the event: [E.G. SALES MEETINGS, SIGN-UPS, DONATIONS, COMMUNITY]
Channels: [EMAIL LIST SIZE, SOCIAL ACCOUNTS, PARTNERS, PAID BUDGET]

Deliver:
1. Registration target and the funnel behind it (invites → registrations → attendees), with assumptions marked.
2. Week-by-week plan from announcement to event day: channel, message, asset needed, owner.
3. Email sequence: announcement, early-bird deadline, speaker spotlight, last chance, confirmation, reminders (1 week, 1 day, 1 hour), with subject lines.
4. Social and partner promotion: post ideas, a speaker share kit, partner email copy.
5. No-show reduction tactics for this format.
6. During the event: live posting, engagement and lead capture.
7. Follow-up: thank-you email for attendees, recording email for no-shows, sales or next-step sequence, feedback survey.
8. Metrics to track and a simple results table.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start at least 6–8 weeks out** for in-person events if you can; the plan adapts to shorter timelines.
2. **Give speakers the share kit early**; their audiences often bring many registrations.
3. **Send follow-ups within 24–48 hours** while the event is fresh.

## Tips

- For free events, expect many registrants not to attend; reminders and calendar invites help.
- Decide the post-event offer before the event, not after.
