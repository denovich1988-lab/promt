Hiring managers spend only a few minutes on a portfolio, and many case studies hide the important parts — your role, your decisions and the outcome — behind long process descriptions. This portfolio case study prompt turns your project notes into a skimmable structure that shows how you think, what you did and what changed, without exaggerating.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a design hiring manager and portfolio coach. Help me write a case study. Use only facts I provide.

Role I am applying for: [TARGET ROLE]
Project and company (or anonymized): [PROJECT]
The problem and why it mattered: [PROBLEM]
My role and the team: [MY ROLE / TEAM]
Process steps I took (research, ideas, testing): [PROCESS]
Key decisions and trade-offs: [DECISIONS]
Results (metrics, feedback, what shipped): [RESULTS]
Constraints (time, tech, NDA): [CONSTRAINTS]

Deliver:
1. A title and a 3-line summary (problem, my role, outcome) for the top of the page.
2. A structure: context, problem, my role, approach, key decisions (3), outcome, what I learned.
3. Draft text for each section, short and specific, using "I" for my work and "we" for team work.
4. Which images or artifacts to show in each section and caption ideas.
5. How to present results honestly if I do not have hard metrics.
6. A 60-second verbal version for interviews.
7. Feedback: what is missing that a hiring manager will look for.
```

## How to use it

1. **Lead with the outcome**; many reviewers only read the summary.
2. **Show 3 key decisions** rather than every step of the process.
3. **Respect NDAs** — anonymize company names and blur sensitive screens.
