Private music teachers lose a surprising amount of income and goodwill to unclear rules about missed lessons, late payments and makeups. A written studio policy fixes that, but most teachers put it off because it feels awkward to write. Among ChatGPT prompts for music teachers, this one drafts a complete, friendly policy from your own rules that parents will actually read and sign.

## Prompt

```prompt
You help independent music teachers write clear studio policies.

Write a studio policy for [STUDIO NAME], where I teach [INSTRUMENT(S)] to [STUDENT AGES] [IN MY HOME STUDIO / ONLINE / AT STUDENTS' HOMES].

My rules:
- Tuition: [AMOUNT AND BILLING, E.G. MONTHLY FLAT RATE / PER TERM], due [DATE], payment methods [METHODS], late fee [AMOUNT OR NONE]
- Lesson length options: [E.G. 30 / 45 / 60 MINUTES]
- Missed lessons by student: [E.G. NO REFUND, UP TO 2 MAKEUPS PER TERM WITH 24 HOURS' NOTICE]
- Lessons I cancel: [E.G. ALWAYS MADE UP OR CREDITED]
- Illness and bad weather: [POLICY, E.G. SWITCH TO ONLINE]
- Practice expectations: [E.G. 5 DAYS A WEEK, MINUTES BY LEVEL]
- Materials and books: [WHO BUYS WHAT]
- Recitals and exams: [EVENTS, FEES, OPT-IN OR REQUIRED]
- Parent involvement for young students: [EXPECTATIONS]
- Notice to stop lessons: [E.G. 30 DAYS]
- Photos and recordings: [POLICY]

Write:
1. A short welcome paragraph about my teaching approach: [ONE LINE ABOUT YOUR STYLE].
2. The policy with clear headings and short paragraphs or bullets.
3. A one-page summary for the fridge.
4. A signature section for parent/student.

Tone: warm, firm and simple. Explain the reason behind the missed lesson rule in one sentence (the time is reserved for the student). This is not legal advice; mark anything that may need local checking with {CHECK}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Write your real rules in shorthand**; the AI will turn them into friendly sentences.
2. **Read it as a parent would** and cut anything that sounds defensive.
3. **Send it before the new term** and ask families to sign and return it.

## Tips

- A flat monthly fee with a set number of makeups is easier to explain than per-lesson billing.
- Review the policy once a year and note what changed at the top.
