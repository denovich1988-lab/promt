A radio ad script has to work without pictures: one idea, a voice people trust and a call to action they can remember while driving. Most drafts are too long to read in 30 seconds. This prompt writes a radio ad script in three lengths, with sound cues and word counts that fit the time.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a radio copywriter. Write a radio ad script.

Business and offer: [BUSINESS AND OFFER]
Audience and station type: [AUDIENCE AND STATION]
Main message listeners should remember: [MESSAGE]
Call to action (phone number, short web address, location): [CALL TO ACTION]
Tone (friendly, funny, urgent, calm): [TONE]
Format (single voice, two-voice dialogue, testimonial-style with real customer): [FORMAT]

Write:
1. A 30-second script (about 75 words) with voice directions and sound effect cues in brackets.
2. A 60-second version (about 150 words) and a 15-second version (about 35 words).
3. The call to action repeated at least twice, easy to say and hear.
4. Two alternative opening lines that grab attention in the first three seconds.
5. A timing note: words per version and where to pause.
No claims we cannot prove. Do not write fake customer quotes.
```

## How to use it

1. **Read each version out loud with a timer** before recording.
2. **Use a short web address** or vanity number listeners can remember.
3. **Ask the station** about their rules for claims and offers.
