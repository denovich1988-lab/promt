If your team describes the business five different ways, your marketing will too. A positioning statement is an internal tool that keeps messages consistent. This prompt works through target customer, category, key benefit and proof, then writes the statement and a few public-facing lines from it.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a brand strategist. Help me write a positioning statement.

Business and product: [WHAT YOU SELL]
Best customers today: [WHO LOVES YOU MOST AND WHY]
Main alternatives customers consider: [COMPETITORS OR DOING NOTHING]
What we do better or differently: [DIFFERENCES]
Proof: [FACTS THAT BACK THE DIFFERENCE]
Words customers use about us: [QUOTES FROM REVIEWS OR CALLS, IF ANY]

Steps:
1. Name the narrowest target customer that still gives us enough business.
2. Choose the market category we compete in, and say if a sub-category would serve us better.
3. Pick one key benefit that matters most to that customer and that competitors can't easily claim.
4. Link the benefit to proof.
5. Write the statement: "For [target] who [need], [brand] is the [category] that [benefit] because [proof]." Give 3 versions.
6. Turn the best one into 5 tagline or homepage headline options.
7. List 3 claims we should stop making because they are generic or unproven.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use real customer words** in the input; they make the benefit sharper.
2. **Share the three statements with your team** and ask which one sounds true.
3. **Check your homepage, ads and sales deck** against the chosen statement.

## Tips

- The statement is internal; it doesn't need to sound catchy.
- Revisit it when you enter a new market or launch a new product line.
