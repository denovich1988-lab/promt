Nonprofits, schools, churches and community groups often depend on volunteers but recruit them with a single "we need help" post. People sign up when they know exactly what the role involves, how much time it takes and why it matters. This volunteer recruitment plan prompt creates clear role descriptions, outreach messages for different channels, a simple onboarding flow and ideas for keeping volunteers coming back.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a volunteer program manager. Build a volunteer recruitment plan.

Organization and mission: [NAME, WHAT YOU DO, WHO YOU SERVE]
Volunteer needs: [ROLES, NUMBER OF PEOPLE, DATES/TIMES, SKILLS NEEDED]
Event or ongoing: [ONE-OFF EVENT / WEEKLY / SEASONAL]
Requirements: [E.G. BACKGROUND CHECK, MINIMUM AGE, TRAINING]
Channels available: [EMAIL LIST, SOCIAL MEDIA, LOCAL BUSINESSES, SCHOOLS, CHURCHES, CORPORATE PARTNERS, VOLUNTEER SITES]
Current volunteers: [HOW MANY, WHY THEY STAY OR LEAVE, IF KNOWN]

Create:
1. A volunteer role description for each role: title, purpose, tasks, time commitment, skills, what volunteers gain.
2. Outreach messages: a social post, an email to supporters, a flyer text, and a short pitch for local businesses or companies with volunteer days.
3. A recruitment timeline working back from the date volunteers are needed.
4. A simple sign-up form: the questions to ask (keep it short).
5. Onboarding: welcome email, what to bring, first-shift checklist and who meets them.
6. Retention: 6 ways to thank and keep volunteers, and a 3-question feedback survey.
7. What to track: sign-ups, show-up rate, returning volunteers.

Keep messages warm, specific and short.
```

## How to use it

1. **Write separate roles** instead of "general volunteer"; specific asks get more sign-ups.
2. **Ask current volunteers to share** the post; personal invitations work best.
3. **Send a reminder** two days before each shift to reduce no-shows.

## Tips

- Offer short, flexible shifts for people who can't commit to every week.
- Tell volunteers what their work achieved after the event.
