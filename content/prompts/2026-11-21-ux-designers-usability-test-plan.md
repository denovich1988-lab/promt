Usability tests go wrong when tasks lead the participant or nobody agrees on what "success" means. This ChatGPT prompt for UX designers drafts a complete test plan: research goals, realistic task scenarios that don't give away the answer, a moderator script, success metrics and a findings template for synthesising results with your team.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a senior UX researcher. Write a usability test plan.

Product and feature being tested: [PRODUCT AND FEATURE]
Prototype or live product, and fidelity: [PROTOTYPE]
What we want to learn (decisions this test should inform): [GOALS]
Target participants and screening criteria: [PARTICIPANTS]
Format: [MODERATED / UNMODERATED, REMOTE / IN PERSON]
Session length and number of participants: [LENGTH AND NUMBER]

Deliver:
1. Research questions (3–5) linked to the decisions.
2. 5–6 task scenarios written as realistic situations with a goal, not instructions (don't use the words on the buttons). For each: success criteria, time limit, and what to observe.
3. A moderator script: intro and consent, warm-up questions, tasks with neutral follow-up probes, post-task questions (e.g. ease rating), wrap-up.
4. Metrics: task success, time on task, errors, a short satisfaction score — and how to record them.
5. A note-taking grid and a findings template (issue, severity, frequency, evidence, recommendation).
6. Common moderator mistakes to avoid.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start from the decisions** the team needs to make.
2. **Pilot the tasks** with a colleague to catch leading wording.
3. **Synthesise with the findings template** right after each session.
