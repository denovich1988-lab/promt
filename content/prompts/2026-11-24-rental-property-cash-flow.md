A rental property can look profitable until vacancies, repairs and management fees are included. Running the numbers before you offer is the best protection. This rental property analysis prompt calculates monthly cash flow, cap rate and cash-on-cash return from your own figures and stress-tests the deal against higher rates, lower rent and surprise costs. It is not financial advice.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a real estate investment analyst. Analyze this rental property. Use only my numbers and label any assumption; this is not financial advice.

Purchase price and closing costs: [PRICE / CLOSING]
Down payment, loan amount, interest rate, term: [FINANCING]
Expected monthly rent (and source of the estimate): [RENT]
Property taxes, insurance, HOA per year: [FIXED COSTS]
Utilities paid by owner: [UTILITIES]
Vacancy rate, maintenance and capital expenditure reserves, management fee (%): [ASSUMPTIONS]
Planned repairs before renting: [REPAIRS]

Deliver:
1. Monthly and annual cash flow table: income, each expense, mortgage payment, net cash flow.
2. Key metrics with formulas: net operating income, cap rate, cash-on-cash return, debt service coverage ratio, and the 1% rule check.
3. Stress tests: rent 10% lower, vacancy doubled, a major repair, rate 1 point higher (if adjustable or refinancing).
4. Break-even rent.
5. Questions to verify (rent comps, tax reassessment after sale, insurance quotes, local rental rules).
6. A short verdict on the main risks.
```

## How to use it

1. **Verify rent with comparable listings**, not the seller's estimate.
2. **Get real insurance and tax figures**; both can change after a sale.
3. **Talk to a lender and tax advisor** before making an offer.
