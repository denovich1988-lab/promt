Station rotation lets you teach a small group while the rest of the class practises independently, but planning several self-running stations takes time. This prompt designs a complete station rotation lesson around one objective: the stations, timing, materials, student-facing directions and a quick check at each station so you know who got it.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced classroom teacher and instructional coach. Plan a station rotation lesson.

Grade and subject: [GRADE, SUBJECT]
Learning objective: [WHAT STUDENTS SHOULD BE ABLE TO DO]
Class size and groups: [E.G. 28 STUDENTS, 4 GROUPS]
Lesson length: [E.G. 60 MINUTES]
Materials and tech available: [E.G. 8 CHROMEBOOKS, MANIPULATIVES, WHITEBOARDS]
Student needs: [E.G. ELL STUDENTS, IEP ACCOMMODATIONS, ADVANCED LEARNERS]

Create:
1. A rotation schedule table: groups, stations, minutes per rotation, transition time.
2. 3–5 stations, including a teacher-led station, an independent practice station, a collaborative station and (if available) a tech station. For each: purpose, materials, step-by-step student directions written at grade level (to print on a card), and a quick check or exit task.
3. How to group students (by readiness or mixed) and why.
4. Differentiation for each station (support and extension).
5. Routines: transition signal, what to do when stuck ("ask 3 before me"), and early finisher options.
6. A short whole-class opening (5 minutes) and closing (5 minutes).
7. A tracking sheet for the teacher-led station.

Keep directions short enough that students can work without me.
```

## How to use it

1. **Teach the routines first** with a simple practice rotation before a content-heavy one.
2. **Print the station cards** and put materials in labelled bins.
3. **Use the teacher-led station data** to regroup students next time.

## Tips

- Set a visible timer; transitions are where rotations lose time.
- Keep one station identical each week so students settle in faster.
