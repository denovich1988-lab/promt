Many people accept the first offer because they don't know what to say or fear losing the job. Negotiation is easier with a number, reasons and prepared answers. This prompt builds your script for a call and an email, based on your own research and priorities.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a career coach who helps people negotiate offers. Prepare my salary negotiation.

Role and company: [JOB TITLE AT COMPANY TYPE]
Offer received: [BASE SALARY, BONUS, EQUITY, BENEFITS, START DATE]
Market range I found and where: [RANGE AND SOURCES]
My strongest reasons (skills, results, competing offers if real): [REASONS]
What matters most besides base pay: [E.G. REMOTE DAYS, TITLE, TRAINING BUDGET, START DATE]
My walk-away point: [MINIMUM]

Create:
1. A recommended counter-offer with a short reason for the number.
2. A call script: thank them, express interest, make the ask, then stop talking. Keep it natural.
3. Answers to 5 likely responses: "That's our max", "Budget is fixed", "What are you earning now?", "We need an answer today", "We can revisit in 6 months".
4. Non-salary items to ask for if base pay can't move, in priority order.
5. A polite negotiation email version (under 170 words).
6. How to accept gracefully once agreed, and what to get in writing.

Keep it confident and friendly, never aggressive. Don't invent offers I don't have.
```

## How to use it

1. **Research the range** on salary sites and with people in similar roles.
2. **Practise the script aloud** twice before the call.
3. **Get the final offer in writing** before you resign from your current job.

## Tips

- In places where asking about salary history is restricted, you can politely decline to share it.
- A pause after your ask feels long but often works in your favour.
