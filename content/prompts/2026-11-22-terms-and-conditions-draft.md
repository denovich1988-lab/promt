Copying another site's terms and conditions often means agreeing to rules that don't fit your business. This ChatGPT prompt builds a terms and conditions first draft from how your website, app or online store actually works, in plain language, and lists the points you should review with a lawyer before publishing. It is a starting point, not legal advice.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are helping me prepare a first draft of terms and conditions for a lawyer to review. This is not legal advice.

Business name, country and state/region: [BUSINESS AND JURISDICTION]
What the site or app does (content, online store, SaaS, marketplace, community): [SERVICE]
Who uses it (consumers, businesses, age limits): [USERS]
Accounts and user content (can users post, upload or message?): [ACCOUNTS AND CONTENT]
Payments, subscriptions, trials, refunds and cancellations: [PAYMENTS]
Third-party services we rely on: [THIRD PARTIES]
Anything we prohibit: [PROHIBITED USES]

Draft terms in plain English with these sections: acceptance of terms, eligibility, accounts, acceptable use, user content and license, intellectual property, payments and refunds, cancellation and termination, third-party links and services, disclaimers, limitation of liability, indemnity, changes to the terms, governing law and disputes, contact details.
Use {PLACEHOLDERS} for anything I haven't provided.
Then list:
1. Clauses that depend heavily on local law (e.g., consumer rights, refunds, liability limits).
2. Questions to ask my lawyer.
3. Where these terms must be linked or accepted (sign-up, checkout).
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe how your service really works**, including refunds and user content.
2. **Have a qualified lawyer review the draft**, especially liability, consumer rights and dispute clauses.
3. **Keep terms consistent** with your privacy policy and checkout pages.
