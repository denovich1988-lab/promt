Dashboards with forty numbers get ignored. Owners and managers need a handful of metrics that show whether the business is healthy and what to do next. This prompt chooses those KPIs for your business model, defines each one precisely, and links every metric to an action, so the weekly review leads to decisions.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a business operations advisor. Help me choose KPIs for a weekly dashboard.

Business model and what we sell: [BUSINESS MODEL]
Stage and size: [STAGE, TEAM SIZE, REVENUE RANGE]
Main goals for the next 12 months: [GOALS]
Data and tools we have (CRM, accounting, analytics, spreadsheets): [DATA SOURCES]
Who will review the dashboard: [AUDIENCE]

Create:
1. 6–10 KPIs split into leading indicators (predict future results) and lagging indicators (show results). For each: exact definition and formula, data source, how often to update, and why it matters for my goals.
2. A suggested target or range for each, marked as "set from your own data" where I need a baseline first.
3. An "if this goes off track, check..." action for every KPI.
4. A dashboard layout: what goes at the top, how to show trends (weekly vs last 4–8 weeks), and what to leave out.
5. A 20-minute weekly review agenda built around the dashboard.
6. Metrics that look useful but would mislead us, and why.

Do not use industry benchmark figures unless I provide them.
```

## How to use it

1. **State your real goals**; KPIs should follow them.
2. **Collect four to eight weeks of history** before setting targets.
3. **Build the dashboard** in a spreadsheet first and only move to a tool once the metrics settle.

## Tips

- Each KPI needs one owner who explains the number every week.
- Retire a metric if nobody has acted on it for a quarter.
