Phonics lessons work best when they are explicit, short and follow a clear routine: review, teach the new sound, practise blending and segmenting, then read it in connected text. Planning that every day takes time. This phonics lesson plan prompt builds a complete lesson for one sound or spelling pattern, with word lists, activities for different groups and a quick check to see who needs more practice.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced early literacy teacher.

Grade or age: [E.G. KINDERGARTEN, GRADE 1, OLDER STRUGGLING READERS]
Target sound or pattern: [E.G. SHORT A / DIGRAPH SH / CVCE SPLIT DIGRAPH A_E / VOWEL TEAM AI AND AY]
Sounds and patterns already taught: [LIST]
Lesson length: [E.G. 20 MINUTES]
Group: [WHOLE CLASS / SMALL GROUP / ONE-TO-ONE]
Learner needs: [E.G. ENGLISH LEARNERS, STUDENTS WITH DYSLEXIA, FAST FINISHERS]
Program we follow (if any): [PROGRAM NAME OR "NONE"]

Create a lesson plan with:
1. Objective in student-friendly words.
2. Warm-up review (2–3 minutes) of previously taught sounds.
3. Explicit teaching: how to introduce the sound, mouth position cues, and a teacher script.
4. Blending practice: 10 decodable words using only taught patterns.
5. Segmenting or spelling practice: 6 words and a dictation sentence.
6. 4 high-frequency or tricky words to review.
7. A short decodable passage (60–100 words) using the target pattern.
8. Two practice activities (one hands-on, one game).
9. Support and stretch ideas.
10. A 2-minute check: what to listen for and how to record it.

Use only words that are decodable with the sounds listed, and flag any word that breaks this.
```

## How to use it

1. **List exactly which sounds are already taught** so the words stay decodable.
2. **Check the word list and passage** yourself before the lesson.
3. **Use the quick check** to form groups for the next day's practice.

## Tips

- Keep the routine the same every day so students focus on the new sound, not the activity.
- If your school uses a set phonics programme, follow its order and terms.
