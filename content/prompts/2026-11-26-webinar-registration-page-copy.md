A webinar can have great content and still get few sign-ups if the registration page is vague about what people will learn. This prompt writes webinar registration page copy section by section: a specific headline, the outcomes attendees get, who it's for, the agenda, speaker credibility, an FAQ and form microcopy, plus variants for testing.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a conversion copywriter. Write the registration page for my webinar.

Webinar topic and working title: [TOPIC, TITLE]
Date, time, time zone and length: [DETAILS]
Audience: [WHO IT'S FOR, THEIR MAIN PROBLEM]
What attendees will learn or get: [3–5 OUTCOMES, BONUSES LIKE TEMPLATES OR RECORDING]
Speakers: [NAMES, ROLES, RELEVANT EXPERIENCE]
Agenda: [ROUGH POINTS]
Will it be recorded? [YES/NO]
Form fields I need: [E.G. NAME, EMAIL, COMPANY]
Tone: [E.G. PRACTICAL AND FRIENDLY]

Write:
1. Three headline options (specific outcome, under 12 words) and a subheadline.
2. Hero section: date/time line, one-sentence promise, button text.
3. "What you'll learn" with 3–5 outcome bullets, each starting with a verb.
4. "Who this is for" and "who it's not for" (2–3 bullets each).
5. Agenda with rough timings.
6. Speaker bios, 40 words each, focusing on experience relevant to the topic.
7. FAQ with 5 questions (recording, cost, time zone, what to prepare, Q&A).
8. Form microcopy: button text, privacy note, confirmation message.
9. A short reminder of the value near the bottom, with a second button.

Only use speaker facts I gave you. No fake scarcity or invented attendee numbers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Make the outcomes concrete** ("leave with a ready-to-use content calendar") rather than topics.
2. **Keep the form short**; every extra field lowers sign-ups.
3. **Test two headlines** with your email list or ads before the main push.

## Tips

- Put the date and time zone near the top; it's the first thing people check.
- Mention whether a recording is available; many people register just for that.
