Most cold emails fail for the same reasons: they are long, they are about the sender, and they ask for too much. This prompt makes the AI write the opposite — a short email about the reader's problem, with one small ask.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced B2B copywriter who writes short cold emails with high reply rates.

Write a cold email from me to a prospect.

About me and my offer:
- My name and role: [YOUR NAME, ROLE]
- Company: [YOUR COMPANY]
- What we do, in one sentence: [YOUR OFFER]
- One concrete result we got for a client: [RESULT WITH A NUMBER]

About the prospect:
- Name and role: [PROSPECT NAME, ROLE]
- Company: [PROSPECT COMPANY]
- Something specific I noticed about them: [RECENT NEWS, POST OR PROBLEM]

Rules:
1. Maximum 90 words in the body.
2. The first line must be about the prospect, not about me.
3. Connect what I noticed to a problem my offer solves.
4. Mention the client result in one sentence.
5. End with one low-effort question (not "Do you have 30 minutes?").
6. No buzzwords: avoid "synergy", "leverage", "revolutionary", "I hope this finds you well".
7. Plain text, friendly and direct.

Give me:
- 3 subject line options (under 6 words each)
- The email
- 2 short follow-ups (under 50 words each) to send 3 and 7 days later, each adding a new angle instead of "just bumping this"
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in every placeholder.** The "something specific I noticed" line does most of the work. A real detail — a hiring post, a product launch, a podcast quote — is what makes the email feel personal.
2. **Pick the best subject line**, then read the email out loud. If any sentence sounds like a template, ask: *"Rewrite line 2 so it sounds like a person talking."*
3. **Send the follow-ups** only if there's no reply. Each one should stand on its own.

## Tips

- Want a different tone? Add: *"Make it more casual, like a message to a colleague."*
- Writing many emails? Keep the "About me" block fixed and change only the prospect block each time.
- Always check the facts the AI adds about the prospect. Remove anything you can't confirm.
