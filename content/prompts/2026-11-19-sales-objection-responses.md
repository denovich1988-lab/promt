Objections like "it's too expensive" or "we'll think about it" are often requests for more information, not a final no. Sellers who prepare answers stay calm and curious instead of defensive. This prompt builds a response sheet for your real objections, each ending with a question that moves the conversation forward.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a sales trainer. Create an objection handling sheet.

What I sell: [PRODUCT AND PRICE]
Who I sell to: [BUYER ROLE AND COMPANY TYPE]
Main alternatives: [COMPETITORS OR STATUS QUO]
Real proof I can use: [CASE STUDIES, GUARANTEES, FREE TRIAL, NUMBERS FROM MY OWN DATA]
Objections I hear most:
[LIST OBJECTIONS, E.G. "TOO EXPENSIVE", "NOT THE RIGHT TIME", "WE USE X ALREADY"]

For each objection:
1. What the buyer may really mean (2–3 possibilities).
2. A clarifying question to find out which.
3. A short response (2–4 sentences) for the most likely meaning, using only my real proof.
4. A follow-up question that moves to a next step.

Tone: respectful, never pushy. Don't use manipulation tactics or fake urgency.
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect real objections** from call notes or your CRM rather than guessing.
2. **Role-play:** ask the AI to act as a sceptical buyer and practise your answers out loud.
3. **Update the sheet** whenever you hear a new objection twice.

## Tips

- Practise the clarifying question most; understanding the objection matters more than the answer.
- Share the sheet with your team so everyone answers consistently.
