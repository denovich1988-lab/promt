Blogs and business sites often share information that readers might act on: health tips, money advice, product reviews or DIY guides. A website disclaimer tells readers how to use that content, discloses affiliate links and sets out limits of responsibility. This prompt writes a clear first draft tailored to your site type, with notes on points to confirm with a lawyer.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a legal drafting assistant. I understand this is a first draft for review, not legal advice.

Website: [SITE NAME AND URL]
Owner: [PERSON OR BUSINESS NAME], based in [COUNTRY / STATE]
Site type: [E.G. FOOD BLOG, PERSONAL FINANCE BLOG, FITNESS COACHING SITE, ONLINE STORE, CONSULTANT WEBSITE]
Content that needs a disclaimer: [E.G. HEALTH AND NUTRITION TIPS, FINANCIAL INFORMATION, PRODUCT REVIEWS, DIY PROJECTS, AI-ASSISTED CONTENT]
Monetisation: [E.G. AFFILIATE LINKS, SPONSORED POSTS, ADS, OWN PRODUCTS]
External links and user comments: [YES / NO]
Professional status: [E.G. "I AM NOT A LICENSED PROFESSIONAL" / "LICENSED DIETITIAN IN STATE X"]

Draft a disclaimer page with:
1. A short plain-English summary at the top.
2. General information disclaimer (no professional advice, no guarantee of results).
3. Topic-specific sections only for the content I listed (health, financial, legal, DIY safety).
4. Affiliate and sponsorship disclosure in clear words.
5. External links disclaimer.
6. Errors and omissions, and how readers can report a mistake.
7. Testimonials and results disclaimer (if relevant).
8. Limitation of liability, written plainly.
9. Contact details and last-updated date.

Also give a short in-post disclosure line for affiliate links, and list points to check with a lawyer for my location. Don't claim the disclaimer removes all liability.
```

## How to use it

1. **List only the content types your site really has** so the page stays focused.
2. **Place affiliate disclosures near the links** in each post, not only on the disclaimer page.
3. **Have a lawyer review** if you give health, legal or financial information.

## Tips

- Regulators such as the FTC in the US expect clear and visible disclosure of paid relationships.
- Update the page when you add new content types or income sources.
