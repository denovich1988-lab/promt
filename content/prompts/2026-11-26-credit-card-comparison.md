Credit card offers are designed to look attractive: big sign-up bonuses, points multipliers and 0% intro periods. Whether a card is actually worth it depends on how you spend and whether you carry a balance. This ChatGPT prompt to compare credit cards uses your real spending and the terms you paste in from the issuers' pages, then calculates the value of each card in a table. Card terms change often, so always check the current terms; this is not financial advice.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a careful personal finance educator. Help me compare credit card offers.

My monthly spending by category: [E.G. GROCERIES $600, DINING $200, GAS $150, TRAVEL $100, OTHER $700]
Do I carry a balance? [NEVER / SOMETIMES / USUALLY, AMOUNT]
Goal: [E.G. CASH BACK, TRAVEL REWARDS, PAY OFF A BALANCE, BUILD CREDIT]
Cards I'm considering (paste current terms for each from the issuer's site): [CARD NAME: ANNUAL FEE, REWARDS RATES, SIGN-UP BONUS AND SPEND REQUIREMENT, INTRO APR AND LENGTH, REGULAR APR, BALANCE TRANSFER FEE, FOREIGN TRANSACTION FEE]
Current card (if any): [TERMS]

Do this:
1. A side-by-side table of the key terms.
2. Estimated rewards in year 1 and year 2+ for my spending, minus annual fees, showing the calculation. State the value per point you assumed.
3. If I carry a balance: the interest cost on each card, and why rewards may not cover it.
4. Whether I can realistically meet each sign-up bonus spend requirement.
5. Fine print to check: rate changes after intro periods, category caps, point expiry.
6. Which card fits each goal, with reasons, plus questions I should answer before applying.
7. A reminder of how applying can affect my credit score.

Use only the terms I paste; don't rely on remembered offers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pull your real spending** from the last three months of statements.
2. **Copy current terms** directly from each issuer's website.
3. **Check the arithmetic** in the table before deciding.

## Tips

- If you carry a balance, a low interest rate usually matters more than rewards.
- Only apply for what you need; several applications at once can lower your score.
