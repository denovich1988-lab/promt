Med spa marketing has a tricky balance: you need to fill the calendar, but aesthetic treatments carry health claims that regulators and platforms watch closely. This ChatGPT prompt for med spa marketing builds a full promotion (posts, emails and a booking page blurb) that explains what a treatment involves, who it suits and what to expect, without miracle promises or before-and-after hype.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a marketing writer who specialises in medical aesthetics.

Plan a [LENGTH, E.G. 3-WEEK] promotion for [MED SPA NAME] in [CITY].
Treatment or package: [TREATMENT, E.G. HYDRAFACIAL SERIES / LASER HAIR REMOVAL PACKAGE]
Offer: [OFFER, E.G. 15% OFF A SERIES OF 3 / FREE CONSULTATION]
Ideal client: [AGE RANGE, CONCERNS, LIFESTYLE]
Provider credentials to mention: [E.G. TREATMENTS PERFORMED BY A LICENSED NURSE PRACTITIONER]
Booking link or method: [LINK / PHONE]
Season or hook: [E.G. GET READY FOR SPRING EVENTS]

Deliver:
1. Campaign angle in one sentence and 3 headline options.
2. 6 Instagram or Facebook captions (under 120 words each) mixing education, myth-busting, a provider introduction, an FAQ and two offer posts.
3. 2 emails to past clients: subject line, preview text, body, call to action.
4. A booking page blurb: what the treatment is, how long it takes, typical downtime, who it may not suit, and "results vary" wording.
5. 5 FAQ answers written in plain language.

Rules: no guaranteed results, no "painless" or "permanent" claims unless I confirm them, no medical advice, always recommend a consultation. Flag any sentence that could be read as a medical claim with {CHECK WITH MEDICAL DIRECTOR}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Enter the exact treatment and offer**, including any fine print such as expiry dates.
2. **Have your medical director review** every flagged sentence and the downtime details.
3. **Schedule posts first, emails mid-campaign**, and send the offer reminder in the final week.

## Tips

- Advertising rules for aesthetic treatments differ by state and country; check yours before publishing.
- Use real client photos only with written consent, and label them clearly.
