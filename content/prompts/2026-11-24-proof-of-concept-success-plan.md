Proofs of concept and pilots often drag on for months and end without a decision, because nobody agreed what "success" meant or who would decide. A written proof of concept plan fixes scope, measurable criteria, timeline and the decision step upfront. This prompt drafts one you can review with your buyer before the pilot starts.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an enterprise solutions consultant. Draft a proof of concept (POC) plan.

Our product: [PRODUCT]
Customer company and business problem: [CUSTOMER AND PROBLEM]
Stakeholders on their side (roles) and on ours: [STAKEHOLDERS]
What the customer wants to prove: [GOALS]
Data, systems or users involved: [SCOPE INPUTS]
Desired length: [LENGTH, e.g. 30 days]
Commercial next step if successful: [NEXT STEP]

Draft:
1. Objective in one or two sentences, in the customer's words.
2. In scope and out of scope.
3. 3–5 measurable success criteria with how and when each will be measured.
4. Roles and responsibilities (RACI-style) for both sides.
5. Week-by-week timeline with check-ins.
6. Requirements and dependencies (access, data, security review).
7. Risks that could delay the POC and how to handle them.
8. Decision meeting: date, attendees, what happens if criteria are met.
9. A short email to send the plan to the champion for review.
Keep criteria specific and realistic; no promises of results.
```

## How to use it

1. **Write success criteria together with the champion**, not alone.
2. **Get the decision meeting on the calendar** before the POC starts.
3. **Share a short progress update weekly** against the criteria.
