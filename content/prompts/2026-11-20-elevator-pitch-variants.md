When someone asks "So what do you do?", a vague or overly long answer loses their interest. A strong pitch names who you help, the problem, and what makes you different. This prompt writes three lengths you can use in different situations and tailors them to the listener.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a pitch coach. Write my elevator pitch.

Who I am and my business: [NAME, ROLE, COMPANY]
What we do: [PRODUCT OR SERVICE]
Who we help: [TARGET CUSTOMER]
Problem we solve: [PROBLEM]
What's different about us: [DIFFERENCE]
Proof (customers, results, traction — real only): [PROOF]
Who I'll be pitching to: [E.G. INVESTORS, POTENTIAL CLIENTS, PEOPLE AT A CONFERENCE]
What I want them to do next: [E.G. BOOK A CALL, EXCHANGE CONTACTS]

Write:
1. A 10-second version (one sentence).
2. A 30-second version (problem, solution, difference, ask).
3. A 60-second version with a short story or example.
4. Three opening hooks to choose from (a question, a surprising fact from my input, a customer moment).
5. Two follow-up questions I can ask the listener to make it a conversation.

Write for speaking: short sentences, no jargon, no buzzwords.
```

## How to use it

1. **Read each version aloud with a timer** and trim until it fits.
2. **Practise with a friend outside your industry;** if they can repeat it back, it works.
3. **Adapt the ask** to each event or meeting.

## Tips

- Ask for versions tailored to different listeners — investor, customer, recruit.
- End with a question to turn the pitch into a conversation.
