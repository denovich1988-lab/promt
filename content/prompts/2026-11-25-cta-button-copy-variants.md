"Submit" and "Learn more" tell people almost nothing about what happens when they click. Good CTA button copy names the action and the value, and the small line under the button removes doubt ("No card needed", "Takes 2 minutes"). This prompt writes CTA variants for your exact page and stage of the funnel, grouped by approach, so you can choose a few strong options to test.

## Prompt

```prompt
Write CTA button copy options.

Where the button is: [e.g. pricing page, blog post end, email, signup form, product page]
What happens after the click: [e.g. 14-day free trial starts, demo booking page opens, PDF downloads]
Audience and stage: [COLD / WARM / READY TO BUY]
Main hesitation people have at this point: [e.g. price, time, commitment, privacy]
Brand tone: [TONE]
Button length limit: [e.g. max 4 words]

1. 15 button labels in 3 groups: action + value ("Get my free plan"), first-person ("Start my trial"), low-commitment ("See how it works"). Each within the length limit.
2. For the top 5: a supporting line under the button (max 8 words) that answers the main hesitation. Only promise what's true from my description.
3. A secondary CTA for people not ready yet.
4. 2 pairs to A/B test, with what each test would teach me.

Avoid vague verbs (Submit, Click here) and fake urgency.
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe exactly what happens after the click** — the copy must match it.
2. **Pick one option per approach** and test them against your current button.
3. **Keep the supporting line true**; if a card is needed, don't say otherwise.

## Tips

- Match the button text to the headline promise on the same page.
- Check that the label still makes sense when read out by a screen reader.
