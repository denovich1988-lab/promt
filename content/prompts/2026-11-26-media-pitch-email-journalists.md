Most press pitches get deleted because they read like a press release pasted into an email, with no reason for that reporter to care today. A good media pitch email to journalists is short, tied to what the reporter already covers and built around one clear news hook. This prompt turns your announcement into a personalised pitch, subject lines and a follow-up you can send without sounding like a mass blast.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced PR professional. Write a media pitch email to a journalist.

What I'm announcing or offering: [NEWS, DATA, EXPERT COMMENT OR STORY IDEA]
Why it matters now: [TIMELY ANGLE, TREND, EVENT OR SEASON]
Company / spokesperson: [NAME, ROLE, ONE LINE OF CREDIBILITY]
Journalist: [NAME, OUTLET, BEAT]
Recent articles they wrote: [1–2 TITLES OR TOPICS]
Assets available: [E.G. DATA SET, CUSTOMER STORY, IMAGES, INTERVIEW SLOTS]
Embargo or deadline: [IF ANY]

Write:
1. Three subject lines under 9 words, specific to their beat (no "press release" or hype).
2. A pitch email under 150 words: one opening line connecting to their recent work, the news hook in one sentence, 2–3 proof points, what I can offer them, and a simple yes/no ask.
3. A 60-word follow-up to send 3–4 business days later that adds one new detail.
4. A short checklist of what to attach or link (no large attachments).

Use plain language, no buzzwords, no exaggerated claims. Mark any fact I need to verify with {CHECK}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Read 2–3 of the journalist's recent articles** and paste the topics so the opening line is genuine.
2. **Send one pitch per reporter**, not a BCC list, and adjust the angle for each outlet.
3. **Follow up once** with something new, then move on if there is no reply.

## Tips

- Offer something only you have: original data, a customer willing to talk, or a strong expert view.
- Put the key fact in the first two lines; many reporters read pitches on their phone.
