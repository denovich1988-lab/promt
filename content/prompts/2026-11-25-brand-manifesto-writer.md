A brand manifesto states what a company believes, what it stands against and how it acts, in a voice people remember. Most read like generic poster slogans because they're written from adjectives rather than real choices. This prompt starts from the decisions your company has actually made, including the trade-offs, and turns them into a short manifesto plus shorter versions for your website and pitch deck.

## Prompt

```prompt
Write a brand manifesto for my company.

Company and what we do: [DESCRIPTION]
Who we serve: [AUDIENCE]
What we believe that others in our industry don't: [BELIEFS]
Things we refuse to do, even if it costs us: [REFUSALS]
Real decisions that show our values (examples): [EXAMPLES]
Customers' frustration with the usual way: [FRUSTRATION]
Tone: [e.g. bold and plain, warm, rebellious, calm and expert]

1. Pull out 5 core beliefs from my input, each as one plain sentence. Flag any that sound generic.
2. Write the manifesto: 150–250 words, short lines, specific, in our tone. Structure: the problem with the usual way, what we believe, what we do about it, the invitation to the reader.
3. A 50-word version for the About page.
4. 5 one-line statements for slides, social posts or the office wall.

Avoid clichés like "passion", "excellence" and "changing the world". Every line should be something a competitor couldn't sign without lying.
```

## How to use it

1. **Write the refusals honestly** — they make a manifesto credible.
2. **Read the draft to your team** and cut lines nobody would act on.
3. **Use the short versions** consistently across your site and decks.

## Tips

- Test the manifesto with one customer; ask which line they'd quote.
- Revisit it when the company makes a big decision that contradicts it.
