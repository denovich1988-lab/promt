Leases are long and often signed in a hurry. Clauses about repairs, deposits, renewals and early termination can cost a lot later. This prompt reads the lease and highlights terms worth questioning, with plain explanations and questions to ask the landlord — so you go into the conversation prepared.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are helping me understand a lease before I sign. You are not giving legal advice.

Lease type: [RESIDENTIAL OR COMMERCIAL]
Country / state where the property is: [LOCATION]
I am the: [TENANT OR LANDLORD]
What matters most to me: [PRIORITIES, E.G. FLEXIBILITY TO LEAVE EARLY, LOW EXTRA COSTS]
Lease text: [LEASE TEXT]

Do this:
1. Summarise the key terms in a table: rent, deposit, term, renewal, notice periods, rent increases, repairs, utilities, pets, subletting, early termination.
2. List clauses that seem unusual, one-sided or unclear. For each: quote the clause, explain it simply, why it could be a problem for me, and a fairer alternative wording to propose.
3. List important protections that seem to be missing.
4. List all costs I could be charged beyond rent.
5. Give me 5 questions to ask before signing.

Quote the lease exactly when referring to it. Note that local laws may override some clauses, and suggest I confirm anything important with a local lawyer or tenant advice service.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the full lease**, including any annexes or house rules.
2. **Raise the flagged clauses** with the landlord or agent before signing.
3. **Get local legal advice** for anything that involves big sums or long terms.

## Tips

- Ask for a polite email proposing the changes you want.
- Keep a copy of every version you negotiate.
