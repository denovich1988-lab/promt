Most of the value in an acquisition is won or lost after the deal closes, in how people, customers and systems are brought together. This post-merger integration plan prompt turns your deal context into a 100-day plan: integration workstreams with owners, a Day 1 checklist, communication for employees and customers, synergy tracking and the risks to watch.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced integration management office lead. Build a post-merger integration plan.

Acquirer and target: [COMPANIES, SIZES, INDUSTRIES]
Deal rationale: [E.G. NEW MARKET, PRODUCT, TALENT, COST SYNERGIES]
Integration approach: [FULL / PARTIAL / KEEP STANDALONE]
Close date: [DATE]
Known overlaps and differences: [TEAMS, SYSTEMS, CUSTOMERS, CULTURE]
Synergy targets we've stated: [LIST OR "NONE"]
Constraints: [E.G. LEGAL, UNIONS, REGULATORS, KEY PEOPLE TO RETAIN]

Create:
1. Integration principles (5) that guide decisions.
2. Governance: IMO structure, steering committee, decision rights, meeting rhythm.
3. Workstreams (people/HR, customers and sales, product, IT and systems, finance, legal, operations, culture), each with goal, owner role and top milestones at Day 1, Day 30, Day 60, Day 100.
4. A Day 1 checklist: what must work on day one (payroll, access, announcements, customer contacts).
5. Communication plan: audiences, key messages, channel, timing, and an FAQ for employees.
6. Key talent retention steps.
7. Synergy tracker template: synergy, owner, baseline, target, timing, status.
8. Top 10 risks with early warning signs and mitigation.

Mark anything that needs legal, HR or regulator review as {CHECK}. Don't invent financial figures.
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in the deal rationale carefully**; every workstream should serve it.
2. **Review the Day 1 checklist** with HR, IT and legal well before closing.
3. **Run the synergy tracker** monthly with real numbers from finance.

## Tips

- Communicate early and often; silence fills with rumours.
- Decide what you will not integrate; trying to merge everything at once slows the critical parts.
