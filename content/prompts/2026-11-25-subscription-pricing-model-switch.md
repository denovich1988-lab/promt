Recurring revenue is attractive, but turning a one-off product or service into a subscription only works when customers get ongoing value and want to pay that way. Switching too fast can upset loyal customers and shrink cash flow in the short term. This prompt evaluates whether a subscription model fits your business, sketches plan options, models simple revenue scenarios from your numbers and designs a small pilot before any full switch.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a pricing strategist.

Business and current pricing: [WHAT YOU SELL, HOW AND AT WHAT PRICE]
How often customers buy or use it: [FREQUENCY, REPEAT PURCHASE RATE]
Current numbers: [CUSTOMERS, AVERAGE ORDER VALUE, MONTHLY REVENUE, GROSS MARGIN]
Ongoing value we could offer: [E.G. REFILLS, UPDATES, SUPPORT, CONTENT, MAINTENANCE]
Competitors' pricing models: [WHAT YOU KNOW]
Concerns: [E.G. CASH FLOW, EXISTING CUSTOMERS, CHURN]

1. Assess fit: does the product deliver repeated value? Score 5 fit criteria and explain each.
2. Suggest 2–3 subscription designs (e.g. replenishment, membership, service plan) with what's included, price points to test and billing period.
3. Build a simple 12-month scenario table (conservative, expected, optimistic) using my numbers and clearly labelled assumptions for conversion and monthly churn.
4. List risks (cash flow dip, cannibalisation, churn, operations) and how to reduce each.
5. Design a 60–90 day pilot: who gets offered it, what to measure, and the result that would justify rolling it out.
6. Draft a short message to existing customers introducing the option without removing one-off purchase.

Label every assumption. Don't present scenarios as forecasts.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use real numbers** for customers, order value and margin.
2. **Challenge the assumptions** in the scenario table; churn is the one that matters most.
3. **Run the pilot** with a small group before changing prices for everyone.

## Tips

- Keep the one-off option during the pilot so you can compare.
- Check consumer rules for subscriptions and cancellations in your markets.
