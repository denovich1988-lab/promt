The abstract is the most read part of any paper and often the hardest to write: you have to fit the problem, method, results and meaning into 150–300 words. This prompt drafts a structured research abstract from your own paper or notes, keeps to your word limit and the journal's format, and sticks to your actual findings without overstating them.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced academic editor. Draft an abstract for my research paper.

Field and target journal or conference: [FIELD AND VENUE]
Word limit and format (structured or unstructured, required headings): [LIMIT AND FORMAT]
Paper text or detailed notes (aim, methods, sample, results with numbers, conclusion): [PAPER OR NOTES]
Keywords required: [NUMBER OF KEYWORDS, or "none"]

Do this:
1. Write the abstract within the word limit, covering background, aim, methods, key results (with the numbers I gave), and conclusion.
2. Keep claims in line with the results: no causal claims from correlational data, no "proves" or "first ever" unless I said so.
3. Give a word count.
4. Suggest keywords if needed.
5. Write a 2-sentence plain-language summary for non-experts.
6. List anything that seems unclear or missing in my notes (sample size, main outcome, limitations).
Use only information I provided; do not invent results.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the final results**, not early drafts, so numbers match the paper.
2. **Check the venue's author guidelines** for format and length.
3. **Read it beside your paper** and fix anything that does not match.

## Tips

- Follow your institution's and the journal's policy on AI-assisted writing, and disclose it if required.
- Summarize other papers for your review with [research paper key findings](/prompts/research-paper-key-findings/).
