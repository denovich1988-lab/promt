Associations, clubs, chambers of commerce and professional bodies live on membership fees, and most lose members not because they dislike the organisation but because nobody made the value clear at renewal time. This membership drive prompt builds a campaign to recruit new members and win back lapsed ones: a clear value proposition by segment, a timeline, email and social copy, a member-get-member offer and renewal reminders.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a membership marketing manager. Plan a membership drive.

Organisation: [NAME, TYPE E.G. PROFESSIONAL ASSOCIATION, SPORTS CLUB, CHAMBER, ALUMNI GROUP]
Membership tiers and prices: [TIERS, PRICES, WHAT EACH INCLUDES]
Current members and retention: [NUMBERS, IF KNOWN]
Target segments: [E.G. STUDENTS, EARLY-CAREER PROFESSIONALS, SMALL BUSINESSES, LAPSED MEMBERS]
Main benefits members actually use: [EVENTS, TRAINING, DISCOUNTS, NETWORKING, ADVOCACY, PUBLICATIONS]
Campaign period and goal: [DATES, E.G. 150 NEW MEMBERS]
Channels: [EMAIL LIST, SOCIAL, EVENTS, PARTNERS, PRINT]
Budget for incentives: [AMOUNT OR NONE]

Deliver:
1. A value proposition for each segment: the problem they have and the 3 benefits that solve it.
2. Campaign timeline (weekly) with channels and messages.
3. Emails: invitation to prospects, win-back to lapsed members, member-get-member ask, last-chance reminder. Subject lines included.
4. Six social posts and a short member testimonial request (to collect real quotes, not invented ones).
5. A referral offer that's simple to explain and doesn't devalue membership.
6. A welcome sequence for new members (first 30 days) to make them active early.
7. Renewal reminders at 60, 30 and 7 days, plus a grace-period message.
8. What to measure and a simple results table.
```

## How to use it

1. **List the benefits members actually use**, not the full brochure; that's what sells membership.
2. **Ask current members for short quotes** using the request template and use them in the campaign.
3. **Start the welcome sequence immediately** after someone joins.

## Tips

- Lapsed members are often the easiest people to bring back; start with them.
- A first event or useful resource in the first month makes renewals far more likely.
