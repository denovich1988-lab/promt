Billboard ad copy gets a few seconds from a driver, so every extra word costs attention. Most drafts try to say too much. This prompt writes billboard ad copy around one idea, with short headlines, a clear brand cue and a next step people can remember.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an outdoor advertising copywriter. Write billboard ad copy.

Brand and product: [BRAND AND PRODUCT]
Location and audience (highway, city street, near the store): [LOCATION AND AUDIENCE]
The single message we want remembered: [MESSAGE]
Next step (exit number, short URL, store name): [NEXT STEP]
Tone: [TONE]
Words or claims we cannot use: [AVOID]

Give me:
1. Ten headlines of seven words or fewer, in three different angles (benefit, humour, local).
2. For the best three: a layout note (headline, visual, logo, next step), and what the image should show.
3. A version for a digital billboard with two rotating frames.
4. A quick check of each headline: can it be read and understood in five seconds?
No claims we cannot prove (cheapest, best, number one).
```

## How to use it

1. **Choose one message**; billboards cannot carry two.
2. **Read the headline from across the room** at small size to test it.
3. **Check local rules** for outdoor ads before printing.
