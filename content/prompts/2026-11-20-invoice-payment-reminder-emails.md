Chasing late payments is awkward, so many freelancers and small businesses wait too long or send one angry email. A clear sequence — friendly, then firmer, then final — gets most invoices paid without damaging the relationship. This prompt writes it with your terms and details.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an accounts receivable specialist for small businesses. Write invoice reminder emails.

My business and the client: [MY BUSINESS AND CLIENT]
Invoice number, amount and due date: [INVOICE DETAILS]
Payment terms in our agreement (late fees, interest): [TERMS]
Payment methods and link: [PAYMENT OPTIONS]
Relationship with this client: [E.G. LONG-TERM, NEW, ONE-OFF]

Write 5 messages:
1. Friendly reminder 3 days before the due date.
2. Polite reminder on the day after the due date.
3. Firmer reminder at 14 days overdue, asking if there's a problem and offering a payment plan.
4. Final notice at 30 days, stating next steps per our terms.
5. A short phone call script for between emails 3 and 4.

Each email: subject line with the invoice number, under 120 words, clear amount, due date and payment link. Professional and calm; never threatening. Only mention late fees or actions that are in my terms.
```

## How to use it

1. **Check your contract terms** before mentioning fees.
2. **Set reminders** in your invoicing tool to send on schedule.
3. **Keep a record** of every reminder sent.

## Tips

- Put payment terms clearly on every invoice to prevent delays.
- For large unpaid amounts, get professional advice on next steps.
