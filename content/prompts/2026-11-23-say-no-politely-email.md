Saying no at work is hard, so people write long, apologetic emails or say yes and regret it. A good decline is short, clear and kind, and it keeps the relationship intact. This prompt writes polite "no" emails for requests, meetings, projects or invitations, in a few tones, with an alternative only when you really have one.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me say no politely in an email.

What I'm being asked to do: [REQUEST]
Who is asking and our relationship: [PERSON AND RELATIONSHIP, e.g. my manager, a client, a colleague, a stranger]
Why I'm saying no (I may not want to share all of it): [REASON]
An alternative I can genuinely offer, if any: [ALTERNATIVE, or "none"]
Tone: [TONE, e.g. warm, neutral, firm]

Write:
1. A short decline email (under 100 words) that says no in the first two sentences.
2. A warmer version that keeps the door open for the future.
3. A firmer version for someone who has asked more than once.
4. A two-line version for chat or Slack.
Rules: one short thank-you, no over-apologizing, no made-up excuses, no "maybe later" unless I mean it.
```

## How to use it

1. **Decide what you are willing to offer** before writing, if anything.
2. **Pick the tone** that fits the relationship.
3. **Send it promptly**; a quick no is kinder than a late one.
