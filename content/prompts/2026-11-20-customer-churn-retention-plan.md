Winning a new customer usually costs more than keeping one, yet churn often gets less attention than acquisition. Cancellation surveys and support notes hold the answers, but they're scattered. This prompt groups the reasons, separates what you can fix from what you can't and builds a retention plan.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a customer retention strategist. Help me reduce churn.

Business model and price: [BUSINESS MODEL AND PRICE]
Current churn rate and how it's measured: [CHURN RATE]
Cancellation reasons, survey answers, support notes or reviews from churned customers: [CHURN FEEDBACK]
What we know about how retained customers behave differently: [RETAINED BEHAVIOUR]

Do this:
1. Group churn reasons into themes and count how often each appears in my data.
2. Mark each theme as fixable by product, onboarding, pricing, support, or not fixable (e.g. business closed).
3. Identify early warning signs I could track before customers cancel.
4. Propose 6–8 retention actions, each with the theme it addresses, effort (low/medium/high), and how to measure it.
5. Write a short save offer flow for the cancellation page: questions to ask and options to offer per reason.
6. Suggest a 90-day plan in order of impact and effort.

Base the counts only on the data I gave, and say when the sample is too small.
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect at least 30 churn reasons** from surveys, tickets and calls.
2. **Remove personal details** before pasting.
3. **Start with one or two low-effort fixes** and measure churn by monthly cohort.

## Tips

- Talk to five churned customers directly — surveys rarely tell the whole story.
- Make cancelling easy; hard-to-cancel flows damage trust.
