Short safety meetings at the start of a shift work best when they're about one specific hazard the crew faces that day, not a generic lecture. Supervisors often struggle to come up with fresh, practical topics every week. This toolbox talk prompt writes a focused 5–10 minute talk with talking points, a scenario to discuss, questions for the crew and a sign-in sheet.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a workplace safety coordinator.

Industry and site: [E.G. RESIDENTIAL CONSTRUCTION, WAREHOUSE, ROOFING, LANDSCAPING, MANUFACTURING]
Topic: [E.G. LADDER SAFETY, HEAT STRESS, TRENCHING, FORKLIFT TRAFFIC, HAND TOOL SAFETY, SILICA DUST, SLIPS AND FALLS IN WINTER]
Why now: [E.G. NEAR MISS LAST WEEK, HOT WEATHER COMING, NEW EQUIPMENT]
Crew: [SIZE, EXPERIENCE LEVEL, LANGUAGES SPOKEN]
Length: [5 / 10 MINUTES]
Company rules or procedures to include: [PASTE OR SUMMARISE]

Create:
1. An opening line that connects the topic to today's work.
2. 4–6 key points in plain language, each with a "do this" and "don't do this".
3. A realistic scenario (no real names) and 3 questions to discuss with the crew.
4. A quick demonstration or inspection the supervisor can do on the spot.
5. A short summary to close the talk.
6. A sign-in sheet layout (date, topic, presenter, names, signatures).
7. Follow-up actions to check during the shift.
8. A one-paragraph version for crews who prefer a quick read, and a note on translating it if needed.

Refer to the relevant safety regulations in general terms and remind me to check exact requirements with our safety programme or OSHA (or my country's regulator).
```

## How to use it

1. **Pick a topic linked to today's tasks** or a recent near miss.
2. **Check the points against your company's safety programme** before the meeting.
3. **Keep the signed sheet** with your safety records.

## Tips

- Ask the crew questions instead of reading the whole talk; people remember what they discuss.
- If crew members speak different languages, provide the talk in each language.
