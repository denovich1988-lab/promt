Deals stall when your champion can't explain the return to finance. An ROI business case in the buyer's own numbers solves that. This prompt builds a conservative calculation from the inputs the buyer gave you, shows the payback period and writes a one-page summary your champion can present without you in the room.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a value consultant in B2B sales. Build an ROI business case for my prospect.

What we sell and the price quoted: [PRODUCT AND PRICE]
Prospect company and the problem they described: [PROSPECT AND PROBLEM]
Numbers the buyer shared (team size, hours spent, error rates, costs, revenue at risk): [BUYER NUMBERS]
Implementation costs and time on their side: [IMPLEMENTATION]
Results our customers typically see (only real, approved ones): [PROOF, or "none"]

Create:
1. A table of costs (license, implementation, internal time) over 1 and 3 years.
2. A table of benefits with the formula for each line, using the buyer's numbers.
3. ROI, payback period and a conservative / expected / optimistic view.
4. A list of every assumption, marked "from buyer" or "estimated".
5. A one-page executive summary: the problem, the cost of doing nothing, the investment, the return and the recommended next step.
6. Three questions finance is likely to ask, with honest answers.
Keep it conservative. If a benefit can't be measured, list it separately as a non-financial benefit.
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect numbers on discovery calls** and confirm them with the buyer.
2. **Review the assumptions together** with your champion before it goes to finance.
3. **Attach the summary** to your proposal.

## Tips

- Lay out the steps to signature with the [mutual action plan](/prompts/mutual-action-plan-b2b/) prompt.
- Use the summary as a slide in your [sales pitch deck outline](/prompts/sales-deck-outline/).
