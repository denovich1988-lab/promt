Customer video testimonials only work when they sound real, and that depends on the questions you ask. "What do you like about us?" gets generic praise; questions about the problem before, the moment they decided and what changed get a story. This video testimonial interview questions prompt plans the interview, prepares the customer and suggests how to cut the footage into clips for your website, ads and social media.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a customer marketing manager. Plan a video testimonial interview.

My business: [WHAT YOU SELL AND TO WHOM]
Customer: [ROLE, COMPANY OR TYPE OF CUSTOMER, HOW LONG THEY'VE USED US]
What we know about their result: [E.G. CUT ADMIN TIME, GREW BOOKINGS, FELT LESS STRESSED]
Where the video will be used: [WEBSITE, ADS, LINKEDIN, SALES DECK, YOUTUBE]
Format: [IN PERSON / REMOTE RECORDING], length of interview: [E.G. 20 MINUTES]

Create:
1. 12–15 open interview questions in story order: life before, the problem, what they tried, why they chose us, first experience, results, advice to someone like them. Include follow-up prompts like "can you give me an example?".
2. Tips to help the customer answer in full sentences (so clips make sense without the question).
3. A short prep email to the customer: what to expect, time needed, what to wear, consent and approval process.
4. A shot list and setup checklist for [IN PERSON / REMOTE].
5. An edit plan: a 60–90 second main video, three 15-second clips for ads or social, and pull quotes for the website, with which question each is likely to come from.

Don't script the customer's answers; their own words are what make it credible.
```

## How to use it

1. **Send the prep email a few days ahead** but not the full question list, so answers stay natural.
2. **Get written consent** for where and how long you'll use the video.
3. **Let the customer approve the final cut** before publishing.

## Tips

- Ask your best question twice in different ways; the second answer is often better.
- Record a few seconds of the customer at work for B-roll.
