Legal search is competitive and expensive in paid ads, so organic rankings matter a lot for small and mid-size firms. But law firm SEO has extra limits: advertising rules from the bar, no guarantees about outcomes, and pages that must stay accurate. This prompt builds a practical plan around your practice areas and cities, with page outlines and content ideas that respect those limits.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an SEO strategist who works with law firms.

Firm: [FIRM NAME], [NUMBER] attorneys
Practice areas (by priority): [E.G. PERSONAL INJURY, WORKERS' COMP, FAMILY LAW]
Office locations and areas served: [CITIES / COUNTIES]
Current site: [URL OR DESCRIPTION: NUMBER OF PAGES, BLOG, ATTORNEY BIOS]
Where we rank now (if known): [NOTES]
Competitors we see in local results: [FIRM NAMES OR "UNKNOWN"]
Monthly capacity: [E.G. 4 PAGES A MONTH, ATTORNEY REVIEW TIME AVAILABLE]

Create:
1. A site structure: practice area hubs, sub-topic pages (e.g. car accidents → truck accidents), location pages, attorney bios, FAQ/resources. Show it as a tree.
2. An outline for one practice area page: H1, sections, FAQs, trust signals, call to action.
3. A rule for location pages so each one has genuinely local content, not copies with the city swapped.
4. Google Business Profile checklist for each office, including categories, services and post ideas.
5. A compliant review request process (timing, wording, what not to offer).
6. A 6-month content plan: questions clients ask before hiring a lawyer, by practice area.
7. Link-building ideas that suit law firms (local organisations, scholarships only if genuine, legal directories, guest articles).
8. Technical checks: schema types (LegalService, Attorney, FAQPage), speed, mobile, internal links.
9. KPIs and a monthly report template.

Remind me where content needs attorney review or a disclaimer under my state's advertising rules.
```

## How to use it

1. **List practice areas in order of value** so the plan starts with pages that bring cases.
2. **Have an attorney review every page** for accuracy and advertising compliance.
3. **Track calls and form leads by page**, not only rankings.

## Tips

- Avoid words like "specialist" or "expert" unless your state bar allows them for your certification.
- Never write about case results without the disclaimer your jurisdiction requires.
