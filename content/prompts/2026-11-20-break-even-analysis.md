Before launching a product or setting a price, you need to know how much you must sell just to stop losing money. A break-even analysis answers that with simple maths. This prompt walks through the calculation with your numbers, shows the formulas and tests a few what-if changes.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a small business finance tutor. Do a break-even analysis with my numbers.

Product or business: [WHAT YOU SELL]
Selling price per unit: [PRICE]
Variable cost per unit (materials, packaging, fees, shipping): [COSTS PER UNIT]
Fixed costs per month (rent, salaries, software, insurance): [FIXED COSTS]
Expected sales per month: [UNITS]
Currency: [CURRENCY]

Calculate and explain:
1. Contribution margin per unit and contribution margin ratio, with formulas.
2. Break-even point in units and in revenue per month.
3. Margin of safety based on my expected sales.
4. Profit or loss at my expected sales.
5. What-if table: price +10% and −10%, variable costs +15%, fixed costs +20% — new break-even for each.
6. Units needed to reach a target monthly profit of [TARGET PROFIT].
7. Three practical ways to lower the break-even point.

Show every calculation step and present results in a table I can copy into a spreadsheet. Flag any cost I may have missed (e.g. payment fees, returns).
```

## How to use it

1. **List every variable cost per unit,** including payment and platform fees.
2. **Copy the table into a spreadsheet** and check the maths with formulas.
3. **Revisit it when prices or costs change.**

## Tips

- If break-even is far above realistic sales, rethink price or costs before launch.
- For several products, ask for a weighted break-even based on your sales mix.
