A well-built glossary captures "what is X" searches, helps new buyers learn your field and gives you natural internal links to deeper pages. Thin one-line definitions add little. This prompt plans an SEO glossary page or glossary hub with useful definitions, examples and links, and flags terms that deserve their own article.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an SEO content strategist. Plan and write a glossary for my site.

Industry and site: [INDUSTRY AND SITE]
Audience and their knowledge level: [AUDIENCE]
Terms I want to include (or "suggest"): [TERMS]
Key product or service pages to link to: [KEY PAGES]
Format: [FORMAT, e.g. one glossary page, or a hub with one page per term]

Deliver:
1. A list of 30 terms grouped by topic, with the likely search intent for each; mark terms that deserve a full standalone article.
2. A page structure: intro, A–Z navigation, term entries, related terms.
3. Entries for the first 10 terms: a one-sentence definition people can quote, 2–3 sentences of explanation, a short example, related terms, and a suggested internal link.
4. Title tag and meta description for the glossary page.
5. Advice on whether to use DefinedTerm or FAQ schema and how.
Write definitions in plain language. Do not copy definitions from other sites.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with terms your sales team explains** to customers most often.
2. **Write standalone articles** for the terms marked as deserving one, and link them from the glossary.
3. **Add new terms monthly** to keep the page growing.
