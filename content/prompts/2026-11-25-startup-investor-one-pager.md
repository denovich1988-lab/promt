Many investors look at a one-page summary before they open a pitch deck. It has to explain the company in a couple of minutes: what problem, for whom, why now, what's working and what you're raising. This prompt drafts that one-pager from your facts, keeps it tight, and flags weak spots an investor is likely to question, so you can fix them before sending.

## Prompt

```prompt
Write a one-page investor summary for my startup.

Company name and one-line description: [DESCRIPTION]
Problem and who has it: [PROBLEM]
Solution and how it works: [SOLUTION]
Traction (real numbers with dates): [e.g. revenue, users, growth, pilots, waitlist]
Business model and pricing: [MODEL]
Market (with source if you have one): [MARKET]
Competition and why we win: [COMPETITION]
Team (relevant experience only): [TEAM]
Raise: [AMOUNT, ROUND, WHAT IT FUNDS, MILESTONES IT REACHES]
Contact: [NAME, EMAIL]

Format: headline, then short sections with bold labels — Problem, Solution, Traction, Business model, Market, Competition, Team, The ask. Max 400 words.
Write plainly; no hype words, no claims I haven't given.

After the one-pager, list:
- The 5 questions an investor will most likely ask after reading it.
- The 2 weakest sections and what evidence would strengthen them.
```

## How to use it

1. **Lead with traction** if it's strong; move it up in the order.
2. **Use the investor questions** to prepare your first meeting.
3. **Export to a clean PDF** with your logo and contact details.

## Tips

- Use bottom-up market sizing rather than a big headline number.
- Keep a version for each investor type (angel, seed fund) with the ask tailored.
