Reviewers read the budget justification to see whether costs are necessary, reasonable and tied to the work described. Vague lines like "supplies: $8,000" invite cuts or questions. This grant budget justification prompt explains each cost category in the language reviewers expect, links every item to your aims or activities and flags anything that may break the funder's rules.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced grants manager. Write a budget justification for my proposal.

Funder and programme: [NAME, CALL]
Funder budget rules: [PASTE KEY RULES — ALLOWED/DISALLOWED COSTS, INDIRECT RATE, CAPS, MATCH REQUIREMENTS]
Project summary and aims/activities: [SHORT SUMMARY WITH NUMBERED AIMS OR ACTIVITIES]
Project length: [MONTHS OR YEARS]
Budget lines (category, item, amount per year, how calculated):
[PASTE BUDGET — E.G. PERSONNEL: PROJECT COORDINATOR, 50% EFFORT, $X SALARY + Y% FRINGE]

Write:
1. A justification for each category (personnel, fringe, travel, equipment, supplies, participant costs, contractual, other, indirect), stating what it is, why it's needed, which aim it supports and how the amount was calculated.
2. A short narrative tying the overall budget to the project's scale.
3. A table: line item, amount, calculation, linked aim.
4. A list of possible problems: costs that may be disallowed, totals that look high, missing items reviewers might expect, maths that doesn't add up.
Use the funder's category names. Don't change my numbers; point out errors instead.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the funder's budget rules** from the call; requirements differ widely.
2. **Include your calculation for each line** so the justification can show the maths.
3. **Have your grants office check** rates, fringe and indirect costs before submission.

## Tips

- Personnel justifications should name the role, effort and specific tasks.
- Keep the budget and narrative consistent: every activity in the proposal should have a cost.
