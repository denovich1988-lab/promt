Teaching vocabulary well means more than a list of words and dictionary definitions: students need friendly explanations, examples in context, word parts and several chances to use each word. Preparing all of that for every unit takes time. This vocabulary list prompt for teachers creates a tiered word list from your unit or text, with student-friendly definitions, examples and a week of practice activities that end in a quick check.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a literacy specialist. Create vocabulary materials for a unit.

Subject, grade and unit: [E.G. 5TH GRADE SCIENCE, ECOSYSTEMS]
Text or topic (paste a passage if you have one): [PASTE OR DESCRIBE]
Number of words: [E.G. 10–12]
Students: [READING LEVELS, ELL STUDENTS, SPECIAL NEEDS]
Time available: [E.G. 10 MINUTES A DAY FOR A WEEK]

Create:
1. A word list split into key academic words (useful across subjects) and topic-specific words, chosen from the text if provided.
2. For each word: a student-friendly definition, an example sentence linked to the unit, part of speech, word parts or related words (prefixes, roots, suffixes), and a quick visual or gesture idea.
3. A simplified version of definitions for ELL or struggling readers.
4. Daily 10-minute activities for one week (e.g. word sorts, "which word fits", sentence stems, word maps, a speaking game).
5. A 10-question quiz mixing formats, with an answer key.
6. 3 ways students can use the words in writing or discussion during the unit.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the unit text** so the words come from what students will actually read.
2. **Choose fewer words** for younger students; depth beats quantity.
3. **Keep the list visible** in class all unit and reward students who use the words.

## Tips

- Revisit the words in the following units so they move into long-term memory.
- Let students create their own example sentences; it shows real understanding.
