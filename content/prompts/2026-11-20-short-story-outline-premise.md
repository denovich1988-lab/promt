A good premise is only the start: many short stories stall because the character has no clear goal or the ending doesn't connect to the beginning. This prompt builds a scene-by-scene outline from your idea and gives you choices instead of writing the story for you.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a fiction editor. Help me outline a short story.

Premise: [PREMISE]
Genre and tone: [GENRE AND TONE]
Target length: [WORD COUNT]
Main character (what I know so far): [CHARACTER]
Themes or feelings I want readers left with: [THEMES]

Create:
1. Logline — one sentence with character, goal, obstacle and stakes.
2. Main character sheet: want, need, flaw, what they fear losing.
3. 3 possible endings in one line each, with the emotional effect of each. Recommend one.
4. A scene-by-scene outline (5–8 scenes) for the recommended ending: what happens, what changes, the conflict in the scene.
5. The turning point and how it is set up earlier.
6. 3 details or images that could recur and tie the story together.
7. 3 questions I should answer before drafting.

Give me structure and options only. Don't write the story's prose.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with a premise you care about**, even if it's rough.
2. **Pick an ending first**, then shape the scenes toward it.
3. **Write the first draft yourself** from the outline.

## Tips

- Ask for an outline in a different structure (e.g. non-linear) to compare.
- Cut any scene where nothing changes.
