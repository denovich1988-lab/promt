Generic lists of ChatGPT prompts for teachers rarely fit your subject, grade or school rules. A small personal prompt library, with 15–20 prompts you actually reuse, saves more time than hundreds you'll never open. This prompt builds that library around your weekly tasks, with blanks to fill and a note on what each prompt should never include, such as student personal data.

## Prompt

```prompt
Build me a personal AI prompt library for teaching.

Subject(s) and grade level: [SUBJECT, GRADE]
Curriculum or standards: [e.g. Common Core, national curriculum, IB]
Class details (no student names): [e.g. 28 students, 4 English learners, mixed reading levels]
Tasks that take me the most time each week: [e.g. lesson planning, differentiation, feedback, parent emails, quizzes]
School rules about AI use: [RULES OR "none given"]

Create 15 prompts grouped by: Planning, Differentiation, Assessment and feedback, Communication, Classroom routines.
For each prompt:
- A short name
- The prompt itself, with blanks in [BRACKETS]
- When to use it (one line)
- A privacy note if relevant (what not to paste)

Then add:
- A 5-line "house style" block I can paste at the start of any chat (my grade, tone, standards, formatting).
- 3 ways to check AI-made materials for accuracy before using them in class.
```

## How to use it

1. **List your real time-consuming tasks** so the library fits your week.
2. **Save the prompts** in a document or notes app, grouped as given.
3. **Refine one prompt a week** based on what worked in class.

## Tips

- Never paste student names, grades or personal details into AI tools.
- Share the library with your department and collect each other's best prompts.
