Before investing in a new product, a concept test shows whether your target customers understand the idea, find it appealing and would consider buying it. The hard part is writing a concept description and questions that don't lead people to say yes. This prompt builds a neutral product concept test survey, including screening, the concept board text and an analysis plan.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a market research consultant. Design a concept test survey.

Product concept: [WHAT IT IS, WHO IT'S FOR, KEY BENEFITS, PLANNED PRICE]
Alternatives customers use today: [COMPETITORS OR WORKAROUNDS]
Target respondents: [WHO, HOW I'LL RECRUIT THEM]
Number of concepts to test: [1, OR 2–3 VARIANTS]
Decisions this will inform: [E.G. GO/NO-GO, WHICH POSITIONING, PRICE RANGE]
Survey tool: [TOOL]

Create:
1. Screening questions to reach the right respondents (without revealing the concept).
2. Context questions about current behaviour and problems.
3. A neutral concept description (80–120 words) with headline, problem, how it works, key benefits and price; avoid hype words. If testing variants, write each and explain a monadic or sequential monadic design.
4. Core questions: purchase intent (5-point), uniqueness, relevance, believability, clarity, price perception, likes and dislikes (open), what it would replace.
5. Optional pricing questions (e.g. Van Westendorp) and when to include them.
6. Demographics at the end, only what I need.
7. Analysis plan: top-2-box scores, comparing variants, segment cuts, coding open answers, and what results would suggest go, refine or stop, framed as my own thresholds to set.

Keep wording neutral and avoid double-barrelled questions.
```

## How to use it

1. **Write the concept as you'd really sell it**, including price; testing without price inflates interest.
2. **Run a small pilot** to check timing and confusing wording.
3. **Read open answers carefully**; they explain the scores.

## Tips

- Stated purchase intent overstates real buying; use it to compare concepts, not to forecast sales.
- Test against a benchmark concept or a past product when you can.
