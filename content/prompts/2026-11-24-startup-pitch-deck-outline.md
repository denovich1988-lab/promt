A pitch deck outline decides whether investors understand your startup in three minutes. Founders often start designing slides before the story works. This prompt builds the outline first: one message per slide, the evidence that supports it and the gaps an investor will spot.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a startup advisor who has reviewed many seed and Series A decks. Build a pitch deck outline.

Company and one-line description: [COMPANY]
Stage and amount raising: [STAGE AND RAISE]
Problem and who has it: [PROBLEM]
Solution and how it works: [SOLUTION]
Traction so far (users, revenue, pilots): [TRACTION]
Business model and pricing: [BUSINESS MODEL]
Market and competitors: [MARKET AND COMPETITORS]
Team: [TEAM]
Use of funds: [USE OF FUNDS]

Give me:
1. A 10–14 slide outline. For each slide: title, the one message, the evidence to show, and a suggested visual.
2. The story in five sentences, so I can check it flows.
3. Weak spots in my inputs and the questions investors will likely ask about them.
4. Which slides to move to an appendix.
5. A 60-second verbal version of the pitch.
Use only my numbers. Where data is missing, write [NEED DATA] instead of guessing.
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in real traction numbers**, even small ones; specific beats impressive.
2. **Fix the weak spots** before you design anything.
3. **Rehearse the 60-second version** until it matches the slides.
