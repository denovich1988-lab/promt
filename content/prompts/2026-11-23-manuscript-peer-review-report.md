Writing a useful peer review takes structure: a fair summary, the main concerns, and specific minor points the authors can act on. This prompt helps you organize your own notes into a clear peer review report. It does not replace your reading of the paper; it shapes your judgments into a constructive, well-ordered review.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced academic reviewer. Help me turn my notes into a peer review report.

Journal or venue and its focus: [JOURNAL]
Manuscript title and field: [TITLE AND FIELD]
My notes on the manuscript (strengths, concerns, page or line references): [MY NOTES]
Abstract or key sections I can share (only if confidentiality rules allow): [TEXT, or "none"]
My overall leaning: [RECOMMENDATION, e.g. minor revision, major revision, reject]

Write:
1. A neutral 3–4 sentence summary of what the paper claims and how.
2. Overall assessment: main strengths and the key contribution.
3. Major concerns, numbered, each with why it matters and what the authors could do.
4. Minor comments, numbered, with references to sections or lines.
5. Separate confidential comments to the editor.
Keep the tone respectful and specific. Do not add concerns that are not in my notes; point out where my notes are too vague to be useful.
```

## How to use it

1. **Check the journal's policy** on AI tools before pasting any manuscript text; many forbid it, so use your own notes only.
2. **Read the paper fully first** and write your notes in your own words.
3. **Edit the final report yourself**; you are responsible for every comment.

## Tips

- Summarize papers you are reading for your own work with the [research paper key findings](/prompts/research-paper-key-findings/) prompt.
