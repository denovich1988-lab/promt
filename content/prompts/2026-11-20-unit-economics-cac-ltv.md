A business can grow fast and still lose money on every customer. Unit economics show whether each new customer pays back what it cost to win them. This prompt walks through the calculations step by step with your own numbers, shows the formulas and points to the lever with the biggest effect.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a finance advisor for small and growing businesses. Analyse my unit economics.

Business model: [E.G. SUBSCRIPTION, ECOMMERCE, SERVICES]
Monthly marketing and sales spend: [SPEND]
New customers per month: [NEW CUSTOMERS]
Average revenue per customer per month (or per order and orders per year): [REVENUE]
Gross margin %: [MARGIN]
Monthly churn % or average customer lifetime: [CHURN OR LIFETIME]

Do this:
1. Calculate CAC, monthly gross profit per customer, LTV (gross-margin based), LTV:CAC ratio and CAC payback in months. Show each formula and the numbers you plugged in.
2. Explain in plain words what each result means for my business.
3. Run a sensitivity table: what happens to LTV:CAC if churn, CAC or price each improves by 10% and 20%.
4. Name the single lever with the biggest effect and 3 practical ways to improve it.
5. List any input that looks unusual or that I should double-check.

Do the arithmetic carefully. If an input is missing, state the assumption you used.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use at least 3 months of data** to smooth out unusual months.
2. **Check the maths** in a spreadsheet using the formulas shown.
3. **Rerun quarterly** to see whether the ratios improve.

## Tips

- Split CAC by channel if you can — averages hide expensive channels.
- This is analysis, not financial advice; review big decisions with an accountant.
