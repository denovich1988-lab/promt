A holiday out-of-office message should tell people three things fast: when you are back, who can help now, and what counts as urgent. This prompt writes short versions for clients, colleagues and a fun internal option, with the dates and contacts in the right place so nobody has to read it twice.

## Prompt

```prompt
Write my holiday out-of-office auto-reply.

My role and who emails me most: [ROLE AND MAIN CONTACTS, e.g. clients, vendors, colleagues]
Dates away (include time zone): [DATES]
Who to contact instead and for what: [BACKUP CONTACTS AND TOPICS]
How urgent matters should be handled: [URGENT ROUTE, or "none"]
Will I check email at all? [YES, OCCASIONALLY / NO]
Tone: [TONE, e.g. professional, warm, light]

Write:
1. A short external version for clients (under 70 words).
2. An internal version for colleagues.
3. A warm, slightly playful version I can use if it fits my workplace.
4. A one-line version for a mobile or SMS auto-reply.
Put the return date in the first sentence. Do not promise replies I can't keep, and don't mention where I am going.
```

## How to use it

1. **Confirm your backup contact** agreed before you list them.
2. **Paste the version you want** into your email auto-reply settings.
3. **Set an end date** so the reply turns off by itself.

## Tips

- When you get back, clear the pile with the [email backlog reply drafts](/prompts/email-backlog-reply-drafts/) prompt.
- Leaving out travel details is a simple security habit.
