Without a customer success playbook, every CSM handles onboarding, risk and renewals their own way, and results depend on who owns the account. This prompt builds a practical playbook for the full customer lifecycle: each play has a trigger, an owner, clear steps, timing and a ready message, sized to the team and tools you actually have.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a VP of Customer Success. Build a customer success playbook for my team.

Product and customers: [PRODUCT, WHO BUYS, SEGMENTS]
Contract model: [E.G. ANNUAL SAAS, MONTHLY, USAGE-BASED]
Team: [NUMBER OF CSMS, ACCOUNTS PER CSM, TECH-TOUCH OR HIGH-TOUCH]
Tools: [CRM, CS PLATFORM, EMAIL TOOL]
Biggest problems now: [E.G. SLOW ONBOARDING, SURPRISE CHURN, NO EXPANSION]
What "value" means for our customers: [FIRST OUTCOME THEY CARE ABOUT]

Create plays for: kickoff and onboarding; first value milestone; adoption push for low usage; executive sponsor change; support escalation or bad survey score; expansion signal; renewal (starting 120 days out); churn save.

For each play give: trigger (data or event), goal, owner, steps with timing, the customer-facing message (email under 120 words), internal handoffs, and the success metric.

Also add: a lifecycle overview table, how plays differ by segment (high-touch vs. tech-touch), a weekly CSM routine, and the 5 metrics leadership should review monthly.

Keep it practical for my team size. Mark anything needing new tooling as {NEW TOOL}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with the 3 plays** that address your biggest problem; don't launch all of them at once.
2. **Set up triggers** in your CRM or CS tool so plays start automatically.
3. **Review play results each quarter** and retire plays that don't change outcomes.

## Tips

- Define "first value" in the customer's words; it shapes the whole onboarding play.
- Ask CSMs to suggest edits to messages; adoption is higher when they own the wording.
