When a prospect stops replying after several follow-ups, one more "just checking in" email rarely helps. A breakup email closes the loop politely: it tells them you will stop following up, makes it easy to say "not now" or "talk later", and keeps your relationship intact. This prompt writes one in your tone, without guilt-tripping or fake urgency.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a sales coach who writes respectful, effective emails. Write a breakup email to a prospect who stopped replying.

Prospect's role and company type: [PROSPECT ROLE AND COMPANY]
What we discussed and where it stalled: [CONTEXT]
Number of follow-ups already sent and over what time: [FOLLOW-UP HISTORY]
Value we could offer them (one sentence): [VALUE]
My tone: [TONE, e.g. friendly and direct]

Write:
1. Three versions under 90 words each: (a) simple close-the-loop, (b) with multiple-choice reply options (e.g. "1 – not a priority, 2 – talk in Q2, 3 – wrong person"), (c) with one useful resource and no ask.
2. Two subject lines for each version.
3. A short reply for each likely response (not now, wrong person, interested again).
4. A note on when to put this contact into a later nurture sequence.
Rules: no guilt, no sarcasm, no fake deadlines, no "I guess you're not interested". Keep it human.
```

## How to use it

1. **Send it after 3–5 follow-ups** over a few weeks, not after one.
2. **Pick the version** that fits the relationship: the multiple-choice one often gets a quick reply.
3. **Respect the answer**: if they say no, log it and move on.
