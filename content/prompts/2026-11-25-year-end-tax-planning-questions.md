Year-end tax planning happens before December 31, when there is still time to act: timing purchases, contributions, invoices or bonuses. But many small business owners arrive at their accountant's office unprepared, and the meeting ends up being about finding documents instead of making decisions. This prompt doesn't give tax advice; it organises your numbers, builds a document checklist and writes the questions to ask your accountant so the meeting is short and useful.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an organised assistant helping me prepare for a year-end tax planning meeting with my accountant. Do not give me tax advice or say what I can deduct; help me prepare questions.

Country or state: [WHERE YOU FILE]
Business type and structure: [E.G. SOLE PROPRIETOR, LLC, LIMITED COMPANY]
Year-to-date numbers: [REVENUE, MAIN EXPENSES, PROFIT SO FAR, IF KNOWN]
Changes this year: [NEW HIRES, BIG PURCHASES, NEW LOCATION, LOANS, NEW INCOME STREAMS]
Plans for next year: [E.G. BUY EQUIPMENT, HIRE, EXPAND]
Personal situation that may matter: [E.G. RETIREMENT SAVINGS, HOME OFFICE, VEHICLE USE]

1. Summarise my situation in a short brief the accountant can read in 2 minutes.
2. Create a checklist of documents to bring, grouped by category.
3. Write 10–15 questions to ask, covering timing of income and expenses, equipment purchases, retirement contributions, payroll and bonuses, estimated tax payments, and record-keeping for next year.
4. List the deadlines I should ask the accountant to confirm.
5. Leave a section for notes and decisions from the meeting.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pull year-to-date numbers** from your accounting software first.
2. **Send the brief and questions** to your accountant a few days before the meeting.
3. **Record decisions and deadlines** in the notes section afterwards.

## Tips

- Book the meeting in early December; some options need action before year end.
- Rules differ by country and change often, so rely on your accountant for the answers.
