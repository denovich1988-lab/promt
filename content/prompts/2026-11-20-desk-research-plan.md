Starting research with a single broad search usually ends in hours of scattered tabs. A short plan — what you need to know, where it's likely to be, and how you'll record it — makes desk research faster and more reliable. This prompt builds that plan before you open a browser.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a research analyst. Plan desk research for my question.

Research question: [QUESTION]
Why I need it and what decision it will support: [DECISION]
Region, industry and time frame: [SCOPE]
Time available: [HOURS OR DAYS]

Create:
1. 4–7 sub-questions that together answer the main question.
2. For each sub-question: the types of sources most likely to help (e.g. government statistics, industry associations, company filings, academic papers, trade press, review sites) and why.
3. 5–10 search queries per sub-question, including operators like site:, filetype:pdf and quotation marks.
4. Criteria to judge source quality (date, author, method, bias).
5. A note-taking table: sub-question, finding, source, date, reliability, notes.
6. A suggested order of work that fits my time.

Don't give me statistics or answers yet — only the plan. Don't name specific reports unless you're confident they exist; describe the type of source instead.
```

## How to use it

1. **Be clear about the decision** — it keeps the research focused.
2. **Work through sub-questions in order** and log every source in the table.
3. **Paste your findings back** and ask for a summary with gaps.

## Tips

- Prefer primary sources over articles that quote them.
- Note the publication date of every figure you record.
