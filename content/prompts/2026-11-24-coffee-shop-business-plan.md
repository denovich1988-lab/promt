Opening a coffee shop looks simple from the customer side of the counter, but margins depend on location, rent, labor and how many cups you sell per hour. A business plan forces those numbers onto paper before you sign a lease. This coffee shop business plan prompt builds the concept, menu pricing, startup costs, break-even and a timeline from your own figures.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a hospitality business consultant. Write a coffee shop business plan.

Concept (specialty, cozy neighborhood cafe, kiosk, drive-thru): [CONCEPT]
Location and nearby foot traffic or competitors: [LOCATION]
Target customers: [CUSTOMERS]
Planned opening hours and seats: [HOURS / SEATS]
Rent and lease terms: [RENT]
Budget available and financing: [BUDGET / FINANCING]
Local wages and supplier prices I have found: [COSTS]

Deliver:
1. Executive summary and concept description.
2. Market and competitor overview based on my inputs, with research I still need to do.
3. A menu with prices and estimated cost per item (coffee, milk, cup, lid) and gross margin.
4. Startup cost table: fit-out, espresso machine and grinder, furniture, permits, initial stock, opening marketing, contingency.
5. Monthly operating costs and staffing schedule.
6. Break-even: cups and revenue per day, with formulas and three scenarios.
7. Marketing plan for the first 90 days.
8. A month-by-month timeline to opening, including permits and inspections.
9. Main risks and how to reduce them.
Mark assumptions clearly; do not invent local data.
```

## How to use it

1. **Count foot traffic** at your location at different times before deciding.
2. **Get real quotes** for equipment and fit-out.
3. **Check local permits** for food service, signage and outdoor seating.
