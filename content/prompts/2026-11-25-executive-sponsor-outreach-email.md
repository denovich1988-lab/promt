Deals with one contact are fragile: if your champion leaves or loses priority, the deal stalls. Bringing in an executive sponsor, a senior leader on your side reaching out to their peer, adds weight and a second relationship. Done badly, it looks like going over your champion's head. This prompt plans the move with your champion and writes a short peer-to-peer email that focuses on the buyer's business outcome.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me use an executive sponsor in a deal.

Deal summary (company, stage, value, close date): [DEAL]
Our champion and their role: [CHAMPION]
Target executive at the buyer and what they care about: [EXECUTIVE + PRIORITIES]
Our executive sponsor and their role: [SPONSOR]
Business outcome the buyer wants (in their words if possible): [OUTCOME]
Risks in the deal: [e.g. budget review, competing priority, stalled for 3 weeks]

1. A short message to our champion asking for their input and approval before our executive reaches out (max 80 words).
2. The executive email from our sponsor to their executive (max 120 words): peer tone, reference the outcome, offer one specific thing (a 20-minute call, a customer intro, a commitment from our side), no product pitch, no pressure.
3. A 3-point brief for our sponsor before the call: context, what to ask, what not to promise.
4. What to do if the executive doesn't reply within a week.

Keep it honest: no fake urgency or claims about other customers I haven't provided.
```

## How to use it

1. **Get your champion's agreement first** — it protects the relationship.
2. **Have your executive send it themselves**, in their own voice.
3. **Log the outcome** of the call in your CRM with the next agreed step.

## Tips

- Match levels: a VP to a VP, a founder to a founder.
- Use it before the deal stalls, not only as a rescue move.
