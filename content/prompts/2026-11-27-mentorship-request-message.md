Asking "Will you be my mentor?" to someone you barely know usually gets a polite no, because it sounds like an open-ended commitment. People are much more likely to help when the request is small, specific and shows you have done your homework. This prompt writes a mentorship request that starts with one focused conversation, explains why you chose them and makes it easy to say yes.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me ask someone for mentorship.

About me: [CURRENT ROLE, EXPERIENCE LEVEL, WHAT I AM WORKING TOWARDS]
The person: [NAME, ROLE, COMPANY], how I know them: [E.G. MET AT A CONFERENCE / ALUMNI / FOLLOW THEIR WRITING / NO CONNECTION]
Why them specifically: [E.G. THEY MADE THE SAME CAREER SWITCH / THEIR TALK ON X / THEY BUILT A TEAM LIKE THE ONE I LEAD]
What I want help with: [ONE OR TWO SPECIFIC QUESTIONS OR DECISIONS]
Channel: [LINKEDIN MESSAGE / EMAIL / INTERNAL CHAT]
What I can offer back: [E.G. FEEDBACK ON THEIR PRODUCT, HELP WITH A PROJECT, SHARING RESULTS — OR "NOTHING OBVIOUS"]

Write:
1. A first message (under 120 words for LinkedIn, under 180 for email) that asks for a single 20–30 minute conversation, not a long-term commitment, with my specific question and why I chose them.
2. 2 alternative opening lines, one warmer and one more formal.
3. A follow-up to send after 7–10 days if there is no reply.
4. A thank-you message after the conversation that mentions one piece of advice I will act on.
5. A message 4–6 weeks later sharing what I did with the advice and, if it went well, asking whether they would be open to occasional check-ins.
6. 5 good questions to ask in the first conversation.

Tone: [CONFIDENT AND FRIENDLY / MODEST AND PROFESSIONAL]. No flattery that sounds generic.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pick one specific question** they are well placed to answer.
2. **Personalise the "why them" line** with something real you read or heard from them.
3. **Report back on progress**; it is the step that turns one call into an ongoing mentorship.

## Tips

- Offer times that suit them and keep the call to the length you promised.
- If they say no, thank them and ask if there is someone else they would suggest.
