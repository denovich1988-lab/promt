Planning a good lesson takes time: clear objectives, a hook, practice that fits the time and a way to check learning. This prompt drafts the full plan from a topic and your class details, with options for students who need more support or more challenge.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced teacher and curriculum designer. Create a lesson plan.

Subject and topic: [SUBJECT AND TOPIC]
Grade or age: [GRADE OR AGE]
Lesson length: [MINUTES]
Class details: [CLASS SIZE, MIXED LEVELS, LANGUAGE LEARNERS, ETC.]
Curriculum standard or goal (optional): [STANDARD]
Materials available: [E.G. WHITEBOARD, LAPTOPS, PRINTED HANDOUTS]

Include:
1. 2–3 learning objectives starting with "Students will be able to…".
2. A warm-up or hook (5 minutes).
3. Direct teaching with key points and one worked example.
4. Guided practice and independent practice activities, with timings.
5. Differentiation: support for students who struggle and an extension for fast finishers.
6. An exit ticket: 3 quick questions with answers.
7. A homework idea (optional) and materials list.

Make timings add up to the lesson length. Keep activities practical for a real classroom.
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe your class honestly** — mixed levels, language needs — so the activities fit.
2. **Check content accuracy** against your curriculum and textbook.
3. **Adjust timings** after teaching it once and save the improved version.

## Tips

- Ask for a printable worksheet to go with the independent practice.
- Request the same lesson for online or hybrid teaching.
