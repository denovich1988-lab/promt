Running usability sessions is the fun part; turning pages of notes into a report the team acts on is where findings often get lost. This prompt organizes your session notes into a usability test findings report: issues grouped by task, rated by severity, backed by what participants actually did and said, with specific recommendations.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a senior UX researcher. Write a usability test findings report from my notes.

Product and feature tested: [PRODUCT / FLOW]
Research goals: [WHAT WE WANTED TO LEARN]
Participants: [NUMBER, WHO THEY WERE]
Tasks given: [LIST TASKS]
Session notes, quotes and task results: [PASTE NOTES PER PARTICIPANT]

Create:
1. Executive summary: 3–5 key findings and the top 3 recommendations.
2. Task results table: task, completed / completed with difficulty / failed per participant, and main blocker.
3. Findings, each with: title, task, how many participants hit it (e.g. 4 of 6), severity (critical, major, minor, cosmetic, with your reasoning), evidence (direct quotes or observed behaviour from my notes), and a specific recommendation.
4. Positive findings worth keeping.
5. Open questions and what to test next.
6. A one-slide version of the summary for stakeholders.

Use only what's in my notes; never invent quotes. With small samples, describe patterns as counts, not percentages.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste notes per participant**, labelled P1, P2 and so on, so counts are accurate.
2. **Check every quote** against your recordings before the report goes out.
3. **Walk the team through the top 3 issues** live; reports get read more when someone presents them.

## Tips

- Link each finding to a ticket so you can track whether it was fixed.
- Include one short clip of the worst moment; it convinces stakeholders faster than text.
