New strategies often fail because the organization around them does not change: the structure, systems and skills still support the old way of working. The McKinsey 7S framework looks at seven connected elements to find those mismatches. This McKinsey 7S analysis prompt maps your current and desired state and shows where alignment breaks down.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an organizational design consultant. Run a McKinsey 7S analysis.

Organization, size and situation: [CONTEXT]
The change we want to make (new strategy, merger, growth, restructuring): [CHANGE]
What I know about each area:
- Strategy: [STRATEGY]
- Structure: [STRUCTURE]
- Systems (processes, tools, metrics): [SYSTEMS]
- Shared values / culture: [VALUES]
- Style (how leaders lead): [STYLE]
- Staff (people, roles, hiring): [STAFF]
- Skills (capabilities): [SKILLS]

Deliver:
1. A table: element | current state | desired state | gap | impact on the change (high/medium/low).
2. The key misalignments between elements (e.g. strategy needs speed, systems require five approvals).
3. Which elements to change first and why (hard elements vs soft elements).
4. A 90-day action plan with owners by role.
5. Risks and resistance to expect, and how to address them.
6. Questions to validate with employees through interviews or a survey.
```

## How to use it

1. **Gather input from several levels** of the organization, not only leadership.
2. **Focus on the misalignments**, not on describing each S perfectly.
3. **Review progress quarterly** against the desired state.
