A co-marketing campaign lets two brands with the same audience share the work and the reach, but many partnerships stall because nobody agreed on who does what or who owns the leads. This prompt turns a loose "let's do something together" into a co-marketing campaign plan both teams can sign off on.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a partner marketing manager. Build a co-marketing campaign plan.

Our company and what we sell: [OUR COMPANY]
Partner company and what they sell: [PARTNER COMPANY]
Shared audience: [SHARED AUDIENCE]
What each side brings (list size, social reach, content skills, budget): [ASSETS EACH SIDE]
Campaign idea, if we have one: [IDEA OR "SUGGEST 3"]
Timeline and launch date: [TIMELINE]

Deliver:
1. If no idea is given, 3 campaign concepts (webinar, joint guide, bundle offer, event, etc.) with why each fits both brands. Pick the strongest.
2. The core offer and one-sentence message for the audience.
3. Asset list with an owner for each item (landing page, emails, social posts, slides, ads).
4. Promotion plan: what each partner sends, on which channel, on which date.
5. Lead sharing and data rules to agree in writing (consent wording, who gets which contacts).
6. Timeline from kickoff to post-campaign review, week by week.
7. Success metrics for each side and how results will be reported.
8. Risks (one partner under-promotes, brand mismatch) and how to prevent them.
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in real numbers** for list size and reach so the split of work is fair.
2. **Share the plan with the partner** and agree on owners and dates before building assets.
3. **Hold a short review** after the campaign using the metrics from step 7.

## Tips

- Settle lead sharing and consent wording first; it is the most common reason partnerships fall apart.
- Write the outreach to a potential partner with the [partnership proposal email](/prompts/partnership-proposal-email/) prompt.
