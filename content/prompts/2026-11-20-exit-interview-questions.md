Leaving employees know exactly what's not working, but they often give polite, vague answers in exit interviews. Neutral, specific questions and a safe setting get more useful feedback. This prompt builds the interview and a way to turn answers into changes.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an HR partner. Plan an exit interview.

Company size and industry: [COMPANY]
Leaving employee's role and tenure: [ROLE AND TENURE]
Known context (voluntary or not, team changes, workload): [CONTEXT]
Who will run the interview: [INTERVIEWER, E.G. HR, NOT THEIR MANAGER]
Format: [IN PERSON, VIDEO, WRITTEN]

Create:
1. A short opening script that explains confidentiality and how feedback is used.
2. 12–15 open, neutral questions grouped by: reasons for leaving, role and workload, manager and team, growth, pay and benefits, culture, what would have made them stay.
3. Gentle follow-up probes for vague answers.
4. A 5-question rating survey they can fill in anonymously.
5. A summary template: key themes, quotes, risk level for the team, recommended actions.

Avoid leading questions and anything that could feel like an attempt to change their mind.
```

## How to use it

1. **Schedule the interview** in the final week, not the last hour.
2. **Have someone other than their manager** run it.
3. **Review summaries quarterly** to spot patterns across leavers.

## Tips

- Share themes, not individual answers, with leadership.
- Act on at least one finding so staff see exit feedback matters.
