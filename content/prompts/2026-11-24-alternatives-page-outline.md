People searching "[competitor] alternatives" are already shopping and unhappy with something. An alternatives page can win them, but only if it is fair: readers leave pages that bash every rival. This prompt outlines a competitor alternatives page with honest comparisons, a clear table and a natural place for your product.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a SaaS SEO content strategist. Outline a "[COMPETITOR] alternatives" page.

Our product: [PRODUCT]
Competitor the page targets: [COMPETITOR]
Why people leave that competitor (from reviews, sales calls): [REASONS]
Other alternatives to include: [OTHER TOOLS]
Facts I verified about each tool (pricing, features, limits) and the date checked: [FACTS]
Who our product is NOT a good fit for: [NOT A FIT]

Deliver:
1. Title tag, meta description and H1 options with the keyword "[COMPETITOR] alternatives".
2. An intro that names the common reasons people switch.
3. A comparison table: tool | best for | key strengths | limitations | starting price (from my facts only).
4. A section per alternative with a fair summary, who it suits and who it does not.
5. Where our product fits, written with the same honesty, including its limits.
6. A "how to choose" checklist.
7. FAQ section (3–5 questions) and schema suggestions.
8. A note on how often to re-check the facts.
Do not make claims about competitors I have not provided.
```

## How to use it

1. **Verify every fact** on competitors' official pages and note the check date.
2. **Admit where competitors are better**; it builds credibility.
3. **Update the page** whenever pricing or features change.
