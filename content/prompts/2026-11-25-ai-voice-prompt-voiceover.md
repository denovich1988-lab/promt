Text-to-speech voices sound flat when they get a bare script: no pauses, no emphasis, no sense of who is speaking. An AI voice prompt fixes that in two parts: a voice description (age, tone, accent, energy) for tools that design or pick voices, and a script marked up for pace and emphasis. This prompt writes both, so your voiceover sounds like a person telling a story, not a document being read aloud.

## Prompt

```prompt
Prepare a voiceover for an AI voice tool.

Video type and audience: [e.g. 60-second product explainer for small business owners]
Feeling the voice should create: [e.g. calm and trustworthy, upbeat, documentary]
Voice preferences: [AGE RANGE, GENDER OR NEUTRAL, ACCENT, PITCH]
Target length: [SECONDS]
Script: [PASTE SCRIPT]

1. A voice description in 2–3 sentences I can use to design or choose a voice (tone, texture, pace, accent, energy). No celebrity names.
2. Edit the script for the ear: shorter sentences, no tongue-twisters, numbers and abbreviations written the way they should be spoken.
3. Mark the script: [pause] for short breaks, *word* for emphasis, and a short direction before each section (e.g. "warmer, slower").
4. Estimate the read time and cut lines if it runs long.
5. List 3 lines that are most likely to sound unnatural and an alternative for each.

Keep the meaning of the script; don't add claims.
```

## How to use it

1. **Generate the voice** from the description, or pick the closest stock voice.
2. **Convert the markup** to your tool's format (pause tags, punctuation or speed settings).
3. **Listen once at full speed** and re-generate only the lines that sound off.

## Tips

- Commas and full stops are the simplest pause controls in most tools.
- Only clone or imitate a real person's voice with their permission.
