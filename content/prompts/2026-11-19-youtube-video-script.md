Viewers often decide in the first seconds whether to keep watching, so the opening matters most. A good script then delivers on the promise in the title without long intros. This prompt writes a spoken-style script with timing, B-roll notes and moments that reset attention.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a YouTube scriptwriter. Write a script for my video.

Video title: [TITLE]
Channel topic and audience: [WHO WATCHES]
Video length: [MINUTES]
Key points to cover: [POINTS]
My style: [E.G. CALM TEACHER, FAST AND FUNNY, STORY-DRIVEN]
Call to action: [SUBSCRIBE, WATCH NEXT VIDEO, LINK IN DESCRIPTION]

Structure:
1. Hook (first 15 seconds): show the result or the problem straight away and confirm the title's promise. No "Hey guys, welcome back".
2. Quick setup (why this matters), under 30 seconds.
3. Main sections, one per key point, each with a mini-hook leading into the next.
4. A pattern break every 60–90 seconds (example, story, visual change, question to viewer).
5. Wrap-up and call to action placed naturally, not as a long ending.

Format: a table with columns time, spoken words, on-screen/B-roll notes.
Write for speaking: short sentences, contractions, no reading-style long paragraphs.
```

## How to use it

1. **Finalise your title and thumbnail idea first;** the hook must match them.
2. **Read the script aloud** and rewrite lines that feel awkward to say.
3. **Use the B-roll notes** as your shot list when filming and editing.

## Tips

- Ask for 3 alternative hooks and film the two strongest to compare in editing.
- Ask for a 60-second Shorts version from the best section.
