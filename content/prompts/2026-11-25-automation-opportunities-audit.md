Most small teams know they "should automate more" but don't know where to start. The best candidates are repetitive, rule-based tasks that eat hours every week, while tasks that need judgment or carry risk should stay with people. This prompt turns a plain list of your recurring work into an automation audit with a ranked shortlist and a simple first workflow to build.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me find tasks to automate in my business.

Business type and team size: [DESCRIPTION]
Tools we already use: [e.g. Google Workspace, Shopify, a CRM, Slack]
Budget for new tools: [BUDGET]
Technical skill on the team: [NONE / BASIC / DEVELOPER AVAILABLE]
Recurring tasks (task — who does it — how often — time each time):
[LIST]

For each task, classify it as:
- Automate fully (rule-based, low risk)
- AI drafts, human approves (writing, summarizing, sorting)
- Keep human (judgment, relationships, high risk)

Then:
1. A table: task, category, hours saved per month (estimate from my numbers), risk if it goes wrong, how to do it (built-in feature, no-code automation, AI assistant, or script).
2. The top 5 opportunities ranked by hours saved versus effort.
3. A step-by-step plan for the first workflow: trigger, steps, tools, the check a human does, and how we'll know it works.
4. Risks to watch (data privacy, errors reaching customers) and a simple rule for each.

Prefer the tools we already have before suggesting new ones. Don't name prices you aren't sure of.
```

## How to use it

1. **Track tasks for one week** to get honest time estimates.
2. **Build only the first workflow** from the plan, then measure the time saved.
3. **Review AI-drafted outputs** daily at first, then spot-check once they are reliable.

## Tips

- Start with internal tasks before anything customer-facing.
- Write down each automation's owner so it doesn't break unnoticed.
