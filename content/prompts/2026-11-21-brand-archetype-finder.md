Many small brands sound like everyone else because they never decided what character they play for their customers. Brand archetypes give you a shortcut: a familiar personality (the Caregiver, the Rebel, the Sage) that shapes tone, visuals and promises. This prompt compares the best-fitting archetypes against what you actually sell and who buys it, then turns the winner into practical guidelines.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a brand strategist. Help me choose a brand archetype for my business and turn it into practical guidelines.

Business and what we sell: [BUSINESS AND PRODUCT]
Ideal customer and what they want to feel: [CUSTOMER AND DESIRED FEELING]
3 competitors and how they come across: [COMPETITORS]
Words customers already use to describe us: [CUSTOMER WORDS]
Words we never want to be associated with: [WORDS TO AVOID]

Do this:
1. Shortlist the 3 archetypes (from the classic 12) that fit best. For each, explain why in 2 sentences, using my inputs.
2. Show which archetypes my competitors seem to use, and where there is room to stand apart.
3. Recommend one primary archetype and, if useful, one secondary. Explain the trade-off.
4. For the chosen archetype, give: 5 voice traits with "we say / we don't say" examples, visual direction (colours, imagery, typography mood), 3 core brand promises, and 3 sample social captions.
5. List 3 ways the archetype could go wrong for my business (for example, sounding preachy or cold) and how to avoid them.

Base every recommendation on my inputs. If something important is missing, ask me before deciding.
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect real customer words** from reviews, emails or sales calls before you start.
2. **Run the prompt** and read the competitor comparison closely — the gap is often more useful than the archetype itself.
3. **Share the "we say / we don't say" table** with anyone who writes for the brand.

## Tips

- An archetype is a guide, not a costume. Keep what fits and drop what doesn't.
- Test the sample captions on a few real customers and see which version they react to.
