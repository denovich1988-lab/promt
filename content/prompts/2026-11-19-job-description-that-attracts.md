Long lists of requirements and vague phrases like "rockstar" put off good candidates and attract the wrong ones. A strong job description explains what the person will actually do, what success looks like and what is truly required. This prompt also checks your draft for biased or exclusionary wording.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced recruiter. Write a job description.

Role: [JOB TITLE]
Company: [WHAT YOU DO, SIZE, CULTURE IN A FEW WORDS]
Team and manager: [WHO THEY WORK WITH]
Main responsibilities: [WHAT THEY WILL DO WEEK TO WEEK]
Success after 6 months looks like: [OUTCOMES]
Requirements: [SKILLS AND EXPERIENCE YOU THINK YOU NEED]
Location and work setup: [OFFICE, HYBRID, REMOTE, TIME ZONE]
Salary range and benefits: [RANGE AND BENEFITS]

Write:
1. A 2–3 sentence opening about the role's impact.
2. "What you'll do" — 5–7 bullets starting with verbs.
3. "What success looks like" — 3 bullets.
4. "Must have" — no more than 5 items. Move everything else to "Nice to have".
5. Salary, benefits and work setup.
6. How to apply and what the hiring process looks like.

Then list any wording that could discourage qualified candidates (gendered terms, jargon, unnecessary degree requirements) and suggest replacements.
```

## How to use it

1. **Be honest about "must have".** Each extra requirement shrinks the candidate pool.
2. **Include the salary range** where you can; in some places it is legally required.
3. **Ask a current team member** to check the description matches the real job.

## Tips

- Ask for 3 short versions for LinkedIn, job boards and a social media post.
- Check local employment rules for anything legally sensitive in the ad.
