Interviews with the wrong people waste everyone's time and lead to wrong conclusions. A screener survey filters candidates before you book calls, but poorly written ones reveal what you are looking for, so people answer to qualify. This prompt writes neutral questions with hidden qualifying logic and a simple scoring rule.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a user research lead. Write a screener survey to recruit participants for interviews.

Research goal: [WHAT WE WANT TO LEARN]
Who we need (behaviours, not just demographics): [TARGET PARTICIPANTS]
Who we must exclude: [EXCLUSIONS, E.G. COMPETITORS' EMPLOYEES, RECENT PARTICIPANTS]
Number of interviews and mix needed: [NUMBER AND QUOTAS]
Where we will share the survey: [CHANNEL]

Create:
1. 8–12 screener questions, mostly multiple choice, that check behaviour (what they did recently) rather than opinions. Include at least one question with a decoy answer to catch people who over-claim.
2. For each question: the answer options, which answers qualify, disqualify or are neutral (in a separate table I keep private).
3. One open-text question that tests if the person can explain things clearly.
4. A scoring rule and quotas so we get the right mix.
5. A short invitation text and a thank-you message for people who don't qualify.

Do not reveal the target profile in the question wording. Keep the survey under 3 minutes.
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe the behaviour you need** (for example, "bought software for their team in the last 6 months").
2. **Build the survey** in your form tool and keep the qualifying table separate.
3. **Review the open-text answers** before inviting anyone, even if they score well.

## Tips

- Ask about recent, specific actions — they are harder to fake than general habits.
- Recruit a few extra people; some will not show up.
