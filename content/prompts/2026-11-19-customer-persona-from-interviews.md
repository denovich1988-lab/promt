Personas made up in a meeting room describe who you wish your customers were. A useful persona is built from what real customers said, and it shows how they decide to buy. This prompt sticks to your notes, quotes them, and points out where you need more research.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a customer research lead. Build a customer persona from the research below.

Product: [WHAT YOU SELL]
Research notes (interviews, surveys, sales calls — remove names):
[PASTE NOTES]

Create a persona with:
1. A short descriptive name (role-based, e.g. "The overloaded ops manager") and a one-line summary.
2. Context: role, situation, tools they use today.
3. Goals: what they are trying to achieve, in priority order.
4. Frustrations: what gets in the way, with supporting quotes.
5. Buying triggers: events that make them look for a solution.
6. Decision process: who else is involved, what they compare, what makes them hesitate.
7. Words they use to describe the problem (for marketing copy).
8. How to reach them: channels and content they mention.

Every point must be backed by the notes. List open questions where the evidence is thin.
If the notes clearly show two different types of customer, create two personas instead of blending them.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use at least 5–10 interviews or survey responses** for a persona you can trust.
2. **Share it with sales and support** and ask if it matches who they talk to.
3. **Use the "words they use" section** directly in ads and landing pages.

## Tips

- Re-run the prompt as new interviews come in and compare versions.
- Ask for a one-paragraph version to paste at the top of every brief.
