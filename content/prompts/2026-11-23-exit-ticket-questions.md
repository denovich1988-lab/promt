An exit ticket takes three minutes at the end of class and tells you who got the lesson and who didn't. Good exit ticket questions are short and target the one thing you taught. This prompt writes them for your objective at three levels, with quick ways to sort the answers and what to do next lesson.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced teacher. Write exit ticket questions for my lesson.

Grade and subject: [GRADE AND SUBJECT]
Lesson objective (what students should be able to do): [OBJECTIVE]
Key vocabulary or skill: [VOCABULARY OR SKILL]
Common misconceptions I expect: [MISCONCEPTIONS, or "not sure"]
Format: [paper / digital / verbal]

Create:
1. Three exit ticket sets (2–3 questions each) at different levels: check understanding, apply it, explain reasoning.
2. One question designed to reveal the most common misconception.
3. A self-rating line students can circle (for example, "I can teach this / I mostly get it / I'm lost").
4. A quick sorting guide: what a "got it", "almost" and "not yet" answer looks like.
5. One idea for tomorrow's warm-up for each group.
Keep each question answerable in under 3 minutes.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pick one set** that fits today's lesson.
2. **Sort the tickets into three piles** right after class using the guide.
3. **Start the next lesson** with the warm-up for the largest "not yet" group.
