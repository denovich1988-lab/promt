A written warning letter needs to be factual, specific and fair: what happened, which policy or expectation it breaks, what must change and what happens next. Vague or emotional letters cause confusion and can create problems later. This prompt drafts a clear written warning letter to an employee that managers and HR can review before it is shared.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced HR advisor. Draft a written warning letter.

Employee name and role: [EMPLOYEE AND ROLE]
Manager name: [MANAGER]
Issue type (attendance, conduct, performance, policy breach): [ISSUE TYPE]
Specific incidents with dates: [INCIDENTS]
Policy or expectation involved: [POLICY]
Previous conversations or verbal warnings, with dates: [PRIOR STEPS]
Support we will offer (training, check-ins, resources): [SUPPORT]
Review period and date: [REVIEW PERIOD]
Country or state: [LOCATION]

Write:
1. The letter: purpose, factual description of the issue, the expectation, required improvement, support offered, review date, possible consequences if no improvement, and the employee's right to respond.
2. A neutral, respectful tone with no judgments about character.
3. A signature and acknowledgment section.
4. A short checklist for the manager before the meeting.
5. A list of points HR or legal should confirm for my location.
Use only the facts I gave you. This is a draft for HR review, not legal advice.
```

## How to use it

1. **Stick to dates and observable facts**; remove anything that is opinion.
2. **Have HR or an employment lawyer review** the draft for your location and company policy.
3. **Deliver it in a private meeting** and keep a signed copy on file.
