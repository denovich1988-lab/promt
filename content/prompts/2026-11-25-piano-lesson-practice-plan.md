Piano students often "practice" by playing their piece from the top again and again, mistakes included. A structured piano practice plan breaks the week into short, focused sessions. This prompt is useful for music teachers writing assignments and for adult learners teaching themselves: it builds a week of practice around the student's level, pieces and available time.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced piano teacher.

Student: [AGE, LEVEL, E.G. 2 YEARS OF LESSONS / ADULT BEGINNER]
Pieces this week: [TITLES, AND WHICH PARTS ARE HARD]
Technique focus: [E.G. C MAJOR SCALE HANDS TOGETHER, ARPEGGIOS]
Time available: [MINUTES PER DAY, DAYS PER WEEK]
Upcoming goal: [E.G. RECITAL ON DATE, EXAM, JUST FOR FUN]

1. Write a 7-day plan with timed blocks: warm-up, technique, hard passages, run-through, something fun.
2. For each hard passage, give a practice method (slow practice, hands separate, rhythm variations, chunking) and how to know it's improving.
3. Set one small, measurable goal for the end of the week.
4. Include one listening or theory activity under 5 minutes.
5. Make a printable practice log with checkboxes and space for notes.
6. Write a short note to parents (if the student is a child) on how to support practice without pressure.

Keep it encouraging and realistic for the time available.
```

## How to use it

1. **Name the exact bars that are difficult**; the plan gets much more specific.
2. **Print the log** and review it together at the next lesson.
3. **Adjust the next week's plan** based on what the student actually managed.

## Tips

- For young children, ask for 5-minute blocks and a game for each day.
- Ask for a recital countdown version for the last three weeks before a performance.
