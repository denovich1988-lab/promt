An exit interview tells you why someone left; a stay interview asks why people stay while there is still time to act. Many managers ask vague questions and get polite answers. This prompt prepares stay interview questions, follow-ups and a short action plan so the conversation leads to real changes.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an HR business partner. Prepare a stay interview.

Employee role and time in the company: [ROLE AND TENURE]
Manager running it: [MANAGER]
What we know about their situation (recent changes, workload, goals): [CONTEXT]
Things we can realistically change (schedule, projects, training, pay review timing): [WHAT WE CAN CHANGE]
Team or company concerns right now: [CONCERNS]

Give me:
1. How to open the conversation so it does not feel like a performance review.
2. 10 stay interview questions on what they enjoy, what frustrates them, growth, recognition, manager support and what might make them leave.
3. A follow-up for each question to get specific examples.
4. What not to promise during the meeting.
5. A short template to record answers and agree 1–3 actions with dates.
Keep the tone open and supportive.
```

## How to use it

1. **Schedule it separately** from performance reviews.
2. **Listen more than you talk** and take notes in the template.
3. **Follow up on the agreed actions** within a month.
