An all-hands meeting is the one time the whole company hears from leadership together, yet many turn into a slide dump with no clear message. This prompt helps founders and managers turn their notes into all-hands meeting talking points: one main message, the numbers that matter, honest updates on what isn't going well and prepared answers to the questions people will actually ask.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a chief of staff helping me prepare a company all-hands meeting.

Company and size: [COMPANY, HEADCOUNT, REMOTE/HYBRID/OFFICE]
Meeting length: [E.G. 45 MINUTES]
Period covered: [E.G. Q4, LAST MONTH]
My raw notes: [PASTE: RESULTS, WINS, MISSES, CHANGES, HIRES, PRIORITIES]
Sensitive topics people are worried about: [E.G. REORG, BUDGET CUTS, NEW LEADER, RETURN TO OFFICE]
Speakers available: [NAMES/ROLES]

Create:
1. The one-sentence message everyone should remember.
2. A run sheet with sections, speaker and minutes per section (leave 15 minutes for Q&A).
3. Talking points for each section: 3–5 bullets, plain language, with the 3–4 numbers that matter most and what they mean.
4. A "what didn't go well and what we're doing about it" segment written honestly, without blame.
5. Shout-outs: a format for recognising 3–5 people or teams with specific contributions.
6. Likely questions (at least 8, including the uncomfortable ones) with short, truthful draft answers; mark any answer I must check with legal or HR as {CHECK}.
7. A 150-word follow-up message to send afterwards with the key points and links.

Don't invent numbers or commitments; use only my notes and flag gaps.
```

## How to use it

1. **Dump everything into the notes**, even rough; the prompt does the structuring.
2. **Rehearse the hard answers** out loud; that's where trust is won or lost.
3. **Send the follow-up within a day** so people who missed it get the same message.

## Tips

- Collect anonymous questions in advance; they show what people really want to know.
- Say "I don't know yet, I'll update you by [date]" rather than guessing.
