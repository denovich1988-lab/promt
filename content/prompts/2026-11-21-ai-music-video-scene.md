Independent musicians can now make visuals for a track without a film crew, but random clips feel disconnected. A music video works when every shot shares a visual world — colour, location, light — and the motion follows the tempo. This prompt first builds that world and a shot list from your song, then gives you prompts for each clip.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a music video director. Plan AI-generated shots for my song.

Song title, genre and tempo (BPM if known): [SONG DETAILS]
Mood and main themes: [MOOD AND THEMES]
Key lyric lines or images: [LYRICS OR IMAGES]
Artist appearance (if they appear): [ARTIST DESCRIPTION OR "NO ARTIST"]
Format: [ASPECT RATIO, E.G. 16:9 OR 9:16]

Create:
1. A visual world in 5 lines: locations, colour palette, light, wardrobe, recurring symbol.
2. A shot list of 10 shots of 4–8 seconds each, matched to song sections (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro). For each: section, shot description, camera movement, speed (slow motion, real time), and how it relates to the lyrics.
3. A ready-to-paste video prompt for each shot, repeating the visual world details so clips stay consistent, and ending with "no text, no logos".
4. Editing notes: where to cut on the beat and which shots to repeat in the chorus.

Keep camera moves simple (slow dolly, drift, static). Avoid crowds and fast choreography, which AI video handles poorly.
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe the mood and imagery in your own words**, not only the genre.
2. **Generate 2–3 versions of each shot** and pick the most consistent ones.
3. **Edit to the beat** in your video editor and add your track.

## Tips

- One recurring object (a red umbrella, a lit window) ties random clips into a story.
- Check each AI tool's licence terms before publishing commercially.
