Most plans copy what competitors or past projects did, which bakes in their assumptions. First principles thinking strips a problem down to what's actually true (costs, physics, customer needs, rules) and rebuilds from there. This prompt guides that process step by step and keeps the AI honest about which "facts" are really assumptions you can challenge.

## Prompt

```prompt
Use first principles thinking on this problem.

Problem or goal: [e.g. our delivery cost per order is too high to be profitable]
Current approach and why we do it this way: [CURRENT APPROACH]
What we know for sure (numbers, constraints): [FACTS]
What competitors or the industry usually do: [CONVENTION]

1. Restate the problem as the underlying need, without assuming a solution.
2. List every belief behind the current approach. Label each as Fact (verifiable), Assumption (could be wrong) or Convention (done because others do it). Explain how to verify each assumption.
3. Break the problem into its basic components (cost elements, steps, customer needs) and estimate what each would cost or take if built from scratch. Mark estimates clearly.
4. Rebuild: 4 solutions that start from the components, including at least one that breaks a convention.
5. For each solution: what must be true for it to work, the biggest risk, and a small test.
6. Recommend which assumption to test first and why.

Ask me questions if a key fact is missing instead of inventing it.
```

## How to use it

1. **List conventions honestly** — the most useful insights usually come from these.
2. **Verify the riskiest assumption** with data or a quick test before choosing a solution.
3. **Re-run the prompt** with the new facts you learn.

## Tips

- Use it for one problem at a time; broad goals give vague components.
- Pair it with a pre-mortem once you've chosen a solution.
