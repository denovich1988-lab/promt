The LinkedIn posts people actually read share a specific moment and one useful lesson. The ones they scroll past are full of buzzwords and fake drama. This prompt starts from your real story and keeps it honest.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me write a LinkedIn post based on a real experience.

What happened (rough notes): [THE SITUATION, WHAT YOU DID, WHAT HAPPENED]
What I learned: [THE LESSON IN YOUR OWN WORDS]
Who I want to read it: [E.G. "STARTUP FOUNDERS", "JUNIOR DESIGNERS"]
My role: [YOUR JOB TITLE OR FIELD]

Write 3 versions:
A. Story first: start in the middle of the moment.
B. Lesson first: start with the takeaway, then the story that proves it.
C. Contrarian: start with a common belief this experience challenges.

Rules for every version:
- First line under 12 words and specific enough to make someone stop scrolling.
- Short paragraphs of 1–2 sentences.
- 120–200 words.
- One clear lesson, not five.
- End with a question that invites people to share their own experience.
- No buzzwords ("excited to announce", "humbled", "game-changer"), no fake cliffhangers, no more than 3 hashtags.
- Do not add details that are not in my notes.
```

## How to use it

1. **Give real details.** Names of tools, numbers and what was said make a post believable.
2. **Pick the version that sounds most like you** and edit a few words so it is clearly yours.
3. **Post it and reply to comments** in the first hour; that matters more than perfect wording.

## Tips

- Remove client or employer details you do not have permission to share.
- Keep a note of small work moments during the week; they make the best posts.
