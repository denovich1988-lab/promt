Webinars that feel like long sales pitches lose attendees quickly. The ones that work teach something useful first and make the offer a logical next step. This prompt plans the full session minute by minute and drafts the emails around it.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a webinar strategist. Plan a webinar for my business.

Topic and promise: [WHAT ATTENDEES WILL LEARN OR BE ABLE TO DO]
Audience: [WHO, AND THEIR MAIN STRUGGLE]
Length: [45 / 60 MINUTES]
Offer at the end (if any): [PRODUCT, CALL, TRIAL, DISCOUNT]
Presenter background: [WHY THEY'RE CREDIBLE — REAL FACTS ONLY]

Create:
1. Three title options that state a concrete outcome.
2. A minute-by-minute run of show: opening hook (first 3 minutes), who this is for, 3 teaching points with an example each, a live demo or exercise, transition to the offer, offer, Q&A, close.
3. Speaker notes for the opening and the transition to the offer.
4. 5 audience questions to ask in chat to keep people engaged.
5. Emails: invitation, 24-hour reminder, 1-hour reminder, replay email for no-shows, follow-up for attendees.

Rules: teaching should be useful even for people who never buy. No fake scarcity.
```

## How to use it

1. **Make sure each teaching point stands alone** — some attendees join late.
2. **Rehearse the transition to the offer;** it's where most webinars feel awkward.
3. **Send the replay within 24 hours** while interest is high.

## Tips

- Cut the webinar into 3–5 short clips for social media afterwards.
- Collect questions at signup and answer the best ones live.
