Buying a business, even a small one, means checking a lot of things before you sign. Missing one (a key customer leaving, unpaid taxes, a contract that ends on sale) can wipe out the value. This prompt builds a due diligence checklist tailored to the deal size and type, with the questions to ask, the documents to request and the red flags to watch for.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced M&A advisor. Build a due diligence checklist.

Target business (industry, size, revenue range, business model): [TARGET BUSINESS]
Deal type and size: [DEAL TYPE, e.g. asset purchase, share purchase, acqui-hire]
Why we want to buy it: [STRATEGIC REASON]
Biggest worries so far: [CONCERNS]
Timeline to close: [TIMELINE]

Create:
1. A checklist by area: financial, tax, customers and revenue quality, contracts, legal and IP, people, operations, technology and data, regulatory.
2. For each item: the question, the documents to request, and what a red flag looks like.
3. A prioritized document request list I can send to the seller.
4. 10 questions to ask the owner in person.
5. Which items need a lawyer, accountant or other specialist.
Mark items that matter more for this deal type. This is a planning aid, not legal or financial advice.
```

## How to use it

1. **Tailor it to the deal**; a small asset purchase needs fewer items than a share deal.
2. **Send the document list early** so the seller has time to prepare.
3. **Review red flags with your lawyer and accountant** before you sign.
