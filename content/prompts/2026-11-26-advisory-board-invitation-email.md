Strong advisors open doors and save founders from costly mistakes, but a vague "would you advise us?" email is easy to decline. This prompt writes an advisory board invitation email that explains why you chose this person, what you'd ask of them, how much time it takes and what they get, plus a one-page role outline to share if they're interested.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced founder. Help me invite someone to join our advisory board.

Company and stage: [COMPANY, WHAT YOU DO, STAGE, TEAM SIZE]
Person invited: [NAME, ROLE, HOW I KNOW THEM]
Why them specifically: [EXPERTISE, NETWORK, EXPERIENCE WE NEED]
What I'd ask of them: [E.G. QUARTERLY CALL, INTROS TO RETAILERS, REVIEW PRICING]
Time commitment: [E.G. 2–3 HOURS A MONTH]
Compensation I can offer: [E.G. EQUITY RANGE, CASH, NONE]
Term: [E.G. 12 MONTHS, RENEWABLE]

Write:
1. An invitation email (under 200 words): why I'm writing, why them, what the role involves, the time commitment, a clear low-pressure ask for a 20-minute call.
2. Two subject line options.
3. A shorter LinkedIn message version (under 80 words).
4. A one-page advisor role outline: purpose, responsibilities, time, compensation, term, confidentiality, how we'll work together, how either side can end it.
5. A polite follow-up email for 7 days later.
6. Questions I should ask them on the call to check fit.

Don't promise outcomes or figures I didn't give. Mark items for a lawyer (advisor agreement, equity terms) as {CHECK}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Be specific about why them**; a generic ask signals you'd take anyone.
2. **Put the time commitment and compensation in writing** early to avoid awkward talks later.
3. **Use a standard advisor agreement** reviewed by a lawyer once they say yes.

## Tips

- Start with a trial project or one meeting before a formal role.
- Keep advisors updated monthly; engaged advisors give more help.
