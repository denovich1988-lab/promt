A vague time-off policy creates arguments: who approves requests, what happens to unused days, how holidays work. A clear PTO policy answers those questions before they come up. This prompt drafts a PTO policy in plain language and lists the legal points to check for your location.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an HR policy writer. Draft a paid time off (PTO) policy. This is a draft for HR or legal review, not legal advice.

Company size, locations and employee types (full-time, part-time, hourly): [COMPANY]
PTO model (accrued, fixed annual, unlimited, separate vacation and sick): [MODEL]
Amounts by tenure or level: [AMOUNTS]
Public holidays we observe: [HOLIDAYS]
Request and approval process: [PROCESS]
Carryover, caps and payout on leaving: [CARRYOVER AND PAYOUT]
Busy periods with restrictions: [BLACKOUT PERIODS]

Write:
1. The policy with sections: purpose, eligibility, how PTO is earned, requesting time off, approvals, carryover, holidays, sick time, leaving the company, and questions.
2. Plain-language examples (e.g. how much PTO someone has after six months).
3. A short FAQ for employees.
4. A one-paragraph summary for the handbook.
5. A list of legal points to confirm for each location (minimum leave, sick leave laws, payout rules).
Use my rules only and flag any that may conflict with common legal requirements.
```

## How to use it

1. **List every location**; leave laws differ by country and state.
2. **Have HR or an employment lawyer review** the draft.
3. **Share the FAQ** with managers so answers are consistent.
