Most teams see their business from the inside, so they miss the moments where customers get confused or give up. A journey map forces you to walk the path as the buyer does. This prompt builds the map from what you already know and ranks the gaps by impact, so it ends in a to-do list rather than a poster.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a customer experience strategist. Build a customer journey map for my business.

Business and product: [WHAT YOU SELL]
Main customer type: [WHO BUYS]
How customers usually find us: [CHANNELS]
Steps from first contact to repeat purchase as I see them: [YOUR CURRENT STEPS]
Known complaints or drop-off points: [WHAT YOU HEAR OR SEE IN DATA]

Do this:
1. Define 5–7 stages (e.g. awareness, consideration, purchase, onboarding, use, renewal, referral) adapted to my business.
2. For each stage, give: the customer's goal, what they do, touchpoints, what they think, how they feel (positive / neutral / negative), and the friction they meet.
3. Mark the 3 "moments that matter" where a bad experience is most likely to lose the customer.
4. List 8 improvement ideas ranked by impact and effort (low / medium / high), each tied to a stage.
5. Suggest one metric to track per stage.

Format: one table for the map, then a ranked list of improvements. Where you are guessing rather than using my input, label it "assumption".
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in the complaints field with real data** — support tickets, reviews or analytics drop-offs.
2. **Check every "assumption"** with two or three real customers before acting on it.
3. **Pick the top two improvements** and assign an owner and a date.

## Tips

- Run it separately for each customer type; their journeys are rarely the same.
- Ask for an "ideal journey" version to compare against the current one.
