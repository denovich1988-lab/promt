Writing sub plans when you are sick or away is stressful, and vague plans lead to a lost day. This prompt writes substitute teacher plans a stranger can follow: a timed schedule, activities that need little explanation, your routines and rules, and notes on students who need support, without sharing private details.

## Prompt

```prompt
Write substitute teacher plans for my class.

Grade, subject and class length: [GRADE, SUBJECT AND PERIOD LENGTH]
Date and number of days: [DAYS]
Where we are in the unit: [CURRENT TOPIC]
Materials available: [MATERIALS, e.g. textbook pages, worksheets, devices, a video]
Class routines (start of class, bathroom, devices, dismissal): [ROUTINES]
Support notes without names or private details: [SUPPORT NOTES, e.g. "two students may need instructions read aloud"]
Who to ask for help: [CONTACT, e.g. teacher next door, office]

Write:
1. A timed schedule for each class or period.
2. Activities that review or practice the current topic and need almost no teaching, with step-by-step instructions the sub can read aloud.
3. An early-finisher activity and a backup plan if technology fails.
4. A one-page summary: routines, rules, where things are, emergency procedures to look up.
5. A short note-back form for the sub (what was done, who was absent, issues).
```

## How to use it

1. **Keep a generic emergency set** in a folder, then use this prompt for planned absences.
2. **Never include student names or medical details** in an AI prompt; add them by hand where your school allows.
3. **Leave copies printed** so the sub does not need logins.
