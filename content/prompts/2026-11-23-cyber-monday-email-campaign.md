Cyber Monday inboxes are crowded, and most brands send the same "biggest sale ever" email. This Cyber Monday email prompt builds a short campaign around your real offer: who it is for, why it ends when it does, and what makes it different from your Black Friday emails, so subscribers who already heard from you still have a reason to open.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an ecommerce email marketer. Write a Cyber Monday email campaign for my store.

Store and what we sell: [STORE AND PRODUCTS]
Cyber Monday offer (discount, bundle, free shipping, gift) and exact end time: [OFFER AND DEADLINE]
What we already sent for Black Friday: [BLACK FRIDAY EMAILS, or "nothing"]
Audience segments we can email: [SEGMENTS, e.g. past buyers, subscribers who never bought]
Brand voice: [VOICE]
Anything we must not say (legal, stock limits, exclusions): [RESTRICTIONS]

Write:
1. A plan of 3–4 emails (Sunday teaser, Monday morning launch, Monday afternoon reminder, last call) with the goal of each.
2. For each email: 3 subject lines (under 45 characters), preview text, body copy under 150 words, and one call to action.
3. One version of the launch email for past buyers and one for people who never bought.
4. A short "Cyber Monday extended" email to use only if we truly extend the offer.
Rules: no fake countdowns, no invented scarcity, state the real end time and time zone, and make the offer clear in the first line.
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in the exact offer and end time**, including exclusions.
2. **Pick one subject line per email** and test two on your biggest segment.
3. **Schedule the emails** and check every link and discount code before sending.

## Tips

- If you already ran a Black Friday sequence with the [Black Friday email sequence](/prompts/black-friday-email-sequence/) prompt, ask for a new angle so the emails don't repeat.
- Test subject lines with the [email subject line tests](/prompts/email-subject-line-tests/) prompt.
