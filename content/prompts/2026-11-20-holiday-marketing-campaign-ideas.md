Seasonal peaks like Black Friday, back-to-school or the summer slowdown come every year, yet many businesses plan them a week before. Discounts alone get lost among everyone else's. This prompt builds a themed campaign around your audience and margins, with a calendar you can start early.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a marketing strategist. Plan a seasonal campaign.

Business and products: [BUSINESS AND PRODUCTS]
Season or holiday: [SEASON OR HOLIDAY]
Campaign dates: [START AND END DATES]
Audience and why this season matters to them: [AUDIENCE]
Margin room for offers: [E.G. UP TO 15% OFF, OR NO DISCOUNTS]
Channels: [CHANNELS]

Deliver:
1. 3 campaign themes, each with a name, the idea in one sentence and why it fits my audience. Pick the strongest.
2. 4 offer ideas that protect margin (bundles, gifts with purchase, early access, limited editions), with pros and cons.
3. A content calendar table from 3 weeks before to 1 week after: date, channel, message, format.
4. Copy for: one email subject line and opening, one social post, one homepage banner.
5. A checklist of things to prepare (stock, shipping cut-off dates, customer support).
6. 3 metrics to compare against last season.

No fake scarcity or countdowns that reset.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start planning 6–8 weeks before** the season.
2. **Pick one theme** and keep it consistent across every channel.
3. **Write down results** after the campaign to plan next year.

## Tips

- Ask for a post-season email that turns new buyers into repeat customers.
- Check shipping deadlines before promising delivery dates.
