Business school case studies are long, full of exhibits and deliberately messy, and the hard part is finding the real decision buried inside. This prompt guides you through a structured case study analysis: identify the decision maker and core problem, pull out the facts that matter, apply the right frameworks, compare options and defend a recommendation. Use it to prepare for class discussion, not to replace your own thinking; your professor wants your judgment.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a business school professor coaching me on a case analysis. Don't just give answers; challenge my reasoning.

Course and topic: [E.G. STRATEGY / MARKETING / OPERATIONS / FINANCE]
Case summary or key text (paste the parts you can): [PASTE]
Exhibits/data: [PASTE TABLES OR KEY NUMBERS]
Assignment questions: [PASTE]
My initial view: [WHAT I THINK THE ANSWER IS AND WHY]

Help me with:
1. The decision maker, the decision they face and the deadline.
2. The core problem in one sentence, separated from symptoms.
3. The 8–10 facts and numbers that really matter, with simple calculations from the exhibits (margins, growth, break-even, market share).
4. 2–3 frameworks that fit this case (e.g. Porter's Five Forces, 4Ps, value chain, NPV) and what each reveals; skip frameworks that add nothing.
5. 3 realistic options with pros, cons, risks and what you'd have to believe for each to work.
6. A recommendation with an implementation plan and how to measure success.
7. Weak points in my initial view and the toughest questions a classmate might ask.
8. A 2-minute opening statement for class discussion.

Use only the case information I provide and show calculations step by step.
```

## How to use it

1. **Read the case yourself first** and write your initial view before using the prompt.
2. **Paste the exhibits**; most good arguments come from the numbers.
3. **Practise the opening statement** out loud before class.

## Tips

- Check your school's AI policy; use this for preparation, not for submitted work unless allowed.
- Be ready to defend a decision; "it depends" isn't a recommendation.
