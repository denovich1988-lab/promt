When someone copies your article, photos or product images, a DMCA takedown notice to the website owner or hosting provider is often the fastest way to get it removed. The notice has required elements, and missing one can get it rejected. This prompt drafts a complete DMCA takedown notice from your details, plus a friendlier first email to the site owner.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me draft a DMCA takedown notice. I'm not asking for legal advice; I'll review it before sending.

My name/company and contact details: [NAME, ADDRESS, EMAIL, PHONE]
My original work: [WHAT IT IS, URL, DATE FIRST PUBLISHED]
Where it was copied: [INFRINGING URLS]
What was copied: [E.G. FULL ARTICLE, 12 PHOTOS, PRODUCT DESCRIPTIONS]
Who I'm sending to: [SITE OWNER / HOSTING PROVIDER / SEARCH ENGINE / PLATFORM]
Any licence or permission given: [NONE / DETAILS]

Write:
1. A short, polite first email to the site owner asking for removal within a set number of days.
2. A formal DMCA takedown notice including: identification of the copyrighted work; identification of the infringing material with URLs; my contact information; a statement of good-faith belief that the use is not authorised; a statement that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that I am the owner or authorised to act for the owner; and a signature line.
3. Notes on where to send it (the host's designated DMCA agent, platform forms) and how to find the hosting provider.
4. A checklist of evidence to keep (screenshots with dates, archive links, original files).
5. A note on fair use and counter-notices so I know what can happen next.

Keep it factual and calm. Mark anything I should confirm with a lawyer as {CHECK}.
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect evidence first**: screenshots and archived copies of both pages, with dates.
2. **Try the polite email** when the owner is reachable; many people remove content quickly.
3. **Use the platform's own form** where one exists (search engines and big platforms have them).

## Tips

- Only send a notice for work you own or are authorised to protect; false claims can have legal consequences.
- This prompt gives a draft, not legal advice; ask a lawyer for valuable or disputed works.
