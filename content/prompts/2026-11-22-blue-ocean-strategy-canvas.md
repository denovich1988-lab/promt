Competing on the same features as everyone else usually ends in a price war. Blue ocean strategy asks a different question: which factors can you drop, and which new ones can you create? This ChatGPT prompt walks through a strategy canvas and the four actions grid (eliminate, reduce, raise, create) for your market, then tests whether the new offer would be worth paying for.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a strategy consultant who uses blue ocean strategy tools.

My business and offer: [BUSINESS AND OFFER]
Market and main competitors: [MARKET AND 3–5 COMPETITORS]
Factors the industry competes on: [FACTORS, e.g. price, speed, range, support]
Customers we win and lose, and why: [NOTES]
Non-customers (people who could buy but do not): [WHO AND WHY, IF KNOWN]

Do this:
1. Build a strategy canvas as a table: each competitive factor, how the industry average scores it (1–5), how each competitor scores it, and how we score it now.
2. Describe three tiers of non-customers and what keeps each from buying.
3. Fill in the four actions grid: factors to Eliminate, Reduce, Raise and Create, with a reason for each.
4. Draw the new value curve as a table next to the industry average.
5. Test the idea: buyer utility, a price the mass of buyers could accept, cost to deliver, and adoption hurdles.
6. List three small experiments to test the new curve within 60 days.
Mark which scores are guesses so I can check them with customers.
```

## How to use it

1. **List 6–10 competitive factors** buyers actually compare.
2. **Check the scores with a few customer calls**, not only internal opinion.
3. **Run the cheapest experiment first** and keep what customers respond to.

## Tips

- Pair this with a [Porter's five forces analysis](/prompts/porters-five-forces-analysis/) to see the pressure on your market.
- An idea that only raises factors is not blue ocean; look hard at what to eliminate.
