Real thought leadership comes from people who know the subject — engineers, founders, consultants — but they rarely have time to write. Generic AI articles have no point of view at all. This prompt starts from your interview notes or transcript with a subject-matter expert and shapes their opinions, stories and examples into a thought leadership article that sounds like them.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a B2B ghostwriter. Turn this expert interview into a thought leadership article.

Expert: [NAME, ROLE, COMPANY, EXPERIENCE]
Audience: [WHO SHOULD READ IT, THEIR ROLE AND PROBLEMS]
Where it will be published: [COMPANY BLOG / LINKEDIN ARTICLE / INDUSTRY PUBLICATION]
Interview transcript or notes: [PASTE]
Expert's voice: [E.G. DIRECT AND A LITTLE CONTRARIAN / CALM AND ANALYTICAL]
Length: [E.G. 1,000–1,300 WORDS]

Do this:
1. Identify the expert's 2–3 strongest opinions, especially ones that go against common advice. Pick one as the central argument and explain why.
2. Suggest 5 headlines that state the point of view (no clickbait).
3. Write the article in first person as the expert: a hook from a real story in the notes, the argument, 3 supporting points with their examples, a fair counter-argument, and practical takeaways.
4. Use only facts, numbers and stories from the transcript; mark gaps with {ASK EXPERT}.
5. Pull 3 quotable lines for social posts.
6. List questions to ask the expert in a follow-up to strengthen the piece.
```

## How to use it

1. **Record a 30–45 minute interview** and ask for opinions and stories, not definitions.
2. **Paste the transcript**; detail in the source is what makes the article original.
3. **Have the expert review and edit** before publishing under their name.

## Tips

- Ask "What do people in your industry get wrong?" — it reliably surfaces a point of view.
- One strong idea per article beats a list of ten weak ones.
