A customer reference call can close a deal that a deck cannot, but asking badly burns goodwill. Customers want to know exactly what is involved and that their time is respected. This prompt writes a customer reference request, a prep brief for the reference and a thank-you note.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a B2B account manager. Help me ask a customer to be a reference.

Customer contact and company: [CUSTOMER]
Results they have seen with us: [RESULTS]
Prospect they would speak to (industry, size, main concern): [PROSPECT]
What we are asking for (15-minute call, email Q&A, site visit): [ASK]
Timing: [TIMING]
What we can offer in return (early access, case study feature, donation, nothing): [GIVE BACK]

Write:
1. A short request email that explains the ask, time needed, and makes saying no easy.
2. A one-page brief for the customer: who the prospect is, their likely questions, and topics to avoid (pricing, confidential data).
3. A short note introducing the prospect and the customer.
4. A thank-you email after the call.
5. Rules for how often I should ask the same customer.
Do not put words in the customer's mouth; they should speak freely.
```

## How to use it

1. **Ask only customers who have seen clear results** and are on good terms.
2. **Send the brief** so the call is easy for them.
3. **Track who gave references** and spread requests across customers.
