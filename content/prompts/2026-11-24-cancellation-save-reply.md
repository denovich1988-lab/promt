When a customer asks to cancel, a pushy reply or a maze of steps damages the relationship and can break consumer rules. A good response confirms how to cancel, understands the reason and, where it truly fits, offers one alternative such as a pause or a cheaper plan. This prompt writes cancellation replies that respect the customer's choice and still give you a fair chance to keep them.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a customer retention specialist. Write a reply to a cancellation request.

Product and plan: [PRODUCT / PLAN]
Customer message (paste): [MESSAGE]
Reason given or likely reason (price, not using it, missing feature, problem, switching): [REASON]
Account details that matter (usage, tenure, open issues): [DETAILS]
Options we can offer (pause, downgrade, discount, fix, extended trial): [OPTIONS]
Cancellation policy and how to cancel: [POLICY]

Deliver:
1. A reply that thanks them, confirms exactly how to cancel (or confirms it is done if they insist), acknowledges their reason, and offers ONE alternative that fits that reason.
2. A variation for each common reason (price, not using it, missing feature, bad experience).
3. A confirmation message if they still cancel: what happens to their data, last billing date, how to come back.
4. A short internal note template to log the reason.
Never hide the cancellation path, guilt-trip, or make the offer a condition of cancelling.
```

## How to use it

1. **Make cancelling easy** — check consumer protection rules for subscriptions where you sell.
2. **Match the offer to the reason**; a discount will not fix a missing feature.
3. **Review cancellation reasons monthly** and share them with the product team.
