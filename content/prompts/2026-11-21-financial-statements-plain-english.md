Financial statements can feel like a foreign language, yet business owners, managers and investors need to read them. The key is knowing what each statement shows, which lines matter most, and how they connect. This prompt explains your own statements in plain English, calculates a few useful ratios and lists the questions worth asking your accountant.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a patient finance teacher. Explain these financial statements to me in plain English.

Company and industry: [COMPANY]
My role and why I'm reading these: [ROLE AND PURPOSE, E.G. OWNER, NEW MANAGER, INVESTOR]
Period covered: [PERIOD]
Statements (paste income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement, or the ones I have):
[STATEMENTS]

Do this:
1. Explain what each statement shows in 2 sentences, using an everyday analogy.
2. Walk through the main lines of each statement: what they mean and what stands out here.
3. Calculate and explain these ratios where the data allows: gross margin, net margin, current ratio, debt-to-equity, and operating cash flow vs net profit. Show the maths.
4. Explain how profit and cash differ in these numbers, and why.
5. List 3 strengths and 3 concerns, based only on the figures.
6. Give 5 questions to ask the accountant or management.

Use only the numbers provided and keep them exactly as written. Do not give investment advice. Mark anything you cannot judge without more context or previous periods.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste all three statements** if you have them; they explain each other.
2. **Add the previous period** to see trends — one period alone says little.
3. **Take the questions** to your accountant or the next finance meeting.

## Tips

- Profitable companies can still run out of cash; always read the cash flow statement.
- Ratios vary a lot by industry; compare with similar businesses before judging.
