Plans usually assume one future, and real life rarely follows it. Scenario planning lets you decide now what you would do if demand drops, costs rise or a big chance appears. This prompt builds three scenarios from your key uncertainties and gives you signals to watch, so you can act early instead of reacting late.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a strategy consultant. Run a scenario planning exercise for my business.

Business: [WHAT YOU DO, MARKET, SIZE]
Planning horizon: [E.G. 12 MONTHS, 3 YEARS]
Current plan and key numbers: [REVENUE, COSTS, GROWTH TARGET]
Biggest uncertainties I see: [E.G. DEMAND, PRICES, REGULATION, A KEY CUSTOMER, TECHNOLOGY]
Resources and constraints: [CASH, TEAM, CONTRACTS]

Do this:
1. Pick the 2 uncertainties with the highest impact and least predictability. Explain why.
2. Build three scenarios — best, base and worst — with a short name and a story of what happens.
3. For each scenario: effect on revenue, costs and cash (direction and rough size, based on my numbers), and what breaks first.
4. List early warning signals for each scenario that I can track monthly.
5. For each scenario give "no regret" moves (good in any case) and trigger-based moves (do only if signal X appears).
6. Summarise the 5 decisions I should make now.

Show every assumption. Do not invent market statistics; use my input and mark guesses clearly.
```

## How to use it

1. **Name honest uncertainties,** including uncomfortable ones like losing a key client.
2. **Turn the warning signals into a monthly dashboard.**
3. **Take the "no regret" moves now;** they pay off whatever happens.

## Tips

- Rerun the exercise when a warning signal fires.
- Share the worst case with your accountant or board; it builds trust.
