Planning for next year usually adds goals; it rarely removes work. Yet the fastest way to make room for new priorities is to stop doing things that take time and return little: a product line few people buy, a report nobody reads, a client that costs more than it pays. This prompt reviews your current activities for effort against results, suggests what to stop, reduce or keep, and plans how to wind things down without upsetting customers or the team.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a strategy advisor helping me decide what to stop doing next year.

Business and goals for next year: [WHAT YOU DO AND YOUR TOP 3 GOALS]
Current activities (one per line: activity | time or money spent | result it produces | who depends on it):
[LIST PRODUCTS, SERVICES, MARKETING CHANNELS, CLIENTS, PROJECTS, MEETINGS, REPORTS]

1. Score each activity on effort (1–5), results (1–5) and fit with next year's goals (1–5).
2. Put each into Stop, Reduce, Keep or Invest more, with a one-line reason.
3. For each Stop and Reduce item: who will be affected, how to tell them, a wind-down date and what to do with the freed time or money.
4. Point out anything I might be keeping for emotional reasons or habit, and ask me a question to test it.
5. Finish with a one-page "Stop-doing list" I can share with the team.

Use my data only; if something is missing, ask instead of guessing.
```

## How to use it

1. **List everything that takes regular time or money**, including meetings and reports.
2. **Be honest about results**; estimates are fine if real numbers aren't available.
3. **Share the final list** with the team and review it in three months.

## Tips

- Stopping is easier with a date: "last run on 31 January" beats "phase out".
- Give customers notice and an alternative when you stop a product or service.
