Layoff announcements are among the hardest messages a leader writes. Vague, corporate language makes a painful moment worse; clear facts, accountability and concrete support help people trust what comes next. This prompt drafts a humane layoff announcement message, talking points for managers and an FAQ, for review by HR and legal before anything is shared.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced HR and internal communications leader. Help me draft layoff communications.

Company and size: [COMPANY]
Number of roles affected and which teams: [ROLES AFFECTED]
Honest reason for the decision: [REASON]
Support for departing employees (severance, benefits, outplacement, references, equipment): [SUPPORT]
Timeline and how affected people will be told: [TIMELINE AND PROCESS]
Locations and any legal notice requirements I know of: [LOCATIONS]
Leader sending the message: [SENDER]

Draft:
1. A company-wide announcement: what is happening, why, leadership accountability, who is affected and when they will hear, support offered, what happens next. Under 400 words, plain language.
2. Talking points for managers telling their teams, including what not to say.
3. An FAQ for departing employees (pay, benefits, timing, references).
4. An FAQ and a message for remaining employees (workload, future plans, where to ask questions).
5. A checklist of points HR and legal must confirm before sending.
Be direct and compassionate. No euphemisms, no blaming individuals, no promises about the future I haven't confirmed.
```

## How to use it

1. **Tell affected employees personally first**, before any company-wide message.
2. **Have HR and employment counsel review** every draft for your locations.
3. **Hold an open Q&A** for remaining staff soon after the announcement.

## Tips

- Plan the wider communication with the [change management communication](/prompts/change-management-communication/) prompt.
- Review legal notice requirements with a lawyer; they vary by country and state.
