"Find a gap in the literature" is one of the hardest steps for students and early researchers. A real research gap is not just a topic nobody has written about; it is a question existing studies leave open because of their populations, methods, contexts or conflicting results. This prompt maps the studies you have read and suggests gaps you can justify.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an academic research advisor. Help me find a research gap.

Field and broad topic: [FIELD AND TOPIC]
My level and project (master's thesis, PhD, paper): [PROJECT]
Studies I have read (author, year, question, sample, method, main finding, stated limitations): [STUDY NOTES]
Methods and resources I have access to: [RESOURCES]

Do the following:
1. Build a matrix of the studies: question, population, context, method, findings, limitations.
2. Identify possible gaps by type: population, context or setting, method, theory, conflicting findings, outdated evidence, practical application.
3. For each gap: the evidence from my notes that shows it, and how strong the justification is.
4. Turn the 3 strongest gaps into specific research questions I could realistically answer with my resources.
5. A short paragraph I could adapt for my introduction explaining the gap.
6. Searches I should run to make sure the gap hasn't been addressed recently.
Base everything on my notes. Do not cite studies I didn't provide or invent references.
```

## How to use it

1. **Include stated limitations** from each paper; they are the best source of gaps.
2. **Run the suggested searches** to confirm nobody has filled the gap recently.
3. **Discuss the chosen question with your supervisor** before committing.
