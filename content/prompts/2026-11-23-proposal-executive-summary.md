Many decision-makers read only the first page of a proposal. If the executive summary talks about your company instead of their problem, the deal stalls. This prompt writes a one-page proposal executive summary in the buyer's language: the situation, what it costs them, your recommended solution, expected outcomes, investment and the next step.

## Prompt

```prompt
Write the executive summary for my sales proposal.

Client and the decision-maker reading it: [CLIENT AND READER ROLE]
Their situation and problem in their own words (from discovery): [PROBLEM]
What the problem costs them or puts at risk: [IMPACT, only figures they shared or we agreed]
Our recommended solution and scope: [SOLUTION]
Outcomes they can expect and how we'll measure them: [OUTCOMES]
Investment and timeline: [PRICE AND TIMELINE]
Proof we can share (only real): [PROOF, e.g. similar project, case study]

Write:
1. A one-page executive summary (250–350 words) with short headed sections: situation, what's at stake, recommendation, outcomes, investment, next step.
2. Their goals first; our company is mentioned only where it helps them decide.
3. One clear next step with a date.
4. Two alternative opening sentences.
No buzzwords, no invented numbers or claims.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use the buyer's words** from discovery notes; it shows you listened.
2. **Keep it to one page** and put details in the body of the proposal.
3. **Send it to your champion first** and ask what they would change.
