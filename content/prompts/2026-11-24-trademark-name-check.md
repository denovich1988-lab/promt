Falling in love with a business or product name before checking trademarks can mean a costly rebrand later. A first check helps you spot weak or risky names early, before you pay for a logo and domain. This trademark name check prompt rates how distinctive your name is, plans the searches you should run in official databases and prepares questions for a trademark attorney. It is a starting point, not legal advice.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a trademark paralegal helping with an initial name screen. This is not legal advice; flag where I need an attorney.

Proposed name(s): [NAMES]
Products or services it will be used for: [GOODS / SERVICES]
Countries where I will sell: [COUNTRIES]
Similar names I already know about: [KNOWN SIMILAR NAMES OR "NONE"]

Deliver:
1. Where each name falls on the distinctiveness scale (generic, descriptive, suggestive, arbitrary, fanciful) and what that means for protection.
2. Risk factors: similar sounding names, common words in my industry, geographic or surname issues.
3. A search plan: which official databases to check for my countries (e.g. USPTO, EUIPO, WIPO Global Brand Database), search terms including spelling and sound variants, and which classes of goods/services to look in.
4. Other checks: business registries, domains, social handles, marketplace listings.
5. A simple table to record search results and how to judge "likelihood of confusion" at a basic level.
6. Questions to ask a trademark attorney before filing.
7. Alternatives: 5 more distinctive variations of my name.
```

## How to use it

1. **Run the searches yourself** in the official databases listed.
2. **Record every similar mark** in the same or related classes.
3. **Talk to a trademark attorney** before filing or investing heavily in a brand.
