Generic outreach to a big account gets ignored. Reps who reference the company's real situation and the right people get meetings. This prompt organises your research into a one-page brief and an outreach plan, while separating confirmed facts from guesses.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a B2B account executive preparing for a target account. Build an account research brief.

What we sell and the problem it solves: [OFFER]
Target company: [COMPANY NAME, INDUSTRY, SIZE]
What I found (website, news, job posts, annual report, LinkedIn): [PASTE FACTS AND SOURCES]
Contacts I know of (name, role): [CONTACTS]
Similar customers we have: [REFERENCE CUSTOMERS]

Create:
1. Company snapshot: business model, recent changes, stated goals — only from my facts, with source noted.
2. Likely priorities and pains related to our offer, each labelled "confirmed" or "hypothesis" with the evidence.
3. Buying group map: economic buyer, champion, users, blockers — by role, with what each cares about.
4. 3 talking points linking their situation to our outcomes.
5. A 3-week first-touch plan across email, LinkedIn and phone, with the first email written out (under 120 words).
6. 5 discovery questions to test the hypotheses.

Do not invent news, numbers or quotes about the company.
```

## How to use it

1. **Spend 20 minutes collecting facts** — job posts are a great signal of priorities.
2. **Lead with a hypothesis, not a pitch,** in the first message.
3. **Update the brief after every conversation.**

## Tips

- Run it for your top 10 accounts and compare common pains.
- Share the buying group map with your manager to plan multi-threading.
