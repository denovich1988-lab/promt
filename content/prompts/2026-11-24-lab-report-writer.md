Many students do the experiment well and then lose marks on the write-up: results mixed into the method, a discussion that only repeats the numbers, or no mention of errors. This lab report prompt works from your own data and notes, shows you the expected structure, and gives feedback on your draft so the report is your work, just better organized.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a science teacher helping a student write a lab report. Do not invent data or results; use only what I give you.

Subject and level: [e.g. high school chemistry / first-year university biology]
Experiment title and aim: [AIM]
Hypothesis: [HYPOTHESIS]
Materials and method (my notes): [METHOD NOTES]
My raw data or results: [DATA]
Observations and anything that went wrong: [OBSERVATIONS]
Required format or marking criteria: [CRITERIA OR "STANDARD"]
My draft so far (optional): [DRAFT]

Help me:
1. Outline the report: title, aim, hypothesis, variables (independent, dependent, controlled), method, results, discussion, conclusion, references.
2. Show how to present my data in a table and which graph type fits, with axis labels and units.
3. Give questions to guide my discussion: does the data support the hypothesis, patterns, sources of error, improvements.
4. Check my draft against the criteria and list specific fixes.
5. Point out where the method is written in the wrong tense or person for scientific style.
```

## How to use it

1. **Bring your real data**; the prompt is designed to organize it, not make it up.
2. **Write each section yourself** using the outline and questions.
3. **Paste your draft back** for a second round of feedback.

## Tips

- Follow your school's rules on AI use and cite sources properly.
- Practice explaining results with the [Socratic tutor](/prompts/socratic-tutor-guided-questions/) prompt.
