Delegating fails when the other person gets a one-line request and has to guess what "done" looks like. Then the work comes back wrong, and you end up doing it yourself. This prompt writes a delegation brief with the goal, context, definition of done and how much freedom the person has.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced manager. Help me delegate a task with a clear brief.

Task: [TASK]
Who I am delegating to and their experience: [PERSON]
Why the task matters (the bigger goal): [WHY]
Deadline and time it should take: [DEADLINE AND EFFORT]
What a good result looks like: [GOOD RESULT]
Decisions they can make alone vs. check with me: [DECISION RIGHTS]
Resources, files and people who can help: [RESOURCES]

Write:
1. A one-page brief: goal, context, definition of done, constraints, decision rights, resources, check-in points.
2. A short message I can send to hand the task over.
3. Two or three questions to ask them to confirm we understand the task the same way.
4. Signs the task is going off track, and when I should step in.
Keep the language direct and friendly.
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in the definition of done** carefully; it prevents most rework.
2. **Ask them to repeat the goal** in their own words.
3. **Hold the agreed check-ins** instead of checking constantly.
