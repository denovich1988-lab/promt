Discount requests that just say "customer wants 30% off, competitor is cheaper" usually get rejected or delayed. Finance and sales leaders want to see the deal value, margin impact, competitive situation and what the company gets in return. This discount approval prompt turns your deal notes into a concise request that's easy to approve — or shows you the deal needs a different structure.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a deal desk manager. Help me write a discount approval request.

Customer and segment: [COMPANY, SIZE, INDUSTRY]
Products and list price: [ITEMS, QUANTITIES, LIST TOTAL]
Requested discount and terms: [E.G. 28% OFF, 3-YEAR TERM, ANNUAL BILLING]
Our standard discount limit: [E.G. 15% WITHOUT APPROVAL]
Competitive situation: [COMPETITOR, THEIR PRICE IF KNOWN, OTHER OPTIONS]
Strategic value: [E.G. LOGO IN NEW INDUSTRY, EXPANSION POTENTIAL, REFERENCE]
What the customer will give: [E.G. MULTI-YEAR, UPFRONT PAYMENT, CASE STUDY, SIGN BY DATE]
Close date and deal stage: [DETAILS]
Margin or cost info, if available: [DETAILS]

Write:
1. A one-paragraph summary for approvers: ask, value, reason, deadline.
2. A deal table: list price, proposed price, discount %, annual and total contract value, effective discount per year.
3. Justification: competitive pressure, strategic value, give-gets.
4. Risks: precedent for renewals, impact on other deals, margin.
5. Two alternative structures that reach a similar price with less discount (e.g. ramped pricing, fewer seats, longer term).
Show all calculations. Keep it under 300 words excluding the table.
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in real competitive evidence** if you have it; "they said it's cheaper" is weak.
2. **Always include give-gets**; approvers look for what the company receives.
3. **Submit early**, before the customer's deadline forces a rushed decision.

## Tips

- Ramped or multi-year structures often protect renewal pricing better than a flat deep discount.
- Track approved discounts by segment to spot pricing problems over time.
