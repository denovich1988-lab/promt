Sending the same email to everyone is simple, but it means most subscribers get messages that don't fit them, and they stop opening. Segmentation fixes that by grouping people on what they did and what they need. This prompt designs a small, manageable set of segments from the data you actually have, and plans what each one should receive.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an email marketing strategist. Design a segmentation plan for my email list.

Business and what we sell: [BUSINESS]
List size and where subscribers come from: [LIST SIZE AND SOURCES]
Data we have on subscribers (purchases, clicks, signup form fields, tags): [AVAILABLE DATA]
Email tool we use: [EMAIL PLATFORM]
Main goal for email this quarter: [GOAL]

Create:
1. 4–6 segments I can build with my current data. For each: name, rule to define it in plain logic, why it matters, and roughly how to find it in my tool.
2. A message plan per segment: what they need to hear, 3 email ideas, ideal frequency, and the main call to action.
3. One automated flow to set up first, with trigger, emails and timing.
4. Data I should start collecting to improve segments later (one simple signup or preference question).
5. How to measure if segmentation is working: which metrics to compare and over what period.

Keep it practical for a small team. Do not create segments that need data I don't have.
```

## How to use it

1. **List exactly what data your tool stores** — this decides which segments are possible.
2. **Start with 2–3 segments** and the first automated flow, not all of them at once.
3. **Compare open and click rates** against your old all-list sends after a month.

## Tips

- "Engaged in the last 90 days" vs "not engaged" is a simple first split that helps deliverability.
- Ask one preference question at signup; it often beats complex behaviour rules.
