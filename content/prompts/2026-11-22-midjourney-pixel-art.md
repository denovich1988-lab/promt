AI pixel art often comes out blurry, with pixels of different sizes mixed together. These Midjourney prompts for pixel art describe the look the way a pixel artist would (bit depth, palette size, grid feel, no anti-aliasing), so you get crisp retro scenes, characters and icons for games, streams, wallpapers or posts.

## Prompt

```prompt
Pixel art of [SUBJECT], [SCENE OR SETTING], [8-BIT OR 16-BIT OR 32-BIT] retro video game style, limited palette of [NUMBER] colors ([PALETTE, e.g., warm sunset tones]), crisp uniform square pixels, no anti-aliasing, no blur, clean outlines, [VIEW: side-scrolling view / top-down view / isometric view], [MOOD AND LIGHTING], simple dithering for shading, plain [BACKGROUND] background --ar [ASPECT RATIO] --style raw
```

## How to use it

1. **Pick a bit depth**: 8-bit gives chunky, simple shapes; 16-bit allows more detail and color.
2. **Choose the view** that matches your use: side-scrolling for game scenes, isometric for rooms and maps, front view for character sprites and icons.
3. **Generate, then clean up**: downscale in an editor with nearest-neighbor resampling to snap pixels to a true grid.

## Tips

- For sprite sheets, ask for "character sprite sheet, same character, 4 poses, evenly spaced on a plain background".
- Fewer colors usually look more authentic; try 8 to 16.
- For tidy icon sets in a different style, see the [isometric 3D icon prompt](/prompts/isometric-3d-icon-set/).
