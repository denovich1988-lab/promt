A vague rental listing brings dozens of messages from people the property doesn't suit. A clear one, followed by a few consistent screening questions, saves landlords and agents hours of viewings. This prompt writes both, focused on facts about the property and objective requirements, and reminds you to stay within fair housing rules.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced letting agent. Write a rental listing and pre-screening questions.

Property type, size, rooms and location: [PROPERTY DETAILS]
Rent, deposit, bills included or not: [COSTS]
Available from and minimum lease length: [DATES AND TERM]
Key features and nearby amenities: [FEATURES]
Rules (pets, smoking, parking): [RULES]
Requirements (income, references, credit check): [REQUIREMENTS]
Platform where it will be listed: [PLATFORM]

Write:
1. 3 listing headline options (under 70 characters).
2. A listing description of 150–220 words: who the home suits (in terms of lifestyle, not personal characteristics), layout, best features, location, costs and terms, and how to apply.
3. A short list of all key facts for the listing's detail fields.
4. 6–8 pre-screening questions to ask every enquirer the same way, covering move-in date, length of stay, number of occupants, pets, income and references.
5. A reply template for enquiries and a polite decline message.

Use only objective criteria. Do not mention or ask about protected characteristics (such as race, religion, family status, disability, age or nationality). Add a note to check local fair housing and tenancy rules.
```

## How to use it

1. **List all costs and terms clearly** — it filters out unsuitable enquiries early.
2. **Ask every applicant the same questions** and keep simple records.
3. **Book viewings only for applicants who meet the stated requirements**.

## Tips

- Good photos and a floor plan reduce messages asking basic questions.
- Rules on what you can ask tenants vary by country and city; check before you list.
