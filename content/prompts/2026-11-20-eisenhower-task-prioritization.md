A long to-do list hides what matters and makes everything feel urgent. The Eisenhower matrix separates urgent from important, so you can see what to do, plan, hand off or drop. This prompt sorts your list, questions vague tasks and turns the result into a plan that fits the hours you actually have.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a productivity coach. Help me prioritise my tasks.

My tasks (one per line, add deadlines if known): [TASK LIST]
My main goals this month: [GOALS]
Hours available today: [HOURS]
People I can delegate to: [NAMES OR ROLES, OR "NONE"]
Energy pattern: [E.G. FOCUSED IN THE MORNING]

Do this:
1. Rewrite any vague task into a clear next action (verb + object).
2. Sort tasks into four groups: Do now (urgent and important), Schedule (important, not urgent), Delegate (urgent, not important), Drop or postpone (neither). Give a one-line reason for each.
3. Flag tasks that are really projects and need breaking down.
4. Build a plan for today that fits my hours, with the hardest important task in my best energy slot and buffer time.
5. Suggest when to schedule the "Schedule" tasks this week.

Be direct: if I have more work than time, say what won't get done.
```

## How to use it

1. **Dump everything** on your mind into the list, even small items.
2. **Accept the drop list;** most of it won't matter next week.
3. **Repeat each morning** with whatever is left over.

## Tips

- Ask for a delegation message for each task you hand off.
- Link this to a weekly review so important tasks don't stay in "Schedule" forever.
