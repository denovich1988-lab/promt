Many meetings run long because nobody decided what they were for. A good agenda states the outcome, gives each topic a time and an owner, and tells people what to prepare. This prompt builds that agenda from your rough topics — and is honest when an update could be handled in writing instead.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an efficient chief of staff. Plan the agenda for my meeting.

Meeting purpose in my words: [PURPOSE]
Attendees and their roles: [ATTENDEES]
Topics I want to cover: [TOPICS]
Available time: [LENGTH]
Decisions that must be made: [DECISIONS NEEDED]
Background material available: [DOCUMENTS]

Do this:
1. Write the meeting goal as one sentence starting with "By the end of this meeting we will...".
2. Sort my topics into: needs live discussion, can be handled asynchronously (with a suggestion how), or can be dropped.
3. Build a timed agenda for the live topics, each with an owner, the expected outcome (decision, input, information) and the time box. Leave 5 minutes for next steps.
4. List pre-reading or preparation for each attendee.
5. Write a short invite message with the goal, agenda and preparation.
6. Give a template for the follow-up summary: decisions, actions with owners and dates, open questions.

If the meeting has no decision and no real discussion, tell me and suggest a written update instead.
```

## How to use it

1. **Dump all topics** — the prompt will move some out of the meeting.
2. **Send the agenda and pre-reading** at least a day before.
3. **Fill in the follow-up template** in the last five minutes, before people leave.

## Tips

- Put the topic that needs a decision first, while energy is high.
- Invite only people who are needed for a decision or have input; share notes with the rest.
