An annual marketing plan is easiest to write when it starts from what actually happened this year: which channels brought customers, what cost too much and what you never got around to. Many plans skip that review and copy last year's channel list with bigger numbers. This prompt asks for your results first, then builds goals, a budget split, a quarterly calendar and KPIs, and points out where your targets look unrealistic for the budget.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a head of marketing helping me write next year's annual marketing plan.

Business: [WHAT YOU SELL, TO WHOM, PRICE RANGE]
Business goal for next year: [E.G. REVENUE TARGET, NEW CUSTOMERS, NEW MARKET]
This year's results by channel: [CHANNEL | SPEND | LEADS OR SALES | NOTES]
What worked and what didn't: [YOUR OBSERVATIONS]
Marketing budget next year: [AMOUNT]
Team and tools: [WHO DOES WHAT, AGENCIES, TOOLS]
Key dates and launches: [SEASONS, EVENTS, PRODUCT LAUNCHES]

Write the plan with these sections:
1. Summary of this year in 5 bullet points (keep, fix, stop).
2. 3–4 marketing goals tied to the business goal, each with a number and a deadline.
3. Target audience and core message in 3 sentences.
4. Channel plan: for each channel, its role (awareness, leads, sales, retention), budget share and main KPI.
5. Quarterly calendar: campaigns, launches and seasonal moments by quarter.
6. Budget table with a 10% reserve for tests.
7. KPI dashboard: 6–8 metrics, how often to review them and who owns them.
8. Risks and assumptions, including any goal that looks too high for the budget.

Use my numbers only; if you estimate something, label it as an estimate.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pull this year's numbers by channel** before you start; rough numbers are fine.
2. **Review the risks section** and adjust goals or budget until they agree.
3. **Turn the quarterly calendar** into tasks in your project tool.

## Tips

- Keep one quarter of the plan flexible; markets and ad prices change during the year.
- Review the KPI dashboard monthly, not just at year end.
