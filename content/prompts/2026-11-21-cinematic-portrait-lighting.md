A portrait looks "cinematic" mostly because of light and colour: a strong key light, deep shadows, a motivated light source and a deliberate grade. Naming the lighting setup the way a cinematographer would gives image models much clearer direction than "dramatic". This template lets you pick each element separately.

## Prompt

```prompt
Cinematic portrait of [SUBJECT: AGE, LOOK, CLOTHING], [EXPRESSION AND EMOTION, E.G. QUIET DETERMINATION, EYES SLIGHTLY DOWN], [FRAMING, E.G. TIGHT MEDIUM CLOSE-UP], lit with [LIGHTING SETUP, E.G. REMBRANDT LIGHTING FROM A SINGLE WINDOW ON THE LEFT], motivated by [LIGHT SOURCE, E.G. A STREETLAMP OUTSIDE], deep soft shadows, [BACKGROUND, E.G. DARK APARTMENT WITH BLURRED CITY LIGHTS], shot on [LENS, E.G. 85MM AT F/1.8], shallow depth of field, [COLOUR GRADE, E.G. TEAL AND AMBER], subtle film grain, anamorphic feel, film still from a [GENRE, E.G. NEO-NOIR DRAMA], realistic skin texture
```

Lighting setups to try: Rembrandt, split light, butterfly light, rim light only, neon side light, candlelight. For Midjourney, add `--ar 2:3 --style raw` or `--ar 21:9` for a widescreen still.

## How to use it

1. **Choose the emotion first**; then pick light that supports it (split light for tension, soft window light for calm).
2. **Change only one element** between generations so you learn what each word does.
3. **Use a reference image** of your own if you need the same character across images.

## Tips

- "Motivated light" — naming where the light comes from — makes scenes look more natural.
- Don't create images of real, identifiable people without their permission.
