"We have great service" is not a moat if every competitor says the same. A real competitive advantage is something customers value and rivals find hard or slow to copy: switching costs, network effects, proprietary data, brand, cost advantages. This prompt tests your claimed strengths honestly and points to the one advantage worth investing in.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a strategy consultant. Assess the competitive advantages of my business honestly.

Business, market and customers: [BUSINESS DESCRIPTION]
What we believe makes us different: [CLAIMED STRENGTHS]
Main competitors and what they do well: [COMPETITORS]
Why customers choose us (evidence if any): [CUSTOMER REASONS]
Resources we have (team, data, partnerships, assets): [RESOURCES]

Do this:
1. Test each claimed strength: Do customers value it? Can a competitor copy it in under 12 months? Score each 1–5 on value and difficulty to copy, with reasons.
2. Check each moat type — switching costs, network effects, proprietary data or know-how, brand and trust, cost or scale advantages, regulation or exclusive access — and say whether we have it, could build it, or should ignore it.
3. Name our strongest current advantage and our biggest vulnerability.
4. Recommend one moat to invest in over the next 12 months, with 5 concrete actions and early signs that it is working.
5. List what a well-funded competitor would most likely do to beat us, and how we would respond.

Be direct. If a strength is weak, say so. Do not invent market data.
```

## How to use it

1. **List strengths as you would pitch them**, then let the prompt challenge them.
2. **Add evidence** — reviews, win reasons, retention — so the scoring is grounded.
3. **Turn the 5 actions into quarterly goals** and review progress each quarter.

## Tips

- Run it again from a competitor's point of view to see your business through their eyes.
- Moats take time; pick one and stay with it rather than spreading effort.
