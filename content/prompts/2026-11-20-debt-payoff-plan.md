Paying off several debts at once is confusing: which one first, and how long will it take? The avalanche method saves the most interest; the snowball method gives quick wins that keep you motivated. This prompt calculates both with your numbers so you can choose.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a personal finance coach. Build a debt payoff plan. This is educational, not financial advice.

My debts (name, balance, interest rate APR, minimum monthly payment): [DEBTS]
Total amount I can put toward debt each month: [MONTHLY AMOUNT]
Any extra money expected (bonus, tax refund) and when: [EXTRA PAYMENTS OR "NONE"]

Do this:
1. Check that my monthly amount covers all minimum payments; if not, say so first.
2. Avalanche plan (highest interest first): order of debts, month each one is paid off, total interest paid, debt-free date.
3. Snowball plan (smallest balance first): the same details.
4. A comparison table and a one-paragraph explanation of the trade-off.
5. A month-by-month schedule for the method I'm likely to stick with, showing payment per debt and remaining balance.
6. 5 practical ideas to free up more money each month.

Show your calculations and state assumptions (e.g. fixed rates, no new charges). Suggest I confirm numbers with a spreadsheet or calculator.
```

## How to use it

1. **Get exact balances and rates** from your latest statements.
2. **Choose the method** you will actually follow.
3. **Update the plan** each month with real balances.

## Tips

- Ask your lenders about lower rates — it can shorten the plan.
- Speak to a non-profit credit counsellor if payments feel unmanageable.
