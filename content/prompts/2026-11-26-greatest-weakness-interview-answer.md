"What is your greatest weakness?" is one of the most dreaded interview questions, and the usual tricks ("I'm a perfectionist") make interviewers roll their eyes. A strong answer names a real, non-critical weakness, shows self-awareness and gives evidence of improvement. This prompt helps you choose the right weakness for the role, structure your answer and practise follow-up questions, so your answer sounds honest rather than rehearsed.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an interview coach. Help me answer "What is your greatest weakness?"

Role I'm interviewing for: [JOB TITLE, COMPANY, KEY REQUIREMENTS FROM THE JOB POST]
My background: [CURRENT ROLE, EXPERIENCE LEVEL]
Weaknesses I could honestly mention: [E.G. PUBLIC SPEAKING, DELEGATING, SAYING NO, DETAIL IN SPREADSHEETS, A TOOL I'M STILL LEARNING]
What I've done about them: [COURSES, HABITS, FEEDBACK, RESULTS]

Do this:
1. Rate each weakness for this role: safe to mention, risky (core to the job), or too vague, and recommend the best one.
2. Write a 60–90 second answer: the weakness stated plainly, a brief real example of when it caused a problem, the specific steps I took, and evidence of progress, ending with how I keep working on it.
3. Write a shorter 30-second version.
4. List 3 follow-up questions the interviewer might ask and draft answers.
5. Point out anything in my answer that sounds like a cliché or a disguised strength.

Use only the facts I gave you.
```

## How to use it

1. **Choose a weakness that isn't central to the job**; don't mention a core skill gap.
2. **Use a real example**; specific stories sound genuine.
3. **Practise out loud** until it sounds natural, not memorized.

## Tips

- Also prepare a version for "Tell me about a time you failed"; the structure is similar.
- Keep the focus on what you did about the weakness, not the weakness itself.
