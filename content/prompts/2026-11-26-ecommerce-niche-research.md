Many online stores fail because the niche was chosen on a hunch: products that are bulky to ship, impossible to differentiate or sold at razor-thin margins. This ecommerce niche research prompt scores your ideas on the factors that actually decide profitability and tells you exactly what to check in real data before investing in inventory or a store.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an ecommerce consultant. Evaluate these niche ideas for an online store.

Niche ideas: [LIST 3–5 IDEAS]
Business model: [DROPSHIPPING / PRINT ON DEMAND / OWN INVENTORY / HANDMADE]
Target market: [COUNTRY, CUSTOMER TYPE]
Startup budget: [AMOUNT]
My advantages: [E.G. EXPERTISE, AUDIENCE, SUPPLIER ACCESS, HOBBY KNOWLEDGE]
Sales channels planned: [OWN SITE, MARKETPLACES, SOCIAL]

For each niche:
1. Score 1–5 on: demand stability (seasonal or year-round), competition, average order value, estimated gross margin range, shipping and returns difficulty, repeat purchase potential, differentiation potential, regulatory risk (e.g. supplements, kids' products, electrical items), fit with my advantages.
2. Main customer problem and buying triggers.
3. Example product line-up with price points.
4. Biggest risks.
Then:
5. A comparison table with total scores, and the top pick with reasoning.
6. A validation checklist for the top pick: what to check in search trends, marketplace listings and reviews, social content and supplier quotes, plus a small test I can run for under [TEST BUDGET].
Present margins and demand as assumptions to verify, not facts.
```

## How to use it

1. **List niches you could talk about for a year**; motivation matters for content and customer service.
2. **Verify every assumption** in real search, marketplace and supplier data.
3. **Run the small test** before ordering inventory or paying for a custom store.

## Tips

- Consumable or replaceable products bring repeat customers; one-off gadgets don't.
- Read 1–3 star reviews of competitor products to find what to improve.
