Reps often give away discounts in the first five minutes of a negotiation because they never decided what they would trade, and for what. A concession plan fixes that: you set your limits, list what has value to the buyer and decide the order of trades before the call. This sales negotiation prompt builds that plan, with scripts for the requests you are most likely to hear.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a B2B sales negotiation coach. Build a concession plan for this deal.

Deal: [PRODUCT, DEAL SIZE, CONTRACT LENGTH]
Buyer: [COMPANY, ROLES INVOLVED, WHO SIGNS]
What they're asking for: [E.G. 25% DISCOUNT, NET 90, EXTRA SEATS]
Their alternatives: [COMPETITORS, DO NOTHING, BUILD IN-HOUSE]
Our limits: [LOWEST PRICE / MAX DISCOUNT, TERMS WE CAN'T ACCEPT]
Things we can trade that cost us little: [E.G. ONBOARDING, PAYMENT TERMS, CASE STUDY, START DATE]
What we want in return: [E.G. LONGER TERM, UPFRONT PAYMENT, SIGN BY DATE, REFERENCE]
Timeline pressure: [THEIRS AND OURS]

Give me:
1. Our target, acceptable and walk-away positions, with reasoning.
2. A table of tradeable items: cost to us, value to them, what we ask in exchange.
3. A concession ladder: 3–4 steps, each smaller than the last, each tied to something we get.
4. Word-for-word replies to: "Your competitor is cheaper", "We need a bigger discount", "We have no budget until next quarter", and "Can you just do better on price?".
5. Signs the buyer is near agreement and how to close.
6. Mistakes to avoid in this specific deal.
```

## How to use it

1. **Get your limits approved internally** before the meeting so you never improvise.
2. **Rehearse the replies out loud**, ideally with a colleague playing the buyer.
3. **Log every concession** in the CRM so the next renewal starts from the real terms.

## Tips

- Never give something without asking for something; it signals your price was padded.
- Silence after stating your number is fine; let the buyer respond first.
