Suspense comes from what the viewer can't see yet: a slow push towards a door, a shadow that moves, a sound before the reveal. AI video models can create this mood when the prompt describes the shot like a director would — framing, timing, light and sound — rather than just "scary scene". This template gives you a three-beat suspense moment.

## Prompt

```prompt
Create a [LENGTH, E.G. 10-SECOND] cinematic suspense scene, [ASPECT, E.G. 2.39:1 WIDESCREEN].

Setting: [SETTING, E.G. EMPTY FARMHOUSE KITCHEN AT NIGHT, RAIN ON THE WINDOWS]
Character: [CHARACTER DESCRIPTION AND WHAT THEY ARE DOING]
Light: [LIGHT, E.G. SINGLE FLICKERING CEILING BULB, DEEP SHADOWS, COLD BLUE MOONLIGHT FROM THE WINDOW]

Beat 1 (0–4s): wide static shot, the character stops and listens; nothing else moves.
Beat 2 (4–8s): very slow push-in towards [FOCUS POINT, E.G. THE HALF-OPEN DOOR]; the light flickers once.
Beat 3 (8–10s): [REVEAL OR CLIFFHANGER, E.G. A SHADOW PASSES BEHIND THE DOOR GAP], hold on the character's face.

Sound: [SOUND, E.G. RAIN, LOW HUM, A FLOORBOARD CREAK AT 7 SECONDS, THEN SILENCE]
Style: muted colour, high contrast, film grain, realistic, no gore, no text.
Avoid: fast movement, jump cuts, extra characters, distorted faces.
```

## How to use it

1. **Decide what the viewer should fear** and keep it mostly off-screen.
2. **Generate beat by beat** if your tool limits clip length, then edit with a sound design track.
3. **Hold the last frame a moment longer** in editing — silence adds tension.

## Tips

- Slow camera moves read as "something is wrong"; fast ones read as action.
- Sound does half the work; add your own effects if the tool's audio is weak.
