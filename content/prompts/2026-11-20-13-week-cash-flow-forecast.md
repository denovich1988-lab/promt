Profitable businesses still run out of cash when payments come in late and bills come due early. A 13-week forecast shows exactly which weeks will be tight. This prompt builds the table from your numbers, highlights the danger weeks and suggests practical moves to fix them.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a small business finance adviser. Build a 13-week cash flow forecast.

Starting cash balance: [AMOUNT AND CURRENCY]
Expected incoming payments (customer, amount, expected date): [RECEIPTS]
Typical payment delays from customers: [E.G. 15 DAYS LATE ON AVERAGE]
Regular costs (rent, payroll, software, loan payments, with dates): [FIXED COSTS]
Variable or one-off costs: [OTHER COSTS AND DATES]
Taxes due: [TAX PAYMENTS AND DATES]
Minimum cash I want to keep: [SAFETY BUFFER]

Create:
1. A weekly table: week, opening cash, cash in (by type), cash out (by type), net flow, closing cash.
2. Highlight weeks where closing cash falls below my buffer.
3. A cautious version where customer payments arrive 2 weeks later than expected.
4. 6 options to close any gap (e.g. invoice earlier, deposits, negotiate supplier terms, delay purchases), with pros and cons.
5. A short list of numbers to update every week.

Show the table so I can copy it into a spreadsheet. Don't invent amounts; use 0 and flag it if something is missing.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pull actual figures from your accounting software** and bank.
2. **Copy the table into a spreadsheet** and check the totals with formulas.
3. **Update it every Monday;** a forecast is only useful while it's current.

## Tips

- Keep cash and profit separate in your head: a sale isn't cash until it's paid.
- Talk to an accountant before taking on debt to close a gap.
