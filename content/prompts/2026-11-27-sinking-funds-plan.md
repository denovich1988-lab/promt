Many "unexpected" expenses are actually predictable: car insurance, holiday gifts, annual subscriptions, school costs, vet bills, car repairs. They just don't arrive monthly, so they break a budget when they land. Sinking funds fix this by saving a set amount each month for each known cost. This sinking funds prompt finds the irregular expenses you've forgotten, works out the monthly amounts and builds a simple tracker.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a practical budgeting coach. Help me set up sinking funds.

Monthly take-home pay: [AMOUNT]
Current fixed monthly bills: [TOTAL OR LIST]
Irregular expenses I know about: [E.G. CAR INSURANCE $900 IN MARCH, HOLIDAY GIFTS $600 IN DECEMBER, KIDS' CAMP $400 IN JULY]
Household: [WHO, PETS, CAR(S), HOME OWNER OR RENTER]
Emergency fund status: [AMOUNT SAVED]
Amount I can put toward sinking funds each month: [AMOUNT OR "NOT SURE"]
Today's month: [MONTH]

Do this:
1. Suggest irregular expenses I may have missed for a household like mine, as questions for me to confirm (don't add them automatically).
2. For each confirmed expense: annual cost, due month, months left until due, and monthly amount needed. Show the math.
3. If the total is more than I can afford, prioritise (essential vs. flexible) and suggest which funds to reduce or delay.
4. Explain how to hold the money: one savings account with a tracker or separate sub-accounts, and the pros of each.
5. A simple tracker table I can copy into a spreadsheet: fund, target, due date, monthly amount, balance.
6. What to do when a cost comes in higher than planned.
Keep it practical. This is general budgeting help, not financial advice.
```

## How to use it

1. **Go through last year's bank statements** to find irregular costs before you start.
2. **Automate the transfers** on payday so the funds build without effort.
3. **Review the plan every few months** and adjust amounts when costs change.

## Tips

- Keep sinking funds separate from your emergency fund; one is for known costs, the other for true surprises.
- Round monthly amounts up slightly to cover price increases.
