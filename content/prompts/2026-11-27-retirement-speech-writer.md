A retirement speech marks the end of a career, so it should feel personal: real stories, the person's habits and impact, a little gentle humour and sincere thanks, without turning into a list of job titles. This retirement speech prompt works for both cases (honouring a colleague or giving your own farewell) and turns your notes into a speech timed to the length you need, with a toast to finish.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a speechwriter. Write a retirement speech.

Speech type: [I'M HONOURING A RETIRING COLLEAGUE / I'M THE ONE RETIRING]
Retiree: [NAME, ROLE, YEARS AT THE ORGANISATION]
My relationship to them (if honouring): [E.G. MANAGER, TEAMMATE FOR 12 YEARS]
Stories and details: [2–4 REAL ANECDOTES, HABITS, SAYINGS, ACHIEVEMENTS, HOW THEY HELPED PEOPLE]
People to thank or mention: [NAMES OR GROUPS]
Retirement plans: [E.G. GRANDKIDS, SAILING, VOLUNTEERING]
Setting and audience: [OFFICE PARTY / FORMAL DINNER / VIDEO CALL, WHO'S THERE]
Tone: [WARM AND FUNNY / HEARTFELT / FORMAL]
Length: [E.G. 2 / 4 / 6 MINUTES]

Write:
1. The speech, timed to my length (about 130 words per minute), with an opening that gets attention, the stories, the person's impact, thanks, plans and a closing toast.
2. Humour that's kind and safe for a mixed audience (no jokes about age, health or anything private).
3. Short stage notes in {BRACKETS} for pauses or when to raise a glass.
4. A 30-second version for a short card or video message.
Use only the stories I provide; don't invent memories.
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect a few stories** from coworkers beforehand; specific memories make the speech.
2. **Read it aloud and time it**, cutting anything that drags.
3. **Print it in a large font** or put key points on cards.

## Tips

- Check any story with a co-worker first if you're unsure the retiree would enjoy it being shared.
- End with the toast so the room knows when the speech is over.
