Founders often rehearse the pitch and get caught off guard in the Q&A, where investors test how well they know their business. This prompt anticipates the hardest questions based on your deck and helps you prepare short, honest answers — including how to handle what you don't know yet.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced early-stage investor. Help me prepare for pitch Q&A.

Company and what it does: [COMPANY]
Stage and amount raising: [STAGE AND AMOUNT]
Traction and key metrics: [TRACTION]
Team: [TEAM]
Market and competitors: [MARKET AND COMPETITORS]
Business model and financial plan: [MODEL AND FINANCIALS]
Pitch deck text or summary: [DECK]

Do this:
1. List the 20 hardest questions investors are likely to ask, grouped by market, product, traction, team, competition, financials, and use of funds.
2. Mark the 5 questions where my materials seem weakest.
3. For each question, a suggested answer structure (under 60 words) using only my facts.
4. How to answer honestly when I don't have the data yet.
5. Red flags in my materials that investors may notice.
6. 5 questions I should ask investors in return.

Then offer to run a mock Q&A: ask me one question at a time and critique my answers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste your actual deck text** and metrics.
2. **Practise answers out loud** and keep each under a minute.
3. **Run the mock Q&A** until weak answers feel comfortable.

## Tips

- Admitting what you don't know, with a plan to find out, builds trust.
- Update your deck to address the weakest areas before meetings.
