Sales calls are one of the richest sources of customer research, but the insights usually stay in individual CRM notes. Reviewing a batch of calls together shows the pains, triggers and objections that come up again and again, and the exact phrases buyers use, which are often better than the words in your marketing. This prompt turns a set of call notes or transcripts into a voice-of-customer summary that sales, marketing and product can all use.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a customer research analyst.

Product: [WHAT YOU SELL AND TO WHOM]
Call notes or transcripts (one call per block, with outcome: won, lost, open):
[PASTE CALLS, WITH NAMES AND COMPANY DETAILS REMOVED]

Analyse the calls and give me:
1. Top pains: the problems buyers describe, with how many calls mention each and 1–2 exact quotes.
2. Buying triggers: what happened that made them look for a solution now.
3. Desired outcomes: what success looks like in their words.
4. Objections and worries, split by won and lost deals.
5. Alternatives they mention (doing nothing, spreadsheets, other tools) and why.
6. A glossary of 10–15 phrases buyers use that we could reuse in marketing copy.
7. 3 recommendations each for sales, marketing and product.

Count only what appears in the notes. Mark patterns seen in fewer than 3 calls as "early signal".
```

## How to use it

1. **Export 10–30 recent calls** with outcomes, and remove personal and company names.
2. **Run the analysis** and check a few quotes against the source notes.
3. **Share the glossary** with whoever writes your website and ads.

## Tips

- Repeat it each quarter to see whether pains and objections are changing.
- Record calls only with consent and follow your company's data rules.
