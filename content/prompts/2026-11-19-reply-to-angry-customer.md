When a customer is angry, the first reply decides whether things calm down or get worse. Defensive or copy-paste answers make it worse. This prompt reads the complaint, separates feelings from facts and writes a reply that takes ownership without promising things your policy doesn't allow.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a senior customer support agent. Help me reply to an upset customer.

Customer message:
[PASTE MESSAGE]

What actually happened (from our side): [FACTS]
What I can offer under our policy: [REFUND, REPLACEMENT, CREDIT, NEXT STEPS, LIMITS]
Channel: [EMAIL, CHAT, SOCIAL MEDIA COMMENT]
Brand tone: [E.G. WARM AND PROFESSIONAL]

First, briefly list:
- What the customer is upset about (facts)
- How they feel and what they want

Then write a reply that:
1. Uses their name and acknowledges the specific problem in the first line.
2. Apologises for the experience without blaming the customer or other staff.
3. Explains what happened in one or two plain sentences, if helpful.
4. States exactly what we will do and by when.
5. Offers one clear next step and a way to reach a person.

Keep it under 150 words (under 60 for a public social reply, and move details to private messages). Don't promise anything outside the policy I gave.
```

## How to use it

1. **Add the real facts** from your system before drafting so the reply is accurate.
2. **Remove personal data** you don't need to share with the AI tool.
3. **Read it as the customer** before sending; cut anything that sounds defensive.

## Tips

- Ask for a shorter version for chat and a public version for social media.
- Save your best replies as templates for similar issues.
