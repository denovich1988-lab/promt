Decluttering stalls when the job feels too big: you empty a closet onto the bed, run out of energy and stuff everything back. A plan that breaks the home into small sessions, starts with easy wins and sets clear rules for what stays makes it far more likely you'll finish. This decluttering plan prompt builds a schedule around your rooms, time and energy, with decision rules and a way to get items out of the house quickly.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a practical professional organiser. Build a decluttering plan for my home.

Home: [SIZE, ROOMS, WHO LIVES THERE]
Problem areas: [E.G. GARAGE, KIDS' TOYS, KITCHEN COUNTERS, WARDROBE, PAPERWORK]
Time available: [E.G. 20 MINUTES ON WEEKDAYS, 2 HOURS ON SATURDAYS]
Deadline or reason: [E.G. MOVING IN MARCH, HOSTING FOR THE HOLIDAYS, JUST OVERWHELMED]
Hard parts for me: [E.G. SENTIMENTAL ITEMS, "MIGHT NEED IT" ITEMS, GIFTS, OTHER PEOPLE'S STUFF]
Ways to get rid of things nearby: [DONATION CENTRES, RESALE APPS, RECYCLING, BULK PICKUP]

Give me:
1. An order of rooms and zones from easiest to hardest, with reasons.
2. A day-by-day schedule for the next [2–6] weeks, with each session as one small zone (e.g. "one kitchen drawer", "shoes only").
3. Clear decision rules: keep, donate, sell, recycle, bin, with a "maybe box" rule and a date to decide.
4. A gentle approach for sentimental items and for things belonging to other household members.
5. A plan to get items out fast (donation run day, a limit on how long sell items wait).
6. Simple systems to keep each cleared area tidy, plus a "one in, one out" habit.
7. A printable checklist.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with the first easy zone today**; early wins build momentum.
2. **Put donation bags in the car immediately** so they don't drift back inside.
3. **Leave sentimental items until the end**, when deciding feels easier.

## Tips

- Set a timer for each session; stopping while you still have energy makes the next one easier.
- Give "sell" items a deadline (e.g. two weeks), then donate whatever hasn't sold.
