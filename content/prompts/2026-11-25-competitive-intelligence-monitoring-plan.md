Most teams check competitors only when a deal is lost or a rival launches something big. A light monitoring routine, with a few signals, clear sources and a short monthly summary, catches changes earlier without becoming a full-time job. This prompt designs that routine around your competitors and the decisions the information should support.

## Prompt

```prompt
Design a competitive intelligence monitoring plan.

Our company and market: [DESCRIPTION]
Main competitors (3–6): [LIST]
Decisions this should inform: [e.g. pricing, roadmap, sales battlecards, messaging]
Team and time available: [e.g. 1 marketer, 2 hours a week]
Tools we have: [e.g. Google Alerts, a CRM, review sites access]

1. Signals to track for each decision (pricing changes, new features, hiring, messaging, reviews, partnerships, content topics, ad activity) and why each matters.
2. Sources for each signal — public and ethical only (websites, changelogs, job posts, review sites, social profiles, earnings or press releases, sales call notes).
3. A cadence table: signal, source, frequency, owner, where it's logged.
4. A one-page monthly summary template: top changes, what it means for us, recommended actions, open questions.
5. How to gather win/loss input from sales in 5 minutes per deal.

No deceptive tactics (fake sign-ups posing as customers, scraping that breaks terms, contacting competitor employees for secrets).
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with the decisions** — track only signals that change them.
2. **Assign owners** and put the routine in calendars.
3. **Share the monthly summary** with sales, product and leadership.

## Tips

- Set alerts for competitor names plus "pricing", "launches" and "hiring".
- Review the plan every quarter and drop signals nobody uses.
