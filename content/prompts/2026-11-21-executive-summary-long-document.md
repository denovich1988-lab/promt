Busy readers decide from the first page whether to read further, approve or ignore. An executive summary should give them the conclusion, why it matters and what is being asked — not a table of contents in prose. This prompt condenses your document into that format and checks that every claim comes from the source.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced business writer. Write an executive summary of the document below.

Who will read it and what they need to decide: [READERS AND DECISION]
Maximum length: [LENGTH, E.G. 300 WORDS OR ONE PAGE]
Document:
[PASTE DOCUMENT]

Write:
1. A one-sentence bottom line: the main conclusion or recommendation.
2. Context: why this matters now, in 2–3 sentences.
3. Key findings: 3–5 bullets, each with the most important supporting figure from the document.
4. Recommendation or ask: what the reader should decide or do, by when, and the cost or resources if stated.
5. Risks and open questions in 2–3 bullets.
6. Next steps.

Lead with conclusions, not background. Use only facts and numbers from the document and keep figures exactly as written. After the summary, list anything important you left out so I can decide if it should go back in.
```

## How to use it

1. **Name the reader and the decision** — the summary changes a lot depending on both.
2. **Paste the full document** (or its main sections if it is very long).
3. **Check every figure** against the original before sending.

## Tips

- If you can't write the bottom line in one sentence, the document may need a clearer conclusion.
- Put the summary on the first page, not in an appendix.
