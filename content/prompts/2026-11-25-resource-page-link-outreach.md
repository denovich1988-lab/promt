Resource pages, such as curated lists of tools, guides or local services, exist to link out, which makes them some of the easiest link building targets. The catch is that editors ignore generic pitches. This prompt checks whether your page truly fits a given list, finds search queries to discover more such pages, and writes a short, specific email that makes adding your link an easy decision.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me with resource page link building.

My page (URL, title and what it offers): [YOUR PAGE]
Why it's useful and to whom: [VALUE]
Topic and niche: [NICHE]
Target resource page (URL, title, sections and a few links it lists): [PASTE TARGET PAGE DETAILS]
Editor name if known: [NAME OR "unknown"]

1. Fit check: does my page belong on this list? Which section? Score fit 1–5 and explain. If it's below 3, say so and stop.
2. 10 search queries to find more resource pages in my niche (using operators like intitle:, inurl: and quotes).
3. An outreach email under 110 words: mention the specific section, one sentence on what my page adds that the current links don't, a suggested anchor text, and no pressure.
4. One follow-up under 50 words.
5. 2 subject lines.

Don't flatter or invent facts about their site. Don't offer payment for links.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste real details** from the target page so the email names the right section.
2. **Skip low-fit targets** — a weak fit wastes the editor's time and yours.
3. **Track replies** by search query to see which ones find the best pages.

## Tips

- Pages that link to dead resources are great targets; offer yours as a replacement.
- Make sure your page is free to access; many resource pages avoid paywalled links.
