Renewals are easier to win than new deals, yet many go out as a one-line invoice reminder that invites the customer to rethink the purchase. A good renewal email reminds them of the value they've had, explains any changes in price or terms clearly and makes the next step simple. This contract renewal reminder email prompt writes a short sequence for subscriptions, service contracts and annual plans.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an account manager. Write a renewal reminder sequence.

Product/service: [WHAT THE CUSTOMER BUYS]
Customer: [COMPANY/NAME, CONTACT'S ROLE, HOW LONG THEY'VE BEEN A CUSTOMER]
Renewal date and term: [DATE, ANNUAL/MONTHLY, AUTO-RENEW OR MANUAL]
Value delivered this term: [USAGE, RESULTS, SUPPORT, NEW FEATURES THEY GOT]
What changes at renewal: [PRICE CHANGE, NEW TERMS, PLAN CHANGES, OR "NO CHANGE"]
Options: [E.G. MULTI-YEAR DISCOUNT, UPGRADE, DOWNGRADE]
Next step: [SIGN, PAY INVOICE, BOOK REVIEW CALL]

Write:
1. 60-day email: a value recap with 2–3 specifics, renewal date, any changes explained plainly, and an offer of a short review call.
2. 30-day email: the options available and the simplest way to renew.
3. 7-day email: short reminder with the date, amount and link placeholder.
4. A version for a customer who seems at risk (low usage), focusing on a call to fix problems first.
5. A reply for "can you give us a discount?" that protects value but offers a reasonable option.

Tone: helpful and professional. Under 150 words each. No pressure tactics or vague threats of service loss.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pull real usage or results** for the value recap; generic praise doesn't help.
2. **Explain price changes up front**; surprises at invoice time cause churn.
3. **Book the review call** for key accounts rather than relying on email.

## Tips

- Check auto-renewal notice rules in your contracts and local law.
- Note what each customer valued most in your CRM for next year's renewal.
