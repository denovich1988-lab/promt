Getting into independent shops and boutiques is often the next step for makers and small brands, but shop buyers get many pitches and decide fast. They want to know whether your product fits their customers, what the margin is and how easy ordering will be. This prompt writes a wholesale pitch around those questions: a short email, a line sheet summary, clear terms and polite follow-ups, without overselling.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a wholesale sales advisor for a small product brand.

Brand and products: [WHAT YOU MAKE, NUMBER OF PRODUCTS]
Wholesale and suggested retail prices: [PRICES]
Minimum order and reorder minimum: [AMOUNTS]
Lead time and shipping: [DAYS, WHO PAYS SHIPPING]
Payment terms: [E.G. PREPAID, NET 30 FOR REPEAT ORDERS]
Proof you can show: [SALES SO FAR, SHOPS THAT STOCK YOU, REVIEWS, PRESS; OR "NONE YET"]
Target shop: [SHOP NAME, TYPE, WHAT THEY SELL, WHY YOU FIT]

Write:
1. An intro email to the buyer (under 150 words): why this shop specifically, the product in one line, margin in plain terms, minimum order, and an easy next step (samples or a short call).
2. A line sheet summary: product, SKU, wholesale price, retail price, case pack, and a one-line selling point for each.
3. A short terms section: minimums, lead times, payment, returns or damaged goods policy.
4. Two follow-ups (after 1 week and 3 weeks), each under 70 words.
5. A reply template for when the buyer says "not right now".
6. 5 questions to ask a buyer on a first call.

No invented sales figures or stockists.
```

## How to use it

1. **Research each shop** and write a real reason why your product fits.
2. **Attach or link your line sheet** and good product photos.
3. **Offer samples** to shops that reply with interest.

## Tips

- Check that your wholesale price leaves you a profit after shipping and fees.
- Ask buyers when they plan seasonal orders; many buy months ahead.
