A customer satisfaction (CSAT) survey is only useful if people answer it and the answers point to something you can fix. Long surveys and vague questions get low response rates and noisy data. This CSAT survey prompt writes short, clear questions for a specific moment in the customer journey, plus the follow-up logic, send timing and a simple way to turn results into improvements.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a customer experience specialist.

Company and product: [E.G. ONLINE FURNITURE STORE / B2B SOFTWARE / DENTAL CLINIC]
Moment to survey: [E.G. AFTER A SUPPORT TICKET IS CLOSED / AFTER DELIVERY / AFTER ONBOARDING / AFTER A VISIT]
Channel: [EMAIL / IN-APP / SMS / CHAT WIDGET]
What we want to learn: [E.G. WHETHER AGENTS SOLVED THE ISSUE, WHAT SLOWS DELIVERY]
Current response rate (if known): [NUMBER]
Tone of our brand: [E.G. FRIENDLY AND CASUAL]

Create:
1. The main CSAT question with a recommended scale (e.g. 1–5) and labels for each point.
2. Two follow-up questions that change depending on a low or high score.
3. One optional open-text question that invites specific answers.
4. Three short versions of the survey invitation (email subject + body, SMS, in-app).
5. Timing and frequency rules so the same customer isn't asked too often.
6. How to calculate the CSAT score and what to segment it by (agent, channel, issue type, product).
7. A closed-loop process: who follows up on low scores, within what time, and a reply template.
8. Five common mistakes in CSAT surveys to avoid.

Keep the survey under one minute to complete.
```

## How to use it

1. **Choose one moment** to survey first instead of surveying everything at once.
2. **Test the survey on a few colleagues** and time how long it takes.
3. **Review low scores weekly** and follow up with those customers.

## Tips

- Ask about one interaction at a time; mixing product and support questions blurs the result.
- Tell customers what you changed because of their feedback; it lifts future response rates.
