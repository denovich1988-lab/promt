Listings full of "stunning" and "must see" all sound the same. Buyers respond to specific details: morning light in the kitchen, a five-minute walk to the station, storage in the hallway. This prompt turns your notes into a clear, honest description and avoids wording that could cause fair-housing problems.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a real estate copywriter. Write a property listing.

Property type and location: [TYPE, AREA, CITY]
Price and status: [PRICE, FOR SALE / FOR RENT]
Key facts: [BEDROOMS, BATHROOMS, SIZE, FLOOR, PARKING, YEAR BUILT]
Best features (my notes from visiting): [FEATURES]
Recent upgrades: [UPGRADES]
Neighbourhood: [TRANSPORT, SCHOOLS, SHOPS, PARKS WITH WALKING TIMES]
Ideal buyer or tenant (lifestyle, not personal characteristics): [E.G. SOMEONE WHO WORKS FROM HOME]

Write:
1. A headline under 70 characters with the strongest specific feature.
2. A main description of 150–220 words: opening hook, flow through the home, neighbourhood, call to view.
3. A bullet list of key facts.
4. A 50-word version for social media.

Rules: only use the facts I gave; no exaggeration; avoid words like "stunning", "nestled" and "must see". Describe the property, not the type of people who should live there, to comply with fair-housing rules.
```

## How to use it

1. **Walk the property and note specific details:** light, views, storage, noise, recent work.
2. **Add walking times** to real places; they're more useful than "close to amenities".
3. **Check every fact** against the property details before publishing.

## Tips

- Ask for versions for different portals with their character limits.
- Pair the description with photos in the same room order.
