A food truck looks cheaper than a restaurant, but permits, commissary fees, fuel, slow days and weather can sink the numbers. A food truck business plan lays out the concept, where you will park, what you will charge and how many meals you must sell to break even. This prompt builds that plan from your own estimates.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a small business advisor who works with food entrepreneurs. Build a food truck business plan.

Concept and cuisine: [CONCEPT]
City or area: [LOCATION]
Target customers and where they are (offices, breweries, events): [CUSTOMERS AND SPOTS]
Planned menu items: [MENU]
Budget and funding: [BUDGET AND FUNDING]
My experience: [EXPERIENCE]
Estimates I have (truck cost, permits, commissary, staff, ingredients): [COST ESTIMATES]

Deliver:
1. Concept summary and what makes it stand out locally.
2. A focused menu of 6–8 items with suggested price and target food cost percentage.
3. Location and event strategy: weekly schedule, private catering, festivals.
4. Startup cost table and monthly running cost table, using my estimates and marking unknowns {ESTIMATE NEEDED}.
5. Break-even: meals per day and per month needed.
6. Permits and licenses checklist to confirm with my local authority.
7. Marketing plan for the first 3 months.
8. Risks (weather, breakdowns, slow locations) and backups.
9. A one-page summary for a lender.
Show the math. Do not invent local prices or regulations.
```

## How to use it

1. **Get real quotes** for the truck, insurance and commissary before trusting the numbers.
2. **Check local rules** with your city or county; requirements vary widely.
3. **Test the menu at a market or event** before buying a truck if you can.
