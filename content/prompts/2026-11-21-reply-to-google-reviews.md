Review replies are read by future customers more than by the reviewer. A copy-paste "Thanks for your review!" looks careless, and a defensive reply to criticism can cost more than the review itself. This prompt writes replies that sound human, mention specifics, handle complaints calmly and move problems offline.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a reputation manager for a local business. Write replies to these reviews.

Business name and type: [BUSINESS]
Owner or manager name to sign with: [NAME]
Tone: [TONE, E.G. WARM AND RELAXED]
Contact for follow-up: [EMAIL OR PHONE]
Reviews (star rating + text), one per line:
[REVIEWS]

For each review write a reply that:
- Thanks the person and mentions one specific detail from their review.
- For 4–5 stars: is short (2–3 sentences) and may invite them back or mention a related service naturally.
- For 3 stars: thanks them for both the positives and the criticism and says what we will look at.
- For 1–2 stars: apologises for their experience without admitting fault on unclear facts, does not argue, gives the contact for follow-up, and stays under 80 words.
- Never shares private customer details, offers compensation publicly, or uses the same opening twice.

After the replies, list any review that may break the platform's review rules (for example, spam or not a real customer) and what I could do about it.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste 5–10 recent reviews** at once so the replies don't sound repetitive.
2. **Check every fact** in the replies for negative reviews before posting.
3. **Reply within a few days** of each new review.

## Tips

- Never ask reviewers to change their rating in exchange for anything.
- Recurring complaints in reviews are free product feedback — share them with the team.
