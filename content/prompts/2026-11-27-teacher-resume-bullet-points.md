Teacher resumes often list duties ("planned lessons, graded papers") that every applicant shares, so hiring committees can't see what makes you different. A strong teacher resume shows results: growth in student learning, programs you started, how you support different learners. This prompt pulls those details out of your notes and matches them to the job posting, whether you are a new graduate or an experienced teacher changing schools.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a resume writer who specialises in education jobs.

Job posting I'm applying for: [PASTE THE POSTING OR KEY REQUIREMENTS]
My level: [STUDENT TEACHER / NEW TEACHER / EXPERIENCED / CAREER CHANGER INTO TEACHING]
Subjects and grade levels: [E.G. 7TH GRADE MATH, K–2 READING, HIGH SCHOOL CHEMISTRY]
Certifications and licences: [E.G. STATE CERTIFICATION, ESL ENDORSEMENT, SPECIAL EDUCATION]
Experience (rough notes are fine): [SCHOOL, YEARS, WHAT YOU DID, ANY RESULTS]
Extras: [E.G. COACHED SOCCER, RAN AFTER-SCHOOL CLUB, LED PD SESSION, CURRICULUM WRITING]
Tools and methods: [E.G. GOOGLE CLASSROOM, DIFFERENTIATED INSTRUCTION, RESTORATIVE PRACTICES, IEP COLLABORATION]

Please:
1. Ask me up to 5 questions to find measurable results (test score growth, reading levels, attendance, participation, parent engagement).
2. Write a 3-line professional summary tailored to this posting.
3. Rewrite my experience into 4–6 bullets per role, each starting with an action verb and showing impact.
4. Create a skills section and a certifications section.
5. List keywords from the posting I haven't used yet, and where they could honestly fit.
6. Suggest a one-page layout and section order for my level.

Only use results I give you. If I don't have numbers, write concrete descriptions instead of inventing figures.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the full job posting** so the summary and keywords match that school or district.
2. **Answer the questions with real figures** from assessments or records where you can.
3. **Make a copy for each application** and adjust the summary and top bullets.

## Tips

- Student privacy matters: describe results in totals or percentages, never with student names.
- Put certifications near the top if the posting lists them as required.
