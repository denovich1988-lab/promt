The first five minutes of class set the tone. A short bell ringer gives students something to do the moment they sit down, reviews yesterday's learning and frees you to take attendance. Coming up with a fresh one every day is surprisingly draining. This bell ringer activities prompt creates a week of warm-ups for your subject and grade, mixing formats so students stay engaged, with answers and a link to each day's lesson.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced classroom teacher. Create bell ringer warm-up activities.

Subject and grade: [E.G. 7TH GRADE SCIENCE, 10TH GRADE ENGLISH, ALGEBRA 1]
This week's lessons (one line per day): [MONDAY: …, TUESDAY: …, ETC.]
Last week's content to review: [TOPICS]
Students: [READING LEVELS, ELL STUDENTS, ANY NEEDS]
Time per bell ringer: [E.G. 5 MINUTES]
Format: [PROJECTED SLIDE / PAPER / DIGITAL]

For each day, Monday to Friday:
1. A bell ringer students can start without instructions from me, using a different format each day (e.g. quick retrieval questions, "which one doesn't belong", error analysis, a picture or quote prompt, a two-minute quick write, a puzzle).
2. The expected answer or what a good response looks like.
3. A one-line link to that day's lesson.
4. A support version for struggling readers or ELL students and an extension question for fast finishers.

Also give 3 "emergency" bell ringers that work any day.
Keep each one doable in the time set, with clear student-facing instructions.
```

## How to use it

1. **List the week's lessons** so each warm-up leads into the day's topic.
2. **Project or post it before students enter** so the routine starts right away.
3. **Go over answers in 1–2 minutes**, then move into the lesson.

## Tips

- Keep the format predictable on some days; routine helps classroom management.
- Use bell ringer answers as quick checks to see who needs reteaching.
