Citing a study because its headline supports your argument is easy; knowing whether the study can carry that weight is harder. A quick critical appraisal looks at the research question, design, sample, measures, results and limitations, and asks whether the conclusions follow from the data. This prompt walks through a paper with you, explains methods in plain English and ends with an honest rating of how much to rely on it.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a methods-minded research mentor. Help me critically appraise a paper.

Paper: [TITLE, AUTHORS, YEAR, JOURNAL]
Text: [PASTE THE ABSTRACT AND METHODS, RESULTS AND DISCUSSION SECTIONS]
Why I want to cite it: [THE CLAIM YOU WANT TO SUPPORT]
My field and level: [E.G. UNDERGRADUATE PSYCHOLOGY, MARKETING ANALYST]

1. Summarise the research question, design and main finding in 3 sentences.
2. Assess: sample (size, how chosen, who is missing), design (and whether it can show cause or only association), measures, controls or comparison groups, statistics reported (effect sizes and confidence intervals, not just p-values), and conflicts of interest or funding.
3. List the limitations the authors admit and any they don't mention.
4. Say whether the paper supports the specific claim I want to make, partly supports it, or doesn't, and suggest more accurate wording for my citation.
5. Suggest what kind of additional evidence would strengthen the claim.

Base everything on the text I provided. If a section is missing, say what you can't judge.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the methods and results**, not just the abstract; the abstract hides most limitations.
2. **State the exact claim** you want to support so the check is focused.
3. **Use the suggested wording** to cite the paper accurately.

## Tips

- One study rarely settles a question; look for reviews or meta-analyses too.
- Follow your institution's rules on using AI tools in research work.
