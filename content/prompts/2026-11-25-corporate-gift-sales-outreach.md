Companies buy client and employee gifts every December, but the people who order them (HR, office managers, executive assistants) are busy and decide late. A good corporate gift pitch makes buying easy: clear price tiers, a deadline, branding options and a reply that takes one line. This prompt writes a short outreach sequence for your gift product or service, built around those buyer needs instead of a product brochure.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a B2B sales copywriter for corporate gifting.

What I sell: [YOUR GIFT PRODUCT, BOX OR SERVICE]
Price tiers: [E.G. 3 TIERS WITH PRICE PER GIFT]
Minimum order and branding options: [MINIMUMS, LOGO, CARDS, PACKAGING]
Order deadline for delivery before the holidays: [DATE]
Delivery: [ONE ADDRESS / INDIVIDUAL ADDRESSES / REGIONS]
Target buyer: [E.G. HR MANAGERS AT 50–500 PERSON COMPANIES IN CITY]
Proof I can mention honestly: [PAST CLIENTS WITH PERMISSION, REVIEWS, OR NONE]

Write a 3-email sequence:
1. First email (under 120 words): the problem the buyer has in December, one sentence on the offer, the tiers in 3 short lines, the order deadline, and an easy reply call to action ("Reply with a headcount and budget and I'll send options").
2. Follow-up 4 days later (under 80 words): one new angle, such as employee gifts vs client gifts or remote teams.
3. Last note 1 week before the deadline (under 60 words): deadline reminder only.

Also write: 5 subject lines, a 2-line LinkedIn message version, and a short reply template for when someone asks for a quote.
No fake urgency, no invented clients or numbers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in real prices, minimums and the order deadline**; buyers decide on these.
2. **Send to a short list first** and adjust the opening line if replies are low.
3. **Use the quote reply template** to answer within the same day.

## Tips

- Personalise the first line with something true about the company, such as a recent office opening.
- Follow local email marketing rules and include an opt-out line.
