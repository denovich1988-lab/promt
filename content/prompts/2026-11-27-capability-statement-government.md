A capability statement is the one-page résumé a business hands to government buyers and prime contractors at meetings, events and in response to sources-sought notices. Buyers scan it in seconds, so it has to show quickly what you do, why you're different, who you've done it for, and the codes and registrations they need. This capability statement prompt writes a tight, scannable page tailored to a specific agency or buyer.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a government contracting proposal writer. Write a one-page capability statement.

Company: [NAME, LOCATION, YEAR FOUNDED, SIZE]
Target agency or buyer: [AGENCY, OFFICE OR PRIME CONTRACTOR]
What they buy that we provide: [SERVICES OR PRODUCTS RELEVANT TO THEM]
Core competencies: [3–6 SERVICES OR CAPABILITIES]
Differentiators: [E.G. CLEARED STAFF, PATENTED PROCESS, RESPONSE TIME, CERTIFICATIONS]
Past performance: [CLIENTS, PROJECTS, CONTRACT VALUES, RESULTS — ONLY REAL ONES]
Company data: [UEI, CAGE CODE, NAICS CODES, PSC CODES, SET-ASIDE STATUSES, CONTRACT VEHICLES]
Contact: [NAME, TITLE, PHONE, EMAIL, WEBSITE]

Write:
1. A headline and 2–3 sentence company overview tailored to the target buyer's mission.
2. Core competencies as short, specific bullets (with keywords the buyer uses).
3. Differentiators: 3–5 bullets with proof, not adjectives.
4. Past performance: 3 entries (client, scope, result).
5. Company data block formatted for quick scanning.
6. Layout guidance for one page: section order, where to put the logo and contact details.
7. A 3-sentence version for an email introduction to a small business liaison or prime contractor.
Keep it to one page. Flag any claim that needs proof before use.
```

## How to use it

1. **Make one version per target agency**; matching their mission and terms matters more than design.
2. **Double-check every code and registration** against your official records.
3. **Export as a PDF** and bring printed copies to industry days and matchmaking events.

## Tips

- Lead with what the buyer needs, not your company history.
- Registration systems and set-aside rules change; confirm current requirements on official procurement sites.
