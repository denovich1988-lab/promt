Many freelancers set their rate by copying others or guessing, then discover that unpaid admin time, taxes and holidays leave them earning far less than planned. This prompt works backwards from what you need to earn to a freelance hourly rate, then turns it into project and retainer prices you can quote with confidence.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a financial coach for freelancers. Help me calculate my rates.

What I do and for whom: [SERVICE AND CLIENTS]
Country or region: [LOCATION]
Take-home income I want per year: [INCOME GOAL]
Yearly business costs (software, equipment, insurance, coworking): [COSTS]
Estimated tax rate I set aside: [TAX RATE]
Weeks off per year (holidays, sick days): [WEEKS OFF]
Hours I can work per week and share that is billable: [HOURS AND BILLABLE SHARE]
Typical project and how long it takes me: [TYPICAL PROJECT]

Show:
1. The calculation step by step: required revenue, billable hours per year, minimum hourly rate.
2. A recommended rate with a buffer, and why.
3. A project price for my typical project, with scope assumptions.
4. A monthly retainer option and what it should include.
5. A table comparing income at 50%, 70% and 90% of planned billable hours.
6. How to present the price to a client in 2–3 sentences.
Show the math clearly. Note that tax figures are my estimate and I should confirm them with an accountant.
```

## How to use it

1. **Be honest about billable hours**; most freelancers bill well under their total working time.
2. **Check the low-utilization row** to see whether you can survive a slow month.
3. **Review rates every six months** or when demand outgrows your time.
