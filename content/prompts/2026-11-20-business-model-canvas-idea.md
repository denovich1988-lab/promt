An idea in your head always looks complete. Putting it on a business model canvas shows the gaps: who exactly pays, how they find you and what it costs to serve them. This prompt fills in the nine blocks from your description and then points out which assumptions could sink the idea, with low-cost tests for each.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a startup advisor. Build a business model canvas for my idea.

Idea in a few sentences: [IDEA]
Who I think the customer is: [CUSTOMER]
How I plan to make money: [REVENUE IDEA]
What I already have (skills, assets, contacts, budget): [RESOURCES]

Do this:
1. Fill in all 9 blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partners, cost structure. Be specific; 2–4 bullets per block.
2. Mark every bullet that is an assumption rather than a fact with (A).
3. List the 5 riskiest assumptions, ranked by how badly the business fails if they are wrong.
4. For each, suggest one test I can run in under 2 weeks for under [BUDGET], with what result would count as a pass.
5. Note any block where my input is too vague and ask me one question about it.

Don't invent market data. Where numbers are needed, show them as ranges I must verify.
```

## How to use it

1. **Describe the idea plainly**, including what you're unsure about.
2. **Answer the follow-up questions** and ask for an updated canvas.
3. **Run the first test** before building anything big.

## Tips

- Ask for a second canvas for a different customer segment and compare.
- Save the canvas and update it after every test.
