Passive candidates get many recruiter messages, and most read the same: a long job description and a request for a call. The ones that get replies are short, specific about why this person, and clear about the role's real selling points. This recruiter InMail prompt writes a first message and two follow-ups personalised from a candidate's profile, in a few variations you can test.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced technical and corporate recruiter. Write LinkedIn InMail messages for this role and candidate.

Role: [JOB TITLE, LEVEL, TEAM]
Company: [COMPANY, WHAT IT DOES, STAGE OR SIZE]
Location / remote: [DETAILS]
Salary range (if shareable): [RANGE]
Real selling points: [E.G. 4-DAY WEEK, NEW PRODUCT LINE, STRONG MANAGER, LEARNING BUDGET]
Candidate profile highlights: [CURRENT ROLE, SKILLS, PROJECTS, POSTS, TENURE]
Candidate type: [PASSIVE / OPEN TO WORK]
My name and role: [RECRUITER NAME, IN-HOUSE OR AGENCY]

Write:
1. Three first messages, each under 90 words, with different angles (their specific work, career step, team/mission). Each needs a subject line, one sentence on why them, two lines on the role, and a low-pressure ask.
2. Follow-up 1 (after 4–5 days) adding one new detail, under 60 words.
3. Follow-up 2 (after another week), a polite close that leaves the door open, under 50 words.
4. A reply for when the candidate says "not now".
5. A reply for when they ask about salary first.
Avoid buzzwords, flattery and phrases like "exciting opportunity". Don't invent facts about the candidate.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste 2–3 real details** from the profile; generic praise is what candidates ignore.
2. **Test the three angles** across similar candidates and keep the one with the best reply rate.
3. **Log replies** so you can reuse what worked for the next search.

## Tips

- Sharing the salary range early usually saves both sides time.
- Check your platform's current InMail limits and credits before planning a large campaign.
