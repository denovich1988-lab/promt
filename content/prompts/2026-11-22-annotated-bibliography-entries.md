An annotated bibliography asks you to summarize each source, judge its value and explain how it fits your project. This ChatGPT prompt helps you draft annotated bibliography entries from the sources and notes you provide, formats citations in the style you need, and never adds sources you didn't give it, a common problem when AI is asked to "find" references.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an academic writing tutor. Help me draft an annotated bibliography from my own sources.

Research topic or question: [TOPIC]
Citation style: [APA 7 / MLA 9 / CHICAGO]
Annotation type: [DESCRIPTIVE / EVALUATIVE / CRITICAL]
Length per annotation: [WORDS]
Sources, each with full citation details and my notes or the abstract: [SOURCES AND NOTES]

For each source:
1. The citation formatted in the chosen style (mark any missing detail like {PAGE RANGE} or {DOI} instead of guessing).
2. An annotation: the main argument or findings, the method or evidence, the author's credibility or perspective, strengths and limitations, and how it relates to my topic.
3. One sentence on how I might use it in my paper.
Then: group the sources by theme and point out gaps in my research.
Use only the sources and information I provide. Do not add new sources or invent details.
```

## How to use it

1. **Read each source yourself first** and add your own notes; the annotation should reflect your understanding.
2. **Check every citation** against the source and your style guide.
3. **Follow your instructor's rules on AI use** and rewrite the annotations in your own words.

## Tips

- Ask a librarian or use your library database to find sources; don't trust AI-suggested references without checking.
- Compare sources side by side with the [literature review matrix prompt](/prompts/literature-review-matrix/).
- Pull out the main points of a paper with the [research paper key findings prompt](/prompts/research-paper-key-findings/).
