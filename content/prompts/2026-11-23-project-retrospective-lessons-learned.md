Teams finish a project, move on and repeat the same mistakes next time. A short, blameless retrospective fixes that. This prompt organizes feedback and project facts into what worked, what didn't and why, then writes a lessons learned report with a few concrete changes, each with an owner.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a facilitator for a blameless project retrospective.

Project, goal and outcome: [PROJECT AND OUTCOME]
Timeline with key events: [TIMELINE]
Planned vs actual (scope, budget, deadline): [PLANNED VS ACTUAL]
Team feedback (paste survey answers or notes): [FEEDBACK]

Do this:
1. Group the feedback into "went well", "didn't go well" and "puzzles".
2. For the top 3 problems, find likely root causes (ask "why" until you reach a process cause, not a person).
3. List what we should keep doing, start doing and stop doing.
4. Propose 3–5 specific actions with an owner role and a due date.
5. Write a one-page lessons learned report for people who weren't on the project.
6. Suggest an agenda for a 60-minute retro meeting to discuss this draft.
Language must be blameless: describe what happened and what the system allowed, not who failed.
```

## How to use it

1. **Collect anonymous feedback** before the meeting with three questions: what worked, what didn't, what surprised you.
2. **Discuss the draft** in the retro and adjust the root causes.
3. **Store the report** where the next project team will find it.
