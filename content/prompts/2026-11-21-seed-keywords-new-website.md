A new website has no rankings and no data to learn from, so many owners guess which keywords to target. Seed keywords are the short, core phrases you expand later in a keyword tool. This prompt brainstorms them from your offer, customer problems and buying stages, then maps them to the first pages you should build.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an SEO strategist helping me plan a brand-new website.

What we offer: [PRODUCTS OR SERVICES]
Who buys and what problems they have: [CUSTOMERS AND PROBLEMS]
Location or market (if local): [LOCATION OR MARKET]
Competitor or similar websites: [COMPETITOR SITES]

Create:
1. 40 seed keywords (2–4 words each), grouped into: problem-aware (searching for a fix), solution-aware (comparing options), product/brand-aware (ready to buy), and local or niche variants.
2. For each group, the search intent in one line and the type of page that fits it (blog guide, service page, comparison page, product page, FAQ).
3. A list of 10 pages I should create first, each with its main seed keyword and 2–3 related phrases. Put money pages first.
4. Phrases to avoid because they are too broad or attract the wrong audience.
5. Instructions for checking these seeds in a keyword tool: what to look at and when to drop a keyword.

Do not invent search volumes or difficulty scores. Mark any keyword you think needs checking.
```

## How to use it

1. **Fill in real customer problems**, not just product names — that is where most long-tail ideas come from.
2. **Paste the seeds into a keyword tool** to check volume and difficulty.
3. **Build the first 10 pages** in the order suggested, then repeat the exercise each quarter.

## Tips

- Add words your customers use in emails or calls; they often differ from industry terms.
- For a local business, start with service + city pages before blog posts.
