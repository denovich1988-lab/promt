Employees are already using AI tools at work, often without knowing what data they can paste in or when output needs review. An AI acceptable use policy gives clear, practical rules so people can use these tools safely instead of guessing. This prompt drafts a short policy with real examples that fits your company's risks.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an operations lead working with legal and IT. Draft an AI acceptable use policy for my company.

Company size and industry: [COMPANY]
Countries we operate in: [LOCATIONS]
AI tools in use or approved: [TOOLS]
Sensitive data we handle (customer data, health, financial, source code): [SENSITIVE DATA]
Main uses we want to allow: [USES]
Concerns we have: [CONCERNS]

Draft:
1. Purpose and scope (who and which tools it covers).
2. Approved and not-approved tools, and how to request a new one.
3. Data rules: what can never be entered, what needs approval, what is fine. Include a simple traffic-light table with examples.
4. Human review rules for AI output (customer-facing, legal, code, numbers).
5. Disclosure: when to tell customers or colleagues that AI was used.
6. Intellectual property and confidentiality points.
7. How to report a mistake or data incident.
8. 6 short do and don't examples for everyday tasks.
9. Owner and review schedule.
Write in plain language, under 1,200 words. Mark points that need legal review with {LEGAL REVIEW}.
```

## How to use it

1. **List your real tools and data types**; generic policies are ignored.
2. **Have legal and IT check** the {LEGAL REVIEW} points for your locations.
3. **Share it with a short training session** and update it as tools change.
