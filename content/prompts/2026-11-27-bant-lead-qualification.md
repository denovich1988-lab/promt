BANT (budget, authority, need, timing) is still the quickest way to decide whether a lead deserves a full sales cycle, but asking the four questions word for word feels like an interrogation. This BANT lead qualification prompt writes conversational questions tailored to your product and buyer, a simple scoring sheet, and what to do next for each score, so reps qualify consistently without sounding scripted.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a sales enablement manager. Build a BANT qualification kit for my team.

Product and price range: [WHAT WE SELL, TYPICAL DEAL SIZE]
Typical buyer: [JOB TITLES, COMPANY SIZE, INDUSTRY]
Who usually signs: [E.G. CFO ABOVE $20K, TEAM LEAD BELOW]
Typical sales cycle: [E.G. 2–6 WEEKS]
Main problems we solve: [PROBLEMS]
Where leads come from: [INBOUND FORM, EVENTS, OUTBOUND]

Create:
1. For each BANT element, 4 natural questions that don't sound like a checklist (e.g. instead of "What's your budget?"), plus what a strong, weak and unclear answer sounds like.
2. Follow-up questions when the answer is vague.
3. A scoring sheet: 0–3 points per element with clear criteria, and total-score bands (pursue now, nurture, disqualify).
4. Recommended next step for each band, including a short follow-up email for nurture leads.
5. Red flags that should override the score.
6. A short note on when BANT isn't enough (e.g. complex deals with many stakeholders) and which extra questions to add.
7. CRM fields to record the answers.
```

## How to use it

1. **Adjust the authority threshold** to how buying really works for your deal sizes.
2. **Role-play the questions** with a colleague before using them on calls.
3. **Review scores after a month** against deals actually won and tune the criteria.

## Tips

- Ask about the problem and its impact first; budget questions land better once the need is clear.
- A low budget score early isn't always a no; budgets often appear when the cost of the problem is clear.
