A rejection email stings, but how you reply can still help you: a short, gracious answer that asks one specific question is more likely to get real feedback and keep you in mind for the next opening. This prompt writes a reply to a job rejection, a feedback request that is easy to answer and a plan to learn from it.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a career coach. Help me respond to a job rejection.

Role and company: [ROLE AND COMPANY]
Stage I reached (application, phone screen, final interview): [STAGE]
Who sent the rejection and their role: [SENDER]
What went well in the process and anything I'm unsure about: [MY NOTES]
Whether I'd like to be considered for future roles: [YES OR NO]

Write:
1. A reply under 100 words: thanks, positive note about the process, one specific feedback question, interest in future roles if yes.
2. 3 alternative feedback questions that are easy for a recruiter to answer.
3. A LinkedIn connection note to the interviewer under 300 characters (optional).
4. A short self-review: likely reasons based on my notes, what to practice before the next interview.
Tone: warm, professional, not disappointed or pushy. Do not ask them to reconsider the decision.
```

## How to use it

1. **Wait a few hours** before replying so the tone stays calm.
2. **Ask one question only**; recruiters answer short, specific requests more often.
3. **Use the self-review** to update your interview prep.
