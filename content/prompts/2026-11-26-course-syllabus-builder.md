A good syllabus is a contract and a roadmap at once: students should know what they'll learn, what's due when and how they'll be graded. Starting from a blank page takes days. This course syllabus prompt drafts the full document, from learning outcomes to a week-by-week schedule and policies, based on your course details and your school's rules.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an instructional designer. Draft a course syllabus.

Course title and code: [TITLE, CODE]
Level and audience: [E.G. HIGH SCHOOL 11TH GRADE, FIRST-YEAR UNDERGRADUATE, ADULT LEARNERS]
Length and format: [E.G. 15 WEEKS, TWO 75-MINUTE CLASSES A WEEK, IN PERSON/ONLINE]
Main topics I must cover: [LIST]
Required materials: [BOOKS, SOFTWARE]
Assessments I plan: [E.G. QUIZZES, MIDTERM, PROJECT, PARTICIPATION]
School policies I must include: [ATTENDANCE, LATE WORK, ACADEMIC INTEGRITY, AI USE, ACCESSIBILITY]
Holidays or breaks: [DATES]

Create:
1. Course description (100–150 words, student-friendly).
2. 4–6 measurable learning outcomes using action verbs.
3. Week-by-week schedule table: week, topic, readings, activities, assignments due.
4. Grading breakdown table that adds up to 100%, plus the grading scale.
5. Policies section written clearly and kindly, based on what I listed.
6. How to succeed in this course: 5 practical tips.
7. Support resources section with placeholders for office hours, tutoring and accessibility services.

Map each assessment to the outcomes it measures. Use {CHECK WITH DEPARTMENT} where my school's wording is needed.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste your school's required policy wording** where you have it, so the draft doesn't paraphrase official language.
2. **Check the schedule against your calendar** and adjust for exam weeks and holidays.
3. **Share a one-page summary** in the first class and keep the full syllabus online.

## Tips

- Front-load low-stakes assignments so students get feedback early.
- Add a clear AI-use statement; students increasingly look for one.
