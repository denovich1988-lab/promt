Laundromats look simple, but the numbers depend on the location, the machine mix, utility costs and whether you add services like wash-and-fold or pickup. Lenders and sellers expect a plan that shows you understand those drivers. This laundromat business plan prompt structures your research into a full plan, builds a cost and revenue model from your inputs and lists the assumptions to verify before you sign a lease or buy a store.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a small business advisor who has helped owners buy and open laundromats.

Plan type: [NEW BUILD / BUYING AN EXISTING LAUNDROMAT / RENOVATION]
Location: [ADDRESS OR AREA], nearby: [E.G. APARTMENTS, STUDENT HOUSING, PARKING, COMPETITORS]
Store size and rent: [SQUARE FEET, MONTHLY RENT, LEASE LENGTH]
Equipment plan: [NUMBER AND SIZE OF WASHERS AND DRYERS, CARD OR COIN SYSTEM]
Services: [SELF-SERVICE / WASH-AND-FOLD / PICKUP AND DELIVERY / COMMERCIAL ACCOUNTS / VENDING]
Pricing I am considering: [PRICES]
Known costs: [UTILITIES ESTIMATE, INSURANCE, STAFF, MAINTENANCE, LOAN TERMS]
Existing store data, if buying: [REVENUE, UTILITY BILLS, EQUIPMENT AGE]
My budget and financing: [AMOUNTS]

Write:
1. Executive summary.
2. Market and location analysis based on the details above, and the local data I should gather (population density, renter share, competitor prices and condition).
3. Services and pricing, with reasoning.
4. Operations: hours, staffing, maintenance schedule, cleanliness and safety.
5. Marketing plan for opening and the first 6 months.
6. Startup cost table and a monthly operating budget.
7. A simple 12-month revenue and cash flow model with turns per machine per day as the key assumption; show low, base and high cases and the break-even point.
8. Risks (lease, utility price changes, equipment failure) and how to reduce them.
9. Due diligence checklist if buying an existing store (verify utility bills against claimed revenue).

Use only my numbers; label every estimate you add as {ASSUMPTION} and do not invent industry statistics.
```

## How to use it

1. **Gather real local data** (rents, competitor prices, utility rates) before filling the prompt.
2. **Check the model's key assumption**, turns per machine per day, against what you observe at nearby stores.
3. **Have an accountant review the numbers** before you present the plan to a lender.

## Tips

- When buying, water and gas bills are a useful cross-check on the seller's revenue claims.
- Wash-and-fold and commercial accounts can raise revenue without more machines.
