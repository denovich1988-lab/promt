Looking at competitors' prices is easy; making sense of different plans, limits and add-ons is not. A structured comparison shows what customers really pay for each level of value and where your offer stands out or falls short. This prompt organises the pricing information you collect and draws careful conclusions from it.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a pricing analyst. Help me analyse competitor pricing.

Our product, plans and prices: [OUR PRICING]
Our target customer: [TARGET CUSTOMER]
Competitor pricing information I collected (copy from their pricing pages, with date checked): [COMPETITOR PRICING DATA]

Do this:
1. Build a comparison table: competitor, plan, price, billing period, main limits (users, usage, features), key add-ons, free trial or free plan.
2. Normalise prices to a comparable unit (for example, per user per month, or per typical customer) and show the calculation.
3. Describe the pricing patterns: value metrics used, common tiers, what is held back for higher plans, discounts.
4. Show where our pricing sits for a typical customer: cheaper, similar or more expensive, and for what difference in value.
5. List 3–5 pricing questions or experiments worth considering, with the reasoning and risks.
6. Note gaps in the data I should fill before deciding.

Use only the data I provided. Do not guess prices that are not listed; mark them as unknown.
```

## How to use it

1. **Copy pricing pages** for 3–6 competitors and note the date you checked.
2. **Define a "typical customer"** (for example, a 10-person team) so comparisons are fair.
3. **Treat the output as input** for a pricing decision, then test changes with real customers.

## Tips

- Check "contact sales" plans through reviews, public documents or sales calls; never guess them.
- Repeat the research every six months; pricing pages change often.
