A perceptual map plots competitors on two dimensions customers care about, such as price and ease of use, so you can see crowded areas and open space. The hard parts are choosing axes that matter to buyers and placing brands honestly. This prompt builds a perceptual positioning map from what you know about the market and turns it into a positioning choice.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a brand strategist. Help me build a perceptual positioning map.

Our product and market: [PRODUCT AND MARKET]
Target customer: [CUSTOMER]
Competitors (include alternatives like spreadsheets or doing nothing): [COMPETITORS]
What customers say matters when choosing (from reviews, interviews, sales calls): [BUYING CRITERIA]
What I know about each competitor's price, strengths and reputation: [COMPETITOR NOTES]

Deliver:
1. 4 possible axis pairs based on buying criteria; recommend the pair that best separates the market and matters most to customers.
2. A table placing each brand on both axes (score 1–10) with the reason and how confident the score is.
3. A text sketch of the map (quadrants and where each brand sits) that I can redraw in a slide.
4. Crowded areas and open gaps; for each gap, whether customers actually want that position.
5. Recommended position for us and a one-sentence positioning statement.
6. How to validate the map with 5–10 customer conversations.
Base placements only on my notes; mark guesses clearly.
```

## How to use it

1. **Choose axes from customer language**, not internal features.
2. **Validate placements with real customers** before using the map in strategy decisions.
3. **Redraw the map yearly** as competitors move.

## Tips

- Write the final statement with the [brand positioning statement](/prompts/brand-positioning-statement/) prompt.
- Gather buying criteria from reviews with the [competitor review mining](/prompts/competitor-review-mining/) prompt.
