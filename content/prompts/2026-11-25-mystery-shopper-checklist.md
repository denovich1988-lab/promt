Mystery shopping shows what customers really experience, but only if every visit is scored the same way. A clear mystery shopper checklist turns a vague impression into comparable data. This prompt builds a checklist, scoring form and report template for your own business or a competitor review, focused on observable behavior rather than opinions.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a customer experience researcher designing a mystery shopping program.

Business type: [E.G. COFFEE SHOP, CAR DEALERSHIP, HOTEL FRONT DESK, E-COMMERCE CHECKOUT]
Locations or channels: [IN STORE, PHONE, CHAT, WEBSITE]
Our service standards: [E.G. GREET WITHIN 30 SECONDS, OFFER HELP, SUGGEST ADD-ON]
What we want to learn: [E.G. WHY CONVERSION DROPPED, CONSISTENCY ACROSS STORES]
Scenario for the shopper: [E.G. FIRST-TIME CUSTOMER ASKING ABOUT A GIFT UNDER $50]

1. Write the shopper's scenario brief: who they are, what they ask, what they must not reveal.
2. Create a checklist by stage (arrival, greeting, needs discovery, product knowledge, checkout, follow-up) with observable yes/no or 1–5 items only.
3. Add a weighting for each stage and explain how to calculate a total score.
4. Write a report template: score summary, best moment, biggest gap, quotes from staff (no names), photos allowed or not.
5. Suggest how to share results with staff in a constructive, non-punitive way.

Keep it fair and legal: no recording without consent where it's required, and no trick questions.
```

## How to use it

1. **Base the checklist on standards staff already know**; testing unknown rules feels unfair.
2. **Run the same scenario at every location** so the scores are comparable.
3. **Review results monthly** and coach on the biggest gap, not every item.

## Tips

- Ask for a version for checking competitors' websites or chat support.
- Check local laws on recording calls or video before shoppers use them.
