An overflowing inbox mixes urgent client requests with newsletters, notifications and CCs, so important emails get buried. This prompt designs a simple email inbox rules and triage system for Gmail or Outlook: a few folders or labels, filter rules based on the email you actually receive, and a short daily routine to keep it under control.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a productivity coach who sets up email systems. Design inbox rules and a triage routine for me.

Email app: [GMAIL / OUTLOOK / APPLE MAIL / OTHER]
My role and who emails me most: [ROLE, KEY SENDERS (BOSS, CLIENTS, TEAM)]
Emails per day: [ROUGH NUMBER]
Types of email that clutter my inbox: [E.G. NEWSLETTERS, TOOL NOTIFICATIONS, CC THREADS, RECEIPTS, CALENDAR INVITES]
Senders or domains that must always reach me: [LIST]
How often I can check email: [E.G. 3 TIMES A DAY]

Create:
1. A label/folder structure of no more than 6–7 items (e.g. Action, Waiting, Read Later, Receipts, Notifications).
2. A list of filter rules: condition (sender, domain, words, "to/cc"), action (label, skip inbox, mark read, star), and step-by-step instructions to create each in my email app.
3. A VIP rule so key senders always stand out.
4. A daily triage routine using the 4 Ds (delete, delegate, do in 2 minutes, defer) with timings.
5. A weekly 15-minute clean-up checklist.
6. Templates for 3 quick replies I can save (acknowledge, delegate, decline).
7. A plan to clear my current backlog in one session without reading everything.

Keep it simple enough to keep using. Note any feature that depends on my email app's version as {CHECK IN SETTINGS}.
```

## How to use it

1. **List your real clutter**; rules built on your actual email work best.
2. **Set up rules in one sitting**, then watch for a week and adjust anything filtered wrongly.
3. **Check email at set times** instead of keeping it open all day.

## Tips

- Unsubscribe from anything you haven't opened in a month before writing rules for it.
- Turn off desktop notifications for everything except VIP senders.
