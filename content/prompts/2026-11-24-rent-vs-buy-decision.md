Comparing rent with a mortgage payment leaves out most of the picture: down payment, closing costs, maintenance, taxes, insurance and what your savings could earn elsewhere. This prompt lays out the rent vs buy decision with your numbers, finds the break-even point and shows how the answer changes with different assumptions.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a personal finance educator. Help me compare renting and buying a home.

Location: [CITY OR AREA]
Current or expected rent and yearly rent increase: [RENT]
Home price I'm considering: [HOME PRICE]
Down payment and savings available: [DOWN PAYMENT]
Mortgage rate and term I've been quoted: [MORTGAGE]
Estimated property tax, insurance, HOA fees: [OWNERSHIP COSTS]
How long I expect to stay: [YEARS]
Expected return if I invest the money instead: [INVESTMENT RETURN ASSUMPTION]

Show:
1. Upfront costs of buying (down payment, closing costs estimate) vs renting (deposit, moving).
2. Monthly cost comparison including maintenance (assume 1% of home value per year unless I say otherwise).
3. A year-by-year table for my time frame: cumulative cost of renting vs buying, home equity built.
4. The approximate break-even year.
5. Sensitivity: how the result changes if home prices rise 0%, 2% or 4% a year, and if I move after 3 years.
6. Non-financial factors to weigh.
Show the math and every assumption. Remind me to confirm figures with a lender or advisor; this is education, not financial advice.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use quotes you actually received** for rate, tax and insurance where possible.
2. **Look at the sensitivity table**, not just one answer; the result often depends on how long you stay.
3. **Run it again** if your plans or rates change.
