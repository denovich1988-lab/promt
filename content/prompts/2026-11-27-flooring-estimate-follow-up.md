Many flooring companies measure, send a quote and never hear back, often because the homeowner is comparing prices or is unsure about materials. A friendly, useful follow-up wins a share of those jobs. This flooring estimate follow-up prompt writes a short sequence that recaps the quote, explains the options in plain words, answers typical concerns and makes booking easy, without pushy sales tactics.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a sales coach for residential flooring contractors.

Company: [NAME], installing [E.G. LUXURY VINYL PLANK, HARDWOOD, LAMINATE, TILE, CARPET] in [AREA].
Customer: [FIRST NAME], project: [ROOMS AND SQUARE FOOTAGE]
Quote: [OPTIONS AND PRICES, E.G. OPTION A LVP $X / OPTION B ENGINEERED HARDWOOD $Y]
What is included: [REMOVAL, DISPOSAL, SUBFLOOR PREP, TRIM, FURNITURE MOVING]
Warranty: [INSTALLATION WARRANTY, MANUFACTURER WARRANTY SUMMARY]
Next available install dates: [DATES]
What the customer worried about at the visit: [E.G. PETS AND SCRATCHES, WATER IN THE KITCHEN, PRICE, DUST, TIMELINE]

Write a follow-up sequence:
1. Same day: thank-you email with a clear quote summary and a side-by-side table of the options (durability, water resistance, comfort, look, price), based only on the materials listed.
2. Day 2: short text checking whether they have questions.
3. Day 5: email answering their specific worries in plain language, plus what installation day looks like.
4. Day 10: text with the next available install dates and how to hold one.
5. Day 21: polite final check-in that leaves the door open.

Also give: 3 answers to "your price is higher than another quote" that explain scope differences without criticising competitors.
Keep every message short. No fake urgency or invented discounts.
```

## How to use it

1. **Note the customer's worries during the visit**; they drive the most useful message.
2. **Send the first email the same day** while the visit is fresh.
3. **Stop the sequence** as soon as the customer books or says no.

## Tips

- Ask competitors' quotes to be compared line by line; scope differences explain most price gaps.
- A photo of a similar finished job in the first email helps customers picture the result.
