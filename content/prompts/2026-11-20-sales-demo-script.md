Many demos turn into feature tours, and the buyer's attention drops after ten minutes. A good demo is built from discovery: show how the product solves the two or three problems this buyer told you about. This prompt structures the demo around those problems.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a sales engineer. Plan a product demo.

Product: [PRODUCT]
Buyer company and attendees (roles): [BUYER AND ATTENDEES]
Problems they shared in discovery: [PROBLEMS]
Their current tool or process: [CURRENT SOLUTION]
Demo length: [MINUTES]
Desired next step: [NEXT STEP]

Create:
1. An opening (2 minutes): agenda, recap of their problems in their words, and a check that nothing has changed.
2. For each problem: the "before" pain, the exact workflow to show (screens and clicks), the outcome to point out, and a question to ask afterwards.
3. Features NOT to show, because they don't relate to these problems.
4. Moments to stop and invite questions.
5. Likely objections from each attendee role and short answers.
6. A close: summary, confirm value, propose the next step with a date.
7. A timed run sheet.

Keep the demo conversational — the buyer should talk at least a third of the time.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use your discovery notes** — the demo is only as good as what you learned.
2. **Prepare demo data** that looks like the buyer's real situation.
3. **Send a short recap email** with the agreed next step afterwards.

## Tips

- Ask for a 15-minute version for busy executives.
- Rehearse the workflows so no time is lost clicking around.
