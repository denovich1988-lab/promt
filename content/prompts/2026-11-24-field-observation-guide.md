Watching people in their real setting shows what interviews miss: workarounds, interruptions and habits people do not mention. Without a guide, observers write vague notes that are hard to compare. This prompt creates a field observation guide with focus areas, a note template, ethics checks and an analysis plan.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a qualitative researcher experienced in field studies. Create a field observation guide.

Research question: [RESEARCH QUESTION]
Setting (store, clinic, office, home, public space): [SETTING]
Who we observe: [PARTICIPANTS]
Number of sessions, length and observers: [SESSIONS]
Observation type (passive, participant, contextual inquiry with questions): [TYPE]
Permissions and consent situation: [CONSENT]

Produce:
1. Focus areas (environment, actions, tools, interactions, pain points, workarounds) with what to look for in each.
2. A note-taking template with time stamps and space to separate observations from interpretations.
3. 5–8 short questions to ask at natural moments (if allowed).
4. Ethics and privacy checklist: consent, recording, sensitive data, how to stop.
5. A debrief template for right after each session.
6. How to analyse the notes into themes and compare sessions.
Keep the guide to what one observer can manage in real time.
```

## How to use it

1. **Get permission** from the site and participants before you start.
2. **Do a short pilot session** and adjust the template.
3. **Debrief within an hour** while details are fresh.
