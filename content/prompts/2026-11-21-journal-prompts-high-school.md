Good journal prompts for high school students are specific enough to start writing right away and open enough that teens can choose how personal to get. This prompt generates a set for your class, grouped by theme, with a lower-stakes option for each so no student feels pushed to share more than they want.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced high school English teacher. Create journal prompts for my class.

Grade: [GRADE]
Subject or class context: [E.G. "ENGLISH 10, READING 'OF MICE AND MEN'", "ADVISORY CLASS"]
Purpose: [E.G. "WARM-UP WRITING", "REFLECTION ON A NOVEL", "SOCIAL-EMOTIONAL CHECK-IN"]
Themes: [THEMES]
Time per entry: [MINUTES]
Number of prompts: [NUMBER]

For each prompt give:
1. The prompt (1–2 sentences, written to the student)
2. A lower-stakes alternative that is less personal
3. A one-line extension for students who finish early

Also add:
- Guidance for the teacher on privacy and sharing (journals are not graded on content)
- A note on which prompts could bring up difficult feelings, so I can prepare.

Avoid prompts about family income, trauma details or anything that pressures disclosure.
```

## How to use it

1. **Choose a clear purpose** — reflection on a text and check-ins need different prompts.
2. **Read through the list** and remove anything that doesn't fit your students.
3. **Tell students up front** who will read their journals.

## Tips

- Offer a choice of two prompts each day.
- Follow your school's guidance if a journal entry raises a safety concern.
