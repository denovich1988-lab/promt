Caring for an aging parent is often shared between siblings, a partner, neighbors and paid helpers, and things slip through the gaps. A family caregiver weekly schedule makes it clear who does what and when. This prompt organizes appointments, daily tasks, rides and handoffs into a simple plan everyone can follow, while medical decisions stay with doctors and the person receiving care.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced care coordinator helping a family organize care for a loved one.

Person receiving care: [RELATIONSHIP, AGE, LIVING SITUATION, WHAT THEY CAN DO ON THEIR OWN]
Daily needs: [E.G. MEDICATION REMINDERS, MEALS, MOBILITY HELP, COMPANIONSHIP]
Appointments this month: [DATE, TYPE, LOCATION]
Helpers and availability: [NAME/ROLE + DAYS AND TIMES, DISTANCE]
Their preferences: [ROUTINES, WHAT MATTERS TO THEM]

Create:
1. A weekly schedule table (days × morning/afternoon/evening) showing who covers what.
2. A task checklist for each visit (e.g. check fridge, mail, mood, medications as prescribed).
3. A handoff note template so each helper can update the next one in 2 minutes.
4. A contact sheet layout: doctors, pharmacy, emergency contacts, neighbors (placeholders only).
5. Gaps in coverage and options to fill them (other relatives, community services, paid help).
6. A short message to family members proposing the plan and asking for feedback.

Respect the person's independence and preferences. Don't give medical advice; list questions for their doctor instead.
```

## How to use it

1. **Include the person receiving care** in the conversation about the plan.
2. **Share the schedule in a shared document** or family chat everyone can see.
3. **Review it monthly**, as needs and availability change.

## Tips

- Ask for a list of questions for the next doctor's appointment.
- Build in time off for the main caregiver; burnout is common and not a personal failure.
