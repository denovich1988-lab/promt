Emailing parents about behavior is one of the hardest messages teachers write. Too vague and nothing changes; too harsh and parents get defensive. This prompt writes a calm, factual email that starts with something positive, describes specific behavior, explains what you are doing in class and invites the family to work with you.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced teacher. Help me write an email to a parent about their child's behavior.

Grade and subject: [GRADE AND SUBJECT]
Student's first name: [NAME]
Something positive about the student: [POSITIVE]
Specific behavior, with dates and what happened (facts only): [BEHAVIOR]
Impact on the student's learning or the class: [IMPACT]
What I have already tried: [STRATEGIES TRIED]
What I'd like from the family: [REQUEST, e.g. a talk at home, a meeting, a check-in system]
School policy or next steps if it continues: [POLICY, or "none to mention"]

Write:
1. An email under 220 words: positive opening, factual description, impact, what I'm doing, a clear request, and an offer to meet with two time options.
2. A gentler version for a first contact and a firmer version for a repeated issue.
3. Short replies for three possible parent responses: supportive, defensive, no reply after a week.
Avoid labels ("lazy", "disruptive child"), avoid diagnosing, and describe behavior, not character.
```

## How to use it

1. **Write down facts and dates** before you start.
2. **Choose the gentle or firm version** depending on history.
3. **Follow your school's policy** on who should be copied and keep a record.

## Tips

- Prepare for the follow-up meeting with the [parent-teacher conference notes](/prompts/parent-teacher-conference-notes/) prompt.
- Share good news too, for example in a [class newsletter for parents](/prompts/parent-newsletter-teachers/).
