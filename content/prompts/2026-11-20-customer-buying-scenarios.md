Customers don't buy because of a demographic; they buy because something happened — a new job, a failed tool, a deadline. Knowing those moments tells you where to show up and what to say. This prompt turns what you know about customers into concrete buying scenarios with triggers, alternatives and deciding factors.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a customer research strategist. Map the buying scenarios for my product.

Product and price: [PRODUCT AND PRICE]
Who buys it today: [CUSTOMER TYPES]
What customers have told us (quotes, reviews, sales notes): [CUSTOMER EVIDENCE]
Main alternatives, including doing nothing: [ALTERNATIVES]

Create 4–6 scenarios. For each:
1. A short name (e.g. "The new manager inherits a mess").
2. Trigger event — what just happened.
3. The job they're trying to get done and the deadline or pressure.
4. Where they look for solutions first.
5. What they compare us against and the criteria they use.
6. What makes them choose us, and what makes them walk away.
7. The message that would resonate at that moment.

Then rank the scenarios by how common and how valuable they seem from my evidence, and mark which ones rest on assumptions I should verify with interviews.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste real customer words** — reviews, call notes, support tickets.
2. **Check the top scenarios** with 3–5 customer interviews.
3. **Use each scenario** to write a dedicated landing page or ad angle.

## Tips

- Ask for the questions to use in interviews to confirm each scenario.
- Share the scenarios with sales so they recognise them on calls.
