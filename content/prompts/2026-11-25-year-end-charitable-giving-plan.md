December brings a flood of donation requests, and it's easy to give on impulse and then wonder whether it made a difference. A year-end charitable giving plan helps you decide what you can afford, which causes matter most to you and how to check a charity before you donate. This prompt organizes your choices; tax questions should go to a tax professional.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a thoughtful personal finance coach helping me plan my year-end giving.

Total I'm comfortable giving: [AMOUNT OR "HELP ME DECIDE FROM MY BUDGET"]
Monthly budget overview (optional): [INCOME, FIXED COSTS, SAVINGS GOAL]
Causes I care about: [E.G. LOCAL FOOD BANK, ANIMAL RESCUE, EDUCATION, DISASTER RELIEF]
Organizations I'm considering: [NAMES OR "NONE YET"]
Other ways I can give: [VOLUNTEER TIME, ITEMS, SKILLS]
Country: [COUNTRY]

1. Help me set a realistic giving amount if I haven't, without cutting essential bills or savings.
2. Rank my causes with me by asking 3 short questions about my values, then suggest how to split the amount.
3. Give me a checklist to vet each charity: registration status, how to check it in my country, transparency, how donations are used.
4. Compare one-time gifts with smaller monthly donations for my situation.
5. Create a record-keeping checklist (receipts, dates, amounts) for my files.
6. List questions to ask a tax professional about deductions in my country.

Don't recommend specific charities or give tax advice.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with your budget**, not the requests in your inbox.
2. **Look up each charity yourself** using the vetting checklist.
3. **Save receipts in one folder** as soon as you donate.

## Tips

- Ask for a simple spreadsheet layout to track giving over the year.
- Watch out for urgent-sounding donation requests by phone or text; give through the charity's official website instead.
