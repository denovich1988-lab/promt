Finding the right words for a sympathy card is hard, and most people either freeze or fall back on phrases that feel empty. A good condolence message is short, personal and honest. This sympathy card message prompt uses your relationship and one real memory to draft a few gentle options in your voice, from a colleague's card to a close friend's letter.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me write a sympathy card message.

Who it's for: [NAME AND MY RELATIONSHIP TO THEM, E.G. MY COWORKER SAM]
Who died: [NAME AND RELATIONSHIP TO THE RECIPIENT, E.G. HIS FATHER, DAVID]
Did I know the person who died? [YES/NO — IF YES, ONE SPECIFIC MEMORY OR QUALITY]
Tone: [E.G. WARM AND SIMPLE / FORMAL / FAITH-BASED / NON-RELIGIOUS]
Cultural or religious considerations: [IF ANY]
Practical help I can genuinely offer: [E.G. MEALS NEXT WEEK, COVERING SHIFTS, OR NONE]
Length: [2–3 SENTENCES FOR A CARD / A SHORT LETTER]

Write 3 versions that sound like a real person, not a greeting card. Avoid phrases like "everything happens for a reason", "they're in a better place" (unless I chose faith-based) or "I know how you feel". If I offered help, make it specific and easy to accept. Then give one line I could add if I'm signing a shared card from a team.
```

## How to use it

1. **Add one real memory or detail**; it matters more than perfect wording.
2. **Pick the version closest to how you talk** and change a few words so it's yours.
3. **Handwrite it** if you can; a handwritten card means more.

## Tips

- Short is fine. "I'm so sorry, and I'm thinking of you" is better than a long, generic note.
- Only offer help you will actually give, and follow through.
