Without salary bands, pay decisions depend on who negotiated hardest, which creates unfairness and makes budgeting hard. Pay ranges by level give managers clear rules and help with pay transparency laws in some places. This prompt helps you build salary bands from your own market data, with levels, ranges, placement rules and a communication plan.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a compensation consultant. Help me design salary bands.

Company size, industry and locations: [COMPANY CONTEXT]
Roles and current headcount per role: [ROLES]
Market data I have (source, percentiles, by role/location): [MARKET DATA]
Our pay philosophy (e.g. pay at market median, above market for engineering): [PHILOSOPHY]
Current salaries (anonymized, by role and level): [CURRENT PAY OR "NOT SHARED"]
Constraints (budget, pay transparency requirements): [CONSTRAINTS]

Deliver:
1. A job level framework (e.g. 5–7 levels) with criteria for scope, impact and autonomy.
2. A band table per job family: level | minimum | midpoint | maximum, built from my market data, with the range spread and overlap you used and why.
3. Rules for placing new hires and current staff in a band (compa-ratio guidance).
4. How to handle people above maximum or below minimum, and the likely cost to fix underpayments.
5. Promotion and annual review rules linked to the bands.
6. A plan to explain the structure to managers and employees.
7. Legal and data points I should check with an employment lawyer for my locations.
Use only the market numbers I provide; do not invent salary data.
```

## How to use it

1. **Use a reliable salary survey** or benchmark data for your roles and locations.
2. **Anonymize current pay** before sharing it with any AI tool.
3. **Review the plan with legal and finance** before rolling it out.

## Tips

- Pay transparency rules differ by country and state; check what applies to your job posts.
- Link bands to the [professional development plan](/prompts/professional-development-plan/) so growth paths are clear.
