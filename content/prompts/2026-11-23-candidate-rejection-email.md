Candidates remember how a company says no, and silence is the worst answer. A good rejection email is prompt, clear and kind, and gets more personal the further someone went in the process. This prompt writes candidate rejection emails for each stage, with optional specific feedback and a genuine way to stay in touch.

## Prompt

```prompt
Write candidate rejection emails for our hiring process.

Company and role: [COMPANY AND ROLE]
Hiring stages: [STAGES, e.g. application review, phone screen, interviews, final round]
Our tone: [TONE]
Feedback policy: [FEEDBACK POLICY, e.g. no feedback, short feedback after final round only]
Can we keep them in mind for future roles? [YES OR NO]

Write:
1. A rejection email for each stage, getting more personal at later stages (under 120 words each), using [CANDIDATE NAME].
2. For the final round: a version with [SPECIFIC FEEDBACK] placeholders and a note on how to keep feedback factual and job-related.
3. A short phone-call script for final-round candidates.
4. A reply template for candidates who ask for more feedback.
Rules: say the decision in the first two sentences, thank them for their time, no false promises, nothing about age, family, health or other protected characteristics.
```

## How to use it

1. **Send rejections within a few days** of the decision.
2. **Check feedback with HR** before sending it.
3. **Only offer to keep in touch** if you really will.
