Small businesses rarely plan for disruption until it happens: the payment system goes down, a key person is ill, a supplier fails or the office floods. A short continuity plan answers in advance who does what, how you keep serving customers and how you recover. This prompt builds one sized for a small team.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a business continuity consultant for small businesses. Create a continuity plan for my company.

Business, size and location(s): [BUSINESS]
Critical activities customers depend on: [CRITICAL ACTIVITIES]
Key systems and tools: [SYSTEMS]
Key people and what only they know or can do: [KEY PEOPLE]
Main suppliers and partners: [SUPPLIERS]
Past disruptions or near misses: [PAST INCIDENTS]

Create:
1. A business impact table: each critical activity, how long we can survive without it, and the impact on customers and revenue.
2. The 6 most likely disruption scenarios for my business, with likelihood and impact (low/medium/high).
3. For each scenario: first-hour actions, workaround to keep serving customers, who is responsible, and recovery steps.
4. Single points of failure (people, systems, suppliers) and how to reduce each.
5. A contact sheet template and a customer communication template for a disruption.
6. A plan to test and update the plan twice a year.

Keep it practical, in checklists, and short enough to use under stress.
```

## How to use it

1. **Be honest about single points of failure**, especially knowledge that sits with one person.
2. **Store the plan where it can be reached** when your main systems are down.
3. **Run a 30-minute tabletop exercise** with the team on one scenario.

## Tips

- Start by documenting the passwords, accounts and processes only one person knows, stored securely.
- Check what your insurance covers before a disruption, not after.
