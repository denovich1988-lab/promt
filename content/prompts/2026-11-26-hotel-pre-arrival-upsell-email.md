The days before check-in are when guests are most open to adding a room upgrade, late checkout, breakfast or a spa slot, and also when they need practical details such as parking and check-in times. This hotel pre-arrival email prompt writes a helpful message for hotels, B&Bs and short-stay properties that answers questions first and then offers a few relevant extras, matched to the guest's reason for travel.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a hotel guest experience and revenue manager. Write pre-arrival emails.

Property: [NAME, TYPE (HOTEL / B&B / BOUTIQUE / RESORT), LOCATION]
Practical info: [CHECK-IN/CHECK-OUT TIMES, PARKING, ACCESS, WI-FI, DIRECTIONS]
Add-ons with prices: [E.G. ROOM UPGRADE, EARLY CHECK-IN, LATE CHECKOUT, BREAKFAST, AIRPORT TRANSFER, SPA, CELEBRATION PACKAGE]
Guest segments: [E.G. BUSINESS, COUPLES, FAMILIES, EVENT GUESTS]
Season and local highlights: [E.G. CHRISTMAS MARKET, SKI SEASON, RESTAURANTS]
Brand voice: [WARM / LUXURY / CASUAL]

Write:
1. A pre-arrival email sent 3–5 days before arrival: subject line, a personal greeting with {GUEST NAME} and {ARRIVAL DATE}, practical info, then 2–3 add-ons that fit the guest segment, each with one line on the benefit, price and how to book.
2. Versions of the add-on section for each guest segment I listed.
3. A 2-day-before SMS (under 300 characters) with check-in time and a link placeholder.
4. 3 local tips for this season.
5. A short line inviting special requests (dietary needs, accessibility, celebrations).

Keep the main email under 200 words, helpful first and sales second, with no false urgency.
```

## How to use it

1. **Price the add-ons accurately** and offer only ones you can deliver for every guest.
2. **Use your booking system's fields** for {GUEST NAME} and {ARRIVAL DATE}.
3. **Track which add-ons sell** and drop the ones nobody books.

## Tips

- Families respond to practical extras (connecting rooms, cots); couples to experiences.
- Respond quickly to special requests; it's often what guests remember.
