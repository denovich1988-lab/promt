Young readers need many short, varied repetitions of high-frequency words before they recognise them instantly. Teachers and parents often run out of fresh ideas after flashcards. This sight word activities prompt builds a week of practice for any word list, with games, sentence reading, a simple story and a quick check, and it notes which words can be sounded out so practice supports phonics too.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an early literacy teacher.

Child's age or grade: [E.G. 5 YEARS OLD, KINDERGARTEN, GRADE 1]
Sight words this week: [E.G. THE, SAID, WAS, YOU, COME, HERE]
Words already known: [LIST]
Setting: [CLASSROOM / SMALL GROUP / HOME]
Time per day: [E.G. 10 MINUTES]
Child's interests: [E.G. DINOSAURS, SOCCER, PRINCESSES, TRUCKS]
Learning needs: [E.G. NEEDS MOVEMENT, ENGLISH LEARNER, EASILY FRUSTRATED]

Create:
1. For each word, note which letters follow regular phonics patterns and which part is "tricky" to remember.
2. A 5-day plan (one short activity per day) using movement, games and writing.
3. Ten simple sentences using this week's words plus known words.
4. A short story (80–120 words) about the child's interests that repeats the target words.
5. A home practice sheet with instructions for parents.
6. A 1-minute check with a simple record sheet.
7. Ideas for a child who learns the words quickly, and support for a child who struggles.

Keep sentences short and decodable where possible.
```

## How to use it

1. **Enter the words your school or programme uses** for the week.
2. **Do one short activity a day** rather than a long session.
3. **Use the quick check** on Friday and carry missed words into next week.

## Tips

- Pointing to the "tricky part" of a word (like the "ai" in said) helps children remember it.
- Praise effort and keep sessions short so reading stays fun.
