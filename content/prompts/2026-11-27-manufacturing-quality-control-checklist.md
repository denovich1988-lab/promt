Quality problems are cheapest to catch at the point they're made, not at final inspection or after a customer complaint. A good QC checklist names the characteristics that matter, where and how often to check them, what counts as a defect and what to do when one is found. This manufacturing quality control prompt builds a checklist for incoming materials, in-process checks and final inspection that your floor team can actually use.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a manufacturing quality engineer. Build a quality control checklist for this product.

Product: [WHAT IT IS, MATERIALS, KEY DIMENSIONS OR SPECS]
Process steps: [E.G. CUTTING, MOLDING, WELDING, ASSEMBLY, PAINTING, PACKING]
Customer requirements and standards: [DRAWINGS, TOLERANCES, STANDARDS, CUSTOMER SPECS]
Known problems or past defects: [LIST]
Production volume and batch size: [NUMBERS]
Inspection tools available: [E.G. CALIPERS, GAUGES, CMM, VISUAL ONLY]
Who will use the checklist: [OPERATORS / QC INSPECTORS / SUPPLIER]

Create:
1. A list of critical, major and minor characteristics with how each is defined.
2. Incoming material inspection checks.
3. In-process checks for each process step: characteristic, method/tool, frequency, acceptance criteria, who checks.
4. Final inspection and packing checks, including labelling.
5. A sampling approach for this volume (explain it in plain terms and flag that it should be confirmed against the standard your customer uses).
6. Defect classification examples with photos-to-take notes.
7. What to do when a defect is found: stop, segregate, record, escalate, root cause.
8. A one-page checklist version for the floor and a record sheet layout.
Mark any tolerance or limit I didn't give as "to be confirmed", don't invent it.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste real specs and past defects**; the checklist focuses on what actually goes wrong.
2. **Walk the line with operators** and adjust check frequencies to what's practical.
3. **Review the records monthly** to tighten checks where defects appear.

## Tips

- Add photos of good and bad examples to visual checks; they reduce disagreements between inspectors.
- Confirm sampling plans and acceptance levels with your customer's quality requirements.
