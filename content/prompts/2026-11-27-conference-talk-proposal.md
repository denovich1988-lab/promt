Program committees read hundreds of submissions for a call for papers (CFP), often in a few minutes each. Proposals that get picked have a specific title, a clear problem, concrete takeaways and evidence that the speaker has done the thing they're talking about. This conference talk proposal prompt turns your idea and experience into a submission that fits the event's tracks and format, with a title, abstract, outline and bio.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced conference speaker and program committee reviewer. Write a talk proposal.

Conference and track: [EVENT NAME, TRACK OR THEME]
Audience: [WHO ATTENDS, EXPERIENCE LEVEL]
Format and length: [E.G. 30-MINUTE TALK, 10-MINUTE LIGHTNING, WORKSHOP]
Submission limits: [E.G. ABSTRACT 300 WORDS, TITLE 80 CHARACTERS]
My talk idea: [WHAT YOU WANT TO TALK ABOUT]
My real experience with it: [PROJECTS, RESULTS, MISTAKES, DATA YOU CAN SHARE]
What's new or different about my angle: [ANGLE]
Speaker background: [ROLE, COMPANY, PAST TALKS IF ANY]

Write:
1. Five title options: specific, not clickbait, within the limit.
2. An abstract within the word limit: the problem, why it matters now, what I did, what attendees will leave with.
3. Three to five concrete takeaways.
4. A timed outline for the talk.
5. Notes for reviewers (if the CFP has a private field): why me, why this audience, whether it's been given before.
6. A speaker bio in 50 and 100 words, third person.
7. A self-review against common rejection reasons (too broad, sales pitch, no clear takeaway, wrong level) and fixes.
Don't invent results or credentials.
```

## How to use it

1. **Read the CFP and last year's program** and paste the track description into the prompt.
2. **Use real numbers and stories** from your work; they're what makes a proposal stand out.
3. **Submit early** and adapt the same idea for other events' formats.

## Tips

- A narrow, specific title ("How we cut build times from 40 to 8 minutes") usually beats a broad one.
- Avoid anything that reads like a product pitch; most committees reject those.
