A single price invites a yes-or-no decision, and often a request for a discount. Three options let clients compare value and pick what fits their budget. This prompt builds good, better and best packages for a specific client from your services and costs.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a pricing consultant for service businesses. Build three quote options.

Client and their goal: [CLIENT AND GOAL]
What they asked for: [REQUEST]
Services and deliverables I can offer: [SERVICES]
My costs or time for each: [COSTS OR HOURS]
My target price for the core request: [TARGET PRICE]
Budget hints from the client: [BUDGET OR "UNKNOWN"]

Create:
1. Good — the essential version that solves the core problem.
2. Better — the recommended option with the most value per dollar.
3. Best — the full version with extra speed, support or results.

For each: name, who it's for, deliverables, timeline, price, and estimated margin based on my costs.
Then:
4. A comparison table clients can scan in 30 seconds.
5. A short paragraph explaining why I recommend "Better" for this client.
6. Replies to "Can you do Best for the Better price?" and "Can you go lower?".

Keep every option profitable. Don't add filler deliverables just to pad the top option.
```

## How to use it

1. **Calculate your real costs** before setting prices.
2. **Present the options in a call**, not just by email.
3. **Track which option clients choose** and adjust packages over time.

## Tips

- Make the middle option the one you most want to sell.
- Ask for a proposal page layout for the three options.
