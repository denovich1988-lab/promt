A good demo can still stall if the follow-up is a generic "thanks for your time". The best recap emails repeat the buyer's own problems back to them, tie each one to what they saw, and make the next step easy to say yes to. This prompt builds that email from your rough notes so you can send it within the hour.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced B2B account executive. Write a follow-up email after a product demo.

Prospect name, role and company: [PROSPECT DETAILS]
Their main goals and problems (in their words if possible): [GOALS AND PROBLEMS]
What we showed and how they reacted: [DEMO NOTES]
Questions we could not answer yet: [OPEN QUESTIONS]
Other people involved in the decision: [STAKEHOLDERS]
Proposed next step and date: [NEXT STEP]

Write:
1. 3 subject line options (under 50 characters).
2. An email under 180 words that: thanks them in one line; recaps their 2–3 priorities; links each priority to a specific part of the demo; answers or promises answers to the open questions with a date; proposes one clear next step with two time options.
3. A short version (under 70 words) to send by LinkedIn or chat.
4. A one-paragraph summary they can forward to [STAKEHOLDERS].

Use plain, friendly language. Do not invent features, prices or results that are not in my notes.
```

## How to use it

1. **Write your notes right after the call**, while their exact words are fresh.
2. **Paste the notes** and fill in the next step you want.
3. **Send within a few hours**, then set a reminder if there is no reply in three working days.

## Tips

- If they mentioned a deadline or event, put it in the recap — it gives the next step urgency.
- The forwardable summary helps your champion sell internally; keep it free of jargon.
