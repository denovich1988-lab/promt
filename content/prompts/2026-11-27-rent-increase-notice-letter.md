A rent increase letter has to do two things: meet the legal notice rules where the property is, and keep a good tenant from feeling ambushed. Letters that are vague about the date or the amount cause disputes, and cold ones push tenants to start looking elsewhere. This rent increase letter prompt writes a clear, respectful notice with the figures and dates, a short reason, and a reply for tenants who want to negotiate.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced property manager. Write a rent increase notice to a tenant.

Landlord / company: [NAME, CONTACT DETAILS]
Tenant(s): [NAMES]
Property address: [ADDRESS]
Current rent: [AMOUNT AND FREQUENCY]
New rent: [AMOUNT]
Effective date: [DATE]
Date the notice will be delivered and how: [DATE, METHOD E.G. EMAIL AND POST]
Lease type: [FIXED TERM ENDING ON DATE / MONTH-TO-MONTH / PERIODIC]
Location (city/state/country): [LOCATION]
Reason(s) to mention: [E.G. HIGHER PROPERTY TAXES, INSURANCE, RECENT IMPROVEMENTS, MARKET RATES]
Anything offered: [E.G. LONGER LEASE AT A LOWER INCREASE, UPGRADE]
Tone: [WARM / NEUTRAL AND FORMAL]

Write:
1. The rent increase letter: current and new rent, effective date, payment method reminder, short reason, thanks for their tenancy, how to ask questions, and any option offered.
2. A short email or text to send with it.
3. A reply to a tenant who asks to negotiate, with one reasonable counter-option.
4. A checklist of legal points I must verify for my location before sending (required notice period, rent control or caps, allowed frequency of increases, required form or wording, delivery method). Don't state the rules as fact; tell me where to confirm them.
```

## How to use it

1. **Check your local notice period and any rent caps** before choosing the effective date.
2. **Send the letter in a way you can prove**, such as email plus post, and keep a copy.
3. **Use the negotiation reply** if keeping a reliable tenant is worth more than the full increase.

## Tips

- A specific reason (taxes, insurance, improvements) is usually received better than "market conditions" alone.
- This is a drafting aid, not legal advice; confirm requirements with a local professional or housing authority.
