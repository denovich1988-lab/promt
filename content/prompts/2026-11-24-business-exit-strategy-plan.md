Most owners think about an exit strategy only when they are tired or forced to sell, and that is when the business is worth least. Planning a few years ahead lets you fix what buyers look at. This prompt compares exit options for your small business and builds a timeline of steps that make any exit easier.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a small business advisor. Help me plan an exit strategy. This is planning support, not legal, tax or financial advice.

Business, industry and age: [BUSINESS]
Revenue, profit trend and main customers: [FINANCIAL SNAPSHOT]
How dependent the business is on me: [OWNER DEPENDENCE]
Team and key people: [TEAM]
Exit options I am considering (sell to a third party, sell to employees, family succession, merge, close): [OPTIONS]
My goals (timeline, money needed, role after exit, legacy): [GOALS]

Produce:
1. A comparison table of my exit options: pros, cons, typical timeline, what each needs.
2. The value drivers buyers usually look at and how my business might score on each, based on my inputs.
3. The top 5 changes to make in the next 12–24 months (reduce owner dependence, document processes, clean up financials).
4. A timeline with milestones up to the exit.
5. The advisers I will need (accountant, lawyer, broker) and questions to ask each.
Do not estimate a sale price. Flag where professional advice is essential.
```

## How to use it

1. **Be honest about owner dependence**; it is often the biggest issue.
2. **Start documenting processes** now, whatever exit you choose.
3. **Take the plan to an accountant and lawyer** before acting.
