Accepting a job offer is exciting, but the email you send is also a record: it should confirm the title, pay, start date and anything you agreed during negotiation. This job offer acceptance email prompt writes a warm, professional reply that confirms the key terms, asks the right next-step questions and keeps the tone right for your new manager or recruiter.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me write an email accepting a job offer.

Recipient: [NAME, ROLE (RECRUITER / HIRING MANAGER)]
Company and job title: [COMPANY, TITLE]
Offer details to confirm: [SALARY, BONUS, START DATE, LOCATION/REMOTE, BENEFITS, ANYTHING NEGOTIATED]
Did we negotiate? [YES/NO, WHAT CHANGED]
Things I still need: [E.G. WRITTEN OFFER, BACKGROUND CHECK STEPS, EQUIPMENT, FIRST-DAY DETAILS]
Tone: [E.G. WARM AND PROFESSIONAL, FORMAL]

Write:
1. Three subject line options.
2. The acceptance email (under 180 words): thanks, clear acceptance, confirmation of the key terms as a short list, request for the written offer if not received, next-step questions, and enthusiasm without overdoing it.
3. A shorter version (under 80 words) for a quick reply to a recruiter's call.
4. A one-line LinkedIn-style message to the hiring manager, if appropriate.
5. A checklist of what to do after accepting (signing, resignation timing, references, notice).

Only list terms I gave you. If something important is missing (like a start date), add {CONFIRM} instead of inventing it.
```

## How to use it

1. **Copy terms exactly** from the offer letter or your notes from the call.
2. **Send it from your personal email** and keep a copy with the signed offer.
3. **Wait for the signed offer** before resigning from your current job.

## Tips

- If a negotiated item isn't in the written offer, ask politely for it to be added.
- Keep the email short; long acceptances read as nervous.
