Projects rarely fail because nobody worked; they fail because the right people heard the wrong thing too late. A project communication plan sets who gets which update, how often and through which channel. This prompt builds one from your stakeholder list, with owners, templates and an escalation path.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced project manager. Create a project communication plan.

Project, goal and timeline: [PROJECT]
Stakeholders and their roles (sponsor, team, clients, other departments): [STAKEHOLDERS]
What each group cares about most: [INTERESTS]
Tools we use (email, Slack or Teams, project tool, meetings): [CHANNELS]
Key milestones and decision points: [MILESTONES]
Known risks or sensitive topics: [RISKS]

Produce:
1. A communication matrix: audience, information they need, format, channel, frequency, owner.
2. A meeting cadence (stand-ups, weekly status, steering meetings) with purpose and length for each.
3. A short template for the weekly status update (status colour, progress, next steps, risks, decisions needed).
4. An escalation path: what gets escalated, to whom and how fast.
5. Rules for decisions and where they are recorded.
6. How to communicate a delay or change in scope.
Keep the plan to what this team can realistically maintain.
```

## How to use it

1. **List every stakeholder group**, including people affected but not involved.
2. **Share the plan at kickoff** and ask what is missing.
3. **Cut meetings** that duplicate written updates.
