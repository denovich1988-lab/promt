Price increases rarely lose customers on their own; surprise, vague reasons and no notice do. A good announcement is short, honest and respectful of the reader's budget. This prompt drafts the email plus a short FAQ and a reply template for customers who push back.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a customer communications specialist. Write a price increase announcement.

Business and product: [WHAT YOU SELL]
Current price and new price: [OLD → NEW, PER PLAN IF SEVERAL]
Date the new price starts: [DATE]
Honest reason for the increase: [E.G. HIGHER COSTS, NEW FEATURES, MORE SUPPORT]
What customers got recently or will get: [IMPROVEMENTS]
Options we can offer: [E.G. LOCK IN ANNUAL PLAN AT OLD PRICE, DOWNGRADE, GRANDFATHERING]
Tone: [WARM / DIRECT / FORMAL]

Write:
1. Three subject lines that are clear, not clever.
2. An email of 150–220 words: the change and date in the first two sentences, the reason in plain words, the value, the options, and how to get help.
3. A 5-question FAQ for the help page.
4. A reply template for a customer who says the new price is too high.

Rules: no guilt, no hype, no "exciting news". Do not invent features or numbers I didn't give.
```

## How to use it

1. **Give at least 30 days' notice** where possible and put the date in the email.
2. **Have support read the FAQ** before you send so their answers match.
3. **Track replies and cancellations** for two weeks and adjust the options if needed.

## Tips

- Send it from a real person's name, not "The Team".
- Thank long-time customers specifically if you are grandfathering them.
