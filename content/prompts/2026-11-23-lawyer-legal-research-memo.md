ChatGPT prompts for lawyers are most useful for structure and first drafts, not for finding authority. AI tools can invent cases, so every citation must be checked in a trusted legal database. This prompt helps you organize a legal research memo: it frames the issues, lists the rules and sources you need to confirm, drafts the analysis from facts you give it, and lists open questions, all clearly marked for verification.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are assisting a licensed attorney in drafting an internal legal research memo. The attorney will verify all law. Do not invent cases, statutes or quotes; if you mention any authority, mark it "[VERIFY]".

Jurisdiction: [JURISDICTION]
Area of law: [AREA OF LAW]
Client situation (anonymized facts): [FACTS]
Question(s) the memo must answer: [QUESTIONS]
Authorities I have already found (paste names and key points): [AUTHORITIES, or "none yet"]

Draft:
1. Question presented, phrased precisely.
2. Short answer (2–3 sentences), clearly marked as preliminary.
3. Statement of key facts, noting which facts are missing or uncertain.
4. Issues and sub-issues to research, with the type of source likely to answer each (statute, regulation, case law, secondary source).
5. Analysis that applies the authorities I provided to the facts, with counterarguments.
6. Research checklist: search terms, sources to consult, things to verify.
7. Open questions to ask the client.
Use neutral, professional language.
```

## How to use it

1. **Remove client names and confidential details** before pasting, and follow your firm's AI policy.
2. **Paste authorities you have already found** so the analysis builds on real sources.
3. **Verify every rule and citation** in your legal research database before relying on the memo.

## Tips

- This prompt helps with drafting only; it is not legal advice and does not replace an attorney's judgment.
- Explain the result to a client with the [lawyer plain-English client letter](/prompts/lawyer-plain-english-client-letter/) prompt.
