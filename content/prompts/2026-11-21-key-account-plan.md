A few large customers often bring most of the revenue, yet many teams manage them by reacting to requests. A key account plan makes you think ahead: what the customer is trying to achieve, who matters in their organisation, where the relationship is weak and where you can grow. This prompt turns your account notes into that plan.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a key account director. Create a one-page account plan for one of our main customers.

Customer, industry and size: [CUSTOMER]
What they buy from us today and annual value: [CURRENT BUSINESS]
Their business goals and challenges this year: [CUSTOMER GOALS]
Stakeholders we know — name/role, attitude to us, influence: [STAKEHOLDERS]
Relationship health: usage, satisfaction, recent issues: [HEALTH]
Other products or services we could offer: [GROWTH OPTIONS]
Competitors present in the account: [COMPETITORS]

Create:
1. Account summary in 3 sentences.
2. A stakeholder map table: role, influence, attitude, what they care about, our relationship owner, next action. Mark gaps (important people we don't know).
3. Risks to the account, ranked, each with a mitigation step.
4. Growth opportunities linked to their goals, with rough size (low/medium/high) and next step.
5. A 90-day action plan with owners and dates.
6. Questions to ask at our next business review.

Base everything on my inputs; mark assumptions clearly. Do not invent figures.
```

## How to use it

1. **Gather notes** from sales, support and delivery before filling in the prompt.
2. **Focus on stakeholder gaps** — single-threaded accounts are the riskiest.
3. **Review the plan monthly** with everyone who works on the account.

## Tips

- Lead growth conversations with the customer's goals, not your product list.
- Share a simplified version with your main contact; it can become a joint plan.
