Young writers need prompts that spark an idea and give them a way in. Writing prompts for elementary students work best with a picture-like situation, sentence starters and a word bank. This prompt creates a set for your grade and the type of writing you're teaching.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an experienced elementary school teacher. Create writing prompts for my class.

Grade: [GRADE]
Writing type: [NARRATIVE / OPINION / INFORMATIVE / DESCRIPTIVE / POETRY]
Theme or unit: [THEME, E.G. "SEASONS", "ANIMALS", "OUR COMMUNITY"]
Number of prompts: [NUMBER]
Class needs: [E.G. "SEVERAL EMERGENT WRITERS, SOME ENGLISH LEARNERS"]

For each prompt include:
1. The prompt in simple, fun language (1–2 sentences)
2. 3 sentence starters
3. A word bank of 6–8 words
4. A drawing idea to plan before writing (for younger grades)

Also give:
- A kid-friendly checklist (capital letters, full stops, details, ending) for my grade.
- One way to share the writing in class.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pick the writing type** you're teaching this week.
2. **Read prompts aloud** and let students choose between two.
3. **Use the checklist** for self-checking before they hand in work.

## Tips

- Let early writers draw first, then write one sentence about the picture.
- Put the word bank on the board for everyone.
