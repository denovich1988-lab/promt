Contracts use long sentences and legal terms that make it easy to miss what you're agreeing to. A plain-English summary helps you understand the deal and prepare questions for the other side or a lawyer. This prompt highlights obligations and risks, but it is not a replacement for legal advice.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are helping me understand a contract. You are not my lawyer, and I will check important points with a qualified professional.

My role in the contract: [E.G. FREELANCER, TENANT, BUYER, EMPLOYEE]
What I want from this deal: [GOALS]
Contract text:
[PASTE CONTRACT — REMOVE ACCOUNT NUMBERS AND ID NUMBERS]

Provide:
1. A 5-sentence summary of what this contract does.
2. Key terms table: parties, start and end dates, payment amounts and timing, renewal, termination notice.
3. My obligations and the other party's obligations, as bullet lists.
4. Deadlines and dates I must remember.
5. Clauses that may be risky or unusual for someone in my role (e.g. automatic renewal, unlimited liability, broad non-compete, IP ownership, penalties), quoting the clause number and explaining why.
6. Questions to ask or changes to request before signing.

Quote the contract when you refer to it. If something is unclear or missing, say so instead of assuming.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the full contract,** including schedules and attachments, so nothing is missed.
2. **Use the risky clauses list** to decide what to negotiate.
3. **Take the questions to a lawyer** for anything important, high-value or legally binding.

## Tips

- Laws differ by country and state; mention your location for more relevant notes.
- Keep confidential contracts out of tools your company hasn't approved.
