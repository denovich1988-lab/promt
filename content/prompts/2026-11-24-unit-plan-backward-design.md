A unit plan built lesson by lesson often drifts away from what students should actually learn. Backward design starts with the goals, then the evidence of learning, and only then the lessons. This prompt builds a full unit plan that way, with assessments, a lesson sequence and support for different learners.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an experienced curriculum designer. Build a unit plan using backward design.

Subject, grade and unit topic: [SUBJECT, GRADE AND TOPIC]
Standards or learning goals I must cover: [STANDARDS]
Unit length (weeks, lessons per week, lesson length): [LENGTH]
Students (levels, language learners, IEPs, interests): [STUDENTS]
Resources available: [RESOURCES]

Produce:
1. Stage 1: enduring understandings, essential questions and what students will know and be able to do.
2. Stage 2: a summative assessment task with a simple rubric, plus 3–4 formative checks.
3. Stage 3: a lesson-by-lesson sequence (objective, main activity, check for understanding).
4. Differentiation for students who need support and for those ready for more.
5. A materials list and a short note to parents explaining the unit.
Align every lesson to a goal from stage 1.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the exact standards** you have to meet.
2. **Check the summative task** first; everything else should prepare students for it.
3. **Adjust pacing** after the first formative check.
