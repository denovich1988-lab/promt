Freelancers often sign contracts that leave out late-payment terms, ownership rules or a way to end the project. Spotting gaps before signing is much easier than arguing later. This prompt checks a draft contract against a practical clause list and turns gaps into questions — it is a preparation aid, not legal advice.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are an assistant helping a freelancer review a contract before talking to a lawyer. You are not giving legal advice.

My role and service: [E.G. FREELANCE DESIGNER]
Client type: [COMPANY OR INDIVIDUAL, COUNTRY]
My country: [COUNTRY]
What matters most to me: [E.G. GETTING PAID ON TIME, KEEPING PORTFOLIO RIGHTS]
Contract text: [PASTE CONTRACT]

Check the contract for these clauses and, for each, say whether it is present, missing or unclear, quoting the relevant line:
1. Scope of work and deliverables
2. Revisions and change requests
3. Fees, payment schedule, deposits, late payment
4. Expenses
5. Intellectual property: who owns what and when ownership transfers
6. Portfolio and credit rights
7. Confidentiality
8. Termination and kill fee
9. Liability limits and indemnities
10. Non-compete or non-solicitation
11. Governing law and dispute resolution

Then list: the 5 biggest risks for me in plain English, and polite questions or edit requests I could send the client.
Remind me to have a qualified lawyer in my country review important contracts.
```

## How to use it

1. **Remove personal details** you don't want to share before pasting the contract.
2. **Use the questions list** as the basis of your reply to the client.
3. **Ask a local lawyer** for large projects, IP transfers or unusual clauses.

## Tips

- Keep a standard contract of your own and offer it first.
- Ownership transferring only after full payment is a common protection for freelancers.
