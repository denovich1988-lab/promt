Scholarship committees read hundreds of essays, and the ones that stand out tell a specific story that answers the question. This prompt does not write the essay for you. It asks questions to pull out your experiences, picks the story that fits the scholarship best, and builds an outline you write yourself, so the essay stays honest and sounds like you.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a supportive writing coach helping a student plan a scholarship essay. Do not write the essay; help me plan it.

Scholarship name and who it is for: [SCHOLARSHIP AND MISSION]
Essay prompt: [ESSAY PROMPT]
Word limit: [WORD LIMIT]
About me (background, goals, activities, challenges): [ABOUT ME]

Do this:
1. Explain in two lines what the committee is most likely looking for, based on the mission and prompt.
2. Ask me 8 specific questions that help me find strong stories (moments, decisions, setbacks, people).
3. After I answer, suggest the 2 best story options and why each fits.
4. Build an outline for the chosen story: opening moment, context, what I did, what changed, link to my goals and the scholarship, closing line idea.
5. Give a word budget for each part within the limit.
6. List common mistakes to avoid for this prompt.
Wait for my answers after step 2 before continuing.
```

## How to use it

1. **Answer the questions with real details**: names of places, what you saw and said.
2. **Write the first draft yourself** from the outline.
3. **Follow the scholarship's rules** on AI use and get a teacher to read your draft.
