Internal job postings are often a copy of the external ad, which misses what employees want to know: what the move would mean for their career, whether they can apply without upsetting their manager, and how the process works. This internal job posting prompt writes a posting aimed at current staff, with the growth case, honest requirements, and a clear and fair process.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an HR business partner. Write an internal job posting for current employees.

Role: [TITLE, TEAM, LEVEL]
Hiring manager: [NAME, ROLE]
Why the role is open: [NEW ROLE / BACKFILL / GROWTH]
What the person will do: [MAIN RESPONSIBILITIES]
Must-have requirements: [SKILLS OR EXPERIENCE]
Nice-to-haves and what we'll train: [LIST]
Location / hybrid / remote: [DETAILS]
Pay band or level change: [IF SHAREABLE]
Eligibility rules: [E.G. 12 MONTHS IN CURRENT ROLE, GOOD STANDING]
Process: [HOW TO APPLY, MANAGER NOTIFICATION RULES, INTERVIEW STEPS, DEADLINE]

Write:
1. The internal posting: a headline aimed at current staff, why this role matters, what you'll do, what you'll learn, requirements split into must-have and trainable, eligibility, how to apply and the timeline.
2. A short paragraph on confidentiality and talking to your current manager.
3. An announcement message for Slack/Teams/email.
4. A note managers can share with their teams encouraging internal moves.
5. A respectful rejection message for internal candidates that offers feedback and a development conversation.
Use inclusive, plain language and avoid inflated requirements.
```

## How to use it

1. **Check eligibility and manager-notification rules** with your HR policy before posting.
2. **Post internally first** for a set period before opening the role externally, if that's your policy.
3. **Follow up personally** with every internal applicant, whatever the outcome.

## Tips

- Splitting "must-have" from "we'll train" encourages people who meet most but not all requirements to apply.
- Internal rejections affect retention; a real feedback conversation matters more than the message.
