Garage door businesses get two kinds of customers: urgent repairs ("my door won't open") and planned upgrades (new doors and openers). Each needs different marketing. This garage door company marketing prompt builds copy for both, plus a maintenance plan offer that turns one-time repair customers into repeat clients.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a marketing consultant for home service companies.

Company: [COMPANY NAME], serving [CITIES / AREA]
Services: [E.G. SPRING REPLACEMENT, OPENER REPAIR, NEW DOOR INSTALLATION, COMMERCIAL DOORS, 24/7 EMERGENCY]
Brands we install (if any): [LIST]
Differentiators: [E.G. SAME-DAY SERVICE, UPFRONT PRICING, LIFETIME WARRANTY ON SPRINGS, FAMILY-OWNED]
Pricing approach: [E.G. FLAT SERVICE CALL FEE, FREE ESTIMATES ON NEW DOORS]
Current channels: [GOOGLE BUSINESS PROFILE, WEBSITE, ADS, REFERRALS]

Create:
1. Website copy for an emergency repair page: what to do while you wait, safety warnings, call button text.
2. A new door installation page: style options, insulation, how the quote works, timeline.
3. A local service area page template that stays unique for each city (local details to include).
4. A maintenance plan offer: what's included, yearly price placeholder, sign-up copy.
5. Five Google Business Profile posts and three photo ideas.
6. A post-service text with a review request, and a 12-month tune-up reminder.
7. An estimate follow-up email for new door quotes.
8. Short ad copy for urgent repair searches (3 versions).

Include a safety note that broken springs and cables should be handled by trained technicians. No invented reviews or awards.
```

## How to use it

1. **Add your real service areas and prices** so the pages and posts are accurate.
2. **Launch the maintenance plan** with past repair customers first.
3. **Track calls from each page** to see which services bring the best jobs.

## Tips

- Before-and-after photos of new doors work well on social media and in quotes.
- Answer the "how much does it cost" question honestly, even if only with a range.
