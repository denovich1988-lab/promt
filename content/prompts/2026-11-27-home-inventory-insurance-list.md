After a fire, flood or burglary, it's hard to remember everything you owned, and insurance claims go faster with a clear list, photos and receipts. Most people mean to make a home inventory and never start because it feels huge. This prompt breaks it into a room-by-room plan with a ready table, so you can finish it over a weekend and keep it updated.

## Prompt

```prompt
Help me create a home inventory for insurance purposes.

Home type and size: [E.G. 3-BEDROOM HOUSE WITH GARAGE AND SHED / 1-BEDROOM APARTMENT]
Rooms and spaces: [LIST]
High-value items I know about: [E.G. JEWELLERY, LAPTOPS, CAMERA GEAR, BIKES, ART, INSTRUMENTS]
Insurance type: [HOMEOWNERS / RENTERS / NOT SURE]
Time available: [E.G. ONE WEEKEND / 30 MINUTES A DAY]
Tool I'll use: [SPREADSHEET / NOTES APP / PAPER]

Please give me:
1. A step-by-step plan to finish the inventory in my time available.
2. A room-by-room checklist of item categories people often forget (e.g. contents of closets, kitchen appliances, tools, seasonal items).
3. A table template with columns: room, item, brand/model, serial number, quantity, purchase date, estimated value, receipt (yes/no), photo file name, notes.
4. Tips for a quick video walkthrough of each room.
5. A list of high-value items that may need separate coverage or an appraisal, with questions to ask my insurer.
6. Where to store the inventory and backups safely (outside the home).
7. A yearly update routine and a trigger list (new purchases, gifts, moves).

Keep it practical and quick to fill in.
```

## How to use it

1. **Pick one room to start** and finish it before moving on.
2. **Take photos or a video as you go** and note the file names in the table.
3. **Ask your insurer** whether your policy covers replacement cost or actual cash value, and about limits on valuables.

## Tips

- Photograph serial numbers and model labels; they help with claims and stolen-item reports.
- Keep a copy in cloud storage or with a trusted person, not only at home.
