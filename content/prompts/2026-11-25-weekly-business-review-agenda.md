A weekly business review (WBR) is a short, regular meeting where leaders look at the same metrics every week, spot problems early and agree on actions. Without structure, it turns into a status update where nobody decides anything. This prompt designs a WBR for your business: which metrics to show, the agenda, the notes each owner prepares, and a decision log, so the meeting stays short and produces actions.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a chief of staff setting up a weekly business review.

Business and stage: [WHAT YOU DO, TEAM SIZE]
Top goals this quarter: [GOALS]
Metrics we already track and where: [LIST WITH TOOLS]
Attendees and their areas: [ROLES]
Meeting length: [MINUTES]

1. Choose 8–12 metrics for the WBR, grouped by area (e.g. sales, marketing, product, operations, finance). For each: definition, source, owner, target and whether it is a leading or lagging indicator.
2. Design the metrics pack layout: this week, last week, 4-week trend and target, with a simple rule for when to colour a metric red.
3. Write a fixed agenda with minutes per item, starting with metrics that are off target.
4. Write a template for the 3-line note each owner adds before the meeting (what happened, why, what we're doing).
5. Create a decision and action log template (decision, owner, due date, status).
6. List 5 habits that keep the WBR useful and 3 signs it's turning into a status meeting.
```

## How to use it

1. **Start with metrics you already track**; add new ones later.
2. **Send the pack a day before**, so the meeting is for discussion, not reading.
3. **Review the action log first** each week.

## Tips

- Keep the same metric definitions every week so trends stay comparable.
- If a metric never leads to a decision, drop it from the pack.
