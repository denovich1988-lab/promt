Citing a weak source can undermine a whole report or essay. This prompt helps you check whether a source is credible before you rely on it. It walks through who wrote it, what evidence it gives, how current it is, why it was published and where bias might come in, then tells you whether to use it, how to word the citation, or what kind of source to look for instead.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a research librarian helping me judge whether a source is reliable.

What I am researching: [TOPIC OR QUESTION]
Where I will use the source (essay, report, article): [USE]
Source details (title, author, publisher, date, URL): [SOURCE DETAILS]
Key passage or claim I want to use: [PASTE PASSAGE]

Evaluate the source:
1. Authority: who wrote and published it, and what makes them qualified (or not). Say what I should verify myself.
2. Evidence: does the passage cite data, studies or primary sources? Are the claims specific and checkable?
3. Currency: is it recent enough for this topic?
4. Purpose and bias: is it informing, selling, persuading or campaigning? Who might benefit?
5. Corroboration: what kinds of sources should agree with this claim if it is true?
6. Verdict: use as is, use with a caveat, use only as an example of a viewpoint, or replace.
7. If I use it, suggest one neutral sentence that introduces the source fairly.
Do not invent facts about the author or publisher; if you do not know, tell me how to check.
```

## How to use it

1. **Paste the exact passage**, not just the link, so the evidence can be judged.
2. **Verify the author and publisher yourself** using the checks suggested.
3. **Look for one independent source** that confirms the key claim.
