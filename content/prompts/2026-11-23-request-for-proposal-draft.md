A vague request for proposal gets proposals you cannot compare and prices that do not match. This prompt drafts a clear RFP for any service or software purchase: background, scope, must-have and nice-to-have requirements, timeline, budget guidance, evaluation criteria and submission rules, so vendors know exactly what to send and you can compare them fairly.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a procurement specialist. Draft a request for proposal (RFP) for my project.

Organization and background: [ORGANIZATION]
What we want to buy and why: [PROJECT AND GOAL]
Scope of work and deliverables: [SCOPE]
Must-have requirements: [MUST-HAVES]
Nice-to-have requirements: [NICE-TO-HAVES]
Timeline (questions due, proposals due, decision, start): [TIMELINE]
Budget range or "not disclosed": [BUDGET]
How we will choose (priorities): [PRIORITIES]
Contact and submission method: [CONTACT]

Write the RFP with these sections:
1. Introduction and background.
2. Project goals and scope of work.
3. Requirements as a numbered list, each marked must-have or nice-to-have.
4. Questions vendors must answer (experience, approach, team, references, support, security).
5. Pricing format so all quotes are comparable.
6. Timeline and submission instructions.
7. Evaluation criteria with weights that add up to 100%.
8. Terms placeholders (confidentiality, right to reject proposals) marked for legal review.
Also give a scoring sheet I can use to compare proposals.
```

## How to use it

1. **Agree on must-haves internally** before you send the RFP.
2. **Have legal review** the terms section.
3. **Score every proposal** with the same sheet, ideally with two reviewers.
