Many SaaS blogs publish generic articles that bring visitors but no trials. SaaS SEO works better when content is built around the product: use cases, integrations, alternatives, templates and problems your tool actually solves. This prompt builds a content strategy across the funnel, prioritised by how close each page type is to a signup, with outlines your team can start writing.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as an SEO strategist for B2B SaaS companies.

Product: [PRODUCT NAME AND ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
Ideal customers: [ROLES, COMPANY SIZE, INDUSTRY]
Main jobs the product does: [LIST 3–6]
Integrations: [LIST]
Main competitors: [LIST]
Free trial or freemium: [YES / NO]
Current site content: [E.G. 40 BLOG POSTS, NO USE-CASE PAGES]
Domain strength and resources: [E.G. NEW SITE, 4 ARTICLES A MONTH, ONE WRITER]

Create:
1. A list of page types with examples for this product: use-case pages, industry pages, integration pages, comparison and alternatives pages, templates or free tools, glossary, problem-focused blog posts.
2. A topic map table: topic, page type, funnel stage (bottom/middle/top), search intent, example target query, priority (1–3) and why.
3. Outlines for the top 3 bottom-of-funnel pages.
4. Guidelines for fair, accurate comparison pages.
5. Internal linking rules between blog posts, feature pages and signup.
6. A 6-month publishing plan that fits my resources.
7. Conversion elements for content pages (in-line product demos, template CTAs, trial prompts).
8. KPIs: organic signups, assisted conversions, rankings for priority queries, and how to track them.

Mark example queries as ideas to validate in a keyword tool, not as confirmed search volumes.
```

## How to use it

1. **Validate the example queries** in your keyword tool and drop those with no demand.
2. **Write bottom-of-funnel pages first**, since they convert soonest.
3. **Review organic signups per page** every month and update the plan.

## Tips

- Use real screenshots and workflows from your product; generic copy is easy for competitors to match.
- Keep competitor comparisons factual and dated, and update them when their product changes.
