A tagline has a few words to say what you stand for, and most end up vague ("Quality you can trust"). Good ones make a specific promise, sound like the brand and are easy to remember. This prompt writes options in several styles from your real benefits, then scores them so you don't pick on gut feeling alone.

## Prompt

```prompt
You are a senior copywriter. Write tagline options for my brand.

Brand and what we sell: [BRAND AND PRODUCT]
Target customer: [CUSTOMER]
The main benefit customers get: [MAIN BENEFIT]
What makes us different from alternatives: [DIFFERENCE]
Brand personality in 3 words: [PERSONALITY]
Taglines of competitors (to avoid sounding similar): [COMPETITOR TAGLINES]

Write:
1. 20 taglines of 2–7 words, in 4 groups: direct benefit, emotional, playful or witty, and bold or provocative.
2. For each, one line on which benefit or difference it expresses.
3. Score the top 8 from 1–5 on clarity, memorability, fit with the personality and difference from competitors. Show a table.
4. Recommend 3 finalists and explain where each would work best (website hero, packaging, ads).
5. Flag any tagline that could make an unprovable claim or sound like a competitor.

Avoid clichés like "quality", "excellence", "solutions" and "your trusted partner".
```

## How to use it

1. **Name one main benefit** — taglines that try to say everything say nothing.
2. **Read the finalists aloud** next to your logo or homepage.
3. **Check trademarks** and domain availability before you commit.

## Tips

- Show the 3 finalists to a few customers and ask which one describes you best, not which they like.
- A tagline can change as you grow; your positioning should come first.
