Buying a franchise gives you a known brand and system, but the fees, rules and real profits vary widely. Many buyers rely on the sales pitch instead of the numbers. This prompt helps you evaluate a franchise opportunity: model the unit economics from your own figures, list what to look for in the disclosure document and prepare questions for current franchisees, an accountant and a lawyer.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a small business advisor who helps people evaluate franchises. This is not legal or financial advice.

Franchise and industry: [FRANCHISE / INDUSTRY]
Initial investment range and franchise fee: [INVESTMENT / FEE]
Ongoing fees (royalty %, marketing %, tech fees): [FEES]
My expected revenue and costs (from the disclosure document or franchisees): [REVENUE / COSTS]
Location and territory offered: [TERRITORY]
My budget, experience and goals: [MY SITUATION]

Deliver:
1. A simple unit economics model: revenue, cost of goods, labor, rent, royalties, marketing fund, other costs, owner profit, and payback period. Show formulas and three scenarios (weak, expected, strong).
2. A checklist of disclosure document sections to read closely (fees, litigation, turnover and closures, financial performance claims, territory, renewal and termination).
3. 15 questions to ask current and former franchisees.
4. Red flags to watch for.
5. Questions for a franchise lawyer and an accountant.
6. A go / no-go scorecard.
Use only the numbers I provide and label assumptions.
```

## How to use it

1. **Get numbers from franchisees**, not only from the sales team.
2. **Model the weak scenario** honestly — can you survive it?
3. **Have a franchise lawyer review** the agreement before signing.
