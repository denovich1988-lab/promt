Interviews capture what people remember; a diary study captures what actually happens over days or weeks, in context. It is ideal for habits, long journeys and things that happen at unpredictable moments. This diary study plan prompt designs the goals, participant criteria, entry prompts, schedule and analysis so your study produces useful data instead of empty entries.

## Prompt

```prompt
Act as a senior UX researcher. Design a diary study.

Research goal and key questions: [GOAL / QUESTIONS]
Product or behavior to study: [TOPIC]
Target participants: [PARTICIPANTS]
Study length and number of participants: [DURATION / NUMBER]
Tool for entries (app, form, messaging): [TOOL]
Budget for incentives: [BUDGET]

Deliver:
1. Study overview: objectives, method, timeline.
2. Participant screener criteria and recruitment message.
3. Onboarding instructions for participants, including an example entry.
4. Entry prompts: a daily check-in, event-triggered prompts ("each time you…"), and 2–3 deeper weekly prompts. Include photo or screenshot requests where useful.
5. A reminder and engagement plan to prevent drop-off.
6. An incentive structure linked to completion.
7. Analysis plan: coding framework, how to track individual journeys over time, and how to present findings.
8. Ethics and privacy: consent, what data to avoid collecting.
```

## How to use it

1. **Run a 2-day pilot** with one or two people to fix confusing prompts.
2. **Read entries daily** and send follow-up questions while memories are fresh.
3. **Follow up with interviews** on the most interesting entries.
