ChatGPT can be a patient tutor who explains a topic five different ways at midnight before an exam. It can also be a shortcut that leaves you knowing nothing when the test arrives. The difference is mostly in the prompt. These **ChatGPT prompts for students** are written to make you do the thinking while the AI explains, quizzes and organises.

Each prompt has blanks in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`. Fill them in, paste the prompt into ChatGPT (it also works in Claude and Gemini) and keep the conversation going with follow-up questions.

## Before you start: three rules

1. **Tell it who you are.** "I'm a first-year nursing student" gets a very different explanation from "I'm 14 and in year 9."
2. **Paste your own material.** Lecture notes, the assignment brief or a textbook passage keep answers tied to what you are actually studying.
3. **Know your school's AI policy.** Many schools allow AI for studying and planning but not for writing graded work. When in doubt, ask your teacher.

## Understand a topic

### 1. Explain it at my level

```prompt
I'm a [YEAR / LEVEL] student studying [SUBJECT]. Explain [TOPIC] to me.
Start with a two-sentence plain-English summary, then explain the key ideas one at a time.
Use one everyday analogy and one example from [SUBJECT].
Finish by asking me two questions to check that I understood.
```

If the analogy doesn't click, reply "Try a different analogy." For more, see the [everyday analogies prompt](/prompts/explain-with-everyday-analogies/).

### 2. Be my Socratic tutor

```prompt
Act as a tutor for [SUBJECT]. I want to understand [TOPIC OR PROBLEM].
Don't give me the answer. Ask me one guiding question at a time, wait for my reply,
and give a small hint if I'm stuck. If I'm wrong, explain why before moving on.
```

This is the most useful prompt on the list for real learning. Our [Socratic tutor prompt](/prompts/socratic-tutor-guided-questions/) has a fuller version.

### 3. Walk me through a worked problem

```prompt
Here is a problem from my [SUBJECT] homework: [PASTE PROBLEM].
Don't solve it. Show me how to solve a similar problem with different numbers, step by step,
explaining why each step is done. Then let me try mine and check my working.
```

### 4. Clear up a confusion

```prompt
I keep mixing up [CONCEPT A] and [CONCEPT B] in [SUBJECT].
Explain the difference in a short table, give one example of each,
and tell me a simple way to remember which is which.
```

## Revise for exams

### 5. Build a revision plan

```prompt
I have exams in [SUBJECTS] on [DATES]. Today is [TODAY'S DATE].
I can study about [HOURS] hours a day, and I'm weakest at [WEAK TOPICS].
Make a day-by-day revision plan that covers every topic, gives extra time to weak topics,
includes short breaks and leaves the last day for light review only.
```

### 6. Turn my notes into a study guide

```prompt
Here are my notes for [SUBJECT / UNIT]: [PASTE NOTES].
Turn them into a one-page study guide: key terms with short definitions,
the 5 most important ideas, and any formulas or dates I must memorise.
Mark anything in my notes that looks incomplete or unclear.
```

The [exam study guide prompt](/prompts/exam-study-guide/) adds likely exam questions.

### 7. Quiz me

```prompt
Quiz me on [TOPIC] at [LEVEL]. Ask 10 questions, one at a time, mixing multiple choice
and short answer. Wait for my answer each time. Tell me if I'm right, explain any mistake,
and at the end list the topics I should review.
```

Testing yourself is one of the most reliable ways to remember material, so this beats rereading your notes. For a printable version, try the [quiz with explanations prompt](/prompts/quiz-with-explanations/).

### 8. Make flashcards

```prompt
Make [NUMBER] flashcards from this material: [PASTE TEXT OR TOPIC].
Format each as "Q: ... / A: ...". Keep answers under 20 words.
Include a few cards that ask "why" or "how", not just definitions.
```

You can import the result into a flashcard app. See the [spaced repetition flashcards prompt](/prompts/flashcards-spaced-repetition/) for a review schedule too.

## Plan and improve your writing

These prompts help you think and improve your own work. They don't write the essay for you.

### 9. Brainstorm and test a thesis

```prompt
My essay question is: [ESSAY QUESTION]. My first idea for a thesis is: [YOUR THESIS].
Tell me whether it is arguable, specific and answerable in [WORD COUNT] words.
Suggest two sharper versions, and list the main counter-argument I should address.
```

### 10. Check my outline

```prompt
Here is my outline for an essay on [TOPIC]: [PASTE OUTLINE].
Check whether each paragraph supports the thesis, whether the order is logical,
and where I need more evidence. Don't rewrite it; give me questions and suggestions.
```

The [essay outline and thesis check prompt](/prompts/essay-outline-thesis-check/) goes into more detail.

### 11. Get feedback like a teacher

```prompt
Here is my draft for [ASSIGNMENT] and the marking criteria: [PASTE CRITERIA].
Draft: [PASTE DRAFT]
Give feedback the way a teacher would: two strengths, the three most important
improvements in order of impact, and one sentence I should rework, with the reason.
Don't rewrite the essay.
```

### 12. Make sense of a difficult reading

```prompt
I'm reading [TITLE / TYPE OF TEXT] for [COURSE]. Here is a passage: [PASTE PASSAGE].
Explain the main argument in plain English, define any hard terms,
and give me three questions to think about before my seminar.
```

Don't ask ChatGPT to find sources or quotes for you without checking them. It can invent references that look real. Use your library database and check every citation yourself.

## Organise your time

### 13. Break a big project into steps

```prompt
I have a [TYPE OF PROJECT] due on [DUE DATE]. The brief is: [PASTE BRIEF].
Break it into small tasks with a suggested date for each, working back from the deadline.
Put the tasks I'm most likely to put off near the start.
```

### 14. Plan a realistic week

```prompt
Here is my week: classes [TIMES], work [TIMES], other commitments [LIST].
Tasks this week: [LIST WITH DEADLINES].
Make a weekly schedule with focused study blocks of 25–50 minutes,
time for meals, sleep and rest, and a buffer for things that run over.
```

### 15. Write a polite email to a teacher

```prompt
Write a short, polite email to my [TEACHER / PROFESSOR], [NAME], asking for
[AN EXTENSION / CLARIFICATION ON / A MEETING ABOUT] [TOPIC].
Reason: [YOUR REASON]. Keep it under 120 words and make it sound like a student, not a lawyer.
```

## How to get better answers

- **Follow up.** "Simpler please", "give another example" and "quiz me on that" turn one answer into a lesson.
- **Ask it to show uncertainty.** Add "Tell me if you're not sure about any fact." It won't catch every error, but it helps.
- **Check against your course.** Your teacher's notes and textbook decide what is right for your exam, not the AI.
- **Do the last step yourself.** Write the final answer, essay or summary in your own words. That's where the learning sticks.
