Most business owners try ChatGPT once with a vague request, get a vague answer and decide it is not worth the time. The fix is not a magic phrase. It is giving the model the same context you would give a smart new hire: what you sell, who buys it, what you have tried and what a good result looks like.

Below are 25 **ChatGPT prompts for business**, grouped by job: strategy, marketing, sales, customers, finance, operations and hiring. Each one has `[BLANKS]` you fill in. They also work in Claude and Gemini with no changes.

## How to get better business answers from ChatGPT

Before the prompts, four habits that make every one of them work better:

1. **Lead with context.** One or two sentences about your business, customer and goal change the answer more than any clever wording.
2. **Name the output.** "A table with columns X, Y, Z" or "a one-page memo with three options" beats "give me ideas".
3. **Ask it to ask you.** Add "Ask me up to 5 questions before you answer" when the task is big. You will get fewer generic replies.
4. **Check the facts.** ChatGPT can state wrong numbers, laws and market data with confidence. Use it for structure and drafts, not as your source of truth.

If you want a reusable base for all of this, our post on [how to write AI prompts](/blog/how-to-write-ai-prompts/) explains a simple 5-part formula.

## Strategy and planning prompts

**1. One-page business plan**

```prompt
Act as a small-business advisor. Write a one-page business plan for [BUSINESS IDEA] serving [TARGET CUSTOMER] in [LOCATION]. Include: problem, solution, customer, how we make money, main competitors, first 90-day goals and the three biggest risks. Mark any number you had to guess as an assumption.
```

For a longer version with fixed sections, use the [one-page business plan prompt](/prompts/one-page-business-plan/).

**2. SWOT with actions**

```prompt
Run a SWOT analysis for [COMPANY], which sells [PRODUCT] to [CUSTOMER]. Facts: [KEY FACTS]. For each strength, weakness, opportunity and threat, add one concrete action we could take in the next quarter. Present it as a table.
```

See also the [SWOT analysis with actions prompt](/prompts/swot-analysis-with-actions/).

**3. Decision memo**

```prompt
I need to decide between [OPTION A] and [OPTION B] for [GOAL]. Constraints: [BUDGET, TIME, TEAM]. Write a one-page decision memo: criteria, a scoring table, the risks of each option, what would change your recommendation, and a recommendation.
```

**4. Pressure-test an idea**

```prompt
Here is my plan: [PLAN]. Act as a skeptical investor. List the 7 strongest reasons it could fail, ranked by likelihood. For each, suggest a cheap test I could run in under two weeks to find out.
```

**5. Quarterly goals**

```prompt
Our strategy for next quarter is [STRATEGY]. Turn it into 3 objectives with 2-3 measurable key results each. Current numbers: [CURRENT METRICS]. Flag any key result that cannot be measured with the data we have.
```

## Marketing prompts

**6. Ideal customer profile**

```prompt
Based on these notes about our best customers: [NOTES], write an ideal customer profile for [PRODUCT]. Include job or life situation, main problem, what they tried before, objections, where they spend time online and the words they use to describe the problem.
```

**7. Value proposition options**

```prompt
Write 5 different one-sentence value propositions for [PRODUCT] aimed at [CUSTOMER]. Each should lead with a different benefit. Then say which one you would test first and why.
```

**8. 30-day content plan**

```prompt
Create a 30-day content plan for [BUSINESS] on [CHANNELS]. Audience: [AUDIENCE]. Goal: [GOAL]. Return a table with date, channel, format, topic, hook and call to action. Mix educational, behind-the-scenes and promotional posts.
```

**9. Rewrite a web page**

```prompt
Rewrite this homepage copy so a first-time visitor understands within 5 seconds what we do, for whom and why it is better: [CURRENT COPY]. Keep it under [WORD LIMIT] words. Give a headline, subheadline, three benefit bullets and one call to action.
```

More marketing templates are in our [ChatGPT prompts for marketing](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-marketing/) guide.

## Sales prompts

**10. Cold email**

```prompt
Write a cold email to [ROLE] at [TYPE OF COMPANY] about [PRODUCT]. Their likely problem: [PROBLEM]. Keep it under 120 words, plain and specific, with one question as the call to action. Give 3 subject line options.
```

**11. Objection handling**

```prompt
Prospects for [PRODUCT] often say: [OBJECTIONS]. For each objection, write a short, honest reply that acknowledges the concern, adds one fact or example, and ends with a question. Avoid pressure tactics.
```

**12. Follow-up sequence**

```prompt
Write a 4-email follow-up sequence for a prospect who [SITUATION, E.G. ATTENDED A DEMO BUT WENT QUIET]. Space them over [TIME PERIOD]. Each email must add something new: a resource, a use case, a question or a clear close.
```

**13. Pricing tiers**

```prompt
We sell [PRODUCT] to [CUSTOMERS]. Our costs per customer are about [COST] and competitors charge [COMPETITOR PRICES]. Suggest 3 pricing tiers with names, prices, what each includes, who it is for and which tier we want most people to choose.
```

The [pricing tiers strategy prompt](/prompts/pricing-tiers-strategy/) goes deeper on this.

## Customer service prompts

**14. Reply to an angry customer**

```prompt
A customer wrote: [MESSAGE]. Draft a calm reply that apologises for what went wrong without blaming them, explains what we will do next ([ACTION]) and when. Keep it under 150 words and match a [TONE] tone.
```

**15. FAQ from support tickets**

```prompt
Here are recent customer questions: [QUESTIONS]. Group them into themes, then write a FAQ with the 10 most common questions and clear answers based only on this information: [POLICY DETAILS]. Mark any answer you are unsure about.
```

**16. Review response templates**

```prompt
Write response templates for [BUSINESS TYPE] reviews: one for 5-star, one for 3-star and one for 1-star reviews. Each should be short, personal, mention a detail placeholder like {CUSTOMER DETAIL}, and invite further contact where relevant.
```

## Finance prompts

**17. Cash flow forecast**

```prompt
Build a simple 13-week cash flow forecast table for [BUSINESS]. Opening cash: [AMOUNT]. Expected weekly income: [INCOME]. Regular costs: [COSTS]. One-off payments: [ONE-OFFS]. Highlight any week where cash drops below [MINIMUM].
```

For a fuller template, see the [13-week cash flow forecast prompt](/prompts/13-week-cash-flow-forecast/).

**18. Explain the numbers**

```prompt
Here is our monthly profit and loss summary: [DATA]. Explain in plain English what changed versus last month, the three line items that matter most, and two questions I should ask my accountant.
```

**19. Cost-cutting review**

```prompt
Here is a list of our monthly expenses: [EXPENSES]. Sort them into must-keep, review and cut candidates. For each review item, suggest a cheaper alternative or a question to ask the supplier. Do not cut anything that affects [CORE SERVICE].
```

## Operations and productivity prompts

**20. Standard operating procedure**

```prompt
Turn these rough notes into a step-by-step standard operating procedure for [TASK]: [NOTES]. Include purpose, who does it, tools needed, numbered steps, common mistakes and a short checklist at the end.
```

**21. Meeting notes to actions**

```prompt
Summarise these meeting notes: [NOTES]. Return decisions made, open questions, and an action list as a table with owner, task and deadline. If an owner or deadline is missing, write "unassigned".
```

**22. Weekly priorities**

```prompt
Here is everything on my plate this week: [TASKS]. My main goal this month is [GOAL]. Sort the tasks into do now, schedule, delegate and drop, with one sentence explaining each choice.
```

## Hiring and team prompts

**23. Job description**

```prompt
Write a job description for a [ROLE] at [COMPANY], a [COMPANY DESCRIPTION]. Include a short intro, 5-7 responsibilities, must-have and nice-to-have skills, what success looks like after 90 days, and salary range [RANGE]. Use inclusive, plain language.
```

**24. Interview questions**

```prompt
Create 8 structured interview questions for a [ROLE] that test [KEY SKILLS]. For each, add what a strong answer would include and one follow-up question.
```

**25. Onboarding plan**

```prompt
Draft a 30-60-90 day onboarding plan for a new [ROLE]. Include who they should meet, what they should learn, first small wins, and how we will check in. Our team uses [TOOLS].
```

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Copying answers straight into decisions.** Treat market sizes, legal points and tax rules from ChatGPT as leads to verify, not facts.
- **Sharing sensitive data without checking.** Strip client names and confidential figures unless your plan's data terms allow it.
- **One giant prompt.** For big jobs such as a full marketing plan, break the work into steps. Our guide to [prompt chaining](/blog/prompt-chaining/) shows how.
- **Accepting the first draft.** Reply with "make it shorter", "more specific to [CUSTOMER]" or "give me three alternatives". The second answer is usually better.
