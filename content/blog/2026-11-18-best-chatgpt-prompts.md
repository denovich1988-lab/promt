Most "best prompts" lists give you a hundred one-liners like *"Write a blog post about marketing."* They are easy to copy and almost useless, because the AI has to guess everything that matters.

The prompts below are different. Each one gives ChatGPT a role, the context it needs and rules for the output. The parts only you know are marked as blue blanks: click one, type your answer and press **Copy prompt**. They also work in Claude and Gemini.

## Writing and editing

### 1. Rewrite anything in plain English

```prompt
Rewrite the text below in plain English for [AUDIENCE]. Keep every fact and number. Use short sentences, everyday words and no jargon. If a technical term is unavoidable, explain it in brackets the first time.

Text:
[PASTE TEXT]
```

### 2. Make AI text sound human

The full version, with a list of AI words to ban, is in our [make ChatGPT write like a human](/prompts/make-chatgpt-write-like-a-human/) prompt.

### 3. Shorten without losing meaning

```prompt
Cut this text to about [TARGET LENGTH] words. Keep the main point, every number and the call to action. Remove repetition, filler and anything the reader already knows. Show the shortened version, then list what you removed.

Text:
[PASTE TEXT]
```

### 4. Find the weak spots in your draft

```prompt
Act as a tough but fair editor. Read my draft for [AUDIENCE] and list the 5 biggest problems, in order of importance: unclear points, weak arguments, missing evidence, confusing structure. For each one, quote the passage and suggest a fix. Do not rewrite the whole draft.

Draft:
[PASTE DRAFT]
```

## Email and communication

### 5. Reply to a difficult email

```prompt
Help me reply to this email. My goal: [WHAT YOU WANT TO HAPPEN]. Tone: calm, polite and firm. Keep it under 150 words. Acknowledge their point in one sentence, state my position clearly, and propose a next step.

Their email:
[PASTE EMAIL]
```

### 6. Cold email that gets a reply

Use our full [cold email prompt](/prompts/cold-email-that-gets-replies/), which also writes two follow-ups.

### 7. Turn meeting notes into a summary

```prompt
Turn these meeting notes into a summary for people who were not there. Use three sections: Decisions, Action items (owner and deadline for each), Open questions. Keep it under 200 words. Do not add anything that is not in the notes.

Notes:
[PASTE NOTES]
```

## Work and business

### 8. Explain a topic before a meeting

```prompt
I have a meeting about [TOPIC] in an hour and I know very little about it. Explain the 5 things I must understand, in simple words, with one real-world example each. Then give me 3 smart questions I could ask in the meeting.
```

### 9. Compare options and decide

```prompt
Help me decide between [OPTION A] and [OPTION B]. My situation: [CONTEXT]. What matters most to me: [PRIORITIES]. Compare them in a table against my priorities, list the risks of each, and tell me which you would choose and why. Then tell me what information would change your answer.
```

### 10. Pre-mortem a plan

Imagine the plan has already failed, then find out why. The full template is in our [pre-mortem prompt](/prompts/pre-mortem-for-any-plan/).

### 11. Write a clear job description

```prompt
Write a job description for a [ROLE] at [COMPANY TYPE]. Include: a 2-sentence summary of the role, 5 main responsibilities, must-have skills (maximum 5), nice-to-have skills, and what success looks like after 6 months. Plain language, no clichés like "rockstar" or "fast-paced environment".
```

### 13. Explain it like I'm new

```prompt
Explain [CONCEPT] to me as if I am a smart beginner. Start with a one-sentence definition, then an everyday analogy, then how it works step by step, then one common misunderstanding. Finish by asking me 2 questions to check that I understood.
```

### 14. Quiz me

```prompt
Quiz me on [TOPIC] at [BEGINNER / INTERMEDIATE] level. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer. After each answer, tell me if I was right, explain briefly, and make the next question slightly harder if I got it right or easier if I got it wrong.
```

## Planning and productivity

### 15. Plan your week

```prompt
Here are my tasks for this week: [PASTE TASKS]. I have about [HOURS] hours of focused time. Sort the tasks into Must do, Should do and Can wait. Then build a simple day-by-day plan that puts the hardest work in my most productive hours: [YOUR BEST HOURS].
```

### 16. Break a big project into steps

```prompt
Break this project into small steps that each take under 2 hours: [PROJECT]. Order them, mark which steps depend on others, and highlight the first step I can start today in under 30 minutes.
```

### 17. Turn a messy idea into a plan

```prompt
Here is a messy idea I have: [DESCRIBE IDEA]. Ask me 5 questions to clarify it. After I answer, turn it into a one-page plan: goal, who it is for, first 3 steps, what it costs, and how I will know it worked.
```

## Creativity

### 18. Brainstorm without the obvious ideas

```prompt
Give me 15 ideas for [WHAT YOU NEED]. Skip the 5 most obvious ideas anyone would think of first. For each idea, add one line on why it could work for [AUDIENCE].
```

### 19. Name something

```prompt
Suggest 20 names for [WHAT YOU ARE NAMING], which is [SHORT DESCRIPTION]. Mix styles: descriptive, invented words, metaphors and short punchy names. Avoid names that are hard to spell or pronounce. Mark your top 3 and explain why.
```

### 20. Get honest feedback on an idea

```prompt
Here is my idea: [IDEA]. Play devil's advocate. Give me the 5 strongest reasons it might fail, then the 3 strongest reasons it might succeed. Be direct; do not soften the criticism.
```

## How to get better answers from any prompt

- **Fill in every blank with specifics.** "Owners of small cafés in Tashkent" beats "small businesses".
- **Keep the conversation going.** The first answer is a draft. Ask for shorter, simpler, more examples, a different tone.
- **Give an example of what you like.** Pasting a sample of good output is often worth more than a paragraph of instructions.
- **Check facts.** ChatGPT can sound confident and still be wrong, especially with numbers, names and recent events.
