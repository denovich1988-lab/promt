A job search is mostly repetitive writing and reading: scanning job ads, adjusting your resume, writing messages to people you don't know, preparing answers and following up. That is exactly the kind of work an AI assistant can speed up. The catch is that a vague request like "help me find a job" gets you vague advice.

The **ChatGPT prompts for job search** below are built to fix that. Each one asks for your real details, tells the model what to produce and sets limits so the result is usable. They also work in Claude and Gemini. Fill in the `[BLANKS]`, paste the prompt, and edit the output before you send anything.

## Before you start: give the AI the right inputs

Every prompt here works better with two things on hand:

1. **Your background.** Your resume, or five to ten lines covering your roles, skills, tools and two or three results you are proud of.
2. **One specific job ad.** Copy the whole posting, not just the title. The duties and "nice to have" lines carry most of the useful signal.

Strip out what the model doesn't need, such as your address, phone number or anything confidential from a current employer. And keep one rule in mind for the whole search: ChatGPT sometimes adds skills or numbers you never gave it. Read every line it writes about you and delete anything that isn't true.

## Step 1: Decide what to apply for

### 1. Find roles that fit your skills

Many searches stall because the target is too broad. This prompt turns your experience into a short list of job titles worth searching for.

```prompt
You are a career coach. Based on my background below, suggest 6 job titles I could realistically apply for now.

My background: [PASTE RESUME OR SUMMARY]
What I want more of: [E.G. REMOTE WORK, LEADING PEOPLE, LESS TRAVEL]
What I want to avoid: [E.G. SALES TARGETS, NIGHT SHIFTS]

For each title give: why my background fits, the main gap I would need to explain, and 3 search keywords to use on job boards. Put the safest fits first and one stretch role last.
```

If you are switching fields rather than moving up, the [career change plan prompt](/prompts/career-change-plan-skills/) goes deeper on transferable skills.

### 2. Decode a job ad before you apply

Job ads mix must-haves with wish lists. Ask the model to separate them so you don't skip roles you could get, or waste time on ones you can't.

```prompt
Analyse this job ad for me.

Job ad: [PASTE FULL JOB AD]
My background: [PASTE RESUME OR SUMMARY]

Give me:
1. The 5 requirements that matter most, in order
2. Which of those I clearly meet, partly meet or don't meet
3. Words and phrases from the ad I should reuse in my resume
4. Two questions I should ask the employer about this role
5. A fit score from 1 to 10 with one sentence explaining it
```

## Step 2: Tailor your documents

### 3. Match your resume to one job

Sending the same resume everywhere is the most common mistake in a search. This prompt rewrites only what needs to change.

```prompt
Tailor my resume to the job ad below. Keep every fact true: do not add skills, tools, titles or numbers I did not give you.

Resume: [PASTE RESUME]
Job ad: [PASTE JOB AD]

Rewrite my summary in 3 lines, reorder and reword my bullet points so the most relevant come first, and list any keywords from the ad that are missing from my resume. Mark each changed line with (changed).
```

For a version with more structure, use our [resume tailoring prompt](/prompts/chatgpt-resume-tailoring/), and see [12 ChatGPT prompts for your resume](/blog/chatgpt-prompts-for-resume/) for bullet points and summaries.

### 4. Write a short cover letter

```prompt
Write a cover letter of no more than 220 words for this job.

Job ad: [PASTE JOB AD]
Why I want this job: [ONE OR TWO HONEST REASONS]
Two results that prove I can do it: [RESULT 1], [RESULT 2]
Tone: [E.G. WARM AND DIRECT]

Open with why this company and role, not with "I am writing to apply". Use plain sentences. End with a simple next step.
```

The [cover letter from a job post prompt](/prompts/cover-letter-from-job-post/) is a fuller template if the employer expects a longer letter.

### 5. Polish your LinkedIn profile

Recruiters often look at your profile before your resume. Keep it consistent with the roles you are targeting.

```prompt
Rewrite my LinkedIn headline and About section for someone targeting [TARGET JOB TITLE] roles.

Current headline: [PASTE]
Current About section: [PASTE]
Key skills and results: [LIST]

Give 3 headline options under 220 characters and one About section of 150-200 words in the first person. Avoid buzzwords like "passionate" and "results-driven".
```

## Step 3: Reach people, not just job boards

### 6. Ask for an informational chat

```prompt
Write a short LinkedIn message to [PERSON'S ROLE] at [COMPANY]. I want a 15-minute chat to learn about their team, not to ask for a job directly.

Who I am: [ONE LINE ABOUT YOU]
Why them: [WHAT YOU NOTICED ABOUT THEIR WORK OR COMPANY]

Keep it under 80 words, make it easy to say no, and give one specific question I would ask.
```

### 7. Follow up after applying

Many applications get no reply. A brief, polite follow-up a week or two later is reasonable.

```prompt
Write a follow-up email for a job I applied to [NUMBER] days ago.

Role and company: [ROLE] at [COMPANY]
Who I'm writing to: [NAME AND ROLE, OR "HIRING TEAM"]
One thing I can add since applying: [NEW DETAIL, PROJECT OR LINK]

Under 100 words, friendly, no guilt-tripping, with a clear subject line.
```

## Step 4: Prepare for interviews

### 8. Predict the questions

```prompt
I have an interview for [ROLE] at [COMPANY]. Based on the job ad below, list the 10 questions I am most likely to be asked: 4 about skills, 4 behavioural and 2 about motivation. For each, explain in one line what the interviewer is really checking.

Job ad: [PASTE JOB AD]
```

### 9. Build STAR answers from your experience

```prompt
Help me answer this interview question with the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result): "[INTERVIEW QUESTION]".

My rough story: [DESCRIBE WHAT HAPPENED IN A FEW LINES]

Turn it into an answer I can say in about 90 seconds. Keep my facts, make my own actions clear, and ask me for any missing result instead of inventing one.
```

Our [STAR interview answers prompt](/prompts/star-interview-answers/) and [questions to ask the interviewer](/prompts/questions-to-ask-interviewer/) cover both sides of the conversation.

### 10. Run a mock interview

```prompt
Act as the interviewer for a [ROLE] position at a [TYPE OF COMPANY]. Ask me one question at a time and wait for my answer. After each answer, give two lines of feedback: what worked and what to tighten. After 6 questions, summarise my strongest and weakest answers.
```

## Step 5: Close the loop

### 11. Send a thank-you note

```prompt
Write a thank-you email to [INTERVIEWER NAME] after my interview for [ROLE] today. Mention this topic we discussed: [TOPIC]. Add one sentence that reinforces why I fit: [YOUR STRONGEST POINT]. Under 120 words.
```

### 12. Prepare for the salary talk

```prompt
I received an offer for [ROLE] at [COMPANY]: [OFFER DETAILS]. My target is [TARGET NUMBER] because [YOUR REASONS, E.G. MARKET RESEARCH, CURRENT PAY, SCOPE OF ROLE].

Write a short, polite script for asking for more, a reply if they say the budget is fixed, and two non-salary items I could ask for instead. Keep the tone collaborative.
```

Check pay ranges yourself on salary sites or in job ads for similar roles; don't rely on a number the AI suggests. The [salary negotiation script prompt](/prompts/salary-negotiation-script/) adds more structure for the call.

## Keep the search moving each week

A job search drifts when you only react to new postings. Once a week, ask the model to plan the next seven days with you: how many tailored applications, which people to contact, which skills to practise. Our [weekly job search plan prompt](/prompts/chatgpt-job-search-weekly-plan/) does exactly that.

Then keep a simple tracker of where you applied, who you messaged and when to follow up. Paste last week's list into the chat and ask what to prioritise next.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Sending AI text unedited.** Recruiters read many applications, and generic phrasing stands out. Rewrite openings and endings in your own words. Our guide on [making ChatGPT write like a human](/blog/make-chatgpt-sound-human/) helps.
- **Letting the model invent results.** Add "do not invent facts" to every prompt and check the output anyway.
- **One prompt for every job.** Paste the specific job ad each time; that is what makes the output relevant.
- **Sharing too much personal data.** Leave out contact details and confidential company information.
