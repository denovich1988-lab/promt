Social media asks for a lot of small pieces of writing, every week, on several platforms. That is exactly where ChatGPT helps: it can plan a month of topics, draft captions in your voice and turn one article into a dozen posts. The catch is that a vague prompt gives you the same bland post everyone else gets.

The **ChatGPT prompts for social media** below are built to avoid that. Each one asks for the details that make a post specific: who it is for, which platform, what voice and what you want readers to do. Fill in the `[BLANKS]`, paste the prompt into ChatGPT (or Claude or Gemini) and edit the result before posting.

## Before you start: build a brand brief

Every prompt here works better if the model knows your brand. Run this once at the start of a chat, then use the other prompts in the same conversation.

```prompt
Remember this brief for the rest of our conversation.

Brand: [BRAND NAME]
What we offer: [PRODUCT OR SERVICE]
Audience: [WHO THEY ARE AND WHAT THEY CARE ABOUT]
Voice: [3-4 WORDS, E.G. FRIENDLY, DIRECT, A LITTLE DRY]
Words and phrases we avoid: [LIST]
Main platforms: [PLATFORMS]
Current goal: [E.G. EMAIL SIGN-UPS, BOOKINGS, AWARENESS]

Here are two posts that sound like us:
[PASTE 2 PAST POSTS]

Reply with a one-paragraph summary of our voice so I can check you understood it.
```

## Planning prompts

### 1. Content pillars

Pillars are the three to five themes you post about again and again. They stop you from starting from zero every week.

```prompt
Using my brand brief, suggest [NUMBER] content pillars for [PLATFORM]. For each pillar give: a name, why it matters to my audience, three example post ideas and the format that suits it best (carousel, short video, text post, etc.).
```

For a deeper version, see our [content pillars prompt](/prompts/social-media-content-pillars/).

### 2. A 30-day calendar

```prompt
Create a 30-day posting calendar for [PLATFORM] based on my content pillars. Post [NUMBER] times per week. Show it as a table with columns: date, pillar, post idea, format, hook (first line) and call to action. Include [EVENTS OR LAUNCHES THIS MONTH].
```

The [30-day social media calendar prompt](/prompts/30-day-social-media-calendar/) gives you a fuller template.

### 3. Post ideas from customer questions

Real questions make the best posts because you know someone wants the answer.

```prompt
Here are questions customers have asked us recently:
[PASTE QUESTIONS]

Turn each one into a social post idea for [PLATFORM]. For each, give a hook, the main point in two sentences and a format suggestion. Flag any question that would work better as a longer piece.
```

## Writing prompts

### 4. Scroll-stopping hooks

The first line decides whether anyone reads the rest.

```prompt
Write 10 opening lines for a [PLATFORM] post about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE]. Mix these styles: a surprising statement, a specific question, a short story opener, a common mistake and a bold opinion. Keep each under [NUMBER] words and avoid clickbait or claims I can't back up.
```

### 5. Captions in your voice

```prompt
Write 3 caption options for a [PLATFORM] post. The image or video shows: [DESCRIBE IT]. The point of the post: [KEY MESSAGE]. Call to action: [CTA].

Option 1: short and punchy. Option 2: a mini story. Option 3: practical tips. Match the voice from my brand brief and suggest up to [NUMBER] relevant hashtags for each.
```

Our [Instagram captions prompt](/prompts/instagram-captions-for-brand/) is tuned for Instagram specifically.

### 6. One idea, five platforms

```prompt
Adapt this idea into posts for LinkedIn, Instagram, X, Facebook and TikTok (as a short video script): [IDEA].

Change the length, tone and structure to suit how people use each platform. Keep the core message the same. Label each version clearly.
```

The [five-platform post prompt](/prompts/social-media-post-five-platforms/) adds more structure if you post everywhere.

### 7. A LinkedIn post from a real experience

```prompt
Turn this experience into a LinkedIn post: [WHAT HAPPENED]. What I learned: [LESSON]. Write it in first person, in short paragraphs, with a clear opening line and a question at the end to invite comments. No buzzwords, no "I'm humbled to announce".
```

## Repurposing prompts

### 8. A thread from an article

```prompt
Turn the article below into a thread of [NUMBER] posts for [X OR THREADS]. Post 1 must work on its own as a hook. Each post should make one point. End with a summary and a link placeholder {LINK}.

Article:
"""
[PASTE ARTICLE]
"""
```

See also the [X thread from article prompt](/prompts/x-thread-from-article/).

### 9. Short clips from a long video

```prompt
Here is the transcript of a [LENGTH] video: [PASTE TRANSCRIPT].

Find [NUMBER] moments that would work as standalone short clips. For each, give the start and end lines, a suggested on-screen title and a caption. Choose moments with a clear point, a strong opinion or a useful tip.
```

## Community and review prompts

### 10. Replies to comments

```prompt
Draft replies to these comments on our [PLATFORM] post about [TOPIC]:
[PASTE COMMENTS]

Keep replies short, friendly and in our voice. For complaints, acknowledge the issue and suggest moving to [SUPPORT CHANNEL]. Don't promise refunds, dates or anything I haven't confirmed. Mark any comment I should handle personally.
```

### 11. A competitor content scan

```prompt
Here are recent posts from [COMPETITOR OR SIMILAR ACCOUNT]:
[PASTE POSTS]

Summarise the themes, formats and hooks they use. Point out gaps my brand could fill and suggest 5 post ideas that take a different angle. Don't suggest copying their wording.
```

For a full audit, try the [competitor social media audit prompt](/prompts/competitor-social-media-audit/).

### 12. Monthly performance review

```prompt
Here are last month's posts with their results:
[PASTE TABLE: POST, FORMAT, PILLAR, REACH, ENGAGEMENT, CLICKS]

Identify which pillars, formats and hooks performed best and worst. Suggest 3 things to do more of, 2 to stop and 1 experiment for next month. Base everything only on the numbers I gave you and say if the sample is too small to draw a conclusion.
```

## Tips for better results

- **Keep one chat per brand.** The brief stays in context, so you don't have to repeat it.
- **Ask for options, not answers.** Three versions make it easy to pick the one that sounds most like you.
- **Edit before you post.** Add a personal detail, a real number or a recent example. That is what makes a post feel human; our guide on [making ChatGPT write like a human](/blog/make-chatgpt-sound-human/) has more fixes.
- **Check facts and trends.** ChatGPT may not know about recent platform changes, current trends or your latest offer. Verify before publishing.
