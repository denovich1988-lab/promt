Most marketing prompts fail for the same reason: they ask for copy before the thinking is done. "Write a Facebook ad for my product" gives you something that could advertise anything. The prompts below follow the order a marketer would work in, starting with audience and message, then moving to campaigns, channels and review.

Each one is a template. Replace the `[UPPERCASE]` parts with your own details, and paste in real material wherever the prompt asks for it. They work in ChatGPT, and also in Claude or Gemini.

## Strategy and positioning

### 1. Define a sharp target audience

```prompt
I sell [PRODUCT OR SERVICE] for [PRICE RANGE].
Our current customers are mostly [WHAT YOU KNOW ABOUT THEM].

Suggest 3 possible target segments. For each, give:
- who they are in one sentence,
- the main problem we solve for them,
- where they already look for solutions,
- one reason they might not buy.

Then recommend the segment to focus on first and explain why in 3 sentences.
```

### 2. Find your unique selling point

```prompt
Here is what we offer: [PRODUCT DESCRIPTION].
Our main competitors are [COMPETITOR 1], [COMPETITOR 2] and [COMPETITOR 3], and they mostly promise [WHAT THEY CLAIM].

List 5 possible unique selling points we could own. For each one, rate how believable it is for us (high / medium / low) based on the facts I gave, and what proof we would need to show.
Do not invent features we did not mention.
```

For a deeper version with competitor gaps, use the [unique selling proposition finder](/prompts/unique-selling-proposition-finder/).

### 3. Write a positioning statement

```prompt
Write 3 versions of a positioning statement for [BRAND] using this structure:
For [TARGET CUSTOMER] who [NEED OR PROBLEM], [BRAND] is the [CATEGORY] that [MAIN BENEFIT], unlike [MAIN ALTERNATIVE], because [REASON TO BELIEVE].

Keep each under 50 words. After each, note who inside the company it would most appeal to and one weakness.
```

### 4. Turn customer language into messaging

```prompt
Below are real customer reviews, support messages or interview quotes about [PRODUCT].
Pull out:
1. The exact phrases customers use to describe their problem.
2. The outcomes they care about most.
3. Words they use that we never use in our marketing.

Then write 5 headline ideas that use their language, not ours.

Customer text:
[PASTE REVIEWS OR QUOTES]
```

### 5. Build a buyer persona from evidence

```prompt
Create a buyer persona for [PRODUCT] based only on the notes below.
Include: role and context, goals, frustrations, buying triggers, objections, and the channels they trust.
Mark anything that is a guess with (assumption) so I can verify it.

Notes:
[PASTE INTERVIEW NOTES, SURVEY ANSWERS OR SALES CALL NOTES]
```

If you have interview transcripts, the [customer persona from interviews prompt](/prompts/customer-persona-from-interviews/) goes further.

## Campaigns and planning

### 6. Write a campaign brief

```prompt
Write a one-page marketing campaign brief.
Product or offer: [OFFER]
Goal: [GOAL, E.G. 200 TRIAL SIGN-UPS]
Audience: [AUDIENCE]
Budget and timeline: [BUDGET], [DATES]
Channels we can use: [CHANNELS]

Sections: objective, audience insight, key message, proof points, channel plan, deliverables, how we will measure success.
Keep it under 400 words and flag any gap in my inputs.
```

Our [marketing campaign brief prompt](/prompts/marketing-campaign-brief/) has a fuller template with stakeholder sections.

### 7. Plan a launch timeline

```prompt
We are launching [PRODUCT OR FEATURE] on [LAUNCH DATE] to [AUDIENCE].
Team: [WHO IS AVAILABLE]. Channels: [CHANNELS].

Create a launch plan split into 3 phases: 4 weeks before, launch week, 4 weeks after.
For each phase list the tasks, owner role, channel and a simple success measure.
Present it as a table.
```

### 8. Brainstorm content pillars

```prompt
My business: [BUSINESS DESCRIPTION]. Audience: [AUDIENCE].
Suggest 4 content pillars we could post about for the next 6 months.
For each pillar give: why the audience cares, 5 post ideas, and the best format (short video, carousel, article, email).
Avoid topics that only talk about us; at least 3 pillars should be useful to the reader without buying anything.
```

See the [social media content pillars prompt](/prompts/social-media-content-pillars/) for a version that also maps pillars to goals.

## Channel copy

### 9. Ad copy variants for testing

```prompt
Write ad copy for [PLATFORM] promoting [OFFER] to [AUDIENCE].
Create 6 variants, each built on a different angle:
1. pain point, 2. desired outcome, 3. social proof (leave a placeholder, do not invent reviews), 4. objection handling, 5. curiosity, 6. urgency based on [REAL DEADLINE OR LIMIT].

For each: primary text (under [CHARACTER LIMIT] characters), headline (under 40 characters) and the angle name.
```

The [Facebook ad copy variants prompt](/prompts/facebook-ad-copy-variants/) is tuned for Meta ad formats.

### 10. Email subject lines worth testing

```prompt
Write 10 subject lines for an email about [EMAIL TOPIC] sent to [LIST DESCRIPTION].
Mix styles: question, benefit, number, personal, plain and direct.
Keep each under 50 characters. Add a preview text of under 90 characters for each.
Then pick 2 to A/B test and explain what each test would teach us.
```

### 11. A short nurture email sequence

```prompt
Write a [NUMBER]-email sequence for people who just [ACTION, E.G. DOWNLOADED OUR GUIDE ON TOPIC].
Goal of the sequence: [GOAL].
Email 1 delivers value, the middle emails handle [MAIN OBJECTION] and show proof, the last email makes a clear offer: [OFFER].

For each email give: send day, subject line, body under 150 words and one call to action.
Voice: [3 WORDS DESCRIBING YOUR VOICE].
```

### 12. Repurpose one piece into many

```prompt
Turn the content below into:
- 3 LinkedIn posts (under 200 words each, different hooks),
- 5 short social posts for [PLATFORM],
- 1 email newsletter section of about 150 words,
- 3 ideas for short videos with a one-line hook each.

Keep the facts exactly as written. Do not add statistics.

Content:
[PASTE ARTICLE, TRANSCRIPT OR TALK NOTES]
```

### 13. Landing page section review

```prompt
Review the landing page copy below for [AUDIENCE] who arrive from [TRAFFIC SOURCE].
Score it 1-5 on: clarity of the offer, relevance to the visitor, proof, and strength of the call to action.
Then list the 5 changes most likely to increase [CONVERSION GOAL], in order of impact, with a rewritten line for each.

Copy:
[PASTE LANDING PAGE TEXT]
```

## Measurement and review

### 14. Make sense of campaign results

```prompt
Here are the results of our [CAMPAIGN NAME] campaign:
[PASTE METRICS BY CHANNEL OR AD, E.G. SPEND, CLICKS, SIGN-UPS, SALES]

Our goal was [GOAL].
1. Summarise what worked and what did not in 5 bullet points, using only these numbers.
2. Suggest 3 possible explanations for the weakest result, and how we could test each.
3. Recommend what to keep, cut and try next month.
Do not calculate metrics I did not give you unless you show the formula.
```

### 15. Monthly marketing retro

```prompt
Act as a facilitator for our monthly marketing review.
What we planned: [PLAN]
What happened: [RESULTS AND NOTES]
What the team said: [FEEDBACK]

Produce: 3 wins, 3 problems with their likely causes, 3 lessons, and a short list of actions with owner roles for next month.
Keep the tone factual and blame-free.
```

## How to get better marketing output from ChatGPT

- **Feed it real material.** Reviews, sales call notes and past results make answers specific. Without them, you get average copy.
- **Ask for options, then choose.** Five headline variants and one edit from you beat one "perfect" headline.
- **Keep a voice guide handy.** Paste a short description of how your brand sounds, or create one with the [brand voice guide prompt](/prompts/brand-voice-guide/).
- **Watch for invented proof.** The model may add statistics, awards or testimonials that do not exist. Remove them or replace them with real ones.
- **Check platform rules yourself.** Character limits and ad policies change, so confirm them in each platform's current help pages.
